    “What is your name?”

    She groaned in frustration. “I told you, I don’t fucking know.” And wasn’t that even more frustrating? That she really didn’t know? Because she really didn’t.

    The last thing she remembered is an intense headache, which still hadn’t dulled completely by the way, and then she remembered a few faces floating above her head, but none of them were clear and none of it made sense. And then she’d opened her eyes in this interrogation room.

    The room was empty except for the heavy steel table and chair that were bolted to the floor. And she was currently sitting on the chair, with her hands in cuffs and tied to the table. In all the time she’d been in here, which must be around two days now, nobody had entered the room. A hidden panel on her right opened to supply her with water and something edible and the chain that tied her cuffs to the table was just long enough for her to get up and reach it. It was not long enough, however, to comfortably lie on the floor. So the few hours shed managed to get some sleep, she also managed to get herself a stiff neck and an aching back. And really, why didn’t they just come in? It was almost like they were afraid she was going to do something, but she didn’t even know what. She didn’t even know her own name goddammit!

    Which was why when the intercoms crackled again and that same voice asked “What is your name?”, like she hadn’t told him a million times over by now, she slammed her hands on the table. “I. Don’t. Fucking. Know!”

    She screamed, more to let her frustration out than anything else, and then let her face drop on her arms. This was just really, really annoying.

    Something on her left creaked, and she peered up. A door opened, and a tall blonde woman came through, a gun in a holster on her thigh, and a clipboard and pen in her hand. Once the woman was inside the door closed again and clicked shut.

    She inspected the wall, but saw absolutely nothing that indicated where the door was. Fascinating.

    Then she looked at the woman, who was now moving opposite from her and leaned against the far wall. She eyed her curiously, but the woman ignored her in favour of leafing through the papers on the clipboard, before she got to an empty page and took the pen. Finally, the woman looked up.

    “So you don’t know your name?”

    Immediately, she rolled her eyes. It wouldn’t be much improvement if they just send someone inside to ask her the same stupid question she couldn’t even answer. “No,” she replied nevertheless, because the woman was still looking at her like she expected an answer. “I really don’t. Trust me, I’m as frustrated as Intercom Guy seems to be.” The woman cracked a tiny smile. “He doesn’t believe you. Luckily for you, I do, and he got sent away for not getting anywhere. However, I do need a name. We can’t just keep calling you ‘girl in the interrogation room’.”

    She ignored the implications of more people outside this room that were probably discussing her. And also maybe probably knew more about her than she knew about herself. “So I just make something up?”

    The woman shrugged, then cocked her head. “Think of it as a new start. You get to try again. So yeah, a new name. Whatever you like.”

    She thought for a moment. A new start. Try again. That meant she was free, right? That she could do anything? For some reason, that thought unnerved her, but she also felt a longing. It was a weird combination, and she physically shook herself still under the watchful eye of the other woman.

    She could do anything. There was that expression, what was it? Sky is the limit. She looked at the woman. “Skye,” she said. “I like the name Skye.”

    The woman nodded, seemingly satisfied, and jotted something – probably her new name – down on the clipboard. “It’s nice to meet you, Skye.” She moved pen and clipboard to one hand, and stepped forward, her other hand held out for the newly named Skye to shake. Skye shook it, noting the strong grip with which the woman shook her hand.

    “My name is Bobbi,” the woman said, before retrieving her hand. Skye mourned the loss of contact; it was the first physical touch she could remember since ever. Her brain was just incredibly empty in the memory department. And even if she focussed really hard, she just came up blank.

    She shook herself again, and focussed back on the woman – Bobbi. “Is that a made up name too?” she asked, mostly just to make conversation. It wasn’t like she had anything to say. Bobbi smiled. “Well, my real name is Barbara, and Bobbi is somewhat of a nickname, so yeah. I suppose that counts as a made up name?”

    Skye nodded. “I think it does,” she decided. She also decided Bobbi wasn’t so bad. She did have a gun strapped to her thigh and Skye got the feeling from her that she wasn’t afraid to use it. But she was being nice and she believed Skye and wasn’t asking her the same question over and over again. In her book, that was a massive plus already.

    Bobbi leaned back against the wall. “So, tell me. What do you know about yourself?” Now that was a good question. The answers... well, Skye didn’t like that as much. “I’m a woman,” she started. “I know what things are, so I haven’t lost any and all memory. And I know I have a headache.”

    “A headache?” Bobbi repeated, while scribbling something down. Skye nodded. “Since you picked me up. Or since someone picked me up. My memory on who exactly did what is a bit fuzzy.”

    “But you remember being... picked up by us?”

    Skye shrugged. “I wouldn’t say remember. My last active memory is a couple of faces hovering above me and then I woke up here. Logic would determine that it was you who picked me up and brought me here, though I do not really remember that happening. Probably because I was out cold.”

    Bobbi nodded, but didn’t write anything down. She cocked her head at Skye. “I’ll consult with the doctor about giving you some painkillers.” Skye smiled. “That would be great, thank you.”

    There was a moment of silence as both gathered their thoughts. Not that Skye had a lot, because thoughts were based on memories and she had very little of those. She still appreciated that Bobbi seemed willing to give her a few seconds.

    Suddenly the other woman veered of the wall. Skye felt her body tense at the unexpected reaction, and Bobbi immediately stilled. “Sorry,” she said, though Skye didn’t really think she was sorry. “I didn’t mean to startle you.” Again, Skye doubted it. Something was up with this woman, as nice as she was being. It felt like she was being tested. She inclined her head, the smallest sign that she had heard her. Bobbi looked at her. “What can you remember? From before we picked you up, I mean.”

    Skye thought for a long time. What did she remember? Nothing... substantial. Small things. Feelings, sensations. The burning of liquor down her throat. The pain of a knife sliding across her skin. Her eyes flashed to her right arm, a long thin line running across it from her elbow up to where it disappeared under her T-shirt. Huh.

    She tried to remember more. There was more pain, a sting in her lower abdomen, a fire in her left shoulder that still lingered from time to time. But also, the smell of rain and snow when she lay outside. The feeling of someone else’s skin against hers. And a woman. There was something about this woman, but Skye couldn’t for the life of her figure out what. It was all jumbled and vague and not enough. But she knew, instinctively, that this woman was important.

    She looked up at Bobbi, who had been patiently waiting for an answer all this time. “It’s not... it’s not memories that make sense. It’s flashes, and feelings, and sensations. I... I feel pain. But I feel snow too. Cold and wet. The smell of the rain. Drinking. It’s all just fleeting.” She huffed and clenched her fists. “I can’t hold on to anything.”

    She decided to keep the woman to herself for now as she watched carefully for Bobbi’s reaction. The blonde just nodded, and scribbled some stuff down. “It’s okay. If you put enough still images together you’ll eventually get a film.” She gave Skye an encouraging smile, and Skye did feel slightly better.

It was something she’d be wondering since she woke up, but she had no one to ask the question to, so really wondering was her only option. She was convinced the answer couldn’t surprise her, but when Bobbi chuckled and replied “Washington DC” she kind of was.

    “I’m in DC? Damn.” She really had been expecting something more... interesting. Oh well. “And why am I here? This doesn’t seem like a usual prison.” She frowned. “Not that I remember doing anything that would require me being in prison.”

    “You attacked one of my co-workers,” Bobbi quipped. Skye’s eyebrows raided to her hairline. “I’m sorry? Are they okay? Why would I do that?”

    Bobbi gave her a intrigued look, but took her time responding. “That’s what were trying to figure out,” she finally answered, her voice carefully void of emotion now. “But yeah, she is okay.”

    After a moment of hesitation, Bobbi sighed and continued. “To come back to your previous question, you’re in a SHIELD holding cell. Since it was a SHIELD-agent you attacked, we have the right to hold you here until we have our answers. No lawyers, and not a lot of regular law.”

    Until they had their answers. That would be quite a while then. Also... “SHIELD?” That name, she knew that name. “That’s a spy organisation. Except not a public one.” She looked at Bobbi. “You’re a spy. An agent,” she realised. It made sense with how the woman held herself, and the way she interrogated. Also, the gun on her thigh.

    She shook her head. How was she tangled up in all this?

    Bobbi eyed her for a few moments longer. “Have you ever had chocolate?” she suddenly asked.

    “I’m not allowed,” she responded without missing a beat. Then she frowned. That had felt... automatic. Like someone had drilled that into her head. Was she allergic or something? Bobbi’s eyes flashed, and for a moment Skye thought she saw victory in those intense blue eyes. But before she could be sure, it was gone, and she was back to knowing nothing. She watched as Bobbi moved the pen to the hand holding the clipboard. “I’ll come back tomorrow with chocolate. And if anyone tells you you’re not allowed to have it, you just tell them that I said its okay.”

    That sounded good. Chocolate was supposed to be good, right? She’d heard about it. Not in a way she remembered, but she must have, because she associated it with sweetness and comfort and winter. And yet she could say with certainty that she’d never had it. She looked up at Bobbi, who was moving backwards in the general direction of the door. “You’re leaving?” she asked, and her voice sounded sad to her own ears. Bobbi gave a kind smile. “I am, but I will be back.”

    “Tomorrow?” Skye asked.

    “Tomorrow,” Bobbi promised.

    And then the door opened and she was gone, and Skye was left alone with even more jumbled thoughts and a name that was not her own but maybe it was now. She laid her head down on her arms and closed her eyes.

    It was easier to keep track of time now, with Bobbi coming once a day, so she knew she’d been stuck in here for a total of nine days when it happened.

    The second day Bobbi had come, she had indeed brought chocolate. And it tasted amazing. And she had not been allergic, because she was alive and thriving. Which led her to a half- hour breakdown on why she was never allowed chocolate and who had never allowed it. The third day Bobbi brought more chocolate, and also painkillers against the headache, but also a whole lot of questions Skye couldn’t answer. And as patient as Bobbi was, she could see it frustrated her too. And she figured it was a bad idea to frustrate the woman with the gun, so she tried, she really tried. But her memories remained jumbled and vague. Day four and five were much the same, only without the chocolate. The sixth day, no one came. She’d woken up from an uncomfortable sleep and had stretched her neck in the hope to get the kinks out, and then stretched her back. She’d felt her joints pop and sighed. The table did not make for a good pillow. She’d expected Bobbi to enter and start asking questions, but nothing had happened. At some point during the day, her food had arrived to the wall panel on the right, and she stood up with a small groan to get it. It was as tasteless as usual. The rest of the day remained lonely, with more food coming through later, and then the lights in the room were dimmed, the only sign she had that it was time to sleep. Though she supposed, they could be messing with her and ruining any form of biological clock she had. But she chose to ignore her doubts and laid her head on her arms and tried to sleep. And now it was the seventh day since Bobbi first came, and her ninth day in total. In a way it was the ninth of her life. She was preparing herself for another day in solitude while eating her breakfast. Cheep cereal and a bottle of water. She really missed the chocolate. Maybe that is why someone hadn’t allowed her to eat it.

    The door opened, and she looked up. Bobbi entered, the same all-black outfit se wore every time, gun strapped to her thigh. She was holding a folded chair and her clipboard with pen. What was more surprising was the guy behind her holding a tray, with a breakfast similar to Skye’s own.

    “Good morning, Skye. I apologise for not stopping by yesterday. Do you mind if we have breakfast together?” Bobbi asked cheerily as she placed the clipboard on the bolted down steel table Skye was using as breakfast table and pillow. She shrugged. “If you want to. Do I have a choice?”

    Bobbi cocked her head. “We always have a choice.”

    Skye frowned. “I don’t think I ever had.” She gestured with her spoon to the other side of the table, the cuffs and chain clinging together. “Sit down.” Bobbi took the invitation with a smile and put down the chair. She moved to take the tray from the other guy, who turned around and left, the door clicking shut behind him. Skye watched it all happen. “The door doesn’t open from the inside out, does it?”

    Bobbi smiled at her as she put down her own breakfast. She had soggy cereal too. Maybe it was some sort of ration. “Why, planning your escape yet?”

    Skye lifted her cuffed hands, the spoon still in her hand. “As you can see I’m stuck. I was just wondering that if I made a move to kill you and they wouldn’t be in time to open the door you would be stuck here.”

    She didn’t know what answer she’d been expecting, but laughter wasn’t the one. As Bobbi poured orange juice into two glasses and shoved one towards Skye, she got a hold of herself again. “The only reason they are even letting me in here is because both me and my superiors are confident I can hold you off until help arrives.”

    Skye nodded. “Yes I’ve seen the gun. But if you’re so sure of yourself, then why am I chains? I don’t even remember what I did. I don’t even know what I could do to you.” She looked at her glass of orange juice longingly, but didn’t touch it. “Just, sleeping on the ground would already be so much better.”

    After Bobbi had stared at her for a few moments, the agent nodded. “You make a good point. Except, my superiors are afraid that you’re just fooling all of us, and that when we take away the cuffs, you’ll do what you came here to do.”

    Instead of asking Bobbi what the fuck it was that she came here to do, she cocked her own head. “And do you believe that?”

    Bobbi picked up her glass and drank the contents in one long gulp. “I think you’re fooling yourself. Whether intentionally or not.”

    That didn’t make sense. Why would she fool herself? Why would she hold her own memories from herself? So many questions, so few answers. She looked at her by now empty bowl of breakfast, then at the orange juice. Hesitantly she reached out, half expecting it to be some ploy, but nothing happened and she tipped it back quickly. It was nice, if a bit sour. But it wasn’t water, and that alone was really good.

    She set down the glass and watched as Bobbi finished her own cereal. The agent had been nice, if a bit frustrated at their lack of progress so far. But Skye was frustrated herself too, so she couldn’t hold it against the blonde. However, she reminded herself, this was SHIELD. A spy agency. Bobbi was a spy, an agent. She could be playing games with her head. Or she could be genuinely being nice. She just didn’t know. She didn’t know anything. Bobbi must have felt her looking, because she looked up between bites. “What?” Skye hesitated. “You believe me, right?”

    It was silent for a beat. “I believe you when you say that you don’t remember anything,” she settled for. Skye supposed that was more than she could expect. “Do you know how I can get my memory back?”

    Now, Bobbi put her spoon down in her bowl and pushed it away. “I have two theories. Would you care to hear them?” She didn’t wait for an answer as she started to put the bowls and glasses onto the tray and put the tray away. “My first theory, is that you went through something traumatic and thus your brain blocked your memories. To protect you, in a way.” Skye frowned. “You don’t believe that.” She didn’t know what made her think that Bobbi didn’t, but she knew it was true the moment she said it. Bobbi dipped her head I’m acknowledgement.

    “I don’t. It doesn’t add up with you forgetting pretty much everything. And the traumatic thing would have to have happened between you shooting my colleague and us taking you. And that is a time span of mere minutes.”

    “Well,” Bobbi started. “It’s more likely, but at the same time I’m not even sure its possible. My second theory, is that someone wiped your brain from all memories.” She was staring at Skye like she was expecting some sort of reaction, but Skye just looked back, and Bobbi continued, choosing her words carefully. “I think that you were after my co-worker for a reason, and that when we moved to take you in, they somehow wiped your brain. I think that your memories are still somewhere inside your brain, just... locked away.” Skye took a moment to process that, and Bobbi let her. “And do your superiors agree with this... theory?”

    Bobbi shook her head slowly. “This theory is new. We have two scientist working on whether its even possible to wipe someone’s brain like that.” She looked at Skye. “Would you be okay with a scan? To see if we can find something in your brain?”

    “I suppose. I like this as much as you do,” Skye told her. “Would that mean I can get my memory back?”

    Bobbi eyed her carefully for a few long moments, then the gun twisted in her hands and suddenly it was on the table in front of her. Half an hour ago, there had been a bowl of cereal and now there was a gun. She really hated her new life. She tore her gaze from the gun to Bobbi, not relaxing in the slightest. She had a feeling the woman could pick it back up as fast as she could put it down, and that was fast enough to kill her before she could scream. “What was that for?” she asked, the steadiness of her voice surprising herself. Bobbi nodded to the gun. “Disassemble it.”

    Skye blinked at her. “Excuse you? Do I look like the person who would know how to do that?”

    “Since you shot my co-worker, yeah,” Bobbi deadpanned.

    Skye crossed her arms. “Sorry to disappoint, but I don’t fucking remember that.” Bobbi crossed her arms too. “Pick it up.”

    Skye looked at the gun, hesitated for a moment and then picked it up. It was heavier than she expected. Heavier than she remembered, a little voice in the back of her mind said. She twisted it in her hand, then took it properly and pointed it at Bobbi, her finger brushing the trigger. Bobbi was looking at her with a raised eyebrow, curious but not afraid. “It’s an ICER,” Skye said while lowering the gun.

    How the fuck did she know that? Maybe the weight combined with the fact that Bobbi didn’t look afraid of being shot? But then again, what did she knew about the weight of guns? Across from the table, Bobbi nodded. “It is. Now, dismantle it.”

    Skye looked at the gun. She wouldn’t know where to start. But apparently something in the back of her mind that did know. She twisted the ICER in her hands. Maybe if she... She closed her eyes and let her hands control her movements. Even if her memory had been wiped, muscle memory was harder to get rid of. And when she opened her eyes again, the gun was disassembled, all parts laid out neatly on the table. She would so love her cereal back.

    She looked up at Bobbi, who was hiding both excitement and impression behind a thoughtful expression. “Reassemble it.” This time, Skye didn’t close her eyes just watched in amazement as her hands puzzled the gun back together. With a sound that echoed through the room she clicked the magazine back in place and loaded the gun.

    Before she could put it down, Bobbi commanded “Shoot.”

    Skye shot. She didn’t really aim at anything, and the dendrotoxin bullet splashed harmlessly against the wall to her left. Bobbi looked shocked, but Skye hardly had time to interpret it, because her mind was doing... things.

    Gunshots. Echoes of bullets and pain. Blood, bright red on the otherwise unblemished snow. More gunshots.

    She threw the gun away and pressed her hands to her ears, hissing when she cut her cheek on the chain.

    A knife cutting open her cheek. Screams. Pain. A pool of crimson no one could survive. And that fucking woman again, her hair as red as the blood on her hands.

    Were these really her memories? If so, she wasn’t sure she wanted them back. She was vaguely aware of Bobbi screaming, and maybe she was screaming too, but her head hurt like mad and she swore she could smell blood. She slammed her head on the table in a last attempt to quiet the screaming in her head, and the last thing she heard before everything went black was Bobbi screaming her name.

Well, her gun… With a sigh she started disassembling the gun Skye had shot her with. Yes, she kept it. In her defence, it was a good model, good on the weight distribution. And her own had been useless after one of the widows attacking HQ had tried to put a bullet through it. A weird tactic, but quite effective.

    Once all the parts were neatly in front of her on the table, she started assembling it again. This process repeated itself a few times, and then her phone rung. She was just clicking the mag back into place and loading it, and almost pulled the trigger in a reflex at the sudden sound, but her annoyance disappeared when the screen read ‘Mel’. She quickly picked it up. “Mel? Everything alright? What did you find?”

    Normally Melinda would have made fun of her voice, somewhere between desperation, exhaustion and hope. But now, she sounded oddly serious. “We found a few bodies; none of us were hurt in any way. On another note, I’m going to send you two pictures.” Maria pulled the phone from her ear and checked her screen. Sure enough, two messages from Melinda popped up. She opened them, and her heart immediately stopped. As if in a trance, she put the phone back to her ear. “Dreykov. And Petrovich.” Holy shit. “Yeah, that’s what I thought. The other bodies were probably guards, though they haven’t done a great job. But it seems that whoever broke into your apartment left us a present, and it’s the two leaders of the Red Room. Dead.” It was silent for a moment. “Dead for a few days as well, by the looks of it.”

    Maria was having trouble fully understanding what was happening. “Get Natasha there, if she is willing. We need her to confirm it’s them. Especially for Ivan Petrovich. We thought we had them once before, but…” She didn’t have to finish her sentence.

    Her eyes caught on the bottle of orange juice again. It had seemed insane. Impossible. A mad thought. But… She put Skye’s gun down on the table and walked over to the window, looking out over the city. “She’s not dead,” she said softly, almost in wonder. “What? Who isn’t?” Mel asked impatiently.

    “Skye.”

    A moment of silence. “You’re mad.” Then, after another pause, “We buried her today.” “We buried some rocky shards today,” Maria snapped. “And then my apartment was broken into, I found a set of coordinates leading to Dreykov and Petrovich dead, and a fucking bottle of orange juice.” She took a deep breath. “I’m not mad, Melinda. Wouldn’t call myself delusional either. But this… Call it a gut feeling, but I don’t think she’s as gone as we think.” For a long few minutes, there came no response. Then, finally, Melinda sighed. “I’m going to call Natasha, get her on a jet this way. Bobbi too, I think, because I’m not quite sure how she will handle this.”

    Maria would be mad about the lack of response to her thoughts, but she found them quite unbelievable herself, so she let it go. “Please do,” she said instead.

    “You go get some sleep, finally. Don’t dare to show yourself at work for tomorrow. I’ll have Phil call me if you do.” The ‘and then you’re in trouble’ remained unsaid, but Maria caught it anyway. “I’ll handle this with Woods.”

    “Okay,” Maria promised. “I’ll take the day off.” She was suddenly feeling very tired anyway. “I’ll see you later.”

    “See you later.”

    Maria hung up, and put her phone back in her pocket, her gaze still on the city outside. The lights twinkled, a stark contrast against the dark sky. Melinda might think her mad. But she knew. She knew she was right. Her eyes trailed the city skyline, vaguely visible in the night. Svoboda.

    Yeah, it seemed that Skye had indeed finally found her freedom.
