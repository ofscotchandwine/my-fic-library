Lena gave a slightly guarded, wary smile, as she stepped out on the balcony. “Hello, Supergirl.”

Synthesis

“Supergirl may have saved me, but Kara Danvers… you are my hero.”

Kara was always so warm. Lena held her tightly, overwhelmed with gratefulness. Gratefulness that Kara had been able to convince Supergirl to believe in her, convincing the kryptonian to save her. Gratefulness that Kara had worked so hard to clear Lena’s name. Gratefulness.

And a twinge of unbearable guilt.

Lena let go of the blonde, watching as she turned away and left Lena’s office, leaving Lena alone in her thoughts. Lena briefly stared after her, her eyes unseeing - all she could see was the cloud of green that resulted from Metallo’s explosion, how Supergirl had risked her life to pull Lena out from the blast. 

Lena sank back into her office sofa, her stomach doing flipflops as anxiety licked at her nerves. Supergirl trusted Kara, and Kara trusted her. And the transitive property that led Supergirl to trust her was, thus far, unbroken. But Lena knew it would be fragile at best. If she ever danced too close to the edges… the super would change.

The problem was, Lena had already danced along such an edge, when she manufactured the synthetic kryptonite. It seemed no one suspected her, believing instead that it had been synthesized by Cadmus, despite the kryptonite being taken from L-Corp. And she never intended for it to fall into Metallo’s hands, into Lillian’s hands. It was a safeguard, a drastic measure to be used only in the most dire of circumstances.

Could Supergirl understand that?

Lena leaned forward, picking up the white knight from Lillian’s chess set. Lillian was partially right - while Supergirl’s reputation would uphold her, the public forgiving the occasional mistake, Lena’s reputation would flip to “villain” with ease… and it’d always be an uphill battle to bring it back.

It wasn’t going to stop Lena from trying. But it had the potential to be a very lonely road. Doing good was complicated, messy. And she wasn’t always going to see eye-to-eye with Supergirl, even if they were on the same side. That was something she was willing to wrestle with, but it was going to become harder, knowing that Kara and Supergirl were close, all the while her own feelings towards the reporter were becoming less professional… and less platonic.

Lena swallowed hard as she gazed at the chess piece, thinking back to Lex and Lillian’s reaction to her childhood self arriving at Luthor mansion. She had picked up on chess quickly, learning to beat Lex quickly, eventually racking up trophy after trophy. A prodigy, they had called her. Her bloodline and upbringing virtually guaranteed that secrecy and strategy would always be her instincts.

Lena closed her eyes. Shedding the Luthor legacy was going to be hard - trust didn’t come easy to her, and maybe it wouldn’t to Supergirl either. But she… she had proven herself somewhat, hadn’t she? Helping shut down Veronica’s alien fight club, taking down Chet Miner’s gang, making Medusa inert?

Everything in her was screaming to play the game, keep her secrets, keep doing good but keep everything close to the vest. She was a Luthor, not a Danvers. And this was the gamble that could lose her everything.

Lena leaned forward, gently placing the white knight on the chess board. But this is my chance, she thought to herself. My chance to start all over.

Kara landed softly on Lena’s condo balcony. She tried to hold back her surprise - she had always met Lena in her office, and didn’t know what to expect when Lena had asked her (Kara Danvers) if she (Supergirl) could stop by that evening. Lena’s place was… nice. Really nice. Kara schooled her expression, trying to keep the definitely-unheroic dazzled look off her face.

Lena, having heard the taps of Kara’s landing, turned around from her place on the kitchen island. She nodded in greeting, grabbing her glass - a whiskey, maybe a scotch - and walked towards the balcony. Kara tilted her head… the Luthor’s heartbeat was unusually fast.

Lena gave a slightly guarded, wary smile, as she stepped out on the balcony. “Hello, Supergirl.”

Kara smiled warmly, toning down her usually-formal way of interacting with Lena as Supergirl, in favor of trying to make the clearly nervous woman more comfortable. “Miss Luthor. Kara Danvers said you wanted to speak with me?”

“Metallo had synthetic kryptonite,” Lena replied, turning to overlook the city. “It exploded because it was unstable.”

“I made it.”

Lena glanced down at her drink, biting nervously at her lower lip. “It… it didn’t make much of a splash outside of your city. People barely knew who you were. Some small headline about Superman’s little cousin having a bad day, blowing up a couple of cars. God, the tabloids wrote it off as PMS.”

Lena turned again, facing the kryptonian, her face tense with stress. “But it still highlighted a problem to me. That someone with your abilities could go unchecked.”

Kara could feel her own heart racing at this point. A Luthor with kryptonite. A Luthor with kryptonite. A Luthor that can make kryptonite. It slowly registered in her head that Lena had been talking about the red kryptonite incident, though she likely didn’t know the true cause. “That wasn’t– I didn’t–”

“I know,” Lena said, cutting her off. “I know that’s not you. That something must’ve happened.” Kara paused, her chest rising and falling a touch too rapidly. Lena glanced nervously in her direction, before turning her head back to the city. “But if something like that happened to you again? Or your cousin? Or if someone with your abilities shows up and wants people to suffer, and we don’t know how to make the one thing that can stop them…”

Kara closed her eyes tight, trying to control the nauseous feeling overwhelming her body. Every instinct in her being wanted to fight what Lena was saying. But amidst the panic was the thought: What if they had this against Non? What if the other kryptonians hadn’t gone into stasis on Fort Rozz before she fought him and Indigo?

She could never have fought them all.

Kara opened her eyes, examining the woman in front of her, half turned away. Lena was scared, that much was apparent. But she was… trying. Kara let out a slow breath. “Why are you telling me this?”

Lena looked back at her glass. “You saved me. You risked your life for me. Because Kara Danvers believes in me.” Lena turned back up at Kara again. “I’m not sure you would’ve done that if you knew.”

Lena pulled back from the railing, taking a step closer to Supergirl. Her gait was steady, and her shoulders were squared, but Kara could hear the hammering of her heart as she spoke. “If you ever feel threatened by me, I’d be easy to take care of, Supergirl.” Kara’s eyebrows drew together, as Lena continued, “You could kill me, and tell the world I tried to kill you - and there’s not a soul who would doubt you.” Lena paused for a moment, as the seemingly silent not even Kara Danvers filled the gap. 

Lena’s eyes landed on Kara’s, the strange sadness in them hitting Kara in the chest as she continued. “You have an out that you can use at any time.” Kara held her gaze for a moment. The woman before her terrified her. But for a moment, she can feel how scared Lena is, that Kara might take that option now.

Kara’s mind wanders, to a world where Lena had let Medusa go off as planned - all aliens besides Kara and Kal, dead. Lena working with Lillian to manufacture enough kryptonite to finish the job. It would have been so easy, but it wasn’t the world Lena wanted. 

The shock began to fade, as Kara’s expression softened. Lena wasn’t asking for trust, she was asking for a chance.

Lena glanced down at her feet. “Good night, Supergirl,” she said softly, before turning back to walk inside. Kara watched as Lena stepped through the door, the glass slowly falling shut behind her. 

With a step, Kara turned, and launched into the night sky.

She’s avoiding me, Lena thinks to herself, as she stepped into the CatCo elevator, pushing the button for the ground floor.

She and Kara had been planning to visit a new kombucha place - but Kara had forgotten. Or had she? Supergirl told her about the kryptonite, Lena thought, her mind racing. Kara doesn’t want to be around me anymore.

Lena clenched her eyes shut as the elevator began its movement downwards. Kara really had seemed to forget. And there was good reason - Lillian was abducting aliens, and Snapper wasn’t letting Kara publish any sort of warning… of course Kara was stressed. The abductions were repulsive as it was, and Kara had a personal stake in this, being so close to an alien.

Lena shook her head. It didn’t matter whether Kara was avoiding her or not. Lillian needed to be stopped, and Lena was going to follow every L-Corp breadcrumb until she found her.

Now the sweet, kind reporter was going to overhear the last moments of a friend’s brutal murder - and Lena hated the thought of leaving that with her. 

But the breeze didn’t last long, and yet the ground never came. Lena suddenly found herself in strong arms, floating incredibly back to the top. 

“Drop something?” the body cradling her asked, as adrenaline continued to course through Lena’s veins. She watched as Supergirl breathed out, bashing her assailants’ heads against the wall. Relief flooded through her, and Supergirl gently set her down on the solid balcony.

They chatted briefly - about Supergirl’s lucky rescue, having been catching up with Kara over coffee at the time, before diving into business on Cadmus. Lena knew that the location of the old LuthorCorp facility would be enough - if she was right about Lillian’s location, the kryptonian would be able to stop the alien deportations.

Supergirl nodded another thanks, nudging Lena to call up her security team to deal with her still-unconscious assailants, clearly intent on staying until the men were in cuffs.

Lena turned to her desk phone, finger reaching for the button - but she paused for a moment, her hand lowering to grip the table as she slumped forward over it. Assassination attempts… she could usually manage through them in the moment, but they always sapped her energy. The adrenaline crash, she supposed.

Lena shut her eyes, murmuring under her breath, knowing the super would hear, even if a normal human would struggle. “You could’ve let me fall,” Lena says quietly.

She could sense the hero tense up behind her - in shock, in confusion. Lena heard her intake of breath, hearing boots coming towards her, rounding the desk so that the steely blue eyes could pierce into Lena’s own. 

“I’m not going to let you die just because I’m nervous,” Kara said emphatically from across the desk. “You’re saving so many people, Miss Luthor.”

“You didn’t know that until after you caught me. Why? Why are you trusting me?”

The kryptonian shuffled in place, tensing her jaw, before speaking. “Kara Danvers still believes in you.”

Lena wondered how long it would haunt her. Jack, choking as he fought the nanobots with the ghost of free will he had left - as Supergirl fought to breathe, her life moments from ending. Lena’s emotions had been cycling for hours. Hating Beth, the bitter loathing that came with wanting her to die in prison. Loving Jack, that sense of rekindling something old, or at least having an old friend back in her life. Protecting Supergirl, the odd and terrifying sense of the tables being turned, the kryptonian’s life in her hands.

Lena leaned into Kara, as Kara pulled her close. Kara was always so warm, comforting and cozy in a way that Lena had rarely felt past the age of 4. Maybe in Andrea’s arms, once upon a time. It didn’t matter to Lena, in that moment, whether Kara would ever be romantically interested in her. Lena could think of nothing more precious than this friendship.

“I’m right here. And I’m not going anywhere.”

Lena trembled, ever so slightly, before quashing the impulse. She wondered if Kara had felt it. A flutter of weakness from the usually-composed Luthor. “Promise?”

“I will always be your friend,” Kara replied, as Lena closed her eyes. Kara’s forehead rested gently against Lena’s, her arms tightening, “And I will always protect you. I promise.”

Lena closed her eyes, willing herself to feel again.

It was the sort of meeting that could only happen under an alien invasion. The four of them were certainly unlikely allies. Superman’s face was tense, eyeing both Lillian and Lena with hints of wariness.

But Lena was relieved that Supergirl seemed more cautiously optimistic. Lex’s kryptonite dispersal device was designed to kill her and her cousin - but with some amount of tweaking for compatibility with lead, they could get rid of all the Daxamites instead.

“Mon-El would have to leave Earth,” Kara said somberly, turning to Kal. 

Lena’s stomach twisted. Was this a betrayal of Kara? The only person who believes in me, and I’m going to stab her through the heart. “Did you know that he’s dating Kara Danvers?” Lena said.

Kara’s gaze left Kal’s to meet Lena’s, only to drift to Lillian’s, and then Lena’s again. “Kara Danvers would understand what’s at stake,” she said firmly. “Start working.”

“I… I bought it.”

“You’re kidding. … … Are you kidding?”

Lena was flustered. A rare occurrence, but one increasingly common as the Kara’s hold over Lena’s heart continued to tighten. Not that Lena would ever expect anything to be reciprocated by the tragically straight reporter. And definitely not after Lena was nearly single-handedly responsible for forcing Kara’s boyfriend off the planet.

Really, she was lucky Kara was talking to her at all.

“I mean, I trust you? How often do you get to work with your best friend?”

Something flashed in Kara’s eyes, and Lena wondered if she overstepped. It wouldn’t take a therapist to see why this friendship could begin fraying. 

Lena put the thought away, as Morgan Edge barged his way into her office. Her friendship with Kara might be fragile… But a classic male chauvinist? This, she could handle.

Lena knew it was too good to be true. 

It had been an adjustment period, after Mon-El had left. But slowly, the reporter had started coming around L-Corp again, bringing laughter and coffee and invitations to girls’ nights. Lena didn’t know how badly she had missed the heart text messages, until they started appearing on her phone again.

Sometimes Lena wondered, if there was a lingering look, if there was an expression that wasn’t just about friendship. But she shoved it aside as always.

But now - now she was the Luthor that poisoned children. And god, she couldn’t even deny it. Everything she touched was poison, and Kara would be better off putting as much distance between Lena and herself as humanly possible.

Lena didn’t want to be here. Lena didn’t want to be in Sam’s place - what if Ruby had been among the poisoned? She didn’t want to watch Kara clean up the broken wine glass that Lena had shattered. She didn’t want Kara to be fighting, promising to turn over every rock, to follow every lead.

“I know you believe that everything is good, and kind. And that- is one of the things I love about you,” Lena said, her voice cracking as she held Kara’s gaze, trying to will her to understand. “But that’s not the real world.”

Her heart raced, but her muscles didn’t scream, adrenaline running through her veins as she crawled up and up. With a last frantic leap, she felt her arm brush against the kryptonian’s, feeling a warm hand grip hard at Lena’s forearm. Lena gasped as her body jerked against her shoulder, the cockpit falling away beneath her.

“Why?” Lena whimpered. She couldn’t finish her question, but it was clear nonetheless - why risk National City for me?

Kara’s breath hitches for a moment, before she murmurs her response. 

“Kara Danvers would never forgive me if I let something happen to you.”

It was eating at her. She needed to know.

For her best friend.

Lena leaned forward, pulling her friend into a tight embrace, fighting the tears threatening to appear on her own face. “Kara,” she whispered, burying her head in her friend’s shoulder, as Kara’s arms tentatively wrapped around her. “Kara, Kara, Kara…”

Lena’s eyes roved over the city before her, glowing in the cool night air from her place on the L-Corp balcony. It had… been a strange few weeks. It had been a strange year.

Things were starting to settle back to normal. She and Kara were spending a lot of time together - though without Sam, who felt she’d be much more comfortable back in Metropolis. So it was back to the quick lunches during the week and joining in on game nights. Lena learned a lot more about Kara’s work as Supergirl, laughing as Kara sheepishly explained how she had tried to hide her conflicting responsibilities. 

Okay, the destruction of the alien detector device was a little less funny. Weeks of wasted debugging on a nonexistent overheating issue. Lena ultimately pulled the device - feeling stranger about the ethics as she got closer to Supergirl - but the principle of proper engineering…

But overall, Lena’s life settled into something… warm. It was an odd sensation, having this family she was invited into.

The only downside were the occasional nightmares, the familiar spike of anxiety causing fluttering feelings in her chest. Because it had been Kara who Jack’s nanobot swarm surrounded and almost killed. It had been Kara who Reign left in a beaten heap on a concrete sidewalk. It had been Kara who risked her life in Metallo’s blast.

To her left, Lena heard the familiar double-tap of sturdy boots, bringing a smile to her lips as she turned to the blonde. Kara smiled back, though her stance was uncharacteristically stiff, with her arms behind her back and a nervous expression on her face. Lena’s head tilted, eyebrows slightly drawn, in a silent question.

Kara took a step towards her, fidgeting slightly. She opened her mouth, as though about to speak, before pursing her lips together again. With a small huff, she drew her arms from behind her back, revealing a small bouquet of plumerias. 

Lena couldn’t hold back a small gasp, her eyes moving from the flowers to Kara’s eyes. A small smile appeared on Lena’s lips, as she reached out to accept the suddenly-shy kryptonian’s offering. She looked up to see the blonde sheepishly smiling back.

“Kara Danvers likes you, too.”
