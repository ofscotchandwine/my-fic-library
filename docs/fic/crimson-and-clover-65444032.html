    Given that Agatha was twenty their relationship wasn’t illegal, but it would certainly have been frowned upon. Rio would have lost her job on the spot, and then where would the two of them have been? As it was, she had been able to leave the place that had been her home all her life with dignity, if not contentment, and start afresh.

    But she missed Agatha every day, from the moment she woke up until the moment she fell asleep.

    She had seen bits and pieces about her in the tabloids — photographs of her leaving nightclubs with friends, shades on, skirts short as ever. The pictures would bring Rio to a standstill in the grocery store when she saw them on the covers of magazines by the checkout, her heart beating to the rhythm of Agatha, Agatha, Agatha. She yearned for her. She hadn’t been with anybody else since, and she didn’t know how she would even begin to contemplate that.

    Rio had thought about trying to contact her, but she didn’t know where to start, and hadn’t Agatha told her, venom in her voice, that they would never see each other again?

    Rio realised that she’d been standing on the sidewalk in the baking sun for at least five minutes, and disgruntled passersby were having to push past her. She decided to do what she always did to celebrate good news and go and buy herself a new book. She would inscribe the first page with the date and the event, so she would always remember why she’d got it. She turned the corner and headed for her favourite queer indie bookshop, The Witches’ Road. She’d buy a poetry book, she decided, and sit there and read for a while to calm herself down. The unspoken, unshared excitement threatened to consume her.

    The bookshop was cool inside. The bell tinkled as she entered and Lilia, the owner, looked up and smiled from behind the register. They’d become quite familiar with each other over the past few months, always exchanging greetings. Rio knew she should tell her about the book deal, but for some reason she couldn’t bring herself to. She just nodded to her as she passed and headed to the poetry section, scouring the shelves for something which felt right.

    She didn’t know how long she had been standing there when a commotion outside jolted her out of her concentration. She could hear the clicks of shutters before she could see them. There was always someone being tailed by the paps in LA, and she’d learnt to pay it no mind. There seemed to be a lot of them this time, though, and they were yelling.

    “What do you think of the bill your mother is trying to pass, which could make miscarriage punishable by up to ten years in prison?”

    Rio’s head snapped up.

    “Agatha, what about your mother’s comments about homosexuality at the Republican convention yesterday?”

    The bell tinkled again, this time much more loudly, as someone slammed through the shop door. Rio’s heart almost stopped. Before her eyes, for the first time in six months, stood Agatha.

    Her chest was rising and falling fast as though she was trying to catch her breath. She had huge sunglasses on and her hair was loose, falling almost to her waist.

    “You can go over there, dear, they aren’t allowed to come in,” Lilia said, waving in Rio’s general direction. “Just wait it out, they’ll give up soon enough.”

    Rio’s feet felt rooted to the spot, but she managed to slink back into a corner that was more hidden. She couldn’t form a single coherent thought in her mind now that she’d seen Agatha. She didn’t know what she would say if Agatha saw her too.

    Agatha crossed the room quickly and hid behind some shelves, pulling her sunglasses off and running her hand through her hair. She looked older, Rio noticed with a pang. She’d lost some weight, and there was none of that brazen confidence in the way she carried herself now. She was hunched over, still catching her breath.

    The paparazzi crowded outside the window, one of them knocking on the glass. Lilia, to her credit, strode across the room, flung open the door with a dramatic jangle of the bell and shouted, “Be gone! There’s nothing to see here!”

    Chastened, the paps began to slink away.

    Closing the door, Lilia went over to Agatha. Rio watched as she composed herself as if she hadn’t just been seconds from a panic attack.

    “It’s alright, dear,” Lilia said softly, laying a hand on Agatha’s arm. “They’ll be gone soon enough. You can stay as long as you want.”

    Watching this interaction, Rio felt certain that the two of them must have met before. She knew from experience that Agatha didn’t accept kindness from just anyone. She watched as Agatha nodded and brushed a tear from her eye.

    “Thank you,” she said quietly. “But I really should go.”

    “You don’t have to,” Lilia said. “Let me make you some tea. Come into the back room.”

    Agatha, with a shyness that Rio had never seen in her before, nodded, and followed Lilia behind the cash register and through a red curtain into the back, disappearing again, just like she had six months before.

    Rio let out a breath she hadn’t realised she’d been holding. She had to get out of here, now.

    Wanting to get as far away from the bookshop as possible, powered by something like fight or flight, Rio had ducked into a taxi and crossed the city, barely thinking about where she was going.

    She ended up in a bar she’d never been to before, something a little more upmarket than she was used to. It had been quiet when she arrived, but as the evening wore on it began to pack out. By 10pm, she was sitting at the bar nursing what she had decided would be her final drink, feeling a sleepy drunken buzz that did very little to calm her inner turmoil.

    Seeing Agatha had shaken her completely. All of the feelings that had never gone away had come back even sharper, along with questions. Was the bookshop that Rio was a regular at also a favourite of Agatha’s? How had they never crossed paths there before?

    The bar was showing signs of turning into a club, a dancefloor opening and a crowd of young people pouring in, glammed up in sparkly dresses and heels. This was decidedly not Rio’s milieu. She signalled to the bartender that she wanted to close her tab, and was moving to climb off the bar stool when something made her pause.

    She would know the scent of that perfume anywhere.

    But surely not. Not twice in one day, not after all this time.

    The scent grew headier, and Rio turned her head slightly to look at the woman who had just appeared next to her, stumbling slightly into her side with no apology.

    “A double shot of vodka, please,” the woman shouted at the bartender, slurring slightly, swaying on her feet. Her profile was the same as the one Rio saw every night in her dreams. Agatha. Again.

    The bartender hadn’t heard her, and Agatha leaned forward to ask again. She was wearing a scandalously short black mini dress, and stiletto heels that could not possibly be comfortable. She kept knocking into Rio’s side without realising. Rio could tell that she was incredibly drunk, her eyes glazed over.

    “Don’t serve her, she doesn’t need any more!” someone yelled over Rio’s shoulder, presumably one of Agatha’s friends.

    The bartender made a sympathetic face. “I can give you some water?” she suggested.

    “Fuck this,” Agatha snapped, and pushed herself off the bar, stumbling into the crowd behind her in the direction of the bathroom. Rio was moving before she’d even made the decision mentally, heading after her.

    “Excuse me, your tab!”

    “Shit, sorry.” Rio leaned over a few people to hand over the cash. “Keep the change,” she shouted, and then practically sprinted for the bathroom.

    There was only one cubicle, of course, and a queue of irate-looking people outside it. Agatha was not one of them. Rio knocked on the door hard.

    “She just cut the queue,” someone grumbled.

    “Agatha,” Rio called, knocking louder. “Let me in.”

    Miraculously, the door clicked open, and Rio practically fell into the bathroom, shutting and locking it behind her to sounds of protest. When she turned around, Agatha looked dazed, leaning against the sink and swaying.

    “Agatha?” Rio tried, stepping towards her, reaching out.

    “Not doing this again,” Agatha said in response, closing her eyes. She sounded drained.

    “Not doing what?” Rio said.

    “It’s not you,” Agatha said, her voice muffled behind her hands which had come up to cover her eyes. “It’s not fair.”

    “It is me,” Rio said, carefully.

    “You always say that,” Agatha whined, and then almost collapsed to the floor.

    Rio shot forward to catch and steady her. Agatha was even more drunk than she’d originally thought. “Are you on something?” she checked.

    “No.” Agatha didn’t sound convinced. Her head dropped forward onto Rio’s shoulder and stayed there, heavy.

    “Agatha, I think I need to take you home.”

    Agatha stiffened and tried to pull away. “Not home, please.” She looked panicked. Rio ached. “Not your home, my home,” Rio clarified. “Is that okay?”

    The alcohol she had consumed over the past few hours was catching up to her a little, and she felt thoroughly exhausted. Agatha didn’t reply.

    “Come on, sweetheart,” she said, draping Agatha’s unresponsive arm over her shoulder and forcing her to walk to the door. “I’m going to call us a taxi.”

    Out on the street it wasn’t much better. Agatha went completely non-verbal, and Rio almost had to pick her up to get her into the backseat.

    The driver narrowed his eyes. “If she throws up in my taxi, you’re paying.”

    “Not gonna throw up,” Agatha said sleepily, curling into Rio’s side and resisting Rio’s attempts to fasten her seatbelt.

    The drive wasn’t long, maybe fifteen minutes, but by the time they reached Rio’s apartment Agatha was out cold. Rio would have been worried if she wasn’t gently snoring. She paid the driver and then crossed to Agatha’s side, opening the door and picking her up in her arms like a child, not without difficulty.

    Somehow Rio managed to unlock her door and deposit Agatha on her bed, her hair spilling out around her against the white sheets. Pulling her own boots off, Rio set to carefully dealing with Agatha’s heels, which had tiny intricate buckles on them.

    “I want Rio,” Agatha piped up, making her jump.

    “I’m here,” Rio soothed.

    “No you’re not, I’m imagining you again.”

    Rio sighed. “Can I take your clothes off? I’m going to give you something to sleep in.”

    “You can do whatever you want with me,” Agatha said, trying to pull her dress over her head. “Rio doesn’t want me anyway.”

    This was an impossible situation in the first place, and all of these heartbreaking one-liners Agatha kept coming out with weren’t helping. Rio swallowed the lump in her throat and went to her closet, picking out a T-shirt. She undressed Agatha carefully, helping her sit up so she could pull the shirt over her head.

    “Get under the covers,” Rio said, pulling them back.

    Agatha shuffled underneath and sighed deeply as Rio draped the comforter over her. “Smells nice,” she said.

    Rio went to the cupboard and found the bucket she used to mop, placing it by the bed just in case.

    “I’m going to brush my teeth,” she said redundantly. Agatha was unresponsive.

    She did so quickly, staring at her wild eyes in the mirror. This day was not real, she told herself. She’d wake up in the morning and find this had all been a dream.

    “Think I’m going to be sick,” Agatha said as Rio came back into the bedroom, rising up on one elbow.

    “There’s a bucket next to you if you need it,” Rio said, very much hoping that she wouldn’t. She climbed into bed next to her, staying on the very far side, not knowing where Agatha would want her if she were sober.

    Agatha sighed wistfully, flopping back down and cuddling the comforter up to her chest as she was wont to do, shifting closer.

    “I wish you were Rio,” she murmured.

    Rio blinked awake the next morning with a start.

    Her bedroom felt different. She was on the wrong side of the bed, and the sheets were tucked around her as if someone else had done it. Stretching and orientating herself, the night before came flooding back to her.

    She sat up quickly, scanning the room. No Agatha. No bucket on the floor. But lying on the floor in the corner, a pair of stilettos. Rio got up quickly. Unless Agatha had stolen her shoes, she couldn’t have left, so she must still be in the apartment.

    Her suspicions were soon confirmed. Agatha was standing in the living room with her back to her, in just her underwear and the oversized shirt Rio had given her last night. She was engrossed in looking at something, and when Rio realised what it was she felt her heart skip a beat.

    “Agatha?”

    Agatha whipped around, holding a scrap of paper in her hands. She looked exhausted, her makeup smudged dark under her eyes, but a lot more put together than Rio had expected given the state of her the night before.

    “You kept all of it,” Agatha said. Her voice cracked from disuse, or from emotion, Rio wasn’t sure.

    “Yes,” Rio said. Her mouth was dry. On the shelf behind Agatha stood the candle that had been on her nightstand in her dorm, and a single pentagram earring. In her hands, the torn-out Emily Dickinson poem.

    “Why didn’t you try to find me?” Agatha said. “You moved out here… how long have you been here?”

    “I thought you didn’t want me to find you,” Rio said. Her head was throbbing.

    “Rio, I…” Agatha trailed off. She looked so small in Rio’s T-shirt, her bare feet probably cold against the floorboards. Rio felt the protective urge she had always felt for Agatha swell inside her; the urge to gather her up and hold her close and soothe all of her worries away. Did she even have the right to do that anymore? Did Agatha want her to?

    “Can I make you a coffee?” Rio asked, instead of the million other questions that were tumbling around her head. “You must be hungover.”

    Agatha laughed drily. “Sorry you had to deal with me last night.”

    “Don’t be sorry, you did nothing wrong,” Rio said.

    Agatha paused, emotion flitting across her face. “Coffee would be nice,” she said. She watched Rio cross the living room to the small kitchen and set out two mugs on the counter. “It’s nice here,” she said. “It’s very you.”

    Rio looked up. “Thank you,” she replied softly.

    “What’s this?” Agatha said curiously, flipping through the tall stack of paper on the coffee table.

    “That’s my novel,” Rio said. “It’s going to be published. I found out yesterday.”

    She turned away for a moment to get the coffee out of the cupboard, moving slowly, trying to buy herself time to figure out what to say. There was a movement in her peripheral vision and then, suddenly, Agatha was behind her, her hands coming up to rest softly on Rio’s waist. Rio, dazed, put the coffee down. Agatha held her like that for a moment, a hand on each hip, and then she slowly slid her arms around Rio’s waist, resting her head on her shoulder blades. Rio could have cried at how right it felt.

    “Congratulations, my love,” Agatha said. Her arms tightened as if she thought Rio would pull away. The silence which followed felt laden with possibility. Rio didn’t dare to move. “I have thought of nothing else but you for six months,” Agatha continued. “I haven’t stopped loving you.”

    Rio’s brain almost short-circuited at that — at what those words meant. She hadn’t stopped. Which meant that she had at some point started. Agatha loved her.

    “I haven’t either,” she said. Her hands came to rest on top of Agatha’s, coffee entirely forgotten, the packet falling over and spilling over the counter. “I missed you so much.” Her voice broke, and she hated how pathetic she must sound, but—

    “Rio, I missed you like someone cut a hole in me.”

    At those words Rio twisted around in Agatha’s arms, cupping Agatha’s cheek and brushing their noses together. They stood like that for a moment, savouring the closeness, and then Rio closed the distance and kissed her sweetly, deeply, trying to convey all of the emotions of the past six months through the action.

    Agatha sighed, gripping Rio’s hip as though she still wasn’t sure whether or not she was a hallucination.

    “I probably taste like vodka and cigarettes,” she said ruefully.

    Rio laughed, burying her face in Agatha’s hair and inhaling deeply. “You do smell a bit like an ashtray,” she said. “But you also smell like you.”

    Agatha swatted at her gently, but her eyes were smiling, her face transforming before Rio’s eyes into the one that she had known during those hazy summer days — open, relaxed and trusting.

    “It’s been really bad, Rio,” she said. “Since I came back.”

    Rio soothed a hand over her arm. “I’ll make us coffee, and you can tell me about it.”

    Agatha nodded, but made no move to disentangle herself. Rio realised that she didn’t want to let her go. She tapped the counter twice. “Sit here, sweetheart,” she said.

    Agatha hopped up obediently. Rio just looked at her for a moment — her thighs against the marble countertop, her legs swinging, her tangled hair, her sleepy eyes. She looked like everything Rio had ever wanted.

    “You’re never going back there,” Rio said decidedly. “I am never letting you go again.”

    Agatha’s eyes widened.

    “If you’ll have me,” Rio added.

    Agatha rolled her eyes. “If you’ll have me.” she said.

    They stared at each other for a minute, grinning, and then Agatha glanced over at the shelf where the candle stood. “You kept all my trinkets…” she said mischievously, “but what about my strap? I’ve missed her.”

    Rio barked out an incredulous laugh. “Kept that too, obviously.”

    Agatha narrowed her eyes deviously. “Good.”
