Kneeling in the sun-dried dirt of the flower beds that surround her home, Kara prunes what dying leaves she can find, trimming the edges down to promote bushier growth and new flower buds. 

She has too many. Some are wildflowers she’d transplanted from fields in the surrounding cliffs or neighboring islands, and some grown from seeds, but all of them loved.

Even the ones Alex insists are weeds.

Kara takes a deep inhale of the earthy-sweet scent of her garden—its foliage exploding in a disarray of well-loved colours because Kara couldn’t decide what sort of flowers she wanted. They thrive in disorganization, able to grow however they want, blooming unrestrained. She smiles at the bees that love them too, and at the birds and butterflies that flutter in and out like leaves in the fall.

A light wind rustles from the forest behind her cottage and into Kara’s honey-wheat hair as she prunes flowers that are nearing the end of their life. She lays them on the ground in a gentle pile so she can tie them into a bouquet. 

She loves each of them, adores that every flower has its own unique history with a meaning to match. 

Kara’s village is funny like that. They find small symbolism in everything. Flowers, constellations, even dragons and wyverns.

Much like flowers, every part of a dragon tells a story—the colour of its scales, its horns, its eyes—each dragon is unique and perfectly suited to the person it chooses. 

Kara snorts a laugh before glancing across her lawn to check on Krypto. He’s sleeping at the edge of the property where the green grass is longest, heedless of the few carriages and passersby that chuckle at him as they travel the dirt path beside him. 

He’s laying splayed on his back with his pink tongue lolling out of his mouth as he snores, the sunlight glinting off of the lighter, yellow scales of his abdomen with each breath he takes.

Kara shakes her head with an endeared smile, turning back to her flowers to finish her arrangement.

The leafy stems remind her of guarded, sea-green eyes and Kara blushes as she adds a single, yellow daybreak flower to her bouquet, named for their golden petals that glow in the rising sun. They’re common across the islands, and their meaning is well-known amongst the locals—hopeful longing and tender affection.

Kara bites her lip as she caresses one of its soft petals. Hopeful romantics used to offer them to the objects of their affection. If their feelings were returned, their suitor would offer a rarer gift; a pale blue moonstone flower with white edges that fray like lace. They glow a ghostly white on nights where the moon can’t be seen, shining into the darkness and awaiting its return. They are much harder to find, representing devotion, love, and the return of feelings.

The flowers bloom at the same time each year, signaling eternal love, though nobody uses them anymore. Kara likes the history of them. Alex pretends not to; calls Kara fanciful. Kara knows better though. Alex likes flowers too, as long as nobody else is around. 

“Daydreaming again?” 

Kara jumps at Alex’s call before she turns with a grin. Alex walks across Kara’s lawn, ignoring the stone path as she eats a green crabapple. 

“Just thinking,” Kara says as her blush deepens. 

Alex always sneaks up on her, like her dragon, Saber, often does. She was named after Alex’s favored type of sword and is near impossible to see after dusk because of her midnight scales that shine different hues of blue depending on how the light hits them. Saber is a sleek dragon, smaller than most, but fast and powerful, with kind, amber eyes that remind Kara of a cozy hearth.

Kara looks up to the sky to no avail. Saber isn’t there. She’s been missing for a week now. Several dragons across the island have disappeared, along with their humans in some cases, and though chieftain J’onn looks every day, they haven’t been found. 

Alex squints her eyes into the sky for a moment as well, looking lost without her friend.

With a sigh, Alex leans against Kara’s wooden cottage door, picking at the empty, round placard where a family crest should be. 

Unlike the rest of the island, Kara doesn’t have one there. It felt like a betrayal to her birth family to use the Danvers’ crest when she moved out, and Kara can’t remember her own. 

The round Danvers’ emblem glints over Alex’s heart on her silver armor. The dagger in the middle of the crest doesn’t suit Kara. She’s not a warrior like Alex yet. She’s learned how to use her greatsword in practice with Alex but has never seen a real fight, and the fern leaf that curves around the dagger to signal Eliza’s apothecary doesn’t represent Kara’s love of nature in quite the same way. 

With a sigh, Alex tosses her half-eaten apple across the lawn toward Krypto. It bounces and rolls in front of his snout, his nostrils flaring as he sniffs it before his violet eyes blink open and he rolls over. His long, golden scales shine like the finest riches, and his tail wags. His dark pupils widen in delight as he licks the apple into his mouth, chomping away as Alex crosses her arms. 

Alex peers down at the bouquet before rolling her eyes at it. Kara winces as she hopes the colourful display camouflages the singular, yellow flower, but she has no such luck. Alex is too perceptive.

“Why do you have a daybreak flower?” Alex asks, though she already knows the answer. “That story is stupid.” 

Alex’s gaze turns to the ground, and she kicks it with her toe. She doesn’t mean to be rude. She misses her dragon and doesn’t know where to direct her anger.

“It’s not stupid,” Kara pouts. She clenches her jaw instead of chastising Alex. Kara doesn’t know what she’d do if Krypto was the one missing. 

“Stop avoiding the question. You haven’t told me why you put one in a bouquet.”

“No reason.” Kara glances down and clears her throat.

It’s the worst time for a flash of shadow to cascade over them, a very specific shadow, and Kara grimaces at Alex’s knowing look. 

Overhead, a slate-grey wyvern with two heads darts through the air, the powerful wings of its long body pushing it forward in graceful sweeps. 

"Of all the people for you to have a crush on,” Alex mutters. “Who knows how Luthor will react with a wyvern like that."

“You misunderstand the meaning behind a wyvern choosing a person,” Kara says with a shake of her head. It’s rare—most people are chosen by dragons. They’re more social than wyverns, more trusting. People understand more about dragons.

Kara’s never seen a two-headed wyvern before, and only a handful of dragons with more than one head. Those are rare too. Special. Like Lena must be, if such a unique creature chose her. 

“Kara, who else could be responsible for the dragons’ disappearances? For Saber’s disappearance?” Alex asks as she picks at the fraying logs of Kara’s cabin. “Her family is so weird. It’s only a matter of time before she’s caught.”

“Did you forget Lex’s dragon was the first to go missing? It isn’t the Luthors, and I don’t think it’s Lena,” Kara says. It can’t be. 

Kara has seen Lena across the island over the years, but they’ve seldom spoken. When they were young, as the children of the island gathered to learn together, Lena was kept apart from them, taught at home by her family. 

Seeing Lena in the village is rare, and she’s quick about purchasing what supplies she needs before flying back to the Northern Mountains. Each time Kara bumps into her, Lena is polite, and though her eyes are wary there’s kindness in the depths of them—a careful sort of tenderness that can’t be faked or hidden. 

Lena is most often seen soaring through the skies on her wyvern, speed like no other that Kara has seen. Not even Krypto has been able to catch up to her. 

“I think you have too big of a heart,” Alex mutters.

“Not possible,” Kara tosses over her shoulder as she stands up. She brushes the dirt from her knees with her free hand before checking that the flowers she picked are arranged nicely. 

“Where are you going?” Alex complains after Kara as she ambles toward Krypto.

“No use putting good flowers to waste,” Kara says before she rubs Krypto’s snout and hooks her boot into the stirrup behind his shoulder. Kara launches herself over his back and hugs him with her thighs as his wings spread out, beating sticks and dirt into the air as wind gusts around them while they take off. Alex shields her eyes as her cropped red hair billows from the force of it. 

“You’ll never catch up with her!” Alex hollers as Kara rises into the clear sky.

“Watch me!” Kara shouts over her shoulder with a grin. 

She says it every time she tries to chase Lena, tries to talk to her, but Krypto isn’t quite as fast as a wyvern—not half as graceful, and Kara hasn’t been able to close the distance between her and Lena yet.

Still, she has hope.

2. Vines

The sun beats down on Kara, heating the brown leather of her fraying armor, but the rushing wind keeps her cool. 

Lena’s wyvern is like an arrow, pulled taut on a bow and released at break-neck speed. 

Krypto isn’t built like that. He’s too well-muscled, heavier than most dragons. He is a fighting dragon, meant for brute strength. The villagers were shocked to see him choose Kara, at least until they realized how harmless he is. 

Usually. 

His loyalty can be ferocious.

Lena is flying lower than usual, and instead of going north toward the snow-covered mountains where Luthor Manor resides, Lena juts west toward the flying isles—a string of giant boulders connecting two islands that are held together by the massive root systems of trees that cling to life, thick vines connecting the abandoned stones and giving them the illusion of floating over the ocean below. All but the most stubborn family moved from the diminishing land generations ago, leaving a sole stone house on one of the multitudes of clifftops.

Lena swerves into the gaps between the cliffs and tree trunks with ease, disappearing just below the surfaces of them. 

Kara struggles to follow—Krypto has to beat his wings more than the wyvern, and they threaten to get tangled in the moss-covered vines. The bouquet in Kara’s hand makes steering harder, and as they jostle through the air, leaves and petals tumble out of it.

The hidden shadows of the cliffsides are covered with moss, and as Kara pursues, the sun juts between the rocks in flashes, dancing off of Krypto’s golden scales. 

Lena veers to the side in a sharp turn, and Kara narrowly avoids crashing into a thick tree trunk. Lena swerves again before Krypto pummels into the side of a cliff with enough force to crumble stones off of it. They fall into the ocean below as Krypto clings to the roots where he crashed, and Kara’s helmet follows. She groans before they push off of the wall again. 

What few flowers remain are crumbling, and Kara winces at the wilting mess.

Kara’s losing Lena amongst the stones, falling further behind with each blunder. A grumble from Krypto’s chest reverberates off of the cliffs as he complains, but Kara isn’t ready to give up.

“Get to the top of the cliffs!” Kara hollers over the rushing waves below, and Krypto ascends, using his wings for balance as he climbs along the sides of the cliffs to avoid the roots and vines. 

Once they’re on the grassy hilltops, Kara glances ahead in search of movement. Everything is still for a few long moments until the pale tip of a wing pokes between the mountains. 

“There!” Kara points and Krypto gallops forward, digging his claws into the meadow with his four legs to propel himself faster, his wings tucked around Kara’s thighs for speed. 

It’s a surprising advantage—having Krypto run and jump across the clifftops instead of flying, but he doesn’t have to twist and turn as much as Lena does, and the gap between them lessens. 

Kara’s eyes are laser-focused on Lena’s movement, two dozen feet ahead in the shadows as she flies, and Kara doesn’t notice when Krypto launches onto the sole claimed island. He lands in the midst of a garden, the plants there crumbling under his weight as dirt explodes around them. 

“Kara Danvers!” her old school teacher shouts. A horrible woman, really. 

“Sorry, Rhea!” Kara calls over her shoulder but she doesn’t stay to help. 

She’ll return later.

Maybe. Or Mon-El will have some work to do for once.

“Wait until I tell Eliza about this!” Rhea hollers after her, and Kara winces. 

She has one flower left and Lena’s almost out of the cliffs.

“Faster, Krypto!” Kara cries. 

He launches forward with a roar, and his eyes start to glow an electric blue as he dashes toward their goal. His golden scales ruffle up and elongate as a sky-blue light glows between them in a display of his power that few have seen. 

He propels himself even faster, shooting up and forward with such strength that it takes every muscle in Kara’s body for her to hold on. As Lena soars out of the cliffs, Krypto throws himself off the edge of the last boulder in a wide arch, curving in the air and beating his wings in a maneuver that turns them upside down. 

But they did it! 

Lena hasn’t gotten up to her full speed yet, and Kara sails next to her, loose hair falling from her head in a tangled mess.

One of Lena’s wyvern’s heads—the one with red eyes—hisses in surprise as the blue-eyed one curves around Lena’s torso in a protective shield. One of Lena’s dark eyebrows raises as she glances over to where Kara soars upside down beside her. 

Lena shushes her wyvern in a low, soothing hiss, and they slow down a little. 

Kara beams as she holds out the daybreak. What few remaining petals it has are frayed and crumpled, and its broken stem hangs limp in her grasp.

Krypto flips upright, and Kara blushes as another petal falls off.

Lena’s brow furrows as she glances down at it, a contemplative frown pulling her lips as her cheeks turn light pink. It could be the wind, but Kara hopes not. 

Lena takes the flower as if it’s made of glass—and to be fair, it is about to fall apart, but as she opens her mouth to speak, the booming sound of a cannon echoes across the water below them. Before Kara knows what’s happening, she and Krypto are pummelling toward the edge of the nearest island under the weight of a chain-link net. 

“Kara!” Lena shouts after her. 

A horrible time to learn that Lena knows her name. 

The force of the net squishes Kara to Krypto’s body and she can’t move her arms to steer him, nor can he spread his wings under the weight. 

His scales are still raised and jagged, hard like stones as the light between them blazes. There’s nothing he can do—even as hot, electric blue flames shoot out of his mouth. It heats the chains until they glow, and Kara winces as the metal singes into the leather of her armor. 

The shore below them grows as they fall toward it in a dizzying spiral. 

Lena and her wyvern dip beside them, the claws of its wings tugging at the chain. It slows their descent, but the net doesn’t move, and Lena bites her lip as her wyvern hisses.

“It’s too heavy!” Kara calls around the rushing wind. “I’m gonna crash!”

“I’m not going to let you!” Lena yells back. She pushes off of Krypto’s side, looping in the air until she’s underneath and upside down. Krypto growls as Lena’s wyvern digs its claws into his scales, and his chest puffs as if he’s going to fire at them. 

“She’s trying to help, buddy,” Kara murmurs to him, and though he doesn’t attack them, he continues to growl. 

Lena curves to the side, pulling Krypto with her as they propel toward the sandy bank of an island, but there’s not enough time to tilt the net off. 

Lena keeps trying anyway, even when it means they crash together into the sand, creating a deep path in it as they strike into the ground—a huge sand cloud booming around them. They tumble together in a heap and the net rolls off of them. 

Kara falls from her holster and out of the jumbled heap of scaled creatures, crying out with the deep ache in her bones. She lifts her head from the sand, spitting a mouthful of it back to the ground and wishing more than ever that she hadn’t lost her helmet.

Again.  

Growls and hisses drown out Lena’s groans of complaint as her wyvern turns over. Lena is still hooked into the saddle, and though her helmet has fallen off, she looks okay. 

Her braided hair has fallen out, and obsidian locks full of sand stick to her face as her wyvern shakes its heads. Lena brushes the sand away from her face only for Krypto to jolt up and shake more onto her. 

Lena frowns at him, but her eyes grow wide as she looks. His protective stance in front of where Kara lies blocks her view, so she can’t see the front of him, but the red streaks of blood in the sand aren’t a good sign. Regardless, he guards her. 

Lena hops off her wyvern and Krypto growls, but Lena holds her hands out as she approaches, making that same strange, soothing hiss that had calmed her wyvern earlier. The rumble in Krypto’s chest dulls to a low purr, his scales flattening down as Lena gets closer to him. He dips his head low before he lies on his belly, curving his tail around himself as he settles and the light between his scales fades.

“I’m so sorry,” Lena murmurs as she kneels at his front. 

The heads of Lena’s wyvern keep a close watch in all directions as Lena’s focus is occupied, and Kara pushes up onto her knees to crawl the short distance to Krypto. 

“I didn’t mean to hurt you,” Lena whispers to him as Kara kneels beside her. 

The scales of Krypto’s left leg are sliced open in jagged punctures, and the more delicate skin of his wing is torn. Kara glances to the wyvern’s claws, where sand sticks to the blood that dries there. She winces as she reaches out to Krypto, and he grumbles in pain as she presses the surrounding scales, his blood sticking to her fingers. 

Lena bites her lip as she stares at the wound.

In general, Krypto would snap and growl at anybody, especially a stranger trying to poke and prod his cuts, but he levels Lena with a patient, almost understanding gaze as she runs her hands across his battered wing. 

“You’re good with him,” Kara observes. Lena glances at her before she averts her gaze back to Krypto’s wound. 

“He’s gorgeous,” Lena says, and Krypto grumbles a purr at her as Lena scratches the underside of his chin. “Very charming.” 

“He’s usually friendly but not like this,” Kara says. Despite the bleeding wing, Krypto looks as content with Lena in the divot they’d crashed into the sand as he does in the long grass of Kara’s cottage. 

“I”m… good with dragons and wyverns. Anything with scales, really,” Lena says. Her eyelids droop as she pets Krypto’s snout, her lashes so long they almost touch her cheeks. “I like taking time to care for them. They’re so open and genuine with how they’re feeling.”

Krypto nuzzles into Lena, shoves his snout into her face, and presses against her as Lena continues. “Creatures are unashamed and unbidden in their affection, and they don’t care where a person comes from, only that I am kind to them. I love how unfiltered and free they are, and how I can be just as open with them.” 

“Yeah,” Kara murmurs. She blushes when her hand brushes over Lena’s on top of Krypto’s scales, pulling it into her lap as her eyes widen. 

Lena grins at her before shaking her head. 

“You-you should see him when he’s not controlling his power,” Kara says. 

“That’s what the glowing was?” Lena asks and Kara nods. 

“Yeah, he’s like electricity, you know? He’s got all this energy right under the surface, ready to burst, but he’s still so bright, like the sun, and powerful, like a- like a-”

“A heat storm,” Lena supplies.

“Exactly!” Kara says with a snap of her fingers. She beams at the light dusting of pink on Lena’s cheeks, so happy to have been right about her. “What about yours? What’s your wyvern’s power?” 

Lena’s face falls back into her usual guarded mask, and her chin juts up as she looks away. “They don’t have any powers.” 

“Oh,” Kara says with a wrinkled brow. She’s never heard of a dragon or a wyvern with no powers before, but she’s also never seen a two-headed wyvern, so maybe that’s got something to do with it. She shakes her frown away and forces a smile. “That’s okay. They’re still really pretty. And fast.”

Lena chuckles, but her small, content smile fades into a grimace as she brushes her hand over Krypto’s drying blood. 

“I should take him to Winn,” Kara says. Winn is always fixing up injured dragons—or trying to. He can’t always tell what’s wrong with them, but he and his little dragon do their best. 

“I don’t think he can fly,” Lena whispers as if she can feel Krypto’s pain. 

“Hey, it’s not your fault,” Kara says, but Lena’s eyes dart to the chain net that almost captured them, and she bites her lip. Like she recognizes it. 

But that can’t be because Lena saved them both. Risked her own life doing so. Still, the worried furrow of Lena’s brow makes Kara gulp, and she has to ask.

“Do you know who’s behind this? The missing dragons?” 

“I hope not,” Lena says, her green eyes boring into Kara’s. 

“But you… know where that net came from?” Kara hazards a guess, and Lena winces before she casts her face away. 

“He said it was for catching blue sharks,” Lena mutters, “because the herring population has been dwindling. I was trying to help.” 

“Who said?” 

“My brother. He asked me to design it, but I didn’t think-”

Lena’s pleading is cut off by a branch cracking near the edge of the forest that surrounds the beach. Kara tosses Lena her silver, horned helmet before they shoot up, rushing around Krypto to guard him while he’s down. Lena’s wyvern stalks up behind her. The head with icy blue eyes wraps around her while the head with red eyes growls into the shadowed trees. 

Men with leather hide armor and mismatched scimitars start ambling out of the underbrush, grins and sneers on their sun-burnt faces as they start to cut through the thick foliage that separates the forest from the beach. Some men follow behind them carrying large, rust-covered chains.

“I have to go tell Alex!” Kara cries. “She can get J’onn and the guard!” 

“Krypto can’t fly!” Lena says as she tugs her rapier from the scabbard at her waist.

“He hasn’t even tried!” 

“He can’t! Just trust me,” Lena argues as she readies her weapon—she unbuckles the black, leather holster before throwing it to the ground and glares at the men who struggle to approach.

“You go then,” Kara urges. “Go get Alex and bring her here.”

“And leave you here alone? No.” 

“But then both of our dragons will go missing, and us with them!” 

“I am not leaving you here, Kara. This is my fault!” Lena shouts. Her jaw clenches with her anger, and the veins in her forehead pulse down to her furrowed brows. “I will not leave your dragon here injured, and I will not leave you to face these men alone.” 

Kara blinks at Lena for a moment. With the sharp horns of her helmet and the vampire-bat skull that clasps her emerald cloak closed over her dark-grey armor, Lena looks like she’s fought a thousand battles—won every time, and Kara has no fear following her into this one. 

Kara nods. She pulls her greatsword from its holster on her back and holds the golden hilt at the ready before she asks, “Then what do we do?” 

Lena bites her lip, glancing at Kara’s sword and then the men. They’re hacking through the thick underbrush that separates the dense forest from the beach, nature holding them back long enough for Lena to come up with a plan. 

Hopefully.

“Keep an eye on them,” Lena says before she turns to her wyvern.

Lena sticks the point of her rapier into the sand before raising her palms toward her wyvern’s heads, her helmet tilted back until each of their eyes level with Lena’s. Their eyes start to glow, red, blue, and green, until they flash with a blinding, white light.

Kara’s jaw drops and she blinks in shock.

The wyvern’s heads each have a sea-green eye where one used to be red or blue, staring down and waiting for Lena to instruct them.

“Watch over me, as I watch over you,” Lena whispers a prayer, stroking the bottoms of its long necks as they gaze at each other. 

Without further command, her wyvern beats its wide, slate-grey wings, stirring a whirlwind of sand around Kara, Lena and Krypto before they fly toward Kara’s village. Kara gapes at Lena when she turns to pick up her rapier. Her emerald eyes have been replaced—one ice blue and the other molten red, like ice and fire.

Lena looks away and with a sharp flick of her wrist, says, “Get ready to fight!” 

Kara turns back to the group of a dozen men who have almost made it through the thick underbrush. 

Krypto groans as he stands up, but as he steps on his injured arm, he falls forward into the sand. Lena shushes him once more, soothing him back to the ground until he grumbles and falls asleep. 

Kara has a million questions, but she has no time to ask any of them as their enemies break through the foliage and gather in a semi-circle around them, throwing the stained ropes and chains in a pile before unsheathing more blades. 

“Didn’t expect you to be here,” a man with salt-and-pepper hair and a smarmy grin says. 

“Then you’re just as stupid as I thought, Morgan,” Lena taunts him. “I’ve been tracking you for weeks, and you didn’t even notice.” 

He sneers at Lena before tilting his head, gesturing for the men behind him to move toward Kara and Lena. 

“You sure the boss would be okay with us roughing her up?” one of them says.

“Otis, that man will do whatever it takes,” Morgan scoffs, his voice like bitter venom, “you know that.” 

He flicks two fingers forward and the group descends upon Kara and Lena, who pull together like magnets until they are back to back and surrounded.

“You get any combat training while you were homeschooled?” Kara asks over her shoulder, a bead of sweat gathering along her brow as she adjusts her grip on her greatsword. 

“More than you know,” Lena mutters before she flicks her wrist, making her rapier cut through the air like a menacing whip. 

3. Branches

Morgan stays back, watches the other men approach but keeps his hands folded in front of his silk robes as if he’s too important to get his hands dirty—too much of a coward to fight his own battles.

The first man to approach lifts his scimitar as if it were a tree branch and he’s a child playing swords in his mother’s front yard. He aims the dull point of his curved blade up toward Lena’s face, despite it being a slashing weapon. 

The sun glints off of it as Lena ducks down, kicking one of her legs out in front of her and side-swiping the man’s ankles as he walks, toppling the unpracticed scoundrel over. 

He grunts as his back hits the ground and the wind gets knocked out of him, his sword skittering out of his grasp. Lena stomps on his sternum for good measure, lifting her rapier once more as she turns to face the remaining eleven men. 

Their surprise doesn’t last long, and another reprobate smirks at them before he approaches. 

“I love it when they put up a fight,” he mutters as he leers at Kara. He reaches out to grab at the edge of Kara’s breastplate, and Kara lets go of her sword with one hand, making a fist before she hits the inside of the man’s wrist and the outside of his elbow in unison, just like Alex taught her. 

He cries in pain as his arm breaks with a loud crack, and Kara steps forward to knee him in the groin before he, too, falls to the ground. She’s quick to regrip her greatsword as the other men form a circle around them—signaling the battle experience the first two men lacked.

Kara’s heart races and she has to fight to control her breath. She’s fought before, but only to train with Alex so her sister could join the guard. She steels her features, determined not to let the men see how scared she is. She clenches her jaw, swings her greatsword in a sweeping arc, and gets into a wide-stance fighting position.

“Who’s next then?” Kara taunts, hopeful her false bravado might make them question themselves.

It doesn’t.

The men lift their scimitars, closing in around Kara and Lena, Krypto snoring beside them, still too injured to help fight. 

Kara and Lena remain back to back, moving around each other with surprising grace as the men swarm them.

They circle, protecting each others’ backs as the group of men aim killing blows against them.

Kara blocks two hits from a dual-wielding man, his scimitars making an X against Kara’s sword, and sparks fall to the sand as their weapons grind together.

Rage rolls off of Lena like waves, powering through every sharp flick of her wrist and into each blow she strikes. Though she is small, she uses her stature to her advantage to dodge and debilitate her foes. Her rapier cuts through the air, whips in between the less maneuverable scimitars of their enemies, and each time Kara and Lena turn together, more blood has been spilled into thick puddles on the ground, men falling into them and over each others’ corpses. 

Kara spares glances at Lena to ensure she’s still protecting herself, and watching the finesse with which Lena moves is a thrill.

Lena uses her blade as an extension of herself, the way Alex always talks about—as if she anticipates every move her opponents are going to make, her eyes never resting, in constant motion as she blocks, parries, and thrusts her rapier.

There’s an urgent control to the way Lena’s body maneuvers in battle, like the sharp undercurrent below the surface of calm water, and her blade whips through the leather of their enemies’ hide armor, cutting into flesh and tearing the organs before they spill from their wounds. 

One of the few remaining men feints a harsh blow to Lena’s head but changes trajectory, curving his scimitar in midair and around Lena’s rapier, removing her chance to block him. 

Kara gasps in surprise and removes her greatsword from the stomach of the man she just ran through, and at the last possible moment, Kara twists and presses her front against Lena’s back, shoving her greatsword in front of them both as she holds Lena to her body. 

She blocks the attacker’s blade inches away from piercing Lena’s heart. But her one-handed grip and the odd angle makes Kara unable to stop the blow entirely, pushing the scimitar off course instead. 

The very tip of the blade nicks Lena’s ribs and she hisses before she cuts across his chest with a delicate flick of her wrist. 

Kara takes huge gulping breaths as she turns around, trying to regain her balance. Her eyes widen at the disemboweled man that kneels before her. Kara hadn’t meant to cut his abdomen that much, and he tries in vain to hold his guts in place.

“Oops,” Kara chokes out. She’s never seen intestines before. Didn’t mean to spill any, but she shakes her guilt away. He was trying to kill her and there are still five men, including Morgan who remains standing watch over the battle.

Krypto is still lying in the sand a few feet away, the spilled blood from the fight mixing with his on the ground, and Kara glances up at the sky in search of wings or fire, but there’s nothing to be seen. 

“Why don’t they have dragons?” Kara shouts over the din of their fighting.

“I’m not sure, but I’m not about to complain!” Lena shouts back as she kicks an attacker in the chest with the heel of her boot, sending him to the ground before she reaches into her cloak and throws a silver blade into his stomach.

He removes it and blood starts pooling over his tanned leather armor. 

“We don’t need dragons,” Morgan growls as he steps forward to join the fight. He raises his hands in front of him as if pushing against an invisible force, his eyes glow a sickening green and bright currents of the same hue expand from his open palms, dancing across his forearms in rivulets of electric energy. 

Kara’s eyes widen at the display - it’s impossible. It’s like magic, but that can’t be. Only dragons hold souls that can bear the weight of powers like that, but here Morgan is, wielding that great power as if he could ever be worthy of it.
Kara’s stomach churns at the very idea, and Lena cries out, though nobody has harmed her. She falls to her knees, grabbing her stomach like she’s been stabbed as Morgan approaches. 

Kara rushes to stand in front of Lena, and when a man blocks her path, she bashes her elbow into his throat before slashing her blade into the gaps of his leather cuirass, breaking the ribs below. 

It’s only Morgan and three other men left, and Kara’s sure she can take them, even though her bones ache from the fall, and her muscles are heavy with exertion. Kara has gotten herself out of a lot of scrapes over the years. She’s gotten herself into quite a few as well, but that isn’t the point.

She’d sooner die than go down without a fight. Krypto’s life is at stake, and so is Lena’s.

Lena falls forward onto her hands and knees, heaving as if she’s about to vomit and Morgan mocks a grimace down at her. 

“What are you doing to her?” Kara demands. She hovers over Lena, trying to keep an eye on the four men that surround her. Lena starts to shake harder with each step that brings Morgan closer. 

“Me? Nothing,” Morgan says.

Before Kara can respond, Morgan’s eyes flash and he shoots a current of the vile, green energy into Kara’s chest. She topples onto her back and the wind gets knocked out of her as the force of the strike charges through her body, leaving fire in its wake. 

Small sparks of green bounce across her armor and Kara coughs, the back of her throat swollen and thick with a copper taste. 

She and Lena are pulled to their feet before their swords are stolen, and Morgan tucks his hands into the pockets of his robes as he smiles. The rippling green light along his eyes and forearms fades to nothing.

As it diminishes, Lena starts breathing easier again, though her head hangs back as if she hasn’t enough energy to hold it up.

“You’re lucky Lex has use of you,” Morgan sneers at Lena, “otherwise I would kill you where you stand.” 

“Then do it,” Lena taunts back, her voice weak but the fight in it like an inferno under the surface. Her red and blue eyes blaze with fury. Heat burns like acid in Kara’s stomach too—anger on Lena’s behalf for being betrayed by her kin in such a way.

Morgan chuckles at her before he turns his head to Krypto, still sleeping in the sand. He ambles over, nudges the wound on Krypto’s front leg with his boot, causing Krypto to wince and stir. 

“Don’t touch him!” Kara snaps, but her shoulders are captured by large hands that hold her back despite how hard she struggles against them. 

Morgan kneels to stroke Krypto’s odd scales, longer and sharper than the average dragon’s even when he’s not using his powers, almost like fur. “Lex’ll be pleased. He’s had his eyes on this one for quite some time.”

Morgan stands again as a man approaches him with large shackles. They work together to clasp a large muzzle over Krypto’s snout. They cuff each of his legs, folding his wings and crossing the chains over his back so he can’t fly or fight.

They do it with a practiced ease that makes Kara sick, anger boiling in her stomach as she shouts, “Why are you doing this?”

Morgan stands up and saunters in front of Kara with a self-satisfied grin, leaning close to her face to sneer, “It’s for the greater good.” 

Kara jerks her head forward and into his lip, breaking the skin before the man behind her grasps her shoulders tighter. 

“You’re going to pay for that,” Morgan hisses as he pulls a handkerchief out of an inner pocket to dab at his swelling lip. He spits blood at Kara’s feet before pointing to the man who’s holding Krypto on a chain leash. “Otis, wake him up.” 

Kara yells as Otis forces the point of his scimitar into Krypto’s thigh. He jolts awake with a muffled roar, fighting against his restraints and tripping onto his face in his attempt to reach Kara. 

The black pupils of his violet eyes are wide with fear, and his irises glow blue for a moment, but the light sputters out. He groans as he struggles to stand again, crying out when he presses his weight onto his tender arm. 

“It’s okay, buddy,” Kara chokes out as she struggles to get to him. “We’ll be okay.”

Morgan laughs as he pulls thick ropes from the pile in the sand, tossing them one at a time to the men who hold Kara and Lena. 

They’re dragged behind Krypto before their wrists are bound together in thick knots and tied to the chains that hold his wings down. 

“Try to keep up,” one of the men commands in a gruff voice as he tests their restraints. He joins Morgan and Otis in front of Krypto, grabbing another chain to attach to his muzzle before they begin to try to pull Krypto into the long grass and toward the forest.

He resists them, digging his paws into the sand and ducking his head, but Lena coos at him in a whisper. Krypto glances back at her and Lena nods, the mismatched red and blue of her eyes glowing brighter before Krypto begins to follow the men across the underbrush and into the forest beyond. He limps on his front leg as he pulls Kara and Lena behind him.

The weight of Krypto’s thick body clears what twigs and stones Kara might have tripped over, and the man following behind them does so at a distance—ensuring they aren’t followed, and doing what he can to hide the trail of blood Krypto’s wounds leave behind. 

He’s still too close for Kara to try to reach the hunting knife in her boot, and Morgan is leading them too fast for her to bend to get it anyway.

 Lena’s too quiet and Kara peers at the small cut across her ribs, but the blood has already dried, and the small wound doesn’t explain why Lena had been in so much pain. 

“You alright?” Kara huffs in a whisper.

“Am I alright? Are you alright?” Lena mutters back.

“I didn’t get stabbed.” 

Lena glances at the carnage behind them before saying, “Evidently not.” 

“You’re fine, then?” Kara asks. Lena seems alright, but if she’s okay, why was she doubled over in pain? 

“Yes,” Lena says, but she keeps her eyes forward. 

“How did you make Krypto follow them? Or make him sleep on the beach?” Kara asks. 

Lena clenches her jaw, the muscles in it working before she glances at the man behind them. Lena asks, “Do you trust me?” 

Kara blinks at her. Lena’s eyes aren’t her own, but they are every bit as fierce and kind as the sea-green that usually resides there. After everything Lena’s done, how can Kara do anything but trust her? How could Kara ever not? 

“Yes.” 

“Then trust me when I say that I will do everything I can to save him, Kara. I swear to you.” The urgency in Lena’s voice can’t be hidden by the low timbre of it, and Kara nods as they continue to follow Krypto into the cool shadows under the branches of thick trees. 

4. Willow

It’s clever to hide in a forest. It’s ever-changing, and though tracks can be followed and animals hunted, nature is quick to regrow and hide what secrets it holds. Hints only last so long before rain erodes footprints or moss regrows over what wounds it has sustained, or the wind blows leaves over pathways. 

The towering rock formation looks like any other. Its greying boulders are scattered with moss and ivy, flowers fighting to grow in the few sunspots that shine through the thick leaves above.

The hanging vines of the willow tree in front of it sway in the slight breeze, and Morgan leads them all through the branches as if disappearing into the stone beyond. 

As they are tugged through the branches, Lena gasps and heaves again. The vines spill over them, and Kara can’t see through very well, but she tries to loosen the binds of her wrists so she can reach out and comfort Lena, help her in some way. 

“I’m fine,” Lena whispers. “There’s something terrible going on in here, Kara. I can feel it.”
She’s talking at least. Kara takes that as a good sign. 

The mouth of a cave is hidden beyond the itchy leaves of the willow.

The rust-coloured walls of the tunnel beyond are wet with condensation, drips echoing into the puddles along the ground. 

Red puddles, lit by torches in clean wall sconces. 

Small rivers of the reddish water run along the floor, and Kara can’t tell how much of the liquid is water, how much is leftover minerals from the jagged ceiling, or if it is something else entirely. 

She gulps. 

Under the echoes of their footsteps, Lena’s breathing is heavier, and small beads of sweat gather across her forehead, glinting in the dim torch flames. She holds her chin high regardless, pretending she’s unaffected. 

They reach the end of the tunnel and come to a dim cavern. Boulders and rocks are pushed to the side in piles to clear the middle of the area. More red water rushes from a smaller tunnel on the other side, deeper in colour and thicker, and when Lena stifles a heave, Kara wants to vomit as well. 

Large hands clasp Kara and Lena’s shoulders again, though they remain tied to Krypto, much to the delight of the man in the middle of the cavern.

Lex Luthor stands proud amid three curved, wooden desks, each covered in scrolls, vials, and scales that shine even in the darkness. 

“What-” Kara is cut off by Lex clapping his hands, a toothy grin in place before he spreads his arms and gazes around the cavern.

“Not a bad setup, huh sis?” he says. “It’s a bit damp, sure, but near impossible to find.”

“What are you doing here?” Kara asks, but Lex ignores her.

“You know,” he continues as if he hadn’t heard, “it’s not often we have survivors.” 

Lex straightens the silky blue ascot tucked into his grey shirt as he rounds his desk. The Luthor family crest sewn on his chest is more imposing than ever—a bone-white dragon skull. He leans on the edge of the middle desk and crosses his ankles, looking far too pleased for Kara’s liking. 

“Leave it to my bleeding-heart sister to swoop in and save the day.” Lex rolls his eyes and grins. “Try to, at least.” 

“You’re a bastard,” Lena spits at him, her voice ragged, though she tries to hide it.

“No, that would be you,” Lex chuckles before he turns to Morgan.

“Did you check them for weapons?” Lex asks.

“We took their weapons at the beach,” Morgan says around his fat lip. 

“Did you check them for extra weapons?” Lex demands. 

The arms around Kara and Lena tighten even more, and Morgan bends in front of Kara with a scowl, patting her leather boots and pulling out the small, emergency hunting knife she hides there. 

She pouts as he tosses it to the side and continues to pat up her legs and torso for weapons that aren’t there. Kara smirks at his bloody lip and Krypto growls. Lena shakes her head, and Krypto stops before the point of Otis’s blade can poke him again.

Morgan moves in front of Lena, who smirks at him despite the force it must take for her to seem so casual. 

He starts his search at her black, rawhide fur boots. His eyebrows widen as he finds two different pockets in each boot with small throwing knives hidden in them, moving up Lena’s calves to discover a dagger strapped to the back of each leg, hidden by her long cloak. Morgan clears his throat and swallows as he amasses a pile of weaponry on the ground, pausing to glance up at Lena before he tries to undo her cloak. The small teeth of the large bat skull that holds it closed cuts his finger, and he hisses as he pulls his hand back. 

“Why the hell do you have so many weapons?” Morgan complains as he tries again with more care, dropping the emerald cloak to the floor next to Lena’s small armory. 

“What the hell are these?” Morgan demands as he pulls a string of metal ovals from Lena’s waist. They each have a clip on one end with a small pin in them.

“I’d be careful with those,” Lena taunts with a sweaty, raised eyebrow. “I recommend only people with the basic level of intelligence use them. Unless you want to kill us all, you might want to put them down.” 

Morgan sneers at her, looking back at Lex, who nods before Morgan eases the items to the ground.

At the small of Lena’s back, she’s strapped a miniature crossbow, along with darts in leather holders that run across the back of her belt on either side of it. He finds six more throwing knives strapped along Lena’s rib cage, two daggers along her shoulder blades, amassing a creative pile of concealable weapons. Morgan snaps the string of the velvet bag that hangs around Lena’s neck before asking, “What’s this one?” 

“Rosemary,” Lena deadpans. 

Kara can’t choke back the snort of laughter that escapes her. 

“You can’t help yourself, can you?” Lex interrupts with a bark of laughter. His smile sends a chill down Kara’s spine as his beady eyes flick to her. “Lena I might keep alive. See if I can study her and make the effects last longer…” 

“Effects of what?” Kara asks as she glances over to Morgan. There are no longer electrical currents rushing over his skin, but the memory of them burns all the same. 

Kara won’t ever forget them. 

“Let me show you,” Lex smirks. “Untie them.” 

The men hesitate before Lex repeats himself, his voice deepening and echoing around the cavern in a rumble. Bats flutter from the ceiling before cowering in darker corners. 

Lena is cut free first and Lex grins at her. 

“Where’s your only friend, Lena?” he mocks in a low, contemplative tone. He approaches with his hands clasped behind his back, leaning in close to Lena’s face to study her red and blue gaze. “What are you seeing through their eyes right now?”  

“It doesn’t work like that,” Lena bites back, jutting her chin out in defiance of him. 

“Good. Nobody will find us here then, hmm?” Lex tilts his head to the side, studying Lena before he nods toward Kara.

 Otis cuts her free too, leaving Krypto surrounded by Morgan and his degenerates. With no weapons, Kara is helpless. She can’t fight her way out—not with Lena trying to hide the fact that she’s shivering. Not with each of their enemies armed, and with possible impossible powers.

“Follow me,” Lex orders. 

Kara shakes her head before looking at Krypto, still tied and surrounded by people who are willing and able to harm him. 

“Don’t worry about your little pet,” Lex rolls his eyes. “Morgan will watch him.” 

“Don’t you want me to come too?” Morgan asks. “As backup?” 

Lex barks another laugh before looking at Kara as if they’re sharing an inside joke, and it makes the hair on the back of her neck stand up. Lex scoffs. “Please. We both know you’re useless to me right now. You haven’t got any juice left.” 

Morgan stifles his frown as Otis laughs at him. Morgan glares at Kara and Lena as he flexes his powerless hands.

Lex turns on his heel to lead Kara and Lena toward the smaller tunnel on the other end of the cavern. Kara hesitates—feels no better about Morgan not having any more powers. All Kara can do is hope that the men are afraid enough of Lex to follow his orders not to hurt Krypto. 

Lena pats Kara’s hand with her own, tilting her head toward where Lex is walking away from them, sure that they’ll follow. 

“He’ll be okay,” Lena whispers. 

Kara nods before she takes Lena’s clammy hand and follows after her and Lex.

They pass between the three desks—so out of place in a cavern, but not as terrifying as the collection of things that sit on them. 

Horns, nails, and entire jaws of dragons sit across pages of wing diagrams and scribbled notes. A bottle of ink is spilled across a desk and onto the floor with another shattered beside it. There are scales of every colour, in varying shapes and sizes, and Kara bites her lip as Lena’s convulsions get worse. 

5. Caverns

The further into the tunnel they go, the hotter it gets. It twists in hills and divots, getting higher the longer it goes on, and the thick scent of mold and blood makes Kara cough. 

She’s not as bad as Lena though, who has to lean against Kara as they make their way through the tunnel. 

The red water flows heavier in small paths along the floor, and a dull light struggles through the opening at the end of the wide passage. By the time Lex disappears through it, Lena clings to Kara for balance, taking shuddering breaths as sweat beads around her temples.

“What’s wrong?” Kara breathes. 

Lena has to blink tears from her eyes before she whispers, “I can feel them. I can feel all of them.”

Kara looks ahead into the dim light they’re approaching, but she can’t see into it yet. The sound of rushing water drowns out smaller things, like metal scraping against stone and haunting echoes that don’t quite sound like wind. Kara clenches her jaw as she walks through the threshold of the room, her grip around Lena’s waist tightening as her stomach drops and her eyes burn from the scent of smoke and carnage. 

The room is even bigger than the cavern before, and light struggles to shine through the curtain of a waterfall that pours over an exit that must lead to the lake of that island where Nia found her dragon. The lake creates a large pool within the cavern and its clear water turns red as it rushes in thick currents across the bloody floor and into the caverns below. Kara swallows the urge to vomit as her eyes adjust and she glances around the cave.

Dozens upon dozens of dragons are held captive along the walls. Their wrists and ankles are bound with chains that cross over their backs to hold their wings down, rusty muzzles clasped around their snouts and holding them in place. Each of them is unable to summon their powers under such restraint, like Krypto below. 

Blood seeps from under the dragons as if the very walls they are chained to bleed. Dragons that are able to move struggle against their binds, too tired or hungry for any real force. 

There are at least one of most types of dragons. They’re organized into clusters, making a ring of colourful scales around the room, dulled by the dim light and all marred with blood. 

Lena tries to stifle a whimper, and Kara clenches her jaw. Lena can feel them. Lena can feel all of it. Kara doesn’t understand how, but she doesn’t need to. Not right now. She just has to stop it. 

“What are you doing with them?” Kara can’t help asking as she shoots her eyes across the struggling creatures. 

“Isn’t it obvious?” Lex turns to face them again with a raised eyebrow that wrinkles his forehead. “How else do you think Morgan was able to bring you to me? This livestock is where I get my powers.”

“ Livestock, ” Lena scoffs and pushes off of Kara to stand on her own, but she holds Kara’s ribs with one hand for balance. “These are dragons, Lex. We must respect them and-” 

“Yeah, yeah, yeah,” he waves off. “I’ve heard your little speeches before. No need to beat a dead horse—or dragon, I should say.”

“You’re vile,” Kara spits at him. She casts her eyes around the room, but there’s no way out. She can’t leave Lena, can’t go back the way they came, and Lex is sure to stop her if she tries to escape through the waterfall. 

There’s no way to tell what sort of abilities he may have.

“I’m a genius,” Lex snarls. “Who else would be able to figure out a way to take a dragon’s power? Who else would dare?” 

“You are a fool,” Lena growls out. “Nobody should dare to do this.” 

Lena’s clammy hand gestures to the whimpering dragons around them. Some moan out low cries, others remain still enough for a shiver to run down Kara’s spine, but Lena’s presence provokes the dragons into louder cries that struggle to echo over the rushing waterfall. 

“It’s your fault, you know,” Lex says to Lena with a grin. “When I saw how you could connect to your wyvern—use their power like your own—I had to try it for myself.”

Kara’s brow furrows. She’s not sure what Lena’s power is, can’t make sense of the strange symbiosis Lena seems to share with dragons, but Kara knows none of this is Lena’s fault. 

“Is that what happened to Eve?” Lena asks through her pain-filled gasps. 

“Eve was a good little dragon,” Lex says as he frowns at his nails. “A bit small, but useful. She had to die. That’s the way it works, you see? It was the only way I could gain her power. You have to use every scale, every horn, and most importantly, the heart. Then you’ve got real power!”

Lex whips his hands to his sides, his beady eyes clouding over into a blood-red that stretches into the veins of his face. It spreads over every inch of his skin like the water that runs through the caverns. 

“Why work with these beasts when we could harness their abilities? We could be the powerful ones,” Lex spits. His loud, frenzied voice makes some of the dragons flinch. 

“That’s dangerous,” Lena chokes out.

“So are they,” Lex points to the dragons, cowering in corners or fighting against their muzzles. “The problem is that the powers don’t last. Human souls can’t harness their abilities for long, but who decided that we are not worthy of it?” 

Spit flies from his mouth as he complains, the red sludge within him pulsing with his growing rage. 

“That’s where you come in. If I can study you—figure out how your wyvern works through you, then maybe I can find a way to make my powers permanent. If not, well… I have enough livestock to last me a while.”

“You’re a monster,” Lena bites.

“If I’m a monster, so are you!” He shouts at Lena. He lunges into her personal space as he glares. She flinches, and Kara tightens her hold around Lena’s waist as Lex continues. “We wear the same crest, little sister. We carry the same legacy.” 

Lena’s pale skin is a sickly, pallid blue but when she opens her eyes, they hold nothing but contempt. She raises one of her shaking hands to the Luthor Family crest emblazoned across the front of her dark under armor. The top of it tears in a satisfying rip as she digs her nails under its stitches, pulling with what little strength she has until the frayed black string is broken and Lena tosses the crest to the bloody floor, staining the white skull red. 

“I am nothing like you,” she spits in a hoarse whisper. 

“Pity,” Lex says. He raises his hands toward Lena, red energy coursing through the veins there.

“No,” Kara shouts as she wraps her other arm around Lena, pulling her away from Lex and into Kara’s chest. “No, don’t hurt her!” 

Lex levels her with his sneering, maniacal grin.

“You’re right,” he says. “She should watch you die first. See, Lena? Another life lost because of your power.” 

“No,” Lena pants, but she’s too weak to move. Too surrounded by death or evil, and she can do nothing to stop her brother.

“I’ll have fun killing you,” Lex says to Kara as he raises his hand toward her, red smoke gathering in his palm.

“Over my dead body!” 

Kara spins to see Alex in the doorway, her steel crossbow cocked in one hand, ready to be fired and dropped so she can raise her sabre sword to fight with after the initial blow. J’onn stands behind her and Kara’s eyes light up with the hope swelling in her chest.

Rippling sky blue light silhouettes Alex and J’onn, and the low grumbling that emanates from behind them makes stones skitter across the floor.

Alex is there again—always has been anytime Kara needed her, and Kara wants to cry from the relief of seeing her sister again. 

Alex tosses Kara her greatsword like they practiced and Kara catches it mid-air, twisting it so its sheath flies off and shoots onto the floor of the cave, skittering amongst the rubble. 

Without Kara’s grip, Lena stumbles, falling to the ground on her knees, but Kara needs to focus on Lex now—needs to stop him.

She turns to Lex with a smile. 

“Guess it’s time to see what I can do,” Lex jeers. 

“You won’t get the chance,” J’onn says, and Alex fires a bolt through the air before dropping her crossbow. 

Lex presses his hands toward the bolt, and dark, red ink seeps from his fingers like clouds. He sweeps his arms to the side, causing the smoke to push the arrow into the wall instead. 

Alex pulls her sabre from its sheath as she and J’onn enter in matching silver gear—village chieftain and primary protector. Krypto and Lena’s wyvern crawl through the cave behind them. 

“You’re on your own now, Lex,” J’onn says, but Lex cackles at him. 

“You think that’s new?” Lex shouts. Kara, Alex, and J’onn descend upon him while Lena struggles on the ground by his feet, and Lex pulls the chest of his robes open, revealing pockets filled with glowing, colourful vials. 

Krypto limps into the room, his rigid scales sharp enough to tear through the brown leather of his harness. The electric blue emanating from his eyes courses through his body as he growls at Lex, sharp fangs on display. 

Lena’s wyvern curves around Krypto toward Lena, but Lex slashes his arm toward them, releasing a wave of red smoke that knocks everyone but Lena back. Krypto and the wyvern crash into a group of ivory wisp dragons, and some roar out in pain while others remain motionless. 

Kara leans forward into the smoke to avoid falling backward, her sword clasped in front of her in a vice-grip.

“Don’t touch her!” Lex shouts at the wyvern. Several dragons hiss and snarl through their muzzles, but they can’t break free no matter how hard they struggle. 

Fog emanates from the half-dozen snow and ice dragons hidden amongst the rest, and Kara’s hot breath puffs out of her mouth in clouds. The water of the small lake starts to freeze over, creaking echoes into the cave as the ice shifts and fuzes. It’s not even close to the dragons’ true power, but they are getting restless with the fight going on around them. They shuffle and writhe together, some flickering with light while others fail to blow fire.

Lex pulls a blue vial out of his coat, uncorking the stopper and guzzling it down. Purple seeps into his veins before he spreads his hands wide. Indigo currents of fire shoot out of his arms and chest, and he cackles before his face scrunches in exertion, pushing Kara, Alex, and J’onn up against the dripping walls near the entrance of the cave with his new power.

The smoke from the fire clings to Kara’s throat, pours into her nose and mouth, scalding wherever it touches. It’s impossible for her to breathe, and Lex stands there with his arms spread, a wide smile in place as he strangles them. 

“Stop,” Lena chokes from the ground. She takes a chipped dragon horn from the bloody floor, stabbing it into Lex’s thigh, and the harsh pressure of his smoke releases Kara from her hold on the wall, along with Alex and J’onn. “Gus! Where did you even get those? I keep the shop so clean!” Winn complains with a glare at the happy dragon on his shoulder. Winn glances at Alex’s raised eyebrow before he clears his throat. “...Mostly clean.”

Alex snorts.

Lena glances at Kara with wide eyes and her mouth agape, and Kara beams at her. 

Lena deserves everything, especially this if she wants it. 

Lena’s returning smile is shy and crooked, but her dimples peek out under her pink cheeks, and she nods at Winn. 

He cheers before gesturing into the shop, yammering on about all of his inventions—saddles and stirrups, as well as fake legs or tail fins for the dragons that lost theirs. Lena listens with rapt attention, adding more ideas as they disappear into the shop together. 

Kara smiles at her as she leans on Krypto’s side, sighing as she watches them. Neve and Enya stand on Kara’s other side, and though Enya is content to flick their tongue out and otherwise bother Krypto with hisses and playful nipping, Neve keeps a close watch on Lena through the wide, open door.

Alex clears her throat behind Kara, and she spins to face Alex with wide eyes. 

“I guess saving her life wasn’t a bad start for a first date,” Alex says around her smirk.

“She saved mine too!” Kara argues and then grits her teeth as her cheeks blaze. “And it wasn’t a date.”

“Did you give her the daybreak flower?” Alex teases and Kara blush worsens. 

“I… that’s not the point!” Kara cries as she fidgets with the brown straps of Krypto’s harness. “We got captured and almost died a dozen times afterward.” 

“But she took it?” 

“...Maybe? Yes.” 

“Then it was a date.” 

“Some date,” Kara mutters with a shake of her head.

“Are you kidding?” Alex nudges Kara’s ribs as they lean against Krypto together, Saber settling at Alex's feet to sleep. “You gave her a daybreak, discovered a dastardly plot, fought bad guys together, saved each other’s lives, freed over a hundred dragons, and then flew home in the setting sun! What more could you possibly want from a first date?” 

Kara’s nose scrunches up as she laughs. She kicks the dirt with a bashful smile and hopes that Alex is right. 

“I was wrong about her, you know,” Alex murmurs as she looks at Lena. Lena’s hands move as fast as her lips as she picks up some of Winn’s tools and points to the pile of schematics that always lays half-finished on his workbench.

“Not often you admit to that,” Kara teases.

“It’s not often that I have to,” Alex says. “But I was really wrong this time and I want you to know that whatever you feel for her, whatever happens between you, I will support you in it.” 

Kara pulls Alex into a crushing hug that makes her groan, and Kara laughs before pulling away. 

Lena exits the shop with rosy cheeks and a crooked, dimpled smile, taking a deep breath as she approaches. 

“Looks like I got a job,” Lena chuckles.

“Looks like I’ll be seeing more of you,” Kara beams. “You should rest. I’m sure Nia has extra blankets in her fabric shop if you don’t want to fly all the way to Luthor Manor.”

“You can stay at the Inn if you like. Sam would be happy to have you, I’m sure,” Alex offers.

“That’s very kind of you, but I have to do something first,” Lena says to Alex before her eyes bounce to Kara. “It’s very important, but I’ll be back tonight. I promise.”

Kara’s eyes widen when Lena grabs her hand and leans in to kiss her cheek, close to her mouth, and Kara feels the warmth of Lena’s soft lips right down to her heart. 

Lena hops on her wyvern’s back before taking off, and Kara gazes after her until Lena disappears into the lilac sky, the most eager stars beginning to shine through the haze. 

“Oh, you’ve got it bad, ” Alex teases. 

“Mhm,” Kara agrees with a nod. Then she realizes what Alex said, and she blushes, trying to push herself off of Krypto and tripping over her feet. She hits her face on his fraying saddle instead. 

Alex bursts out laughing, and Kara pouts at her, a crinkle forming between her eyes. 

“Shut up,” Kara pouts, “or I’ll tell Kelly about that time when we were kids and you thought it would be a good idea to prank J’onn by attaching a flower crown to Aenach’s head, but he kicked you clean out of the marsh and broke three of your ribs.” 

“I already know about that,” Kelly says, and both Kara and Alex jump before turning toward where Kelly walks with her bellflower dragon following behind. Kara’s never seen Kelly without Slanu, and Kelly doesn’t remember a time without her rich, plum-coloured dragon. The edges of each of Slanu’s scales tinges gold, glowing at different levels of brightness depending on the stress of those around her, which is helpful with kids and healing. 

Kelly grins as she teases, “I’d love to hear any other embarrassing stories you might have, though.” 

“All in good time,” Alex murmurs as she gazes at her wife.

Kara smiles at Kelly, her grin getting soft as she looks into the two little bundles of blankets Kelly holds. Two tiny humans, wrapped in forest-green blankets and slumbering together. Alex’s hard edges melt away as she takes the babies, cooing down at them with a smile. 

Kara swallows the lump in her throat. They’re orphans, like Kara herself, but she knows they’ll have the best home with Alex and Kelly. Kara did after all, and she’s sure they both have the biggest hearts on the planet. 

She leans down to give each of her sister’s children a soft kiss on the forehead, and because she can’t help it, leans back in to kiss both Kelly and Alex’s heads too. 

They laugh at her, but Kara doesn’t mind. 

“I’m glad you’re okay,” Kelly says to Alex as their little family huddles together. 

“Me too. Did J’onn tell you what happened?” Alex asks, and Kelly nods.

“You get to watch the babies while I go help the rest of the dragons. J’onn wants Winn and Eliza to come too, while Lena rests,” Kelly says before looking at Kara. “I’d like to hear more about her powers sometime, if she’s up for it.”

“You’ll have to ask her,” Kara shrugs before looking over her shoulder to where Lena disappeared. 

It’s been a long day, and as much as Kara loves her village, she built a house near the forest because of the silence that greets her there. 

“I’ll see you guys later,” Kara says. 

“Let me know what happens with Lena!” Alex shouts as Kara walks away. Lena, not Luthor. 

Kara shakes her head with a laugh, marveling at the difference a day can make. 

Krypto butts his head under her legs, tossing Kara up into the air before catching her in his saddle, shaking with excited energy as she grabs hold of the carved, wooden bar at the end of his saddle. 

The flight home isn’t long, but through it, the sky changes from violet to navy, and though there’s no moon tonight, light from the stars shines down on them, along with the random bursts of flames from dragons roaming free. 

They land in the long grass of the field in front of Kara’s house and together, surrounded by crickets and fireflies, Kara lies on her back. She folds a hand behind her head as she smiles into the night sky, eager for whatever tomorrow brings. 

Krypto rests his head on her thighs, and she strokes the scales behind his ears as he starts to snore. 

Under the dark sky, Kara’s eyes haze over, and she can’t tell which spots are stars or fireflies. With the gentle floral scent of Kara’s garden spreading through the grass on a gentle breeze, Kara loses track of time. 

Her eyes start to close, and she doesn’t stir at the flutter of wings nearby, sure that it’s one of the freed dragons until a soft voice murmurs, “I hope I’m not barging in.” 

Kara jolts up, jostling Krypto, and he grumbles up at her before shifting away and stomping over to where Lena is dismounting Enya and Neve. 

“Not at all,” Kara says. Her voice cracks from disuse and she clears her throat.

Lena chuckles and Krypto stretches before he settles at the wyvern’s feet—Neve glancing in abject horror as Enya coos and butts their head against Krypto’s. 

Lena reaches into the black side-saddle secured to her wyvern’s leather harness. She pulls out something bright and tries to hide it behind her back as she approaches. She bites her lip as she kneels before Kara, tilting to her side as she produces a blue moonstone flower, its white edges fraying as its pale blue glow lights up Lena’s face. 

“I…” Lena says, her eyes wide and lost as she searches for words, and to Kara, Lena looks like the moon—every bit as solitary, mysterious, and alluring as her face shines in the moonstone light. Lena bites her lip before she whispers, “I found this for you. To say that I return your affection.”

Kara's eyes crinkle at the corners as she beams at Lena, who found one of the rarest flowers across the islands within hours, and is giving it to Kara. The hot blush of her cheeks makes the air around her much cooler, and Kara’s hand shakes as she reaches out.

She bites her lip as she takes it, rubbing her thumb across the soft, pale fringe of its perfect petals. 

“I would understand if you changed your mind,” Lena murmurs as she gazes at Kara. “After everything that happened today-” 

“No,” Kara chokes out. Her breath gets caught in her throat, and she struggles to find the words to describe how much this day has only solidified what she already knew. How could she explain the way Lena’s gaze makes her feel? How could Kara find the words to describe the warmth swelling in her chest, the way her heart flutters, or how Lena takes her breath away—and Kara is glad to give it. 

There are no words to describe how Lena makes her want to laugh and sing and cry with joy, but Lena must know. Kara reaches over to tuck a flyaway curl that has escaped Lena’s thick braid, and as she leans in, Lena’s eyes roam over Kara’s face, landing on her parted lips. 

And Kara no longer has a need for words. 

She wraps her hand around the base of Lena’s neck, and they pull together like an inevitability—as if theirs is a story written in the stars that shine above them. Their lips meet in a tender brush, the flower glowing between their chests as they kiss. 

It’s gentle and tentative, and when Kara pulls away she brushes Lena’s angular jaw, admiring the way Lena looks with her eyes closed as she bites her lip. 

It’s nothing compared to the way Lena looks when she opens her eyes. It's as if the night sky sparkles there. Lena takes a heaving breath before she pushes forward and Kara has to hold the flower out with one arm so it doesn’t get crushed by the force of Lena’s embrace. 

Kara chuckles as she wraps her arms around Lena, content in their shared warmth in the midst of cooling air. Lena pulls away as she clears her throat. It’s hard to tell in the pale light, but her cheeks look darker as she opens the small, velvet bag around her neck. She glances up at Kara before she pulls out the frayed, pathetic remnants of Kara’s daybreak flower, holding them in her open palm next to the moonstone. 

Their hands brush together as Kara leans in to kiss Lena again.

They’re interrupted by Neve and Enya weaving their way through the grass to lay behind Lena, and Krypto groans in annoyance at having to get up and move again. Tiny sparks shoot out of his mouth to dance with the fireflies before he settles against Kara’s outstretched legs again. 

Lena slumps against her wyvern before she asks, “Aren’t you scared of them?” 

One of her thick brows is quirked, but she can’t hide the sorrow of her eyes or the hope that shines through it. 

Looking at the pale grey scales of Lena’s wyvern and the warm, red eyes of Enya or the level blue of Neve’s, Kara doesn’t feel fear. She feels opportunity. 

“No,” Kara murmurs as she leans on the wyvern next to Lena, wrapping a tentative arm around her shoulders. 

“Why aren’t you afraid?” Lena whispers almost too quietly for Kara to hear over the rustling grass. “Everyone else is. Or was. You don’t think a creature like this choosing me makes me as vile as my brother?” 

The wyvern’s grey scales indicate a sort of in-between, no direct colour, but no lightness or darkness either—like it could be anything if given the chance. 

The scales, the two heads with contrasting coloured eyes, every part of Lena’s wyvern is both blank and in stark opposition of itself, which makes Kara want to know everything she can about the human worthy of such a breathtaking creature.

Lena’s eyes are different colours too, even when they’re not red and blue. They’re a shade different—as if this wyvern choosing her was as destined as the old stories say it is.

There’s a duality about everything Lena does—a casual intensity, an enigmatic paradox, and Kara wants to discover every facet of her, but she can’t see how anybody could think that any part of Lena might be evil.

Lena bites her bottom lip as Kara assesses her, holds her hands together in a tight grasp as if trying hard not to fidget with her fingers. 

“I think it means you get to choose who you want to be,” Kara says with a lopsided grin as she reaches a tentative hand up. Enya nuzzles their snout into it as plumes of steam rise from their nose. “Why would I be scared of that?” 

The corners of Lena’s lips quirk up. She smiles at Kara with such gentleness, and even though the night around them cools, surrounded by Lena and wings, Kara feels fire. 

Lena drops her head to Kara’s shoulder, and they look up at the stars together as they lean against Neve and Enya, Krypto snoring beside them.

7. Crest

A Year Later

Kara jogs past the small lake that her village circles, smiling at the wildflowers that bloom in clusters throughout the patches of grass that grow around the buildings. Ivy clings its way up most houses, the shutters of their windows wide open to let the sun shine in. 

Clotheslines stretch between the villagers' homes, and smaller dragons perch amongst the drying sheets and clothes. Sometimes new-born dragons will sneeze sparks of fire onto clothes, but the village only has to worry about that for one month every twelve years when dragons have hatchlings. 

The cobblestone fades to dirt paths leading to peoples’ doors, the wood of each stained a different colour. Family crests shine proudly on all of them.

Kara grins at the mossy bookshop door, decorated with a teal crest engraved with a book that has an hourglass on the cover.

Beyond the great log building, Aisling dives in and out of the water while Doc watches from the quiet safety of a library window. Kara shakes her head, beaming as she waves to Brainy and Nia, who sit together on the porch of his bookshop that overlooks the water. Brainy waves back, but Nia’s eyes are closed, and she rests her head against his shoulder.

Kara is sweaty from her first official person-based combat training session with J’onn and Alex. He’s going to retire soon, and the people chose Alex to become chieftain while Kara will become the primary protector. Fighting is rare on the islands, but if it ever happens again, Kara wants to be ready. She wants to help. 

Krypto’s been on his own all day, and though Kara doesn’t have to worry about dragons disappearing — hasn’t for a long time — she’s still eager to see him. 

She chuckles as she runs past Winn, hammering away at a greatsword while Gus perches on his shoulder to blow fire onto it in between hits. Gus pauses his helping to belch, then tilts his head forward to cough several nuts and bolts onto the ground. 

“Ah, come on!” Winn complains. He shakes his head, and his eyes catch on Kara. “Hey! Hey, Kara!” 

She slows to a stop beside his forge and Winn wipes the sweat from his brow.

“Tell Lena she was right about Aisling. It was a stomach ache from too much herring,” he says. 

Kara walks backward from him with a smile, bouncing on her feet as she says, “Lena would know! Best dragon healer the world’s ever seen!” 

“And tell her I want her opinion on the schematics for Doc’s new leg!” 

“ Okay, Winn.” 

Kara laughs and raises her hand over her shoulder in a wave before turning down the sandy path that leads home. Dirt kicks up from her heels as they patter against the path that runs along an old split-rail fence. Its wooden beams rot from the inside, and the villagers have placed countless bets on it because it always looks ready to fall. 

Kara reckons it’s got three summers left, but Alex says two. 

Lena has half a mind to knock it over herself if it makes through the rest of the storms this summer —if only to build a better one. 

The path twists with the rolling hills that lead to the forest and Kara breathes deeply, her lungs filled with the salty musk of skin that summer brings. 

Krypto is at the very edge of her lawn waiting, and Kara shouts, “Time to fly!” 

She spares a glance to her garden that the town children have dubbed the firework flowers, and Kara beams at the butterflies that surround it before she jumps on Krypto’s back. He spreads his wings, golden-yellow except for the long-healed pink scar, and dirt bursts around them as they fly into the sky, heading west where Kara knows Lena is waiting for her. 

Lena hovers in the air near the edge of Kara’s island, right above where it begins to crumble into the flying isles. She hangs there, swaying with the force of Neve and Enya's wings, looking upward with her eyes closed and letting the light shine on her — a small smile in place. 

Kara barrels past her, laughing as Lena shouts that she’s a cheater. Kara struggles to hear it over the wind whipping against her face, but she laughs anyway, feels like she can’t hold it in. 

Krypto lands at the top of the isles, claws digging into the waving grass as he tucks his wings around Kara’s thighs for less wind resistance. 

Lena, Neve, and Enya fly between the thick tree roots and stones below, their wings reflecting the golden rays of the sun in flashes.

“I’m going to beat you!” Kara shouts down to her as they race. 

“In your dreams!” Lena shouts back as her wyvern ducks under a mossy boulder and around a crumbling root. 

Kara beams down at her. She doesn't care about the race — wins every single day when Krypto and the wyvern compete, even when she loses. As Krypto launches off of the final boulder, sunlight glistening off his scales as he spreads his wings to spin in the air and fly into golden clouds beyond, Kara thinks that Lena is right. 

This is her dream. 

The light of the sun setting on the horizon, so far Kara can’t see the ground, is almost blinding, but they soar toward it together anyway. Fluffy, yellow clouds shift above and below them, and they twirl around each other, dancing through the air as they avoid the fluttering redshank birds that follow in the riptides of scaled wings.

Lena’s emerald eyes glow in the light, and Kara’s breath gets caught in her throat as Lena’s ebony hair flows behind her in a thick braid — bits and pieces falling out and glowing like fire. 

But what’s most breathtaking of all, Kara thinks, is the family crest that rests over Lena’s heart — a blooming flower, half gold, half blue — blazing in the light that reflects on it.

The family crest that sits against Enya and Neve’s chest, Krypto’s chest, and above Kara’s heart as well. 

And Kara knows that when their family is done soaring the skies they will return home together, and there, upon Kara’s door, the same flower will be shining in the filled placard of her door.

8. Of Dragons and Wyverns

Summary for the Chapter:

This is optional reading material. It explains where I drew inspiration for each dragon type as well as their names. There's trivia about how each dragon/wyvern we see in the fic chose their person, along with other fun facts about them and the world.

The difference between dragons and wyverns is that dragons have four limbs, and a set of wings that are independent of those limbs, like Dragon from Shrek. 

Wyverns have back legs, but their front limbs double as wings and their arms, with claws at the joint in the middle—like bats! All the dragons in the videogame Skyrim are actually wyverns, which I find way funnier than I should. Anyway, below is a list of peoples’ dragons in this fic, and random bonus trivia about them.

Winn: Winn has a dragon named Gus (like Gust of Wind! Get it? Gus and Winn? It's a pun. Anyway). The name Gus means excellence and courage in Irish. He’s pretty tiny and earthy brown with rusty accents. He kind of camouflages into the wood and dirt of the blacksmith shop where Winn works, and the horns of his head bend at odd angles, coming up into points that look like wrenches.
Gus found Winn in a time of need—when Winn’s father burnt down the old blacksmith shop and tried to take the city with it. Together, Winn and Gus rebuilt it even better than it was before.

Gus is often seen draped over Winn’s shoulders, or running about his shop with tools in his mouth, or blowing flames onto the blades of swords when Winn needs more heat on them. 

Sometimes Gus accidentally swallows nuts and bolts and coughs them up on Winn's bed like a cat.

Winn doesn't know where Gus keeps finding them, because he's careful to keep his shop clean.

Mostly.

J'onn: SWAMP DRAGON! He’s huge. Bigger than a house. Even as one of the biggest dragon types, he was hard to find, because swamp dragons like to sleep and are often confused as boulders or large hills. Aenach is the oldest dragon alive on the islands. He’s got bushes and trees growing on his back that J’onn carved into a saddle to sit in. His brown horns and tusks even look like branches or tree stumps. Some are broken. 

He doesn’t fly often anymore, but when he does, enemies cower. At least until Aenach falls asleep, that is.

Aenach is an old Irish translation for fen, with is a sort of swamp, where this dragon was found. The word also defines a gathering of people in which laws are made known, or a reunion happens. 

Brainy: His dragon is spring-grass green. He’s a meadow dragon, which tend to prefer solitary lives. His name is Doc (like Dox, as in Querl Dox haha), and he has three white horns that protrude out of his skull like a trident. Doc is excellent at camouflage and is about the size of a cougar, so he’s too small to ride, though he’s happy to carry books and scrolls whenever Brainy has found too many to carry back to his bookshop. 

He’s missing a back leg, but nobody knows how it happened.

He likes to get books down off of high shelves for patrons of the bookshop, too, though he has a tendency to place them on peoples’ heads or where he thinks they should be sitting as opposed to the seat they chose.

Doc is a patient dragon, often found dozing in the sunspots of Brainy’s shop or listening to children learn how to read to him. 

Nia: She'd have a water dragon named Aisling (Dream or Vision in Irish). These are really rare, and it would have taken Nia a while to find Aisling because they live in freshwater, and Nia didn't have much opportunity to travel around the islands until she was older. She thought, for a little while, that maybe she didn't get a dragon, but Aisling is one of the most elegant the islanders have ever seen. 

She can breathe above and underwater, swim and fly (but can fly for less time than other dragons), and can form an air bubble around herself so Nia can go underwater with her. Aisling has a slight bioluminescent glow while underwater, and it gets brighter the deeper she goes. She's a dazzling sapphire blue at the very height of her back, but the scales get lighter as they descend, giving Aisling white, fin-like wings that spill behind her like the train of a bride's dress. 

Nia refuses to put a saddle on Aisling because she doesn't want to ruin the aesthetic.

Kelly: Bellflower dragon. I really like the symbolism of this flower and think it represents Kelly really well—a symbol of affection, constancy, and everlasting love. This flower is eager to grow, and I liked that as a metaphor for Kelly and Slanu's healing tendencies. Her dragon is a rich purple (like her leather jacket in the show!!) with amber eyes that match Saber’s. The edges of each of Slanu’s scales tinge gold, glowing at different levels of brightness depending on the stress of people and dragons around her. 

Kelly’s dragon found her really young—when she was about six, despite most dragons choosing their people during teen years, and together, Kelly and Slanu have been helping people and dragons feel better ever since.

Slanu means Healing, Deliverance, and Yearning in Irish. 

Alex: Saber is a midnight dragon, which means she was born exactly at midnight and her scales reflect that. They look like the night sky. Saber’s powers are interesting because although she can and does use her colourful explosions as a defense mechanism, she mostly uses them to entertain the village children. Alex was about seventeen when Saber found her, and Alex thought it was a mistake at first because Saber was a baby. She was the size of a cat, and Alex thought this lost little dragon just needed help, but it would not leave her alone, no matter which dragon family Alex tried to place it with. It’s unusual for a baby dragon to pick a human, but Saber never left Alex’s side, and they learned how to take care of people together. Saber helped Alex learn how to take care of Kara, too.

Kara: Heatstorm dragon that can summon lightning. I named him Krypto because it felt right. Krypto’s design was based on a golden retriever! He is essentially a giant puppy with a rarely needed protectiveness. Kara was adopted by the Danvers at a young age in this fic. Krypto found Kara a few years after she was adopted, in a time where she started to realize what that meant and spent a great deal of her time wondering about her birth family that she only has hazy memories of. 

She was about thirteen when Krypto chose her, and he helped her learn that people get to choose their families just like dragons choose their people—that blood alone isn’t what matters. 

Alex was jealous when Kara got her dragon first but quickly grew to love Krypto and his fierce loyalty to Kara. 

Lex: Eve was a mousy lil beige dragon. Very Tiny. She was often in his pockets or on his shoulder or found places she wasn’t supposed to be, stealing tiny trinkets. I just really wanted Eve to be a dragon in this fic, I have no reasoning for this. It felt right.

Lena: Her wyvern’s design was based on greyhounds, which I love. They’re the fastest dog, so I reflected that in Lena’s wyvern. Since dragons are resistant to each other’s powers in this universe, Neve and Enya have to work through Lena to heal. I really loved that the artist gave it two heads because Lena has such duality in her, and Neve and Enya reflect that nicely. 

I feel like Neve and Enya chose Lena when she was about nine, and Lex got really jealous when they learned about the powers Lena could control. It would take years, but the thoughts of that power eventually consume Lex. 

As seen in the fic, Neve means snowy or bright. Neve is very careful and reserved with anyone who isn’t Lena. I wanted this to represent Lena’s tendency toward isolation, despite her hidden desires. 

This is where Enya comes in, who represents Lena’s true desires and the more rash part of her personality. Enya means fire, and they are quicker to lash out at surprises or demand to be petted from the people Lena feels safe around. 

I think they're a cliffside wyvern, so its grey scales keep it hidden as it clings to the stones, and one of my favourite things about Ireland are the vast cliffs—both beautiful and terrifying. One of Lena's main personality components is how she has a hard exterior, like stone. Plus the green grass at the top and the blue ocean below are a nice reflection of Lena's different eyes.

As you probably noticed, most of these dragons are based on aspects of nature that I like, and their names are Irish, with specific meanings behind them. This is because I love Ireland, and the setting where this story takes place is a fictional string of islands not too far from Ireland. I completely made them up, but I drew a lot of inspiration from specific places on google maps while I was exploring haha. 

There are a lot of dragon types that I casually mentioned throughout the fic, and though these creatures are mythical, the basic rule I used was as follows:

If it exists in nature, a dragon can be based on it. 

There are dragons inspired by all elements, specific plants, different terrains, and even weather. I did this so each dragon would have connotations attached to each individual aspect of nature, and would feel more unique and personal at the same time. All plants have meaning, and I liked the idea of each dragon having meaning too.

Also, dragons in this universe only produce hatchlings every twelve years! I got the inspiration for this fun fact from Spyro , which is my favourite videogame of all time, and also features dragons. I never have any opportunities to share my useless Spyro lore, so I snuck some into this fic.
