Math is easy, alright? It’s simple. There are rules and laws and standards and it’s always the same. Math has never, never failed Lena before. So clearly, whatever is going on with these numbers is a failing on the behalf of mathematics as a whole and not Lena. Obviously.

Now, could the argument be made that Lena’s just a touch overworked and that’s why it seems like math is suddenly and violently rebelling against her? That maybe she should slow down - not stop, but slow down - just a little bit? Maybe take a nap? Cool it on the caffeine, even? Yeah. Absolutely. You could make that argument. But she would never.

Kara, on the other hand…

“Lena. You’ve been at this non-stop for a week. You need a break.”

Kara’s trying to be serious, Lena’s sure of it. She’s probably wearing that patented Serious Supergirl Face of hers. The one that really should’ve clued Lena into who she really was years ago. But, honestly? Lena doesn’t care to find out. She won’t look at Kara because if she does, she’s sure she’ll actually manage to convince her to stop. And we can’t have that now.

“I’ll take a break once I finish this,” she tells Kara, still not looking up from her laptop.

She can practically feel Kara’s disappointed gaze burning into her. And for a brief moment, she considers looking over and letting her best friend drag her away from her work yet again. But it is in fact a very brief moment. Because immediately after the words leave her mouth, the simulation crashes. Again.

“No. No, no, no. Fuck!” Lena exclaims. She buries her face in her hands and lets loose an irritated groan. “God damn it!”

“Alright, that’s it,” Kara mutters, mostly to herself, but Lena still hears it. She hears it, but doesn’t fully understand it. Not until her laptop is snapped shut. “You’re taking a break.”

She lifts her head from her hands to send a withering glare at Kara. One that simply doesn’t have the effect it should. “Kara!”

She grabs for her laptop at the same moment that Kara does. But, of course, the kryptonian’s superspeed wins out and the computer is whisked away before Lena can even attempt to berate her again.

Lena gets to her feet, an angry ‘ Kara Danvers ’ on the tip of her tongue, when her world flips and suddenly she’s in the air. Scooped up by two very muscular arms that are not letting go anytime soon. And all that anger slips away into a surprised, breathy “Kara,” gasped into the now incredibly small space between them.

“You’re taking a break,” Kara announces.

“Kara, put me down.”

“Nope.” Kara staunchly ignores Lena’s scathing glare and walks them away from Lena’s kitchen in favor of the couch in the living room. “Sorry.”

“I told you, I’m f--”

“If you say you’re fine one more time, Lena, I am never having Big Belly Burger with you again.”

Well, that. That’s just, “Rude.”

Kara shrugs. “I’m comfortable with that.”

Having arrived at their destination, Kara casually plops them both down on the couch. Lena immediately squirms free. Or at least she tries to, because Kara’s holding her tighter now and will not let go no matter how much Lena protests.

“Now, we’re going to take a nap,” Kara announces.

Lena glowers at her. “Kara.”

“And when that’s done, we’re gonna get lunch.”

“Kara!”

“And then , we’ll see about you going back to whatever’s stressing you out so much.”

Lena huffs out in irritation, rubs her temple, and then glares at Kara. Yet again. “I need to finish this.”

“And you will. Later.”

“Kara, I--”

“Whatever this secret project of yours is, I’m sure it’s very important and impressive and brilliant, as always. But you gotta take a minute for yourself. I mean, come on. Today’s your one day off and you’re spending it cursing math . You love math.”

Lena scowls. “Yes, well, now I hate it. It’s not working and it hates me.”

“Sure it does,” Kara says placatingly. “But you’re gonna take a nap anyway. And maybe when you wake up, it’ll hate you a little less.”

“Don’t patronize me,” Lena grumbles.

“I would never.” She runs her hand up Lena’s back comfortingly. Her tireless fingers knead into the tense muscles there. And, damn , if that doesn’t feel amazing. Honestly, you really can’t blame Lena for letting her eyes slide shut under that stimulus. “You should get some rest, Lena.”

Lena exhales sharply and forces her eyes back open. The moment they reconnect with Kara’s blue ones, she can’t breathe for a solid five seconds. “I need to get this done. I can’t seem to get the schematics just right, and if I can’t get the schematics perfect then I can’t order the materials and then I’ll never--” She cuts herself off. Takes a deep breath. And tries again. “It’s important .”

“I know. But you’re not gonna get it done like this.” Kara’s hand finds a particularly tense muscle and Lena feels her resolve fracturing at that contact. “Take a nap. When you wake up, we’ll get some food. And then, when you’re refreshed, I’m sure you’ll use that big brain of yours to think your way out of whatever slump you’re in.”

Lena stares at her. Long and hard. Trying to think up some way to get out of this. Some way to get back to the simulation that has been beating her all day. Some way to just finish it. But, ultimately, Kara’s right. Which is just as annoying as it is endearing.

“Fine,” she grinds out.

“Great!” Kara beams.

She pulls Lena even closer, one arm at her back the other under her knees, and reclines into the couch. Her hand glides up Lena’s back before coming to rest at the back of her head. She guides her down until Lena’s resting against her shoulder. Tucked right up into the crook of her neck. And Lena… well, she freezes. Staring straight ahead past Kara’s arm, staunchly ignoring just how defined her bicep looks holding her like this. Doing her utmost to not notice just how warm Kara is (as always).

“This… wasn’t what I thought you had in mind,” Lena admits.

“I can’t trust you to nap on your own, silly,” Kara answers.

Lena rolls her eyes with a dramatic groan. “Maybe that’s because I don’t need a nap.”

“And that is exactly why we’re staying like this.”

Lena squirms a little. You know, to test out just how committed Kara is to staying like this and not at all to see her arms flexing so she can keep her still. With a defeated huff, she concedes. “Okay, fine.”

Kara’s hand returns to its task of kneading the tension out of Lena’s back. “Good girl,” she says. Teasingly, Lena’s sure of it. But that doesn’t stop her face from burning bright red. “Now get some sleep.”

“Don’t tell me what to do,” Lena grumbles back.

She falls asleep to the sound of Kara’s light laughter rumbling into her chest, so pressed up against each other as they are.

 

It’s late. Very late. Lena’s not sure how late exactly, but her apartment is awash in shadows. The only light comes in through the windows from the streets below. She must’ve slept the entire afternoon. So it’s possible that you could argue that Kara was right, after all.

“Morning, sleepyhead,” a thick and groggy voice reaches Lena’s ears.

Her entire body tenses as she suddenly realizes that she’s still curled up on Kara’s lap, the kryptonian’s arms still wrapped around her, though much more loosely now. Kara stretches underneath her a little bit before relaxing again.

“I guess I should say ‘ evening ’ actually. It’s not really morning anymore,” Kara says. Her voice slowly returning to its normal, far less sleepy tone. “I mean, it wasn’t when we started our nap earlier either, but… you get the point.”

“What time is it?” Lena asks. Her tongue sticks a little and her words slur together.

“Uh…” Kara trails off. She paws around on the couch for a few seconds, likely searching for her phone, before giving up on that. “Late.”

Lena chuckles darkly. “Well said,” she mutters.

She sits up in Kara’s lap, stretches her own limbs out in front of her, and then looks around the apartment. She spies the clock on the TV immediately. 8:13pm. She slept for 6 hours.

“Kara!” She snaps, meeting Kara’s bewildered gaze. “That was supposed to be a nap .”

“It was a nap,” Kara argues.

“We were asleep for six hours ! That’s a short coma !”

“I mean, technically speaking, wouldn’t you say that any amount of sleep would be considered a short coma?”

Lena should be mad. She knows she should be. But damn it. Just looking at Kara melts all of that anger away. Again. So she decides to stop looking at her. She gets to her feet, pushing off of the couch (careful to not touch Kara any more than necessary), and stalks back over to her kitchen. She flicks on a few lights in the kitchen on her way over, wincing slightly under the harsh light.

“If you thought my sleep schedule was out of whack before, it’s even more so now.”

She hears Kara moving around at her back. Likely also getting off the couch. Maybe even following after her.

“Yeah, but don’t you feel better?” Lena can hear the jaunty grin in her voice.

Lena sighs when she reaches the kitchen island where Kara had discarded her laptop. “Yes,” she admits quietly.

Kara’s there in the space of a heartbeat, slamming closed Lena’s laptop before she can finish opening it. Green eyes meet blue in the half-lit apartment. There’s a charged moment where they just stare at each other. Where Lena tries to hold onto the anger that propelled her off the couch in the first place, but it’s already long gone. Where Kara just stares down at her with a challenging grin that eats away at whatever trace amounts of irritation might be left in Lena.

“Food first, Lena. Then you can get back to work,” Kara says.

“I never agreed to that. And besides…” Lena slides the laptop out from under Kara’s hand. “That was when we were just going to take a nap . You know, an hour. Maybe two, tops. Not six.”

“You need food, Lena.”

Lena hops up onto the chair she was evicted from earlier, opens her laptop, and sets back to work. “I can multitask.”

Kara lets out a defeated sigh. “Fine. I’ll get us some food.”

“Great,” Lena says a bit stiffly, her attention split. She’s already breezed through the first of many calculations that were giving her trouble earlier. She lets her gaze flicker over to Kara before adding, “Thank you.”

“No problem.”

Lena stops what she’s doing and leans back in her chair. “Not just for getting us food,” she says. That catches Kara’s attention. Her eyes light up and she visibly can’t stop herself from smiling. “For making me take a break. I…” Lena groans. “I needed that.”

Kara’s grin brightens enough to light up the whole apartment. “Of course! What are friends for?”

Lena smiles softly to herself. Right. Friends .

 

Kara returns about a half hour later with a takeout bag from their favorite Thai place. Lena hasn’t moved an inch since she left. Still bunched up on the one barstool by the kitchen island. Still plucking away at her calculations, schematics, and simulations.

Lena can feel her gaze as she walks over. Comes up right behind her. And sets the bag of food down alongside the laptop. Her hand comes to rest on Lena’s shoulder, and Lena finds herself leaning into it without thinking about it.

“How’s it going?” Kara asks.

“Good,” Lena announces. “It crashed twice and failed once.”

There’s a pause as Lena figures out a few more calculations. It’s truly embarrassing that she forgot to carry the one here. One error. That’s all it took to mess everything else up.

“How exactly is that good?” Kara asks.

“It means it’s working ,” Lena explains. “The failure does, at least. The crashing not so much.”

“Right.” Kara leans a little closer and bends down to look over her shoulder at her computer. “So what is this secret project of yours anyway?”

Lena’s fingers freeze over the keyboard. “Kara.”

“Okay, okay, sorry. I won’t ask anymore. I know it’s supposed to be a surprise.” The kryptonian backs off, lifting both her hands in the air innocently.

Lena sighs deeply and leans back from her computer. She’s finally made some progress today, so she could absolutely leave it there until tomorrow. And if stopping here means she gets to spend more time with Kara, then so be it.

“No, Kara. You don’t have to apologize.” She twists in her seat to meet her gaze. “It’s alright.”

Kara grins beatifically. “Does that mean you’ll tell me about it?”

“Eventually.”

Kara rolls her eyes with a sigh. She plops down onto the seat next to Lena and sets to doling out the various containers of takeout she’d purchased. “Can you at least tell me what it’s called?”

What’s the harm in that? “Baymax.”

Kara stops. Her face scrunches up. Then her gaze meets Lena’s. “Baymax?” She repeats.

“Mhmm,” Lena confirms.

Kara’s nose scrunches a bit more. “It’s weird.” She grins. “I like it!”

 



Lena isn’t avoiding Kara, okay? She’s just very busy. That’s all. What with being the CEO of her own company, running her own experiments and tests, and this new little passion project she has a lot going on. Her absence in Kara’s life these past few days has absolutely nothing to do with the black eye she’s currently sporting. Don’t be ridiculous.

Though, in hindsight, she really should’ve known she’d only be able to bail on lunch with Kara so many times before she came knocking.

Lena’s knee deep in some complex coding, trying to figure out what went wrong with this last test and fix it, when she gets the call. She barely even looks at her phone screen before answering. Not that she needs to.

“Kara, hi,” she says warmly.

“Are you avoiding me?”

Well, shit . “What? No. Of course not.”

“Are you sick? You know if you’re sick you can just tell me, right?”

“Yes, I know-- no, Kara. I’m not sick.”

Lena can practically see the pinch in her brow. The confusion painted all over her face as she tries to puzzle out what’s going on with Lena.

“Okay, well you skipped lunch three times this week. Is everything alright?” Kara asks.

“Yes, everything’s perfectly fine. I’ve just been busy,” Lena assures her.

“So you’re not avoiding me?”

Lena breathes out a sigh that borders on a laugh. “No.”

“Alright, then can you come let me in?”

Before Lena can even ask where she is or what she means (as if she doesn’t already know), there’s a knocking at the door. Not the front door, of course. No, that would be far too mundane. It’s at the back door. The balcony door. The one that Lena always leaves unlocked, but Kara refuses to open without being welcomed in.

Her immediate reaction is to get up and open the door for Kara. But then Kara would see the black eye that much sooner. And if she can stall for even a few seconds longer, she will. So instead, she simply turns over her shoulder (careful to keep her black eye obscured) and calls out to Kara.

“It’s open.”

Still wearing the Supergirl suit and wielding a bag of takeout, Kara strides into Lena’s apartment with a little spring in her step. Because as far as she’s concerned, Lena hasn’t been avoiding her. Lena isn’t hiding a few secrets from her and has nothing out of the ordinary going on. She’s just a little extra busy with that super secret passion project she has outright refused to share any information about with anyone. There is absolutely nothing for her - or Lena, for that matter - to worry about.

“I figured you didn’t eat anything since you skipped lunch, again ,” Kara drawls, the barest hint of disappointment in her words. “So I brought you dinner.” She walks around to get in front of Lena, finally, and plops the greasy bag of Big Belly Burger down on the counter. “Voila!”

There’s a three second gap after the delectable diabetic nightmare is presented before Lena. Three charged seconds where Lena simply waits for Kara to finally notice. At first, she’s a little too proud of herself. A grin so broad and brilliant and downright beautiful it could be considered blinding is spread across her face. But then those blue eyes of hers track a little to the left and they go wide. Her mouth falls open. Her brow pinches. And several emotions flicker across her visage all at once.

Her lips work around a few words, spluttering on air briefly, before she finally settles on “ Lena !”

And Lena can’t help herself. “Kara?”

Kara blinks. “What-- When-- Who did this to you?”

Lena exhales deeply and leans back in her chair. “No one did this to me.”

Kara’s around on Lena’s side of the counter in the space of a heartbeat. Her hands cradling Lena’s face like it’ll break under the slightest amount of pressure as she examines her.

“What happened?” She demands.

“Nothing. I’m fine,” Lena insists.

It’s almost believable, too. But then Kara’s thumb chances a little too close to her left eye. With a hiss of pain, Lena flinches from her hand. Kara’s brow furrows further and her frown deepens.

“Lena--”

“I’m fine, Kara. Really,” Lena says. She takes both of Kara’s hands in her own and pulls them down from her face. “It was just an experiment gone wrong.”

Lena breathes out an indignant little huff of air. “It’ll be easier to just show you.”

Kara’s gaze narrows. “Alright.”

Kara is right on Lena’s heels as they walk to the spare room. The room Lena has taken to calling the nursery, where Baymax is lying in wait. At this stage in his development, he’s pretty infantile at best. He knows a few key phrases and can identify a person as long as they’re standing in front of him. But he can’t hold a conversation and his object permanence is severely lacking. So, yeah. Lena’s gonna call his room a nursery.

Lena had removed a lot of it to make space for his assembly. Once that was done, she decided to keep the room empty after he broke her last laptop during a particularly nasty glitch. So the only thing in here, as far as Kara’s concerned, is some weird red luggage tucked against the back wall.

“What am I looking at?” Kara asks, the worry from before replaced with confusion and curiosity.

“Hopefully something that’ll help a lot of people,” Lena says.

It’s cryptic, she’ll admit. But it’s hard to explain exactly what Baymax is at this point. Because he’s not simply a robot anymore. He’s taken on so much more personality and life in just the few weeks since his first test run. He’s learning. Growing. Like a person.

It takes another 4 seconds for Baymax to fully boot up. His eyes blink to signify that he’s fully functional and ready to assist. A total of 27 seconds. They can do better.

Not that Kara notices. She’s staring open mouthed and wide eyed as Baymax awkwardly stumbles out of his charging pod. His steps are heavy, almost as if he thinks the floor is further down (just another thing to iron out). He stops moving about two feet away from them both. Lifts his hand limply into the air (the fingers not fully inflated or opposable yet).

“Hello. I am Baymax, your personal healthcare companion.”

And then he freezes. Standing right there. Unmoving and, to be frank, a little terrifying.

“Wow,” Kara breathes.

“Yeah,” Lena agrees. “He’s still got a lot of bugs to work out. Hence the black eye.” She gestures at her left eye vaguely. “But when he’s finished, he’s going to help a lot of people.”

“Is there anything I can do to help?” Kara asks.

Well, when she puts it like that…

“Right.” Kara nods.

She looks away from Lena, her eyes landing on Baymax. And then she grins. That unfairly perfect grin. The one that is so infectious it’s a wonder the CDC aren’t investigating it yet.

“This is Kara Danvers,” She says, carefully enunciating each word. “And this is the first test of mine and Lena Luthor’s Baymax Project.”

She reaches forward, just like Lena showed her, and turns Baymax on. She grins again, up at the robot, and waits. Watches as he blinks, tilts his head down, and lifts his hand.

“Hello. I am Baymax, your personal healthcare companion.”

“Hello, Baymax!” She answers cheerily. “Would you please scan me?”

“Beginning scan now,” Baymax announces.

But that’s not what happens. No, it would be too simple if that’s what happens next. Instead, the entire system glitches. And both his arms start vibrating rapidly.

Kara’s face pales. “Uh, Lena,” she calls, not daring to look away from another rogue robot. “Is he supposed to be doing that?”

“Uh-oh!” Kara exclaims.

Lena’s head snaps up. “ Uh-oh ?”

 

“Sorry about your laptop,” Kara says, as if it’s her fault Baymax broke yet another computer. Lena really needs to stick to her no-computers-in-the-nursery rule.

“Next time I’ll stand between the two of you. That way I can better stop his rogue flying arms.”

Lena’s gaze snaps to where Kara sits, finishing off the last of their fries (Lena’s fries, really. But they always share). “Next time? You still want to help after that disaster?”

“Of course,” Kara says earnestly. Then her face screws up. “But do you think we could make him look a little friendlier? A bit rounder? You know. Friend-shaped?”

Lena snickers. “ Friend-shaped ?”

“Yeah. Friend-shaped.”

Lena smiles to herself, and starts typing again. “I think I can make that work.”

 



Despite her insistence to the contrary, Kara’s input is even more helpful than Lena would’ve expected. Sure, she knows that Kara’s smart. Obviously. She’s read all of her articles. She knows . What she didn’t know was just how extensive her knowledge was.

Where Lena was able to assemble and organize a database for Baymax full of thousands of medical procedures (just over 10,000 if we want to be exact), Kara was able to provide insight on how to use those same procedures to help aliens. After getting into fights with who knows how many alien fugitives, you learn a thing or two about their anatomies - or at least that’s the explanation Kara gives. Kara even goes so far as to acquire anatomy diagrams on every known alien from the DEO to upload to the database.

Then there’s the design. Lena knew it would have to be non-threatening, but it was Kara who suggested the huggable aspect (in addition to the friend-shape). She insisted that if this was going to be rolled out into every hospital in the country, then it would have to be something a child could easily and readily look to for help. Hence the round shape.

Kara also pointed out that in certain situations, Baymax would have to lift a patient. Maybe carry them around a bit. Just act as a walking gurney. So he would need to be incredibly stable and incredibly strong (“I’ve picked up some of these aliens. Let me tell you… they are not light,” she’d said). And so, they adjusted his actuators so that he could lift over 1,000 lbs. Machined them in Lena’s private lab.

And, naturally, it’s Kara who insists on having Alex meet Baymax.

“I don’t know, Kara. He’s still not ready yet,” Lena tries to argue.

“I’m not saying we should present him to your investors. I’m just saying that maybe we should show Alex. I mean, she is a doctor. She could help.”

“She could,” Lena admits. Her eyes narrow as she stares at Kara. “But we both know that Baymax’s medical database is perfect. So why do you really want Alex to come see him?”

A pink dusting appears on Kara’s cheeks. She averts Lena’s gaze and starts fidgeting with her glasses. “I just thought maybe she’d think of something we didn’t. I mean she went to school for years to learn this stuff. And she has the most experience with alien anatomy of anyone I know. And--”

“You already told her about Baymax, didn’t you?”

Kara’s mouth hangs open. She stares at Lena for a solid five seconds before a strangled noise that sounds dangerously close to a scoff slips past her parted lips. “What? No. I would never. Absolutely not. I w-- Well, not… exactly.”

Lena arches a brow at her. “What did you tell her? Exactly ?”

 

Alex looks absolutely mortified walking into Lena’s apartment a few hours later. Like she had just watched her grandparents get it on or something. She outright refuses to look at Lena and only sends withering glares at her sister. Not that Kara notices, of course. She’s too excited to show off. Practically walking on air as she leads Alex in through Lena’s apartment straight to the nursery (the mere mention of which makes Alex lose all color).

Now, standing in the nursery, staring up into Baymax’s eyes, she just looks stunned. Stunned and confused.

She puts her hands on her hips. Cocks her head to the side. And narrows her gaze at the inflatable robot.

“So this is why you two have been spending all this alone time together?” She finally asks.

“Yep,” Kara answers emphatically.

“Kara’s been helping me test him out. She’s also come up with a few helpful suggestions along the way,” Lena explains.

“Oh, I didn’t do anything,” Kara waves Lena off, as if she truly believes she didn’t have any hand in Baymax’s creation. “You did all the work. I was just the moral support.”

“Did you make him using vinyl?” Alex asks.

Lena has to tear her gaze away from the brilliant pools of blue so intensely trained on her to look at Alex, who’s stepped up towards the robot to inspect him closer.

“Yeah. The goal was to make him as non-threatening as possible,” Lena answers.

“Non-threatening and friend-shaped,” Kara corrects.

Alex looks away from Baymax long enough to stare at her sister. “Friend-shaped?” She repeats. A pained expression on her face.

“Scanning,” he says simply.

It’s only then that Lena lifts her head from his back. And what she sees nearly floors her.

What is supposed to be a wide, empty void of space is instead flooded with huge hunks of space debris. “In that quadrant.” An image of what Baymax is seeing lights up on Lena’s HUD. There’s a red circle straight ahead and slightly above them. “There is too much interference to be more precise.”

Was all of this debris simply refuse that they didn’t want to keep on-world? Did Krypton use it for anything that they wanted out of sight? Where they threw away everything they didn’t want anymore? Not that matters that much. Because it’s now as they maneuver around yet another massive hunk of what looks like frozen organic matter that Lena finally sees her. Before Baymax even says anything, before his view focuses on her. Lena sees her. Floating out there in the semi-dark. A fine layer of dirt and frost all over her. Rips and tears all along her suit and cape. Her blond hair, somehow still golden and brilliant, floats around her head like a halo.

“Kara,” Lena gasps.

Baymax’s thrusters rumble more as he picks up speed to propel them straight for her.

“Baymax, are we in range to scan her?”

“Yes.”

“Please,” is all she can manage to say.

There’s barely even a moment’s pause before Baymax answers. “Kara’s heart rate is slower than normal. Her core body temperature is also lower than it should be. But she is otherwise unharmed. She is merely asleep.”

“Is it a coma?” Lena asks.

“I cannot tell yet. I will need to check her reflexes and–”

“I know what you have to do,” Lena answers a bit harshly. “Just… get us there.”

Baymax doesn’t answer verbally. Instead, his thrusters rumble even more, so much so that Lena can feel it in her chest and not just her hands and feet. They make a beeline for Kara’s limp body. Once they reach her, Lena doesn’t even hesitate. She vaults up and over Baymax, despite knowing that that is an absolutely terrible idea. She uses her magnets to stay adhered to Baymax - though the connection is far less powerful than when she’s on his back - and settles down in front of Kara.

With one hand on Baymax, Lena reaches out and pulls the kryptonian into her. It’s easy. All too easy. Like she weighs nothing. And while Lena knows that out here in zero gravity she does, it still sends a thrill of terror through her.

Kara looks even worse up close. Her lips and fingertips are blue. Signs of hypothermia and hypoxia. There’s a stiffness to her muscles that makes Lena’s heart sink. She’s been out here too long. Far too long. Who knows what damage she’s suffered just by existing unprotected in this void.

“Baymax, activate the Lena Luthor Protocol,” she instructs.

“Activated,” comes his answer.

She can’t hear it out here, and she doesn’t turn to look, but she knows what’s happening. What it actually looks like as the little drone floats up and off of Baymax’s chest. It’s little more than an orb. Floating around with eight propulsion jets fixed into its sides to give it a full range of motion. And located on what is best described as the face of the drone is the one thing Kara needs more than anything right now. A portable sun.

Lena can feel the faint heat wafting off of the drone as it floats past her head, but even still she doesn’t look. She just watches as Kara’s face comes awash in yellow light. It makes it even more obvious just how weak Kara is right now. Highlighting just how little color is left in her frosty cheeks. Glinting off the ice shards stuck to her eyelashes. Accentuating the hollowness in her cheeks. It twists Lena up inside.

“Fuck,” she mutters under her breath. She watches expectantly as Kara just lies there. Motionless. “Status update, Baymax?”

“Status unchanged,” he answers.

“Fuck,” she repeats. She blinks furiously as she stares, fighting off the tears she knows are just waiting to spring forth. “Bring us home, Baymax. Quickly.”

“Of course.”

Lena doesn’t move from between Baymax’s arms. Even though she knows she’d be safer on his back. Even though she knows it’d be easier for Baymax to just carry one of them in his arms and not both. Even though she knows it’s foolish. She’s not leaving Kara. Not again.

The little drone follows right along with them, having locked onto their orientation, its brilliant light remains trained on Kara. Even as Baymax has to dodge and weave to get around the debris still floating around them. Retracing their path back to the projector. And while Lena can still see Baymax’s field of view in her HUD, she still can’t bring herself to look away from Kara’s lit up face. Not that she wants to.

She asks for periodic updates on Kara’s state. There isn’t much change yet, to be true. Her body temperature and heart rate are about the same for the most part. But Lena can feel a shift as they go. Her limbs are starting to relax a bit more. No longer rigid and frozen in place as before. And it almost looks like there’s a bit more color in her cheeks now.

And just as Lena begins to worry that Kara might not come back to her, that she’ll be in this coma for a while longer if not forever, her face shifts. First slightly, almost imperceptibly. So minutely that Lena marks it off as a wish rather than a reality. But then her nose scrunches up. Her eyes squeeze a little tighter. Her jaw clenches a little. And then those blue eyes are open and wide. Wild, even. Her gaze darts around the space around them, taking in their surroundings, before finally falling on Lena. Making direct eye contact even through her tinted faceplate.

Lena can’t hear her, but she reads her lips and recognizes the familiar movements they make around her name.

Lena’s heart swells up in her chest. And a few stubborn tears burst forth as she splutters into her helmet. “Kara,” she says back, knowing full well that the kryptonian can’t hear her.

Her vision blurs as the tears cling to her eyes instead of falling. Zero gravity once again gets the better of Lena. So she doesn’t see it happen. She doesn’t fully understand what happens, in truth. But suddenly Kara’s blue eyes are beyond Lena. She can see her saying something, but she has no idea what it is she’s saying. Her blue eyes flash brightly as her anger flares, but before she can unleash the attack, Baymax stops flying and wraps his arms around them both.

Lena instinctively pulls Kara closer into her chest as they’re hit so forcefully it sets her teeth to chattering. Kara’s grip, though weak, latches onto her as well. She can feel them spinning, careening really, off-course heading to who knows where.

“Baymax! Level us out!” Lena shouts into her comms.

“My thrusters are inoperable,” the robot answers.

“Fuck!” Lena exclaims.

And then as quickly as their spinning and careening started, it stops. Lena’s gasping and panting, her fear spiking as she tries to understand how the hell that just happened. It’s only as Baymax’s arms start to unwind that she realizes how. It was Kara.

Though still in Lena’s arms, she’s floating quite deliberately. She almost looks like she’s standing, even. She’s using her powers to keep them steady where Baymax can’t.

“Fuck, Kara, stop!” Lena tries, but of course Kara can’t hear her. Not that she would listen even if she could.

The portable sun she brought is powerful yes, but not powerful enough to allow Kara the full-range of her powers. If she tries anything even remotely close to what she’s doing, she could blow out her powers. The little bit she has left.

Lena reaches a hand out to Kara’s shoulder. “Kara, stop!” She tries again.

But Kara doesn’t even seem to acknowledge her. So she changes tactics.

“Baymax! What hit us?” She demands.

“Phantoms,” Baymax answers simply.

A hot beam of light shoots past Lena’s face. She ducks out of the way, her furious gaze falling on Kara, whose eyes are still glowing. “Jesus, Kara!” She snaps, even though she knows the other woman can’t hear her. She shakes her head. “What’s the damage?”

“My thrusters are completely inoperable. The structural integrity of my suit is compromised. Otherwise, I think we’re okay.”

Lena scoffs. “Yeah, I’d definitely qualify this situation as okay ,” she grumbles sarcastically. “Damn it, Kara!” She shouts as the kryptonian tries to float away from them. She tugs insistently until Kara floats back over, not before shooting off another blast of heat vision. “How did they even find us? We were just here!”

“I’m not sure,” Baymax says. “Conditions are exactly the same as before. Only now we have Kara.”

It hits Lena immediately as he says that. “The drone,” she says. She twists until she spies the drone. “The portable sun attracted them. Fuck.”

Focused on the drone now, Lena uses her HUD interface to punch in a few commands to the little robot. There’s a momentary lapse before the new commands settle into the drone. A light beeps on its face and a confirmation flashes across Lena’s screen. And then the drone flies off. Zipping past the three of them for the deeper parts of the Phantom Zone.

It’s only then that Lena finally sees the phantoms around them. Cloistered menacingly close. Just looking at them, Lena can feel her fear spiking. There are dozens of them. Enough to just absolutely decimate them. They’d never be able to get home. Not one of them.

But they’re already distracted. They barely even give the three of them a second glance before flying off after the drone. Kara, however, isn’t content to just let them leave. She lets loose one last beam of heat vision into the void that is the Phantom Zone. However, she doesn’t even hit a single one of them. Instead, the shot goes wide. The beam flares, then stutters a little, and then it’s gone altogether.

“God damn it, Kara,” Lena mutters when she feels the other woman go limp in her arms.

Already wasting the little bit of power and consciousness she’d gotten from the drone that Lena cobbled together at the last minute in the Tower. The very same drone she probably never should’ve brought in here, but still. If Kara had waited even ten seconds longer, she could’ve used some of that power to propel them back to the portal. Now Lena has to figure out some other way to bring them all home. Preferably before Baymax’s suit breaks even further and his vinyl exterior bursts.

Lena stares out across the Phantom Zone to where the portal waits. It’s close enough that it would take minutes, if that, to get back through it. Close enough that they’d just need the smallest little boost to get there. It winks at her across the expanse. Tauntingly. Like it knows just how royally fucked they all are.

“There is still a way I can get you both to safety,” Baymax’s voice crackles across her comms.

Lena’s head snaps up to the robot. His gaze is beyond her, however, as he stares at the portal.

“Baymax,” she warns.

His head tips forward until he’s looking at her. It’s hard to tell exactly with both their helmets, but she knows he’s looking at her. She’s sure of it. “I cannot deactivate until you say you are satisfied with your care.”

Lena’s stomach drops out of her. “No, no, no, no. Wait. What about you?”

“You are my patient–”

“No.” Lena shakes her head irritably. Heat stings at the backs of her eyes as she glares up at the robot. “There's gotta be another way. I'm not gonna leave you here. I'll think of something.”

“There is no time. The phantoms will return. Are you satisfied with your care?”

“Please! No…” Her head tips forward as a sob claws its way up her throat. She swallows thickly to clear it. Her head falls farther forward, bumping her helmet right up against his. ”I can't lose you!”

Baymax shifts a little closer until his gaze falls onto Lena’s eye line and she’s forced to look up at him again. “Lena, I will always be with you.”

Lena’s heart tightens in her chest. Tears spring forth from her eyes; they cling to her eyes blurring her vision once again. Her lip quivers, but she manages to keep herself together - at least a little - as she cries, “I'm satisfied with my care.”

The shock of the cold, however, is nothing compared to crashing across the floor of the Tower. Her entire body locks in place as she skids across the floor. Jarring pain shoots all throughout her body. Until finally she skids to a stop.

The Super Friends are on them immediately. So quickly Lena doesn’t even get the chance to think. She can’t process anything, really, her mind is so numb, her body so sore. The only thing she can seem to understand is that they’re taking Kara from her.

“It’s okay,” she hears Nia assuring her. “You did it.”

Lena barely even looks at her as she stalks past her, following after Alex and J’onn and Brainy as they bring Kara to the med bay. 

“I’m staying with her,” she says to no one in particular.

“Lena,” Nia calls. “You need to rest . You’ve put enough strain on yourself.”

Lena doesn’t stop, and she’s not about to rest either. “I’m staying with her,” she repeats. And that’s that.

 

They put Kara under a sun lamp. She still looks incredibly weak and frail. The color is mostly drained from her visage, her cheeks still hollow. But she’s alive. And she’s home. And she’s safe.
