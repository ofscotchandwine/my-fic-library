Lena leaned on the stone balcony of her room. She pressed her hand flat on the rough stone. The night sky was covered by thick clouds and like dark velvet, it consumed all the light from the stars and the moon above. There was a chill in the air that had Lena breathing in deeply. The lands on the cusp of winter was her favourite part of the changing seasons. It was beautiful and on any other night, she would have stayed to appreciate the view from here. Just not tonight, or any other night from now on.

In the distance, past the lush hills of verdant green and the strip of the barren land, were the snow capped mountains, regal and intimidating against the dark backdrop of the night. The Phantom Mountains were a natural barrier to the Northern Reaches that were outside of Clan Luthor’s territory. It was the one place that her brother had not been able to conquer. Not that it had stopped his endless campaigns. Lena had heard the stories, had seen the result of her brother’s cruelty and she was complicit in it.

She shuddered from the memories and wrapped her black cloak tighter around her shoulders. The thick material brushed against the pack strapped to her back, hidden within the folds of her cloak. She shifted uneasily, unused to the weight of a dagger at her hip and uncomfortably hot from the heat that radiated from the pack. Sweat dripped down the back of her neck despite the onset of winter. Anxiety thrummed through her as she tried to wait as patiently as she could. Time was running out. She needed to go. Now.

The door to Lena’s room opened and she whirled around at the soft footsteps.

‘Milady, it is only me,’ whispered Jess, Lena’s personal secretary and assistant. She hurried to where Lena was standing on the balcony.

‘Oh, Jess, I thought you had been caught.’ Lena took one end of the thick rope from Jess and tied it around one of the balusters in a secure knot. ‘Did you do it?’

‘Exactly as you asked, Milady,’ said Jess. ‘The designs have all been burnt and I’ve destroyed all the prototypes.’

‘Good, because the last thing I want is—’ Lena stopped at the hand on her shoulder and looked up at Jess. She tried for a reassuring smile, but her lips trembled. ‘I’ll be fine. Don’t worry about me.’

‘Please be careful.’

‘You too,’ urged Lena as she stood up. ‘If the Clan finds out that you aided me… They’ll kill you.’

Jess laughed quietly. Her earlier worry dissipated as she looked at Lena. ‘I wasn’t always a personal secretary.’

‘Then we both have nothing to worry about.’ Lena tugged at the rope. Satisfied that it was secure, she climbed over the balcony and tried her best not to look down. ‘Stay safe, Jess.’

Without waiting for a reply, Lena started down the side of Luthor Castle, taking care to test each foothold before she placed her weight down. It was one thing if she broke her leg on a fall, but it was another thing if she broke her precious, stolen cargo before she even made it out of the castle grounds. She only hoped she didn’t fall to her death first. Then her betrayal would have all been for naught.

 

***

Lena snuck out of the castle grounds under the cover of night, ducking into familiar alcoves where the shadows offered the best hiding places. It felt like forever before she slipped into the forest. She stuck to trees and stayed off the worn paths, walking as fast as she could, occasionally breaking out into a run. She had to put as much distance between the castle and her as possible.

She wiped at the sweat on her forehead with the back of her hand. She was a scholar at heart, preferring the safety of her study and books. There was comfort in the designs that flowed from graphite onto paper. The heat she felt now was nothing like the heat of the forge in the lower depths of the castle where she brought life into the creations in metals of all different kinds. She only wished that everything she had done in the past had been for good. With renewed determination, kindled by the guilt in her chest and kept alight by the heat against her back, she continued onwards.

This was her chance to put things right.

If it was the last thing she ever did, at least let it be this.

The memories flowed through Lena as she trekked through the forest. In the silence of the night, there was no stopping her thoughts. Nothing to distract her from thinking about all the lives lost to battles. Lives on both sides. And for what? Her brother’s obsession with the People of Rao who lived beyond the Phantom Mountains. In the once grand and towering city of Krypton.

She didn’t put much stock in fables, even if she grew up with them. Hard facts and numbers were her truths. The stories were numerous. Some so elaborate that Lena knew they were just that. Stories told to entertain children. Then there were the ones told by wizened men and women, hardened warriors from other Clans who her brother welcomed into the castle for their war stories.

The stories about dragons and the People of Rao who could control them.

Or what was left of them.

She pushed the thoughts away. She needed to focus. Her energy was better used on walking then on thoughts of past actions. She couldn’t turn back time. If she could, then she would not be the young naïve girl who had followed her brother without question, creating machines and weapons to destroy.

Lena walked through the night, feet stumbling through the undergrowth and more than a few times, she had tripped and landed on her hands and knees. When dawn broke, Lena was exhausted, knees bruised, and hands dirtied and scratched. It didn’t feel like she had made it very far from the castle at all.

White smoke appeared above the tree line. Lena followed the line of smoke to a chimney. Through the trees, she saw a tavern and stopped. The tavern was a sight for sore eyes, but could she risk going in? Her brother should only have found out about her disappearance this morning. She had supplies, but not enough to reach the Northern Reaches alone. She weighed the risks and stumbled towards the tavern.

 

***

The tavern was cozy and coupled with the heat from Lena’s pack was overly warm. She tugged her hood further over her head and sat quietly at a table in the back, hidden in a corner and hopefully, where she was least bothered.

A woman with short red hair approached Lena. In a gruff voice, she asked,’’What can I get you?’

‘Something to eat and drink,’ replied Lena quietly. ‘Please.’

The woman gave Lena a hard look, no doubt suspicious of her, but before she could question further, a loud commotion by the door distracted her.

‘Hey! What’s going on?’ yelled the woman as two burly men walked through the tavern’s door.

‘Just looking for the owner,’ said the shorter of the two. He moved and sat down at a table near the door. He propped his axe against the table. His companion, the bigger and taller man, dropped into the seat next to him.

Mercenaries. Well paid ones if their gear was anything to go by.

‘You’re looking at her.’ The woman stood in front of the two men, feet shoulders-width apart, and arms crossed over her chest. ‘So what is it?’

‘Nah, nothing much, just looking for a run away.’

Lena ducked her head and shrank away. This was bad. She hadn’t even made it out of clan territory and they were already on her trail—

‘Don’t look up.’

Lena froze. It wasn’t the tavern owner. The voice belonged to someone she didn’t know. Light. Airy. A little too cheery for someone in a tavern on the crossroads between small towns and Luthor Castle. Whoever they were, they settled into the seat opposite Lena. A bowl of stew and a plate of bread appeared in Lena’s line of sight.

‘Eat,’ said the voice. ‘I’ll let you know when it’s good to leave.’

‘Why should I trust you?’ asked Lena. She ate as directed. The threat of danger or not, she still needed to eat.

‘You probably shouldn’t, but right now, you probably don’t have a choice. Whoever those men are, they’re obviously after someone.’

Lena ate her stew as quickly as she could. She peered up from beneath her hood. The stranger stared back at her, and Lena found herself captivated by the intensity of the stranger’s eyes. It was the only uncovered part of them that she could see. It was like looking at the sky on a clear summer’s day, bright and so incredibly blue that it could lift the mood from just the colour of it.

‘Hey, you two.’ The two mercenaries looked over the stranger and straight at Lena. ‘Take off your hoods.’

‘I don’t think we want to,’ said the stranger. Their eyes were fixed on Lena’s.

‘Then we’ll just have to make you,’ grumbled the taller mercenary. He walked closer and reached out a hand to Lena only to jump back and yelp in pain.

Lena watched in confusion as the scene played out in front of her. It was like being a member of the audience to a performance. The mercenaries crumpled to the floor, groaning in pain from a few well placed strikes by the stranger to their neck. It was both ridiculous and impressive how this tiny person managed to floor two towering men.

‘Let’s go!’ They held out their hand. ‘These two won’t stay down for long.’

The urgency in their voice had Lena shooting to her feet. She clumsily grasped onto the stranger’s hand and let herself be pulled through the tavern, past the other unperturbed patrons. Did this person save unsuspecting run aways all the time?

‘Be careful!’ yelled the tavern owner.

‘I always am, Alex!’

They slipped through the kitchen and out the back door. Oh, what was she doing? Lena didn’t know why she was following this person whose face she couldn’t even see.

‘Over there!’

Lena jerked around at the same time the stranger did. Behind them was a group of foot soldiers wearing her Clan’s colours and wielding swords and bows. Lena froze and for a strange moment, so did the soldiers before Lena was wrenched into a run by the stranger who still held onto her hand.

‘You weren’t thinking of just standing there, were you?’

Lena rolled her eyes, but focused on running into the forest. Her heart raced inside her chest. She was both scared at being caught and exhilarated at the turn of events. There must be something wrong with her to feel like this when her life was at stake and, quite literally, in the hands of someone she just met. Her feet hurt from tripping over the undergrowth, but she continued running, pulled along by this stranger with oddly compelling eyes.

Something shimmered up ahead. A thin line that reflected the shaft of sunlight that filtered through the trees. Lena’s eyes widened at the hunting trap. They were running straight for it.

‘Wait!’ The stranger didn’t seem to hear Lena and they kept running. Lena tried tugging her hand from their strong grip, but it was no use. ‘Stop!’

The stranger stopped so abruptly that Lena collided into the back of them.

‘Finally! We—’

The rest of what Lena was going to say was muffled by a hand over her mouth. The stranger pushed her against the trunk of a tree and pressed themselves against Lena’s front. Their hood fell off, revealing shining blonde hair.

Lena protested, but the stranger brought a finger to their lips, or where their lips would be beneath the scarf that hid the rest of their face from Lena’s view. Loud rustling sounded from the other side of the tree, followed by urgent whispers. All Lena could do was focus on the eyes in front of her. She didn’t know how long they stood, staring at each other, waiting as the soldiers passed them by. They were so close. All it would take was for one to venture a little too close and they were done for.

Except they didn’t.

The rustling and footsteps faded into silence, and only then did the stranger remove their hand from Lena’s mouth. They pointed over their shoulder and they slowly crept back the way they had come.

 

***

Night had fallen and Lena had no idea where she was. Her bearing on the land had been lost somewhere between running from her Clan’s soldiers and doubling back to the tavern, and then completely destroyed when the stranger led her east, through another part of the forest.

Lena sat stiffly with her back against the rock wall. The pack rested in her lap and she kept an arm looped around the strap. The stranger —a woman with long blonde hair tied up in a braid— was poking at the small fire with a stick. The woman hadn’t said a thing, not since they arrived here and set up camp in a shallow alcove at the bottom of a cliff. It was deep enough that a fire could be lit without revealing themselves. The trees around them were dense enough to hide them from view.

The woman kept glancing over at Lena who smiled brightly whenever Lena caught her. Each time Lena received a smile, it only left her just as confused as the last with uncertainty squirming her insides. It was the oddest feeling.

Lena expected endless questions, not the water-skin and a strip of dried meat that the woman passed to her instead. She held onto the items in a limp hand. ‘What are you doing?’

‘Feeding you,’ said the woman simply. She cocked her head and a crinkle appeared between her brow. ‘We’ve been running all day. You’ll need the energy if you’re going to keep on the run.’

The fight in Lena’s body drained. The woman was right. If she was going to make it out of clan territory, she needed all the energy she could get. Refusing food was not an option. She looked at food and water and then at the woman. They didn’t even know each other, and this woman was helping her with no questions asked.

‘Thank you,’ said Lena. ‘For helping me today. I wouldn’t have made it this far if it hadn’t been for you.’

The woman shrugged and flicked her hand as if to bat Lena’s thanks away. She poked at the fire, sending up red sparks into the air. ‘Who are you running from? I mean, you don’t have to tell me! It’s fine to keep that to yourself. It must be someone powerful if they’re sending soldiers…’

Lena watched in amusement as the woman tripped over her words before trailing off. There was a soft blush to her cheeks. It was endearing to see, and a small smile of her own appeared on Lena’s face.

‘It’s my brother I'm running from,’ replied Lena. She opened the water-skin and sipped at it. ‘I need to get to the Northern Reaches.’

The muscle in the woman’s jaw tightened. For the first time since Lena met her, she saw an expression other than the blithe smiles directed her way and the stoic mask given to the two mercs in the tavern.

‘There’s nothing there,’ said the woman in a serious tone. Her frown deepened. ‘The journey from here to the Barren Wastes is manageable, but it’s getting past the Phantom Mountains that is the most dangerous if you don’t know the paths. There’s no good reason to risk life and limb for the Northern Reaches.’

Lena’s hands gripped her pack. She felt the heat through the leather as if there was no barrier. Before she could second guess herself, she opened her pack and the woman’s mouth fell open. Nestled inside the pack was an egg almost the size of Lena’s head. The gold shell shimmered in the dark. It seemed to pulse like it had a heartbeat of its own, glowing and dimming with each beat.

‘Where did you get that?’

‘It doesn’t matter,’ said Lena. She brushed her hand against the shell. The woman jerked forward, but Lena closed the pack and held it close to her body protectively. ‘I’m going to take it back to the Northern Reaches where it belongs.'

The woman stared at Lena, a strange expression on her face that she couldn’t decipher. If she tried to put a name to it, it looked like Lena had destroyed her world by showing her the egg. She opened her mouth only to close it again. She swallowed and looked at Lena with a determined gaze, and nodded.

‘I’ll go with you.’

‘What?’

‘I’ll go with you to the Northern Reaches,’ said the woman, strength and determination in voice and eyes as she came to a decision while Lena spluttered.

‘No! You just said so yourself that it’s dangerous!’ Lena protested. She clamped her mouth shut at her how loud she had become. She lowered her voice, ‘I can’t ask you to go with me.’

‘You’re not asking. I’m offering.’

Lena stared at the woman. Confused. In shock. She had no idea how to react. ‘I don’t even know your name.’

‘Kara,’ replied the woman like it was the simplest question in the world. It was in a way. When Kara smiled this time, it lit up her whole face, transformed the seriousness into lightness and it captured Lena completely.

If she hadn’t already felt it earlier when she made the decision to reveal the dragon egg, she definitely felt it now. How odd it was to feel safe in the company of a stranger who she had known for less than a day. There was something about Kara that crept beneath her skin. An itch that she couldn’t quite scratch.

‘Lena.’ She deliberately left her Clan name and title. She didn’t need them now.

‘Now you know me, so what do you say?’ The smile on Kara’s face grew wider. ‘Let’s go to the Northern Reaches together.’

 

***

The journey through the forest to the Barren Waste was manageable just as Kara had said, but it wasn’t without its dangers.

‘Careful!’

Lena stopped abruptly when Kara pulled her back by the hand. Her heart thumped inside her chest at the sight of the rusty metal poacher’s trap she had nearly stood on. She squeezed Kara’s hand in thanks and reluctantly let go. If it hadn’t been for Kara, Lena would have lost more than just her foot.

Nearly ten days had passed since Kara saved her and they still hadn’t reached the edge of the forest. She hadn’t realised how inaccurate her maps were or how expansive the forest was. If she didn’t have Kara, Lena would be hopelessly lost, walking circles around herself, and in all likeliness, footless too.

Kara stayed close by, walking side by side with Lena as they carefully tread through the forest. ‘It’s not just traps. There’s creatures in this forest who will take precious things from unsuspecting victims, and I’m not talking about the animals.’

‘You mean the mythical?’

‘The fae.’ It was whispered quietly by Kara like the word itself was tinged with magic. ‘They’re not known to be kind to humans who wander into their domain.’

A faded memory drifted to the surface of Lena’s thoughts accompanied by a familiar, lyrical voice that wrenched at her heart. She licked her lips and forced the feeling down. ‘But they can be forgiving too.’

‘That is not the fables I have heard.’

‘They are the fables my mother told me.’ Lena held her head high, an unaffected mask settled onto her face. She did not believe in fables, but when it came to the fae, it was one such fairytale that she kept close to her heart. ‘Not all fae are evil as the stories make them. I would argue that humans are more evil than the mythical.’

Kara fell curiously silent. From the corner of Lena’s eyes, she saw Kara’s gaze drifting to Lena to her pack then to the forest. It was like Kara was trying desperately not to ask about the content of her pack.

‘Do you know about the dragons of the North?’ asked Kara suddenly.

Lena nodded. Of course, she knew about them. Her brother was obsessed with them. ‘I hear they are extremely endangered. That the dragon population was wiped when…’ When Clan Luthor tried to invade Krypton.

A rueful smile appeared on Kara’s face. Why did it look like she was going to cry? Lena wanted to bring a genuine look of happiness to Kara, to be the one to change that expression. They had been travelling for over a week through the forest, and in that time Kara hadn’t once stopped talking, filling moments of quiet with chatter about anything. Lena learned all about Kara’s favourite foods. She was told stories about Kara’s older adoptive sister, the tavern owner, and her adoptive mother, a healer who lived in a village far outside Luthor territory.

In the days since Lena had known Kara, the other woman was oddly quiet for the first time and Lena didn’t like it at all. It was so out of character that she reached out for Kara’s hand instinctively. Kara stilled. Her feet stopped and she turned, head ducked and her eyes, that brilliant shade of blue was dark and stormy with loss. Lena swallowed and grasped blankly for words of comfort, anything to assuage the deep dip between Kara’s brow.

As Lena floundered for words she couldn’t find, Kara tried another smile, but to Lena it looked like a pained grimace. They started walking again. This time hand-in-hand.

 

***

‘I’m starting to think that we will never reach the Barren Waste, let alone the mountains or the Northern Reaches,’ grumbled Lena. Sweat dripped down her back uncomfortably. She pulled at her tunic, grimacing at the way the fabric stuck to her skin.

‘You need to keep hydrated.’ Kara passed her water-skin to Lena. ‘Don’t worry. We’ll get there.’ She pointed ahead. ‘The trees are starting to thin, see?’

Lena didn’t see. The forest looked no different to her than when she first started, but she took Kara’s word for it. She drank the water offered to her and passed the water-skin back. ‘Thank you.’

Kara grinned brightly and Lena found herself breathless at the sight, only able to breathe properly again when Kara turned back to the front. Oh no, this was getting worse. The flutters in Lena’s stomach were most certainly from hunger. It definitely was not caused by the woman next to her with her well-defined arm muscles and her handsome face with a firm jawline that curved into a round chin.

Absolutely not. Lena turned her thoughts to other things. That was easier. Less distracting. Maybe. But inevitably they turned to thoughts of Kara too.

Kara had not once asked to see the inside of her pack since she had shown the egg. No questions asked. Not even a hint of curiosity. It was unusual. No one would be able to withhold their curiosity after seeing a—

‘Watch out!’

Lena’s world tilted as she was pushed off her feet. Instead of landing on hard ground, Lena fell on top of Kara who had twisted in the air and cushioned their fall, protecting Lena and the dragon egg. Lena’s heart pounded as Kara wheezed beneath her. Kara’s eyes darted from right to left. Her strong arms were wrapped protectively around Lena’s shoulders, hand cradling the back of Lena’s head. An arrow whizzed through the air and thudded into the trunk of the tree they had stood only moments before. Lena’s eyes widened as she recognised the fletching. No one dyed the feathers of their fletching bright green. No one but her brother.

‘We’re sitting ducks out here,’ Kara mumbled into the top of Lena’s head. Her arms tightened around Lena's shoulders before relaxing. ‘I don’t have a choice.’

Lena leaned up slightly, confused at Kara’s words. There was an intense dip between Kara’s brow, lips pursed so hard that they had turned white, but it was the turmoil in Kara’s eyes that had Lena’s heart clenching. In the brief time they had known each other, she had never been afraid, not with Kara by her side. Now she was afraid for Kara, and she didn’t know why.

‘What do you mean?’ asked Lena frantically as Kara fumbled with a strap at her belt. ‘Kara! Tell me what you mean by not having a choice. Whatever it is, we can get out of this together.’

‘Don’t worry, you’ll be safe, no matter what.’ Kara smiled tightly. She brought a horn up to her face. Intricate designed were carved and chiselled into the wooden surface. ‘Trust me?’

‘Yes,’ replied Lena without hesitation.

Kara took a deep breath and blew into the horn. A long, bellowing sound echoed through the air. Her body shuddered from the deep bass of the horn. Kara blew the horn a second time. The sound slowly tapered off into silence. The forest fell eerily quiet, so quiet that not even a rustle of leaves could be heard in the aftermath of the horn. Lena stayed still, almost as if she was caught by an unforeseen force that kept her immobile. She watched Kara’s face, who was focused on the sky above her.

Then she heard it. A flap of wings. Not from feathers. It wasn’t the flutter of delicate feathers of a bird, but something larger. Something huge. A gust of wind flattened Lena into Kara, whipping her hair and clothes. A sharp screech rang through the air. Lena clamped her hands over her ears, as the shrill cry jarred her down to her bones. It was no animal she had ever heard before. She pressed herself into Kara, only for Kara to haul them both to their feet.

‘Run, Lena!’

Lena ran, driven by pure adrenaline and fear for whatever that followed them above. She wasn’t sure what she was scared of more. Her clan or the huge shadow blocking the light of the sky. She focused on Kara’s back. Arrows whizzed past Lena, dangerously close to her head as she ran and ran.

The greenery passed by in a blur. The trees became sparser. The undergrowth less dense. The soft moss beneath her feet turned hard, became dirt and rock as the forest fell away to the Barren Wastes. Still the archers and the soldiers followed them. The shadow disappeared as she and Kara emerged from the forest.

‘Kara! The cliff!’

Kara skidded to a halt and for one heart stopping moment, Lena thought she would pitch over the side and into the ravine. Kara turned and caught Lena. One more step and they would have gone over.

‘Did it really have to end like this?’

Lena stiffened at the familiar voice. She stepped out of Kara’s arms. Her back straightened, chin lifting as she turned to face her pursuer.

‘Lex.’

Her brother stood with two groups of archers on either side of him. Lena clenched her teeth at seeing the bows wielded by her Clan’s archers. Their bows were reinforced, made with lightweight metal for easy carrying, springs for added power, and strings so durable that the archers didn’t have to worry about snapping them. The design was like nothing anyone had seen before. Except for Lena. Designs she had Jess destroy.

A smirk appeared on Lex’s face. He gestured at one of the bows. ‘You didn’t think you could keep your designs secrets, did you?’

‘I destroyed them for a reason.’

‘Good thing I made a copy before you did,’ said Lex lightly. ‘It would have been such a waste to not at least test them before concluding they were failures. Surely, you know that as a scholar?’ It was rhetorical, not that Lena had any desire to answer. His face hardened, jaw jutting out as he stared at Lena. He held out his hand. ‘Now give me back what is rightfully mine.’

Lena stepped back only to freeze when the archers aimed at her. She shook her head, careful to shield her pack from them. ‘It’s not something to be owned like a prize. It will never be yours.’

‘It’s mine!’ yelled Lex, expression turning nasty with a sneer. ‘Give me the dragon egg, Lena! Or you’ll pay.’

Kara shifted next to Lena, drawing Lex's attention away. He sneered at Kara and crossed his arms over his chest.

‘Kryptonian you should have stayed in hiding, now you’ve just given me another excuse to kill you.'

Kara bristled, practically vibrated with barely concealed anger next to Lena, but she held on, hands clenched in tight fists, a furious glare to her eyes, and lips pressed down in a firm, thin line.

‘You didn’t think I wouldn’t recognise you?’ Lex’s smirk became grotesque. His eyes widening as he shook his head. ‘You’re just like your cousin. But I have to say, kidnapping a member of Clan Luthor, encroaching on our territory. It’s practically a declaration of war. Too bad there’s none of your people left to kill.’

Lena placed her hand carefully down on Kara’s forearm, a gesture that didn’t go unnoticed by Lex, whose face turned mutinous at the sight of the touch. The tension in Kara’s muscles relaxed a fraction and the shaking in her arms lessened.

‘I didn’t think you would fall this low, Lena. Consorting with the enemy.’

‘They’re not our enemies. They never have been,’ seethed Lena through her teeth.

Lex laughed, head thrown back before he jerked forward with a snarl. ‘If it weren’t them first, then it would have been us! Did you think they would share their knowledge on their precious dragons willingly? No, it had to be taken and I will take that dragon egg from your dead hands if I have too.’

‘No, you won’t.’ Kara grabbed Lena’s hands and shuffled backwards. The archers aimed at them.

‘What are you doing?’ murmured Lena beneath her breath. Her pulse jumped as the back of her feet hit empty air. Only her weight on the balls of her feet kept her standing on the edge of the cliff. A light breeze would send her falling to her death.

‘You said you trusted me earlier. That hasn’t changed?’

It went without saying. Lena trusted Kara implicitly. She squeezed Kara’s hand and shook her head. ‘It hasn’t.’

‘Good.’ Kara wrapped her arms around Lena and jumped off the cliff.

Lena expected the rush of wind, her long hair whipping her face, the chill of the air from falling. She did not expect to land on a solid surface. A large, gleaming black eye stared at her. The scream in her throat caught as Kara tugged her down to sit on a worn leather saddle and strapped her in. She placed Lena’s hand on the horn.

‘Hold on tight, okay?’

Lena nodded, speechless and frozen in fear at the sharp teeth that poked out from the closed mouth of the dragon. The shadow from earlier. The screech that shook her from the inside out was a dragon. Kara sat behind Lena, arms around her waist, and whistled. This time Lena did scream as she soared into the air, forced into Kara’s front by speed and velocity she had never experienced before.

Her eyes watered from the wind in her face and she clenched them closed. She turned as best she could and pressed her face into Kara's neck, all while she kept an iron-clad grip on the saddle’s horn.

 

***

When the initial fear of flying faded and gave way to curiosity, Lena lifted her head from the warmth of Kara’s neck. Her joints and bones creaked in protest as she untwisted herself back into a proper saddle posture.

She stared in awe at how wide and expansive the sky was. Her heart lodged firmly in her throat when she looked down. The land below blurred past in various shades of red, brown and yellow of the dusty Barren Wastes. It was magnificent. They covered more distance than Lena thought was possible. With the open sky in front of her and Kara at her back, Lena felt anything in the world was possible.

Lena watched the line of the horizon go from a straight flat line to the raised, jagged peaks of the Phantom Mountains. Her body shook as the temperature dipped further. The sun was setting and it took with it what little heat it gave. Lena shivered despite the warmth of the dragon egg and Kara’s arms tightened around her waist, left hand resting on Lena’s belly that left her shaking even more.

The sky was on the brink of twilight and night when Lena felt the dragon slowly drift in wide circles as it made its descent to the ground. For something so hulking in size, the dragon landed with surprising gentleness. Kara moved first and unwound her arm from around Lena’s waist. Getting off the dragon was harder than when she got on. Lena peered over the side. She couldn’t jump from this height. Not if she wanted to to keep her ankles working.

‘A little help, Maya?’

A disgruntled grumble came from the dragon’s throat, but it lowered its body further and straightened its wing to form a slide. With Kara’s help, Lena made it off the dragon without breaking her ankles.

An awkwardness settled over them. Kara went to gather anything the Wastes had to offer for firewood. Lena took off her pack and sat down on the dusty ground. A sigh of relief left her at finding the egg unharmed. She smoothed her hand over the pebbly shell. Dragon eggs were hardy. They weren’t easily broken, but after carrying it on her back for so long, she couldn’t bear the thought of it breaking because she hadn’t protected it.

Kara started a fire and Lena sighed at the comfort it brought. The Barren Wastes were empty, full of dead trees and not a single spot of green could be seen. It was only less than a day and she already missed the forest after days of complaining.

Lena crept closer to the fire. She tried to ignore the feeling of being watched. The dragon’s black eye followed her every movement.

‘Play nice,’ said Kara with a pout. The dragon snapped playfully at Kara who jumped back with her hands at her hip. ‘Hey!’

If dragons could smirk, Lena thought this one did. Its lips seemed to curl up, revealing razor sharp teeth as long as her fingers.

Kara shook her head and stroked her hand down the dragon’s snout. Lena watched as Kara tended to the dragon, saw the gentleness in which Kara treated the huge beast, and the unconditional love on her face for a living creature that was more than what her brother thought it was. A thing to be controlled and used to conquer.

‘You’re Kryptonian.’ The words were out before Lena could stop them.

‘Yes.’

‘You’re a dragon rider too.’

‘Yes.’ Kara leaned against her dragon. No, Maya. The fire burned brightly and casted odd shadows on Kara’s face. There was something in her expression that made Lena want to hide. It was in the way Kara’s eyes flicked to Lena, then back to Maya again.

‘And you are of Clan Luthor?’

‘I am.’ There was no denying it. It was hardly something she could deny now. Not when Lex, in all his grandiose arrogance, had declared just who he was and by extension, who Lena was.

Lena stared down at her hands. She was part of the problem. She had brought this down on Kara. If it hadn’t been for Lena, then Kara would still be in hiding, safe from Lex and his power hungry vision to rule the lands. If he couldn’t control the dragons, then no one else could.

‘I’m sorry,’ whispered Lena. It was all her fault. Her Clan’s fault that the People of Rao had been destroyed. It didn’t feel like enough, but it was all she knew to say. ‘I am so sorry.’

Kara didn’t say anything. Lena wished desperately that she would, but maybe silence was better. Maybe it was all she deserved.

‘Did you know that dragon eggs burn to the touch?’

Lena frowned, confused by the sudden change in subject. She consciously touched the golden egg. It was hot, but not enough that it could burn her. ‘That can’t be right.’

‘The dragons of the Northern Reaches are creatures born of fire.’ Kara sat down next to Lena on the dusty ground. ‘The eggs are scorching hot to protect them from unwanted hands.’

Lena thought back to when the egg had first arrived at Luthor Castle. It had been wrapped in layers and layers of wool, and contained inside a metal crate. She hadn’t thought much of it when she had picked it up and placed it in her wool-lined leather pack.

‘Then what’s wrong with this one?’

Kara laughed lightly, monetarily wiping the traces of sadness from her face. Her blue eyes sparkled from the fire. ‘Nothing’s wrong.’

‘I don’t understand, Kara. You said that the eggs would burn, but this one doesn’t. What does this have to do with my…’ Lena trailed off when Kara held onto her hand and fingers linked together.

‘I haven’t known you for long,’ said Kara. A small smile played at the corner of her lips. A little shy, a lot hopeful. ‘But you don’t need to know someone for long to know that they’re good.’

‘Kara…’

Kara shook her head. ‘You chose to return the egg to its home, make the journey to somewhere not many people have gone to. No one does that if they don't mean it.’

It was Lena’s turn to shake her head. That didn’t make her good. It didn’t even begin to right the wrongs her family had done. It did not absolve her own actions in being part of it.

‘I know you don’t believe me,’ Kara carefully laid Lena’s hand down on the egg. ‘The dragon inside this egg knows. That’s why it chose you.’

Lena stared at Kara in disbelief. The egg was hot, but so was Kara’s hand in hers.

‘We don’t control the dragons,’ continued Kara. She let go of Lena and placed her hand down on Lena’s thigh. The skin at the corners of her eyes creased with sadness. ‘It’s a relationship built on mutual trust, respect and love. El mayarah. It means stronger together in our people’s language.’

There was a gravity to Kara’s words that had Lena biting down on her lip. She hugged the pack with the dragon egg close, seeking the comfort in the warmth it brought her, even if the one she wanted to hug was Kara.

‘Even if the dragon hadn’t chosen you,’ Kara paused and cupped Lena’s cheek. ‘Even if you were just running away without the dragon egg, I would still be here with you.’

Lena gasped at the quiet declaration. Her heart ached at hearing the words, spoken without inflection or doubt. It filled Lena with yearning for the other woman in front of her. She pressed her face into Kara’s palm, felt the rough calluses of her skin, and the strength in her hand. It could break her, but instead of withdrawing, she turned her face and kissed the centre of Kara’s palm.

‘Even though you know who I am?’ murmured Lena.

Kara carefully moved the pack from between them and shuffled closer, so close that Lena could see the different shades of blue in her eyes. ‘Your name doesn’t have to be the thing that defines you.’

That was all it took, Kara’s unwavering belief that Lena was better than who she was, for Lena to close the gap between them. She felt Kara’s hand sink into her hair, bringing her closer as their lips moved in a slow, languorous kiss that deepened with every second that passed. Lena leaned back, chest heaving for air as the world slowly filtered back into focus. All she saw was Kara, who had a dazed look to her face, lips swollen from their kiss, but still smiling as brightly as ever.

It was always Kara and her sunny smiles that captured Lena. It had a way of making her feel deserving of them. It made her feel like she could do anything at all.

‘Thank you,’ whispered Lena.

‘Don’t thank me just yet.’ Kara leaned in and brushed her lips against Lena’s. ‘We still have a way to go before we reach the mountain.’

‘I think we’ll be fine.’

‘Why do you say that?’ asked Kara, eyebrow raised playfully.

Lena shrugged and bit down on her bottom lip, gazed fixed firmly on Kara’s mouth before she closed the gap for another kiss. Their journey had only started, but with Kara with her, Lena knew they would be fine.
