    When she woke up on the morning of her 24th birthday, her first sensation was soreness. The kind of deep, pleasant ache that came from being absolutely wrecked the night before in the best possible way. Her legs felt heavy, her back warm, her thighs a little tender. She stretched and smiled into the pillow, half expecting Rio’s arm still flung across her waist, their breath against her neck.

    But the bed was empty. For a split second, a flicker of old anxiety whispered through her chest. But then her nose caught it—coffee, pancakes, something sugary and warm—and her entire body sighed with recognition. Of course. Rio.

    Sure enough, a moment later she heard the soft thud of footsteps, then the creak of her bedroom door.

    Rio appeared, balancing a tray that looked way too cute for them to have put together casually. Pancakes shaped like ghosts and little hearts, a steaming mug of coffee, a tall glass of orange juice, and a bowl of strawberries and clementines.

    “Happy birthday, princess,” Rio said, setting the tray down carefully across Agatha’s lap before pulling something from behind their back. “And… one more thing.” Agatha blinked as they handed her a wrapped box. She tore the paper slowly, already suspicious.

    And then she saw it.

    A purple Game Boy Color.

    Brand new.

    She stared at it in disbelief. “You didn’t.”

    “I did,” Rio said with a soft shrug. “I figured you deserved your own. Plus, you keep hogging mine.”

    Agatha looked between the gift and Rio, utterly overwhelmed. “This is… you’re insane. You’re seriously spoiling me.”

    “You’re worth it,” Rio said, completely casual.

    She opened her mouth to argue again, but then she spotted the small stack of cartridges tucked into the bottom of the bag, including her half-finished Pokémon game. She let out a breath and couldn’t help the way she reached for them, pulling them into a kiss. “I love you,” she whispered against their mouth.

    “I know,” Rio murmured back, stealing a piece of her pancake.

    They ate in bed, sharing bites and kisses and laughter. Agatha teased Rio for being too smug, and Rio reminded her that she had moaned their name at least three times the night before, which made Agatha throw a pillow at them while blushing deeply.

    Later, once the tray was cleared and they were curled up on the couch, Rio helped her sort the candy for the trick-or-treaters in the building, unwrapping the bags and pouring everything into the cauldron bowl they’d picked up last week.

    Her heart swelled watching Rio peel back a candy wrapper and sneak it into her hand like a secret. It was dumb. It was sweet. It was so incredibly them.

    She couldn’t believe how lucky she was.

    They spent the late afternoon getting the last details of the decoration ready for the party— Rio climbed the counter again to hang string lights and fake cobwebs while Agatha arranged candles in thrifted holders. Wanda rolled her eyes at them but helped anyway, lighting incense that made the entire apartment smell like clove and cinnamon.

    When the sun started to dip, they finally got ready.

    The costumes were a little ridiculous—Sidney Prescott and Billy Loomis. Agatha had fake blood smeared down her arms and onto her tank top. Rio had blood around their jawline and a wild gleam in their eye that made her shiver for entirely non-horror-related reasons. They stood in the mirror, and Agatha leaned in close.

    “What’s your favorite scary movie?” she asked in a breathy whisper, eyes gleaming. Rio didn’t answer. Just kissed her.

    At the party, Rio didn’t leave her side once. They met her friends from class, humored small talk with people neither of them knew, and stayed close whenever Agatha’s social battery started to wane. They handed her a drink when she needed one, kissed her temple when she got pulled into a conversation, and made her feel anchored, seen.

    Agatha was having so much fun, she almost forgot it was her birthday. But then, Wanda and Natasha showed up with a cake the size of her torso, candles flickering wildly as the whole room sang. And something about that moment—everyone off-key, grinning, all those voices directed at her—made something inside her ache and bloom at the same time. She flushed, overwhelmed, but smiling. She glanced around the room and saw Rio was watching her. Not just watching, beaming, like Agatha had invented the stars. And that’s when she realized—this was it.

    She didn’t need anything more, but she wished to be this happy for a long time. Much later, after everyone left and the apartment was quiet again, she curled into bed next to Rio, who was still wearing their smeared costume and blinking sleepily at the ceiling. “You awake?” she whispered.

    “Mmm.” Rio turned slightly toward her, eyes half-lidded. “Did you like your birthday?” They asked softly.

    Agatha turned into their chest, her voice quiet. “It was the best one I’ve ever had.” “I’m glad,” Rio murmured, thumb brushing over her arm.

    She kissed them, slow and sweet. “Thank you, baby. For everything. These past two weeks were amazing. I wish we could live like this forever.”

    At that, Rio turned fully to face her, more awake now. Their gaze was serious, warm. “Me too,” they said. And then, after a pause: “I know it’s a bit too soon, but I hope you know I want to play the big game here, Agatha. I know you still have a few months ‘til graduation and maybe a post-grad after that, but I want to be here for you. For as long as you’ll have me.”

    Agatha’s chest tightened, tears pricking her eyes before she could stop them. She hadn’t expected that. But of course they knew what to say. Of course they meant it. She blinked back the sting in her eyes. Rio knew exactly what to say at that moment. In every moment, apparently.

    Agatha didn’t speak, just pressed her face into the crook of their neck, letting them brush away a tear she hadn’t meant to let escape.

    And in that quiet, safe, perfect space between their heartbeats, it was enough for her to realize that this was what family was supposed to feel like. This was the home.
