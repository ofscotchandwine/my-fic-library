Chapter 1

Alex accepted the cup from Sam. It smelled like hospital coffee, the kind you only got in hospitals and even then only in hospital emergency rooms. It was the same everywhere. It was like there was a proprietary blend of cheap, mostly stale beans and disinfectant that only hospitals bought. Maybe they mixed it up themselves. “Thanks, Sam,” Alex said, making a face. “What the Hell is this? Artificial sweetener?”

“Hospital regulations disallow sugar,” her partner informed her.

“Because chemical sweeteners are so much better,” the agent groused.

“What are we looking at?”

“I can tell you that six of the migrants were already dead,” Alex reported. “They were dead before the truck was even opened. Beyond that I couldn’t say. Crime scene techs are still processing the site but we’ve got to interview the actual victims, assuming that they can still, you know, speak.”

The brunette shook her head. “Honestly. I don’t know why we have to invent ghouls and werewolves and crap like that. People are monsters enough.” Sam looked around. There had been some sixty people crammed into a tractor-trailer that had been abandoned in the desert. Twenty of the patients had been brought here, to Lord General Hospital. Others had been scattered to other area hospitals. No one facility in the area had enough beds to accommodate everyone, and the field office was scrambling to keep up. “All right. I guess we wait. Explain to me where the locals are again.”

“I’m afraid we’re a little stretched these days,” a voice from his other side commented. “James Olsen, National City police department.” A dark hand reached out to shake her. After a moment Alex accepted the offer. “Local aid cuts, you know how it is. Good thing we have the feds to back us up.”

“I guess,” Alex said. “Agents Alex Danvers and Samantha Arias. Pleased to meet you.”

“It’s a pleasure. Danvers, you said? Any relation to Eliza Danvers?”

What was up with this guy holding her hand for so long? “Yeah. She was my Mom.”

“Great Woman. Hell of a cop. I worked with her for a while in Medieval. I thought I remembered her saying that her girl went fed. She was real proud of you.”

Okay, now the handholding was just really creepy. “Well, thanks James. Any word on security here right now? I’m a little worried about the traffickers being able to get in here and get at these people.”

“Well, that’s why we’re here, right?”

“Right,” Sam said. She glanced around. There were not enough beds in the ER; people were literally on gurneys in the hall. “I’m sure they feel very safe.”

One of the nurses came out of one of the treatment rooms – a blonde, Alex noticed, with short hair. She was pretty but she really kind of frightened him. “Who’s in charge of the investigation here?”

“That would be me, ma’am,” Alex told her, and that’s when the screaming started. “Well that lasted,” Alex muttered, drawing her gun. She knew the difference between grief-screaming and terror-screaming. This was coming from the entrance to the emergency department and this was not a drill. “Is there some way of locking this area down?” Alex asked the nurse.

She nodded and slammed a big red button on the nurses’ station. “Page Doctor Rose ,” she directed one of her colleagues. Alarms sounded. Gunshots rang out somewhere in the distance.

“You paged a freakin’ doctor?” Alex questioned.

“As a matter of fact yes,” she replied. Doors swung shut. “Now stay out of the way. “ She and her colleagues began moving gurneys into treatment rooms as someone paged Doctor Rose to the emergency room “immediately.” Alex knew from hospitals. Weren’t they supposed to say “stat” or something?

The three cops exchanged glances. “I never argue with the ER nurses here,” James advised. “No one would ever find the body.”

“If you think that the nurses are engaging in obstruction of justice it would be inappropriate to avoid filing a report,” Sam told the officer, drawing her own weapon and aiming it at the door.

“Open the door!” a voice called from the admitting area. “We’re not asking twice.”

Alex had been an agent for five years, a cop for a long time before that. Longer than she’d carried a badge, really – it was the family business. She could read a lot about a person in just a few seconds. She didn’t even really need to see them. “Gotham City? Seriously?” She asked Sam.

“That was unexpected,” her partner replied. “I thought the men we were tracking were primarily local. The Gotham groups usually work with Russians, or am I mistaken?”

“We can sort it out later. You’re kidding, right?” Alex called back. “No way we’re letting you in here.”

“This doesn’t concern you” a second voice replied. “We just want our property back.”

“People aren’t property jackass,” Alex retorted. She looked at James. “You want to radio this one in or what?”

“Right, right,” James said, picking up his radio. “Uh, this is Sargent James Olsen at Lord General, we’ve got a situation here…” There was a gunshot, and a screamed obscenity.

“We aren’t playing around here, G-woman!” the first man yelled. “We’ve got a hostage. Open up the doors or he’ll bleed out in a matter of minutes.”

“Asshole, there’s no way you’re walking out of here with twenty people,” Alex shot back.

“Nineteen,” the blonde nurse supplied helpfully. “One of the women died while you were getting into your little pissing contest.”

“Not helping, lady,” Alex growled.

“They’re still ours,” the voice replied.

A different set of doors opened up and two people walked through them. Both of them wore scrubs with white jackets and were apparently of Caucasian descent, although that’s where the resemblance ended. Both of them are women, one was about five foot four with long wavy black hair and pale skin. Her nametag clearly read “Dr. L.Luthor.” She looked pissed. The other was she had a good two - three inches on Alex and Alex wasn’t exactly short. It wasn’t as though the girl was skinny either - the girl looked fit, although scrubs weren’t exactly flattering on anyone. Her nametag identified her as “Dr. K. Rose” although her long blondie hair and slight scar on eyebrow made the title a little unbelievable. She didn’t look particularly pissed. “Resolute” might be a better term. “What’s the situation here?” Rose wanted to know. Her voice was rasping, smooth.

“A couple of coyotes trying to take back their ‘property,’” Sam replied, sizing the giant up.

The woman nodded. “How many of them are there?”

Blonde Nurse checked a screen. “Two. Five hostages, one bleeding.”

“He shot a hostage? What a dick.” Luthor didn’t seem exceptionally worried, or concerned.

“Tell me, exactly what are a couple of extra doctors going to do about a hostage situation?” Alex wanted to know.

“This doctor’s going to work on the trafficking victims here in the ER. You know, the ones who brought the creeps here in the first place,” Luthor smirked. “I assume you’d like for them to actually survive? I’d prefer that sweet cheeks here do the same and not risk those perfect hands of her, but we both know that isn’t going to happen. “

Blue eyes turned to Alex and looked down. Her sister’s eyes had been blue. Alex hadn’t thought much about Kara in… hell, ten years? Must have been. Why would she spring to mind now? “That guy is bleeding out right now, Alex,” Dr.Rose said in a voice that was half growl. “There isn’t time to wait for a friggin’ SWAT team. Are you coming with me or not?”

“You’re not even wearing a vest!” Alex objected.

“Nope.”

“You’re a civilian,” James frowned. “They’re going to shoot you in your face.”

“Then they do. Is one of you going to come and play backup or not?” Dr.Rose was walking toward the barrier already. Alex had to race to catch up.

“What the hell, Rose?” Alex hissed.

The Doctor ignored her and banged on the door. “This is Doctor Kara Rose. I’m a surgeon here at Lord General. I’m going to come out to tend to your hostage, okay?”

“You have to let us in, puppy doc,” the criminal on the other side taunted. “We’re not letting you take our hostage.”

“hey, the hostage is no good to you dead, and then the charges get upgraded to murder, you understand me?” Rose told him. “I’m just going to come out with one negotiator.” She turned to Alex. “Put your gun away.” She turned back to the door. “Just us, okay? I’ll be your hostage, all right? They’ve already radioed this in, you don’t want to make this worse than it already is.” A very tense moment followed. “ a doctor is a higher-value hostage than some receptionist, right? Just let me come out, okay?”

After another couple of seconds the second man spoke. “All right, but no funny business. Just you and the negotiator.”

“You’re a friggin’ idiot,” Alex hissed to the doctor.

Doctor shrugged and pushed the door open slowly, hands raised. She and Alex walked slowly into the reception area. “It’s just me, see?” she said. “Me and this woman here.” She walked over to the hostage, who was struggling to hold a hand over a spurting wound in his shoulder. “Do you mind if I take a look at your patient?” She kept his eyes locked on those of the guy with the gun.

“Go ahead, pretty girl.” The coyote was about forty, maybe forty-five, and bald with blue eyes. He had plenty of scars and bad teeth. His partner had facial hair, obviously compensating for the hair missing from his head. “Let’s make this fast, though. G-woman there can bring him back to your ER and then you and I can settle in and wait.”

“Sure thing, boss. Just let me take a look and see what the best way is to stabilize the patient.” He crouched down in front of the hostage, who looked like he might have been a janitor or something. “How you doing, Robert?”

“I’ve been better,” Robert gasped.

Rose slowly reached into her pocket. “I’m just grabbing some gauze to try to stop the bleeding,” she assured the captor. Both of the bad guys drew closer, suspicious. “Relax. No problems from me, man.” She pulled out a huge wad of gauze and pressed it to Robert’s shoulder. “This probably doesn’t feel great, man. Sorry about this.” She helped Robert to his (shaky) feet and carefully passed him to Alex, passing her an access card while she was at it. As she did she met Alex’s eyes. There was something there, something Alex couldn’t help but feel she was missing.

“Okay, pretty girl, let’s take your seat now,” said Thing Number Two, grabbing Rose by the arm. That was when the doctor moved, and frankly no woman that size should have been able to move so damned fast. Alex dragged Robert out of the line of fire as fast as she could while Rose grabbed the assailant’s arm, pulled it past and broke the elbow. She grabbed the gun out of the trafficker’s hand, used it to hit the enemy hard on the head and aimed it at the first trafficker.

“Lie down on the ground,” she said in a growl, with your hands over your head. The thug snarled something obscene – and moved to pull the trigger. Rose was faster. She shot the him in the shoulder. The enemy yelled and fell to the ground, spurting blood. The doctor then pocketed her gun and turned to the agent. “Toss me your cuffs, Alex,” she instructed calmly. “And open the door, would you? We’re going to need to get him into surgery. Robert too.”

Alex fumbled for her handcuffs and tossed them to the physician, who was already on her knees to stop the bleeding. “What the hell, Doc?” Alex asked. Nurses and orderlies and Sam and James surged in.

“I’d recommend reading this guy his rights or whatever,” Rose advised as she helped to get the bleeding coyote onto a gurney. “I’d hate for him to get out on a technicality. Right, Alex?” She started walking back toward the treatment area.

“Hey,” Alex called. “Hey, Rose!” She called.

Rose turned.

“How do you know my name?” Alex asked. They’d never been introduced.

Rose kept walking.

Chapter 2

It was a few days – three or four – before Alex went back to the hospital. There was paperwork to fill out. That was the problem with her line of work, and of course being a Fed meant tons and tons of paperwork.

 

It wasn’t as though she didn’t have other cases to work on either. She had plenty of work to do. The truck had been found on Friday, and she had to testify in court on Monday, and it wasn’t as though the job went away… It wasn’t as though she wanted to go in unprepared either.

 

Doctor Kara Rose had known her name. They hadn’t been introduced. The way she’d spoken to her made it sound like she’d known more than just her name, too. And those blue eyes… The age was right, or right-ish. It was kind of hard to tell with the doctor. The name was wrong. The accent had nothing of medivel about it either. So she did some research, some digging.

 

She mentioned the issue to Sam and to her supervisor in the field office, J'onzz. Sam was surprisingly more leery of investigating than J'onzz, but ultimately both were willing to do some poking around. Between the three of them, Alex’s wi-fi connection and some beer over the weekend they managed to get some information. Working backwards from Rose’s official Lord General biography, Dr. Kara M Rose was employed by both the diagnostic medicine and trauma departments at Lord General. She’d been in National City for about two years.

 

 

Prior to that she’d worked for Medecins Sans Frontiers working in such sunshiny places as Afghanistan and Somalia. Prior to that she’d been in California, and prior to that she’d completed very prestigious residency programs in Metropolis, Star City and Central City. She’d attended Stanford University both for undergraduate and for medical school, where she’d graduated early.

 

No trace of Kara Rose could be found before matriculation at Stanford. “It would be completely illegal,” Sam said, “to go into Stanford’s records without a warrant and examine the woman’s files to see where she went to high school.” She’d known Sam too long.

 

“Fortunately we’re not at the office,” she pointed out. “We’re at home.”

 

 

“Still illegal, Danvers,” her boss said from where he was already hacking into Stanford’s system.

 

“Fortunately we’re not actually trying to make a case against her for any reason, right?”

 

“What are we trying to do here, Alex?” her partner wanted to know. “Why is this doctor is so important to you?”

 

“I just… well, I mean, don’t you want to know why a friggin’ doctor is Sara freaking Lance?” she asked.
City“Did you see her take down those baddies? I mean, she didn’t even blink an eye.” It would make some sense if Rose was who Alex thought she was, but that was about it.

 

 

“The nurses in the ER seemed to think she would quickly resolve the situation,” Sam concurred.

 

“But why do you feel the need to pry into her life so closely? Have you developed intimate feelings for her, Alex?”

 

Alex blinked. That was a thought that had not even remotely occurred to her. “Er, no, Sam. She just reminds me of someone I used to know.”

 

“She should,” J'onzz told her. “I got bingo right here. On July seventeenth, 2006, Kara Melissa Rose received permission from the university to move in early and was given a job with student housing to allow access to emergency funds.” The date caused a pit to form in the middle of Alex’s stomach.

 

 

“On the same day the school registered a name change from a student who had already agreed to attend the school – changing the name to Kara Melissa Rose from Kara Alura Danvers of Medival, California. Three days before that Ms. Rose-ne-Danvers sought and obtained said name change as well as a restraining order against her mother, Eliza Danvers.”

 

Before Alex knew what she was doing she was across the room, staring at her boss’ screen. “You’re kidding,” she said.

 

“When a kid shows up to Stanford with six broken ribs, a broken wrist and a black eye the authorities are usually more inclined to be sympathetic.” J'onzz shook his head. “Damn, Alex. I know your mom could be a hard-ass…”

 

 

“You should have seen the other woman,” Alex commented. She remembered that fight. “Did you ever meet Mom?”

 

“Eliza? Yeah, a couple of times, right before the end. Why?”

 

“Ever wonder why her nose was so crooked?”

 

 

He shook his head. “Well, if what you told me about what went down between her and those two idiots at the hospital is true then I can’t say as I’m surprised.”

 

The older man shrugged. “So what now, Danvers?”

 

“What do you mean?”

 

 

“We just figured out that your younger sister has been in the same city with you for two years,” Sam said bluntly.

 

“What do you intend to do with this information? We can give you her phone number, we can give you her home address. You can have a reunion.”

 

“Uh… I don’t know, Sam.”

 

“You don’t know.”

 

“No, I don’t. I haven’t seen or heard from her in ten years, you know? She… she walked out on us, she walked out on our mission. She didn’t seem all that excited to see me when we were around each other yesterday, you know?” she shook her head and rubbed her face. She had a sister again.

 

Last week – hell, yesterday – she didn’t have a sister and now she did. Even if she never spoke to her again, never saw her, the sister existed and everything changed. “I didn’t even recognize her. She knew me.”

 

“Were you close?” That was J'onzz.

 

“Apparently not. I mean, yeah. When we were kids I guess we were pretty close. I pretty much raised the kid. But that didn’t stop her from taking off. I guess she did pretty well for herself, huh?” She should be proud.

 

On some level she supposed she was, but it wasn’t like Kara’d been at all happy to see her. She’d been all efficiency and growls. No joy. No apprehension, either. No emotion at all.

 

 

“So why did I waste my weekend digging up her back story and drinking your crappy beer instead of watching movie and baseball in the comfort of my own home?” J'onzz wanted to know. “If you aren’t going to at least go talk to the gir –“

 

“Does she even know that your mother died?” Sam inquired politely.

 

 

“Uh, I don’t know. I never told her.” Alex shook herself.

 

“You know what? I should go talk to her. You know, about the case. Under less stressful circumstances. You know. Not when there’s a rampaging psycho looking to shoot up her workplace. At least we should talk about the case, right?”

 

 

“Right. And if you don’t want to see her after that, well, National City is a big place. There’s no real need for you and her to interact if you don’t choose to,” her boss told Alex.

 

“Why don’t you two head on over there on Tuesday?”

 

Which is how Alex found herself on Tuesday afternoon heading back to Lord General Hospital with her partner.

 

Some intern for the bureau had ensured that Dr. Rose was indeed in the office today. Alex hadn’t bothered to schedule an appointment; it would probably take forever to get on the woman’s calendar, so she aimed for late in the workday and hoped she wasn’t a golfer.

 

The Kara she’d known as a kid wouldn’t have gone for golf, but the Kara she’d known as a kid wouldn’t have let her walk out of the hospital after a ten year absence without so much as a “so long.”

 

 

The Kara she’d known as a kid wouldn’t have let a ten-year absence happen.

 

She and Sam entered the Diagnostic Medicine reception area. It was nice enough, she guessed. It didn’t look much like they did on House. It looked like any other waiting room, complete with outdated copies of Martha Stewart Living.

 

She paced. She couldn’t sit for long. There were other doors, other doctors. The other doctor from the hostage incident had an office here too.

 

“Dr. Rose is in a meeting, she should be back in a few minutes,” the receptionist informed her. “Can I get you anything to drink? Some coffee, or some water?”

 

“No thank you,” Sam said for both of them. “Hospital coffee tastes like disinfectant.” The woman blinked.

 

“Were you raised on planet Krypton?” Alex whispered.

 

“No,” her partner replied, and picked up a magazine.

 

It took about fifteen minutes of pacing before any of the staff returned to the office.

 

None of them were wearing scrubs. Dr. Luthor actually looked pretty hot in her pencil skirt and heels if she had to be honest – and if she didn’t give Alex the most direct look of contempt that she’d ever received from a woman.

 

“Agents Danvers and Arias,” Lena greeted. “To what do we owe this unexpected pleasure?”

 

Kara showed little emotion when her colleague’s voice cut through the babble of post-meeting small talk.

 

“We were hoping to debrief Dr. Rose on last week’s incident,” Alex suggested. “I recognize that she’s a busy woman, this shouldn’t take long.”

 

 

One of the other women present, but Nia frowned. “I’m pretty sure that our legal department is going to take some exception to that,” she said in a quiet, cool voice that made Alex’s skin crawl.

 

“There’s no way that Kara’s talking to anyone without our lawyers present, and I’m reasonably certain that they’ve all gone home for the day.”

 

“We’re not accusing her of anything,” Sam objected, stepping forward. “We just need to take her statement.”

 

“Which you won’t be taking without a lawyer,” the doctor insisted. Kara just crossed her arms and met Alex’s eyes.

 

“It seems rather odd that you would have chosen to attack her without proper procedure – without notifying our legal department, without making an appointment –“

 

“Jesus Christ, girl, no one is attacking anyone,” Alex exploded. “We just want to talk to the woman.”

 

“Agent Arias” Nia said, “would you be a dear and reach out to the legal department? See if they can send someone up to cover, if they have anyone.”

 

“I just want to have a goddamn conversation,” Alex complained. “Kar, you can have a conversation with me.”

 

 

The object of her quest huffed. “Right.”

 

“Kar, please,” Alex pleaded, not really caring how it looked.

 

The doctors all looked at their colleague, but it wasn’t in confusion. They knew. Finally Kara relented, but it wasn’t comfortably. She never lost that defensive stance.

 

“Fine,” she spat, “but only in front of Lena or Nia. You’re not ganging up on me.”

 

Ganging up on her? “Fine. Whatever.” Lena – Dr. Luthor– smirked and led the way to Kara’s office. Did Kara have a thing going with the hot doctor? Alex didn’t think they had that kind of body language, but maybe she was wrong. Maybe they could turn it off in the ER. It wasn’t like that sort of thing was sanitary.

 

 

The office was spectacularly impersonal. There were books, of course – all kinds of books about all kinds of things. Anatomy, disease, disease through history, pathology… Alex’s eyes swam with the effort of looking at them. Some of the books weren’t even in English. There were no photos, no knick-knacks. Everything was meticulously arranged and in place. It smelled like coffee and that was about it. Lena gestured to two chairs on the “guest” side of the desk.

 

 

Lena took the chair behind the desk – looking a little ridiculous, since it was set up at a height for a woman over a inches taller than she was – while Kara stood behind her and looked twitchy. “All right. Debrief,” she instructed.

 

“Good to see you too, Kar,” Alex said.

 

“Can it, Alex,” Kara snapped. “You’re not here for small talk.”

 

“Actually I am,” Alex said with a sigh. “I wouldn’t have thought that I’d have had to schedule an appointment to see my sister again after ten years. Or that you’d think you’d need a lawyer or freakin’ witnesses for the reunion. You look good, though. Looks like you’ve been taking great care of yourself.”

 

 

“Thanks.”

 

“So… doctor, huh?” One-sided conversations sucked, but it wasn’t the first one she’d been involved with. “Good on you. Must be doing well for yourself.”

 

Kara shrugged, moving that massive frame around. “I do okay.”

 

“Kara donates a lot of her salary,” Dr. Luthor – Lena – informed. Her tone was somewhere between hostility and pride. “Not that it’s any of your business, but the tax records are all in order. Is that what this is about? Because that’s definitely something that’s going to wait for a lawyer.”

 

 

“Christ. No. Can’t we just be sisters?”

 

 

“Sorry. The last time she encountered a family member said family member stabbed her in the gut. You’ll have to forgive us if no one around here is exactly enthusiastic about a repeat of the experience.” Lena smirked at Alex.

 

 

“Wait, what?” Alex stood up. “It’s not like – did you see Mom?”

 

 

“How do you think I knew you’d joined the Bureau?”

 

“When?”

 

“Right when Cat Grant got killed.” Kara sighed. “How much do you know about that?”

 

“It was in California. They were there looking for the guy who killed Dad and Cat took a bullet,” Alex remembered.

 

 

“Yeah. I was the surgeon. I did what I could but she’d lost too much blood, there was too much damage.” Kara looked away briefly, toward the window and her eyes looked almost red for a moment. They snapped back to Alex.

 

“I suspected that she’d be there with Eliza but I wasn’t positive, you know? I still went out to talk to the one who brought her in because that’s just kind of what you do and we spoke a bit. She tried to tell me they were just walking but I mean, come on, in that neighborhood at that hour? And I looked at her and she recognized me, and she took this knife out of her pocket and she stabbed me with it.”

 

“She didn’t stab you, Kar,” Alex scoffed. “Mom was a lot of things, and not all of them were good, but she wasn’t a killer.”

 

“I was right there, you buffoon,” Lena said coldly. “And a good thing, too. Kept going on about how Kara had the reign in her or some such crap and how it didn’t matter how far or fast she ran, if she didn’t get her you would hunt her down. So you see why we’re a little reluctant to leave you alone with her.”

 

“Mom never said anything,” Alex assured Kara. No wonder she was less than enthusiastic about seeing her sister again. “I swear. I had no idea. How come they didn’t arrest her? I never knew about that.”

 

“She was a cop who had just lost her longtime partner,” Sam surmised. “They were probably disinclined to make more trouble for her.”

 

“Well, you’re safe from her now. She died. In California , actually. DUI.”

 

“I know. Who do you think pronounced?” For a moment, Kara looked old, impossibly old. Then she straightened up. “So great. Good talk.”

 

“Kar, look. She never even told me she’d seen you. I had no idea. Honestly. As far as I knew, you were dead to us. That’s how she always treated it.” Kara scoffed.

 

“Look. Let’s… let’s try this again. Let’s just go out and grab dinner, or some beers or something. Let’s just try to re-connect, okay? Wife? Kids?”

 

“No.”

 

“What about you, Lexie? Wife? Kids?” Lena smirked again. Alex was pretty sure that Lena's face had frozen that way, in the way of old wives’ tales.

 

“Actually yeah, Kar. You’re an aunty. I’ve got a little girl – well, she’s eight now – back in Gotham City. Things didn’t work out with her mom but you know. She even looks a little bit like you.” Alex went for her wallet and Kara twitched.

 

Not for the first time, Alex cursed her mother silently. Alex passed the photo to Sam, who passed it to Lena, who passed it to Kara. “She’s coming here in August. I’d love for her to meet you when she gets here.”

 

“She doesn’t even know she has an aunty, does she?” Kara sneered. She glanced at the photo and passed it back.

 

“No. But she will, once I talk to her tonight.” Alex sighed and tried to put her emotions to the side. She was good at this. She was one of the best interrogators the local office had. “Girlfriend? Boyfriend?”

 

Lena snorted. “No.”

“Alex, just shut up, okay? She’s not interested. Not like that. Maybe it might have worked years ago, but not now, and not with me, and it’s none of your god damn business anyway so just shut up. Can we please just focus on these jackasses that are apparently trying to kill me and now poor Lee so we can get back to living our lives?” Alex could feel her sister’s muscles vibrating from across the car.

 

 

“You have to switch to decaf. It’s time.”

 

 

“Time to fucking move,” the younger woman muttered, looking back out the window. “Where do you live, anyway?”

 

 

“I’m just up near,” Alex said. “It’s a nice enough place, you know. Not fancy or anything but I like it. So tell me, have Russians been a problem for you before? Or, you know, Albanians?”

 

 

“Yeah.”

 

 

Alex waited for a moment. “Okay, care to elaborate?”

 

 

“Not while you’re driving.”

 

 

Alex pulled onto the interstate. “Why?”

 

 

“Because you’re going to flip your lid and I’d rather you didn’t do that at eighty miles an hour.” She paused. “So. A kid, huh?”

 

 

“Yeah. A girl. I told her about you. She wants you to take her to the zoo when she comes to visit in August.”

 

Kara scoffed. “You’re kidding, right?”

 

 

“What, you don’t want to take your niece to the zoo?”

 

“You don’t know me, Alex. We haven’t spoken in over a decade and you haven’t approved of a goddamn thing I’ve done in a lot longer than that. I’m a witness to you at best. You’re not seriously going to let me just wander off to the zoo with your only child.”

 

 

The Fed sighed. “Kara, you’re more than just a witness to me. I’m actually glad that we have a chance to reconnect. Really. I loved you. I raised you, for crying out loud. I never wanted you to leave and it broke my heart when you walked out that door.”

 

 

“You were pretty eager to take Eliza’s side,” her sister told her darkly.

 

 

“You were walking out. You were disobeying orders. You were turning your back on us, on me!”

 

 

Kara sighed. “You know, normal families see a full ride scholarship to Stanford as a cause for celebration.”

 

 

“You didn’t even tell us it was Stanford, Kara.”

 

 

“It didn’t matter where I went, Alex! Mom would have killed me before letting me leave the state!” She sighed. “It doesn’t matter anymore. What’s done is done. Like you said, you saw it – you see it – as me turning my back on Mom’s mission.”

 

 

“Our mission, Kara. Our mission. We put killers behind bars. It’s what we do. It’s how we honor Dad’s memory.” Kara gave a little laugh again.

 

“That’s funny to you?” It wasn’t nice of her, but the subject matter wasn’t exactly filling her with the urge to pull punches.

 

 

“’Dad’s memory.’ , I was 3 years old when he died. he was mom’s husband, and she loved him. Or she thought she did, I don’t know. I can see getting a little unhinged and obsessed, sure. she didn’t have the right to poison your mind and turn you into a good little soldier, but at least you knew him. You remembered him. You at least thought you were fighting for something. I didn’t even have that. And it’s not even like mom would even talk about him. Or you. I wasn’t fighting for anything. I wasn’t trying to avenge anything, anyone.” Kara shook her head again and massaged the bridge of her nose.

 

“Fuck it. This is just ridiculous.”

 

 

Alex was quiet for a moment. She was angry. Of course she was angry. She wanted to throw a punch at her sister. How dared she crap all over their father’s grave this way, their mother’s? At the same time, she had to admit that the younger Danvers had a point. She hadn’t meant to exclude Kara from their Father’s memory like that, but it couldn’t have been helped, could it? It had been too painful to talk about him, and of course she wouldn’t have any direct knowledge of him. How could she be expected to buy into a revenge plan if she had no real attachment to the person being avenged?

 

 

“What did you expect me to do, Kar?” Alex asked quietly.

 

 

“Huh?”

 

 

“What could I have done differently, so we wouldn’t be having this conversation now?”

 

 

Kara actually considered it. She tilted her head to one side. “Well, not standing there while Eliza broke six of my ribs would have been nice,” she said then, “but a ride to the bus station would have been a good second best.”

 

 

“Would you have stayed in touch?”

 

 

“Maybe. If you promised to keep my location safe from Eliza.” She shrugged again. “It doesn’t matter, Alex. It’s all… you know. You’d still have hated me for going to medical school. And I’d have still gone to medical school.”

 

 

“Is being a cop really that dirty?” Alex asked. “Do you really look down on us that much?”

 

 

“What? No! Some people just shouldn’t be cops. I’m one of them. So was Eliza. If you’re happy being a cop or a fed or whatever that’s great, I’m very happy for you. I’m sure you must be good at it if they’re letting you drive the bus on an investigation like this.”

 

 

Alex would have been lying if she said she didn’t feel a little bit of pride at that. “Yeah. I do like it. I mean, I like the field office, J'onzz is a good guy to work for. I miss being able to see Ruby all the time but you can’t always get what you want, and I get most of her school holidays, you know? It’s nice. She’s a good kid. I’m really proud of her.” Alex glanced at her sister. “Ever think about having any of your own?”

 

 

“No.” Kara shrugged.

 

 

Five full minutes passed in silence. “So you volunteer at a clinic on Saturday mornings, huh?” Alex tried.

 

 

“Yeah.”

 

 

“They not paying you enough at Lord General?”

 

 

“That’s not how volunteer works.”

 

 

“Then why?” Alex looked at her sister.

 

 

“The clinic needed a doctor. It’s not like I’ve got anything better to do.”

 

 

“No hobbies or nothing?”

 

 

Kara sighed. It was easy to see the anger there, roiling just under the skin. So many years of anger and for what? She was the one who had left. She was always going to have left. What was it that Alex really wanted here anyway? She still blamed Alex for leaving, and Kara still blamed Alex for… what? Standing by Mom?

 

“Not to speak of. I work. I work out. I go home.”

 

 

“Dr. Sexy reruns?”

 

 

“You would like that show.”

 

 

“Are you kidding? That show is television gold, sis. Television gold.” Kara was leaning her head on the window now. Even with all of the distance it seemed so incredibly… natural, so normal to have her there in her seat again. Like no time at all had passed. Of course, since it seemed like no time at all had passed she recognized all of the little symptoms even though the face was barely even similar.

 

“God, when’s the last time you slept?”

 

 

“None of your business, Alex.”

 

 

“It is my business, Kar. If I need to keep you safe then I need to know when you’re off your game. Lena told me she didn’t think you’d been sleeping right anyway, and I can see that little twitch your eyebrow that you always get when you haven’t been sleeping. Out with it. Come on, level with me.”

 

 

Kara looked at her. “You didn’t recognize my damn face but now you remember my eyebrow tics? Unbelievable.”

 

 

“I never forgot your eyebrow tic, Kara. Remember, when you left you were a beanstalk. tall already, yeah, but a beanstalk. Now you’re all Supergirl. So, last time you got more than two hours’ sleep or I’m not giving you the combo to the gun safe.”

 

 

The look of pure spite Kara shot her dated back twenty years at least. “Monday.”

 

 

“Seriously? You haven’t had a decent night’s sleep since Monday?”

 

 

“I’ve got the Russian mob after me, I’ve got you trying to force your way back into my life and who knows if you’re going to try to kill me too, there’s all that other crap, yeah, sleep and I aren’t really on speaking terms right now. I’ll be fine.”

 

 

“I’m not going to try to kill you, Kara. It’s my job to keep you safe. Starting by not giving you the damn gun safe combo until I’ve seen you get eight hours at least. You can use my bed, I’ll take the couch.” Alex shook her head. How the Hell was she supposed to fix this if her mere presence was causing the kid insomnia?

 

 

“Gross. No thanks.”

 

 

“I’ll give you clean sheets, geez.”

 

 

“Alex, I’ve slept in much worse places than some Fed’s couch. I’ll be fine.” Kara lapsed into silence again and no attempts on Alex’s part could roust her.

 

 

Fortunately traffic was minimal and it wasn’t long before the little caravan pulled into her driveway. Alex couldn’t help but feel some nerves as she let the company into her abode. J'onzz and Sam weren’t an issue of course. They knew everything anyway and if there were empty pizza boxes on the coffee table chances were they had something to do with putting them there in the first place. These two, though – these strangers – what would they think as they looked around? Kara’s eyes flicked from window to window to door to door – all of the exits. She did not care about the décor, or about the clutter. Well, that made sense. She’d been trained by Eliza Danvers after all.

 

She knew what was important and she wasn’t trying to connect with an older sibling she’d rejected over ten years ago. She was hiding out in a federal agent’s home. Personal crap didn’t matter if it didn’t give her an advantage. Lena on the other hand took her time looking around the room, bags near the stairs. Her eyes lingered on every portrait.

 

 

“Is that your father, Lexie?” Lena demanded, gesturing to a picture near Alex’s favorite chair.

 

 

“Yeah, that’s me and Dad. Taken when I was a little less than four.” It must have been right before Kara had been conceived, just her and her dad. she could see the gazebo of the park across the street in the background, American flags waving.

 

“Fourth of July or thereabouts, I guess.”

 

 

“And this one must be you and Kara,” Lena said, coming over to the mantel. Having her pale hands touching her pictures was really making Alex’s skin crawl. She loved her sister, of that she was certain. Alex did not love Lena, she was equally certain of that.

 

 

“Look at this, Love! You were human sized once!”

 

 

“I think she was about twelve there, Lena,” Alex hastened to add. “She didn’t start growing for real until she was what, fifteen? Hell, we all thought she was going to be a runt until one day she woke up and she was my height. Seriously, though, it was all at once too. Girl was a freak.”

 

 

“Thanks for that, Alex. Where can I put Lena’s stuff?” Kara asked. She grabbed as many of her friend’s bags as she physically could.

 

 

“Upstairs, first door on the left.” Kara left. “Right. She hasn’t slept more than a couple of hours since Monday, she told me,” Alex informed the doctor. “I told her she’s not getting her guns back until I see she gets at least eight hours.”

 

 

“Good luck with that,” Lena said with a little half smile.

 

 

“That’s your mom, right?” Lena pointed at a portrait of their mom in uniform right across from the couch on which Kara would be sleeping.

 

 

“Shit. Sorry.” Alex hesitated for a moment and took the photo down. The wall underneath was clearly a different color – it was obvious that something had been there and hastily removed, and Kara had probably already seen it anyway. So what? Maybe she would appreciate the gesture in the spirit in which it was intended.

 

 

Agents filled the house – J'onzz and Sam of course, but also sketch artists and other agents assessing security, taking statements. Kara continued ferrying Lena’s bags upstairs. Alex felt kind of bad about that but none of the other agents seemed inclined to do so and she seemed kind of reluctant to be in the same room with Lena right now. That was probably Alex’s fault, for teasing her about their relationship.

 

Lena watched Alex go back and forth without comment. By six o’clock the place was starting to resemble a three-ring circus and a new act had been thrown into the mix. Alex had completely forgotten about her date with Kelly, the anchor from the local news channel.

 

 

Kelly walked into the house to find it filled with armed agents, most of whom seemed ready to draw on her.

 

 

“Stand down!” Alex barked in a panic. “Stand down! That’s Kelly, my friend!”

 

 

Kelly laughed. “ friend,’” she repeated. “That’s so very nineteen fifty-six of you, Danvers.” She kissed Alex on the cheek.

 

“What’s going on? I thought we had a date.”

 

 

“Oh, Kelly, I am so, so sorry. This work thing came up kind of suddenly and we’re using my house as a safe house –“

 

 

“Yeah, I kind of figured that when I saw the number of guns in the room.” Kelly waved at Sam, who was working on her laptop. “Looks exciting.”

 

 

Alex shrugged. “We don’t really get involved until it’s exciting for someone,” she pointed out. “I should’ve called, I know, but everything just started moving so fast –“

 

“Hey, I get it. It’s the risk you take when you’re dating a sexy FBI woman.” Alex wasn’t sure whose bitchface was more epic – Kara’s, Lena’s or J'onzz’s.

 

 

“You want to reschedule? I mean, it’s not like we had anything specific in mind.”

 

 

She made a snap decision. “Well, uh, you know what? We still need to feed the witnesses. It’s not exactly candlelight and roses, but if you’re willing to put up with a few extra people and some takeout I’ll make it up to you.” It might not cure everything but she hoped that it would send the right signals.

 

 

“How do you plan to do that, Danvers?”

 

 

“I’ll introduce you to my family,” Alex promised.

 

 

“Your family.”

 

 

“Sure thing. See, it turns out that one of the witnesses is my long-lost baby sister. Kara, c’mere. This is Kelly. You’ve probably seen her on the news. Kelly, this is my sister, Kara. She goes by Kara Rose these days. She’s a surgeon down at –“

 

 

Kelly shook Kara's hand, a huge smile lighting up her face. “Lord General,” she identified. “You were the surgeon who operated on me after my car accident last year. That was before I met Alex, I’m afraid. It’s nice to see you when I’m a little less doped up.”

 

 

Kara put on a professional smile and accepted the greetings. “Nice to see you too, Ms. Parker,” she greeted.

 

 

“Kelly,” she corrected. “Alex, you didn’t even mention you had a sister in the area!”

 

 

“I didn’t know I had a sister in the area until last week,” she admitted. “Maybe we’re reconnecting in a roundabout way but hey – at least we’re reconnecting, right?” Kara and Lena gave tight smiles.

 

 

Kelly’s was brighter. Alex hadn’t planned to do the whole family-introduction thing until August, but then again she hadn’t known she had a family to introduce closer than Central City until last week. And having made a point of proudly introducing Kara to Kelly should have made it clear to Kara that she was proud of her, that she actually wanted her sister in her life.

 

 

Alex had issues, and she knew she had issues. Sometimes she had to take a step back and admit to herself though – she was pretty awesome.

 

 

Eventually statements were taken, preliminary sketches were made – lots of them – and a schedule was drawn up for agents to be on guard at the house. (And that didn’t creep Alex out at all, having her colleagues running around her house while she slept.) Kara and Lena called Nia from Alex’s secure line to let her know that something had come up and where they could be reached.

 

As of now they’d be allowed in to work normal hours with an escort. Kara chafed at the caveat. Lena smirked and hoped that they could keep up. Then most of the extraneous staff went away. All that was left were the core three Feds, Kelly and the doctors with an Indian takeout menu. Kara and Sam went to pick it up, and for a little while it almost seemed like a normal group of friends enjoying a Saturday evening together. There were beers. Kelly had met J'onzz and Sam before of course, so the atmosphere between the four of them was pretty relaxed.

 

 

Lena’s hostility toward Alex herself seemed to have abated a little when she saw her take down the portrait of her mother and she seemed to get along okay with the other agents. She asked for – and got – stories of some of their exploits. Alex preferred to tell the more dashing stories – taking down bank robbers or working counterterrorism cases. Sam and J'onzz on the other hand liked the ones that took Alex down a peg or two, like the one where she’d been held hostage by teenaged girls who thought federal crime was a great way to get out of serving detention. Or the one with the cat-smuggling ring that left her sneezing non-stop for a week.

 

 

Kara had waited until the last moment to choose a seat and had chosen a seat as far from Lena as possible. Alex couldn’t help but feel a little guilty about that, but really – how could a thirty year old girl seriously be such a teenaged girl about a little teasing? And why wouldn’t she go for it? She didn’t contribute much to the dinner conversation, neither asking questions nor reacting much to the stories told. She mostly watched people in silence.

 

“Kara, you must have some fun stories about Alex from when you were kids,” Kelly invited.

 

 

Kara took a sip from her beer. She’d barely touched her food, Alex noticed, mostly moved it around her plate. She’d been nursing the same beer all night.

 

 

“Sorry,” she said. “There aren’t a lot of stories, I’m afraid. It was all pretty run-of-the-mill, nothing interesting to tell.”

 

 

“Well, what was she like?” J'onzz pressed. “As a kid, I mean. As a teenager.”

 

 

Kara paused. “She was a good daughter.”

 

 

Lena intervened. She didn’t glance at the distance between herself and her colleague. “So how does this work, anyway? A Fed and a reporter dating has got to get pretty awkward sometimes.”

 

 

“We were all a little nervous at first,” Alex’s boss admitted. “It seemed like one of those train wrecks that you just can’t look away from, but they seem to have a good handle on the line between professional and private.”

 

 

“You’ve known Kara for what, six years now?” Sam challenged. “You must have some stories to share.”

 

 

“Med school and residency doesn’t exactly lead to the kind of stories being a cop does,” the doctor demurred.

 

“It’s not exactly a thrilling adventure ride and when it is it’s usually because something’s gone horribly wrong like it did last week. It’s mostly just exams and dead bodies and bad smells.”

 

 

“How did you two meet?” Kelly wanted to know.

 

 

The doctors exchanged glances. Alex wouldn’t have noticed Kara’s shrug if she hadn’t been looking for a response. She was giving permission.

 

“It was at some kind of outing for the residents at Fenway,” she explained. “There was this guy in the stands who was a little more attentive than he needed to be. When he got physical Kara intervened.”

 

 

“Lena was perfectly capable of dealing with the guy herself,” Kara hastened to assure everyone. “She just didn’t need to that day.” Lena just smirked.

 

 

Eventually the evening wore down. J'onzz and Sam went home. Kelly stayed, much to Alex’s delight. The agent taking the overnight shift arrived. Lena went upstairs to the guest room although not without going to Kara’s suitcase, opening it and pulling out a prescription bottle.

 

 

“You’re taking these.” She put the bottle into Kara's hand but didn’t let go, blue eyes meeting green and not flinching.

 

 

“You’re kidding,” Kara objected. Lena was as close as she’d allowed anyone all night.

 

“We’re in a completely alien environment, Lee. We don’t know any of these people from, from, circus clowns.” It would have been insulting if not for Kara’s deep-seated phobia of grease paint and squeaky shoes.

 

 

“Their job is to keep us safe, Kara. I’m here, I’m right upstairs if you need me. Your sister is here and I really doubt she’s going to do anything to hurt you right now. Not in front of her girlfriend. You’re no good to anyone if you’re falling over from fatigue, Kara, or if you’re not thinking clearly. You need to sleep.” Kara kept her eyes on Lena and didn’t move her hand until Kara's hand clenched around the bottle.

 

 

Kara looked away as Lena's hand caressed her face lightly and stepped back, away to open the bottle and take two pills. Lena actually counted them before putting them back into Kara's suitcase.

 

 

“Good doctor. You can have a biscuit in the morning.”

 

 

Kara glared and took a place on the couch. Alex got her a blanket that was acknowledged with the coldest of thank yous before the rest of the household retired for the night. No one said anything about the sleeping pills.

 

 

The next morning Kelly woke Alex up with a kiss. It was a nice way to wake up, nicer than Alex’s usual alarm clock and cursing. She didn’t have any problem with letting it develop into something a little more intense either, followed by a leisurely shower. She might have heard a door open downstairs, but it was probably just the changing of the shifts for the duty agent.

 

 

She might have heard Lena’s door open and shut as well but hey – Lena had the run of the place and Alex had much more interesting things on her mind than her sister’s keeper/not-a-girlfriend/whatever. After their shower when they were washed and dressed the pair made their way downstairs to find coffee made with eggs and fruit already prepared. Lena was reading the paper, sharing it with Sam. The couch was empty. The blanket was folded ever so neatly on the center cushion.

 

 

“Where’s Kara?” Alex asked, grabbing coffee for herself and her lady.

 

 

“She went for a run,” Lena informed.

 

 

Alex’s coffee flew across the room. Neither Sam nor Lena seemed to look up but both dodged the flow so that it only splashed across the wall.

 

 

“She what?” the homeowner roared.

 

 

“A run,” the doctor repeated.

 

 

“It’s where a person puts one foot in front of the other, really fast. Kara’s a marathoner. Thought you knew.” Lena speared a piece of cut mango with her fork and ate it, not looking up from the “Science” section.

 

 

“Sam, you let her go?” the older sister seethed.

 

 

“Not I,” her partner demurred, eating from a box of doughnuts she’d evidently brought.

 

 

“Kara and Eve discussed the matter thoroughly. According to Eve she tried to dissuade Kara. She told her that unless she planned to arrest Kara she was going to do her running. She asked what she would do if she tried to take her into custody. Kara told her she would make it look like a heart attack and she desisted.”

 

 

“She threatened a federal agent?” Kelly blinked.

 

 

“Yeah, apparently it’s required behavior at Lord General.” Alex carefully put her coffee down before punching the door. “Damn it! What time did she leave?”

 

 

Sam checked the log. “Seven minutes after eight.”

 

 

Lena checked her watch. “She should be back in the next ten or fifteen minutes,” she assured her.

 

 

“She’s not usually out more than two and a half hours unless she’s actively training for something specific.”

 

 

But they weren’t sleeping together. Right.

 

 

“What the hell was she thinking? There are people trying to kill her! That’s why she’s here instead of at home in her own damn bed!” Alex started pacing. “All right, we’re going to need three cars, we’re going to need the locals –

 

 

“ Alex, don’t you think you’re overreacting?” Sam wanted to know. “No one knows she’s here. Outside the field office and her own office no one knows you’re related, there’s no reason for anyone to suspect she’d be with you or anywhere near your house.” She turned to Lena. “I’ll trade you the ‘Arts and Culture’ section for the ‘Science’ section if you’re done.”

 

 

Lena met her eyes and waited a moment, just long enough for a little bit of pink to form above the former lawyer’s neck.

 

 

“Sure thing, Samuel .” They exchanged sections of newsprint. “Hey, does that log of yours say how much sleep Supergirl got?”

 

 

Sam checked. “Says she started doing calisthenics at about seven thirty.”

 

 

“Seven hours, not long enough to get her guns back.” Alex shook her head and resumed pacing.

 

 

“It’s better than she’s been all week,” Lena argued.

 

 

The door opened and in walked a sweat-soaked Kara. The woman looked like she had just climbed out of the community pool. Her tee shirt and shorts absolutely clung to her.

 

 

“What?” she asked when she saw Alex, Kelly and Sam staring at her.

 

 

“What? What?” Alex raged. “What gave you the idea that running by yourself in a strange part of town when a bunch of organized criminals are trying to kill you is a good idea?”

 

 

Kara shrugged. “Lena was safe, right?”

 

 

“I do my exercise in the gym, Luv. And never before noon.” Lena speared a little more fruit.

 

 

“Lena’s not their primary target, Kara. You are.” Alex stepped into her sister’s space now, inhaling the stink of her sweat. “The whole point of uprooting you and having you sleep on a couch instead of where you’re more comfortable and where you feel more safe is because they want to kill you, not her. So that means that you can’t be running around showing yourself and revealing your location. I know you’re not an idiot, Kar, because you scored a free ride to Stanford and you graduated early and all that but goddamn –“

 

 

Kara held up a hand and gently, gently eased Alex back. “Got a lot of leads overnight did you?” Her words stabbed. Her tone, though, soothed. Her eyes – there was no way to know what was going on there.

 

 

“Look, Alex, they’re a problem. But I’m only here because they’re targeting Lena now, okay? I’m not stopping everything that I do because of them, and if they get me because I run or if they get me because I keep working then they do. The worst they can do is kill me.” Her voice was calm, soft. Alex couldn’t help but hear Kelly’s intake of breath at her last sentence, but Kara didn’t really even seem to notice.

 

 

“It’s nice that you want me to stay here and it’s nice that you want to try to protect me. Thank you. But I’m not giving up my life. I can either do the things that are important to me from here and you can keep an eye on me here, or I can do the things that are important to me from home and you can keep an eye on Lena from here. Dealer’s choice.” Their eyes stayed locked for a full minute.

 

Alex wanted to reach out and shake her sister, but there was nothing she could latch onto. Nothing physical – there was nothing soft about her – and nothing in her argument. She had to look away.

 

 

“Go shower,” Alex said instead. “You stink.”

 

 

“Natural by-product of running, Alex.” A good half of the tension went out of the room.

 

 

“You should try it sometime, especially judging by the contents of your refrigerator.” Kara' head was already in her suitcase, rummaging for clothing.

 

 

“I run,” she countered. “If someone’s chasing me.”

 

 

After Kara showered she re-joined them in the kitchen. Alex tried to foist some breakfast on her but she was only willing to accept coffee. “So Kar,” Alex began. “You want to elaborate on what you told me yesterday in the car?”

 

 

“We talked about a lot of things in the car, Alex.” The left side of Kara’s face wrinkled in confusion. “What’s got you worked up?”

 

 

“The Russians. You said you’ve had Russian problems before.”

 

 

Kara sighed. “Right. You probably don’t want to know this.”

 

 

“I think she does want to know this, Kara,” Sam intoned drily. “She is the one asking. Apparently for the second time.”

 

 

“And I’m not driving the car either. So what’s so earth-shattering that it needed to be saved until the plane came to a complete stop and the trays were in an upright and locked position?”

 

 

Kara sighed again and her shoulders slumped for a moment. “All right. Did you ever wonder why we never met anyone from the Rose side of the family, Alex?”

 

 

“They all died, Kara.” Alex frowned. “Come on, you remember this fight. You were about ten, you waited until she got good and drunk and you asked her that same goddamn question. ‘Mom, why don’t we have any grandparents from Dad's side?’” Kid had never stopped asking questions, although questions about Dad had never really been welcomed by either of the men who knew him.

 

 

“Maybe you should have a chat with your boss. Dad’s father wasn’t dead.”

 

 

“Come on, Kara. He’s been a doornail for decades.”

 

 

“No. I’ve met him.” She stood up again, sipped from her coffee. “Danvers wasn’t Dad’s actual name. It was an assumed name. Dad’s family was deeply involved in the Russian mob and he turned. He ratted and he was in witness protection. he was killed because his family found him.”

 

 

Alex’s mouth dried. Her knees trembled, rebelled against her weight. Kelly managed to get a chair under her. “How could you possibly – no. You’re lying.”
