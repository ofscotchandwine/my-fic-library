    Yelena took them out of the city in her car and drove silently —yes, silently. Even Kate somehow at a loss of words— to one of her favorite places. It was one of the tallest hills, close to the farm. Maybe ten minutes by car. The view duding the day was absolutely amazing, but at night it was otherworldly. Far enough from the light pollution that they could actually see the stars in the sky.

    Yelena took a comforter from the trunk and draped it around both of their shoulders when they sat on top of the car’s roof. Close to each other. Close to the stars.

    And they talked. They talked so much, they even opened up a little about themselves.

    Kate told Yelena things the blonde already knew, her father’s death, her mother training her to be a perfect little superstar. She told her things Yelena didn’t know about, she liked to sing, she was terrible at drawing, she felt like the worst person that ever existed because she betrayed her mother on Christmas Eve, she could lose everything she ever had —her money, her company, her mother— in the span of a few days because she had to face court hearings and act as a witness.

    Yelena, in turn, told her briefly about Ohio, how her family was her family in everything that truly mattered but blood. She told her about her mom and her dad and…Natasha. She told her about the Red Room. Not much. About the chemical subjugation and the widows around the globe that were still, to this day, trapped in that hell.

    “The farm is one of the safehouses for the ones that received the antidote while they try to get back on their feet and figure out their lives” she explained. Her thumb drawing little circles in the back of Kate’s hand.

    “Every single freed widow that owns a property turns it into a Widow Shelter, for anyone that needs it because of a mission or just needs a roof over their heads. Antonia —that’s Dreykov’s daughter, long story— has access to his father’s account and properties, she’s splitting the money to all the newly liberated widows to manage a fresh start, any place of residence that ever belonged to him is ours as well”

    Kate was looking directly into her eyes, her chin resting against Yelena’s shoulder and her warm breath tickling her cheek.

    “And you started all of this?”

    Yelena frowned.

    “No, I didn’t. Oksana did, she was the one that created the antidote.”

    “But you were the one that reunited your family to bring the Red Room down!” she rebuked and Yelena rolled her eyes. “And you are still helping all those women around the globe….you know?” Yelena arched an eyebrow playfully, inquisitively. “For someone that hates the avengers so much…you are pretty heroic yourself”.

    She let out a real scoff at that one.

    “Yeah. Sure”

    Kate glared at her playfully, poking her side to make a point.

    “I’m serious, Tsarina! Y—you are amazing! The first thing you did when you got your freedom and your body back was doing everything you could to help other people! And you go around saving all these women, giving them all a real chance at life…you are better than a hero! You are their hope! Yelena, if hope was a tangible thing amongst us….i—it’ll be you”.

    Kate had this way of looking at people, twinkling wide eyes, absolutely amazed, as if whoever was on the receiving end was the single greatest thing that walked this Earth. Yelena found it hard to hold such a gaze, that maybe she would extinguish that light by staring back with her own haunted ones. Hope? Her? Not in a hundred lifetimes…if someone should be anyone’s hope, then it certainly wasn’t Yelena. Hope was….wait. Thing…hope…

    “The thing with feathers” she muttered with furrowed brows. Kate tilted her head in confusion.

    “Come again?”

    Yelena wasn’t really listening anymore, her mind taking her back to memories she didn’t even remember she had.

    “Hope is the thing with feathers.” she repeated, more to herself than anything. She shook her head under Kate’s inquisitive look. “It’s from a poem my mother used to read when I was little. You should know it, Kate Bishop, it’s from that American poet….Emily What’s-Her- Face”

    Kate arched an eyebrow, still not getting Yelena’s point.

    “Dickinson, yes, I studied about her in class. I didn’t take know your mother was into American poetry”

    “Oh no” Yelena let out a low chuckle. “She hated every second of it.” Un understatement. Every inch of her mother’s body breathed science and facts. Poetry was something she couldn’t understand to save her life. “When I was 2 o 3, I kept waking up in the middle of the night —not because of anything…I just wasn’t tired— so my Mama tried everything in her control to get me back to sleep, reading poetry….didn’t work at all. I would get transfixed by it every time. Which meant I started begging her to read it to me all the time”

    And she wanted her mother to read to her, specifically. Because she supposed she liked to torture her.

    “And you remember all of those poems?” Kate was staring at her surprised.

    “Not at all! I just—I suppose that one in particular got stuck in my head. But, you know, it talks about hope being something with feathers.” She grinned playfully at her. “And between you and me, there is only one of us whose name comes from a thing with feathers.” her smirk only grew once she saw the archer roll her eyes. “So Hawkeye…” her eyes softened. “Kate Bishop, you are the hope. Emily Dickinson says so!”

    Kate’s face had gotten impossibly closer to hers, their noses were barely touching now.

    “And what else does she say?” Kate’s voice was barely audible. Yelena’s gaze turned from her lips to her eyes once more.

    “What? You want me to recite it now?”

    That question was made to be mocking, playful.

    “Ohh, I do. I really do” Kate’s response, though, was absolutely firm. She cleared her throat awkwardly.

    “Well, I don’t know it by heart, but I think it started like this:

    Hope is the thing with feathers That perches on the soul-

    And—”

    Kate’s lips against hers made Yelena Belova forget about everything she’s ever known in her life. Her mind was only available for the fireworks she was feeling at the moment. And for the glorious, splendid, magnificent presence that was Kate Bishop.
