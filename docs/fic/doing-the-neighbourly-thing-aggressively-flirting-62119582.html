doing the neighbourly thing (aggressively flirting)

Lena had lived in the same building for three years. Though lived may have been too strong a word. She slept there and occasionally ate meals there and collected some of her less important mail. She mainly lived in her office, surrounded by piles of paperwork and half-designed prototypes she drew up in any spare moment she had.

Lena had lived in the same building for three years and didn’t know a single thing about anyone else who lived there. She couldn’t tell you if the same people lived there now that did when she first arrived. She couldn’t tell you age, gender, job. She couldn’t tell you if someone in the building was killing and dismembering people – not even the one beside her through their thankfully thick walls.

Which is why, when she returns to her apartment and finds a container on her doormat, she probably shouldn’t pick it up. She does. It’s also why, when she opens said container and finds an admittedly delicious smelling section of banana bread, she shouldn’t eat it. She does.

She figures she lives in a heavily secured building and, frankly, she’s too tired to care if she’ll die from taking a bite. Especially when the first bite is so incredible. It could actually be the best thing she’s ever eaten. Way better than the pretentious spread her board put on for meetings.

Honestly, she didn’t think people actually made banana bread. She figured everyone just looked at their gradually decaying bananas, thought they could make something with them, and then promptly forgot about it until the bananas were way past saving and threw them in the bin. Maybe that was just her.

Who was she to question the process that led to perfection?

She’s still half asleep and dreaming about the perfection of the crumb when she walks to open her door in the morning, called by a knocking. Another thing Lena hadn’t experienced in three years of living here – someone at her door.

Something Lena doesn’t think she’s experienced in her life at all? Opening her door to someone who looked like a hot librarian discovered the wonders of weightlifting. Charming, black frames. A messy bun. A soft looking cardigan that was stretching against the harsh contours of her frame – broad shoulders, thick arms.

“Can I help you?” Please say yes. Please say yes.

“I just came by to see how you liked my baking,” she says with a smile. It makes her even nicer to look at. All shining eyes and pearly teeth.

“It was the best thing I’ve eaten in a long time.” The woman’s smile widens impossibly at the praise. “Try not to let it get to your head.”

“Wouldn’t dream of it,” she assures, hands raised. “I’m Kara, by the way. I moved into 4A about a week ago. I’ve been trying to say hello to people in the building.” She leans in closer to whisper, “4B told me I was wasting my time at your door. Said she didn’t even know your name.”

“I didn’t realise 4B spent so much time thinking about me,” Lena chuckles. “Well, it’s Lena. But don’t tell 4B because I think I might enjoy making her endure the mystery.”

“Your secret is safe with me-“ she raises her fingers in some kind of scout’s honour- “but don’t be surprised when she suddenly starts stealing your mail to find out more about you.”

“I hope she takes some of the junk off my hands in the meantime.”

“Despite not knowing your name, she seemed very willing to take anything from you that you offered. She was quite… forthcoming with her opinion.” Well, that was flattering. She couldn’t say she was all that bothered about what 4B was up to when 4A was standing at her door. She wasn’t really bothered about anything else at all.

Lena cocks a brow. “And how accurate would you say she was?”

Kara’s eyes stroll leisurely across her face. “I think she undersold you.”

“Well, now I’m very glad that I washed your container for you instead of handing it back dirty. Give me a second and I’ll get it for you.” Lena doesn’t need to make her fingers graze Kara’s when she hands it back. She does it anyway. Completely worth the obviously telling move for the way Kara’s gaze locks onto the action. The quiet smile she tries to keep to herself before meeting Lena’s eyes again.

“Thank you, Lena. I’ll see you around?” Lena nods.

She means it.

Only took three years but she might finally start spending time in this building.

Lena’s coffee machine dies not with a bang but a whimper.

She wasn’t known for being melodramatic, but it wasn’t dramatic at all to say it was the worst moment of her life. She wasn’t so much a coffee snob as she was a caffeine addict. She needed it to make it through her long days and longer nights. She needed that first hot, fresh waft of steam to clear out the tiredness. The first scorching sip pouring down her throat to make her feel somewhat alive.

But her coffee machine was dead and so she was half-dead too as she wandered the hallway and slipped into the elevator, hoping she’d been quick enough to grab a coffee on her way to the office (or at least drink one that she begged her assistant to get before back-to-back meetings).

She’s halfway through typing a plea on her phone when a hand darts through the doors to stop them closing. She almost complains about the extra seconds it’s adding to her journey until she takes it in. The tremendous hand attached to an even more tremendous body with a perfectly tremendous face.

A tremendous face blossoming into a smile. “Lena, hi.”

“Morning, Kara.” She really needed that coffee. It might have fortified her enough to not sound so wistful and breathless when she said that. Might have. She really was quite pretty. Lena hadn’t seen something so pretty in a long time.

“You look…” Kara trails off.

Lena watches her bite her lip and carries on, “I look…” 

“I was going to say tired but then I remembered how much I hate when people say that to me. So, I tried to find another word but-“

“But I look tired,” Lena completes. Offering Kara her most unoffended smile. “I feel tired. Consequences of late meetings followed by early meetings and a complete lack of coffee because my machine broke.”

Kara winces. “No coffee? Ouch.”

“Yes, ouch.”

“Do you have a machine in your place of work?” Kara asks earnestly. Lena, just as earnestly, laughs in her face.

“My place of work? That sounds so formal.”

“Oh, sorry, do you not work in a formal setting?” Kara jokes, then shrugs with an easy movement. “I have no idea what you do so it was the best phrasing I had.”

“I own a medical tech company,” she supplies simply. No need to brag more than that. She thinks that was probably enough to paint her in a good light. It also kept it vague enough that Kara couldn’t immediately Google it and read weird rumours about her that would kill this before it even began. The tabloids weren’t known for being kind.

“That’s very formal. I think you owe me an apology.”

“My sincerest apologies then, Miss…”

“Danvers. And you, Miss…”

“Dr. Luthor actually.” There went the hope of not being Googled. Maybe there was a slight need to brag. The need being to see Kara’s brows raise in appreciation.

“Formal and fancy.” Kara gestures for her to leave the elevator first. “Well have a good day, Dr. Luthor. I hope it improves from here.” Lena nods her thanks before getting in her town car, watching Kara walk in the opposite direction.

Her day does get a little better when she returns to find a new coffee machine on her doorstep with a smiley face post-it. Lena doesn’t even go inside her own apartment, just picks it up and walks down the hallway to 4A.

She’s barely knocked once before the door swings open.

“Hello again,” she greets, looking down to Lena’s hands with a nervous smile. “I see you got my gift.” Gift? A gift was a slice of banana bread. A gift was a cup of coffee. A single cup. Not the four-hundred-dollar ability to make one on command.

“I did but you can’t-“ Lena huffs, putting the box down to rest her hands on her hips. She somehow doesn’t believe the power stance is going to work as well here as it did earlier in the day. “Kara this is expensive. You barely know me.”

“I actually got it at a heavily discounted price because I helped the store owner out a few months back if that makes you feel better about it.” She’s good. She’s really good. Suave and cute and hot as she crosses her arms and leans against the door jam.

Lena sighs, dropping her hands back to her rest at her sides. “Still-“

“Look, you needed coffee. I happened past a store that would help make that happen and I can’t return it because it would make Mr. Perez sad. Do you want to make Mr. Perez sad, Lena?” Suave and cute and hot and absolutely devious. How was she supposed to proactively argue against that?

“Thank you. Let me repay you somehow.”

“You could make me a cup in the morning?” Kara offers, pointing down to the box at their feet. “That machine is way nicer than mine.”

“I’ll meet you in the elevator then.”

“Perfect. Sweet dreams, Lena.”

“Good night.”

The coffee is amazing.

Kara’s smile as she sips her own is even better.

Lena’s actually cooking herself a meal for once when there’s a knock on her door. She smiles to herself as she switches off the stove. Stifles the smile as she smooths out her clothing to go to the door since there was only one person it could realistically be. The one person she’d been wanting to see all day.

Kara looks beautiful on the other side of her doorway. Even though her hair is falling from its sharp bun, and she’s definitely got coloured pen on her chin.

Lena would have commented on it, were it not for the fact that she was also holding hands with a small child. Her small child? They didn’t look the same. They didn’t look the same at all. Especially since Kara has a small smile on her face and the girl looks like she’s seconds away from crying. Violently crying. Snot coming from her nose crying. She actually looks like she was only taking a small pause from doing exactly that.

Instead, she softens her voice. “Hi, Kara. Hi, Kara’s small friend.”

“This is my niece.” Oh, thank God. “Say hi, Esme.”

“Hi,” she warbles, managing to offer a weak wave. Lena’s heart melts a little bit.

“So, Esme’s best friend here-“ the little girl lifts the stuffed bear in her hands up towards Lena at the words- “met a little bit of an accident and has lost his arm.” It’s Kara who lifts that piece up for her to see. “And Esme overheard me talking the other day about you being a doctor to my sister and has now insisted that if anyone can save his life, it’s you.” Kara grimaces. Lena’s not entirely sure at what part.

The part where they were distracting her for a bear or the part where she was apparently talking about Lena to other people. Good or bad was Lena’s first thought. Then she was just sort of giddy she was thinking about her when she wasn’t there in the first place. She’d take it either way – she could turn bad to good. Wouldn’t be the first time she’d achieved a miracle.

“I’m not actually that kind of doctor.” The little girl lets loose a sob. Lena takes pity. “I do know bear medicine though so I can definitely help.” She had a sewing kit, a steady hand and a need to not having a crying child on her conscience.

“You sure?” Kara asks, the apology still written on her face.

“Come on, my clinic is this way.” The clinic being her living room. If you could even call it that. There was a sofa and a coffee table and a nice rug. There was also her laptop, way too many files and documents and an air of never having been enjoyed for comfort. Maybe it was a little bit more like a clinic.

Lena pushes the files to the side and offers a seat to her company. Kara looks around curiously as Esme’s eyes stay locked on her ‘ill friend’. “What’s our patient’s name?”

“Hugsy,” a small voice informs, noticeably less tear-sodden now that she’d secured, and apparently approved of, her friend’s new doctor.

“May I?” Lena reaches towards the bear. Smiles softly when it’s handed over with extreme care. “I’ll be gentle, I promise.” She’s done within ten minutes. It’s a pretty good job if she says so herself. You can barely see the stitching where she spent a full minute finding the perfect shade of thread (if she was going to be a bear surgeon, she was going to do it right).

She’s not entirely sure which one of the girl’s watching her she’s putting on the show for when she makeshifts him a sling from bandages and writes out a fake prescription for hugs and kisses. She’ll pretend it’s for Esme. It’s for sure for the grin Kara sends her way.

“That’s not- I don’t-“ Kara fumbles, eyes avoiding landing on anything until she decides Esme is her safest bet. Maybe it was good things then. “We should leave Dr. Lena to her evening, Esme. I’m sure she has plenty of other patients to see.”

“I actually just clocked off. I was making dinner if you two would like some? It’s just pasta but I never get the portions right so I imagine I could feed an army. Or, say, two extra girls and a bear."

“I love pasta,” Esme says like she’s the deciding factor here. Lena supposes she must be because Kara watches her fondly before nodding her consent.

It’s nice having people around. It’s funny watching two messy eaters get sauce on their face. It’s satisfying getting praise for the taste and getting to watch Kara be the fun aunt. Getting to see her be goofy and soft. It endears her to Lena even more. It makes that feeling in her chest swell and swell and swell until she starts to feel like she might just float away.

She hopes it doesn’t pop.

She’s starting to think she wouldn’t survive the fall.

Darkness.

A sudden, very annoying darkness.

Lena turns away from her presentation preparation to flick at her light switches. Nothing. Absolutely nothing. Great.

She looks out the window to find the rest of the block illuminated like nothing was wrong. Pokes her head into the corridor to find the hallway lights just fine too. Just her then. Excellent. Just her luck.

She drops her head to her hands as she stares at her laptop – way less than optimally charged and only showing a half-finished presentation. One that realistically should have been finished two days ago. One that she absolutely needed tomorrow morning.

Normally she’d poke around the wiring herself, but she was tired, and the first rule of touching electrics was to not do it when sleep addled. Well, that, and not putting them in water she supposed.

Instead, she sends a text to Jess to call an electrician in the morning, picks up her laptop and steps out of her apartment before she can second guess her next move.

Honestly she probably could have worked in the dark. Her laptop screen blared bright enough. She had worked under worse time crunches than the dwindling battery of her computer. But maybe she was also using it as an excuse because she was semi-infatuated with her neighbour. Sue her. She had an amazing legal team.

I know it’s sort of late and this is an odd request, but I really need to finish something-“ she lifts up her laptop as evidence- “and I think sitting in the dark might just send me to sleep so could I come inside until I’m done? It’ll be an hour. Tops.”

“I’m an investigate journalist.”

“A reporter?” Lena forces a grimace onto her face, keeps the Kara induced twinkle in her eyes. “I didn’t realise I was crossing enemy lines here.”

“Hey, I’m not the bad guy.”

“I’ll trust you. For now. Mostly because I need you.” If questioned in court, Lena would blame the sultry drop on the time, the lighting, the restless day. She doesn’t mean for the words to drip, soaked in honey, from her tongue. They do regardless. Because maybe she really would have fallen asleep without her lights. Or maybe she just couldn’t fight her subconscious want that hard.

I’m watching Real Housewives of National City at the same time to make myself feel slightly less depressed about my subject.” Lena nods and follows, popping the screen back up of her laptop and resisting the urge to take a peek at Kara’s.

Instead, she asks, “Is your work often depressing?”

“I specialise in serious crime so, yes. It’s pretty much all doom and gloom. I do get the joy of meeting people who my work helped sometimes though so that helps. It’s all about them at the end of the day – seeing them breathe a little easier.”

That made sense. The way she seemed to care too much about people she barely knew. The fire in her eyes. The sense of justice Lena witnessed the other day when she argued with a man on their front stoop when he littered. “Fine. You’re not the bad guy.”

“I got that approval pretty quickly.” Kara looks smug. It should be unattractive. It is most definitely not.

“It doesn’t hurt that you’re nice to look at,” Lena’s mouth works quicker than her brain. Her brain promptly snaps her jaw shut after and starts to panic. “Ignore that. I’m just a little work addled and sleep deprived.”

Lena rolls her eyes. “Just click play on your stupid show.”

Kara gasps, hand pressed to her chest. “Wow, Lena. Maybe you’re the bad guy. It absolutely is not stupid.” Kara continues her rant to explain the women and their pasts and what the current drama they’re experiencing is. She may have underestimated what she was going to feel for her hot neighbour.

On account of the fact that Kara Danvers was sweaty and muscular and panting, and she was… well she was gay.

She doesn’t need it in the end.

A little less enamoured by her inability to draw more than a stick man.

Back to this shared armchair with Kara where her arm rests behind Lena’s back and she whispers in her ear to fill in the blanks of inside jokes. Back to this moment of feeling a part of something.

A part of a group.

And a pair of two within that.

First her coffee machine.
