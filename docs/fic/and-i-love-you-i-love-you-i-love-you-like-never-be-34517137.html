She doesn’t know how or when it happens, but at some point, Esme seems to take some sort of special liking to Lena.

Kara’s not surprised, per se -- it makes a lot of sense, actually. When she herself had first met Esme, she’d been so excited at the idea of bonding with her new niece, of passing down all of the experiences and wisdom she’d gained when she was in that situation, that she’d completely forgotten the fact that Esme’s situation bore some pretty big similarities to Lena’s, too.

Sure, when Lena had been adopted, it had been by a family of evil vultures, not two women and their whole extended group of friends and family just trying to give her the best life possible, but she too knows what it’s like to lose your parents at a young age, to feel like you’re completely alone in the world, surrounded only by strangers who have no clue what to do with you.

If anything, the more she thinks about it, the more she realises Lena might be able to relate more to Esme than she can. Sure, Kara can relate painfully to the struggle of not knowing how to control your powers on Earth, but now, Lena can too -- and not only that, but Lena can relate now.

Sometimes, the memories of her first days on Earth are so overwhelming that Kara could swear they’re real, that she’s there again -- she blinks, and all of a sudden she’s thirteen years old and in Midvale, cowering on the floor after having broken her third bed in as many days because she was spooked by the sound of someone slamming a door five houses down.

Those moments have been happening more and more recently, especially since coming back from the Phantom Zone, certainly more than they ever have since her teenage years, but the thing is, even then, they’re still few and far between. For Lena, though, she’s going through it all now at the first time, just like Esme… it’s an experience that, while Kara can relate to it, she can’t truly replicate the feeling of, wouldn’t even want to if she could.

And so, a bond forms. Alex and Kelly are reluctant to send Esme to school yet, wary of putting too much pressure on her when she’s already struggling so much to settle, so she spends the majority of her time in the Tower while they work, usually glued to Lena’s side.

While Lena pores over her mother’s books, Esme sits with her nose in one of the many books they’d all bought for her. While Lena practises her spells, Esme sits and watches appraisingly, offering occasional commentary on how well it had gone that time. While Lena takes her lunch breaks, Esme sits beside her eating a mini-portion of whatever it is she’s got that day, devouring kale salads and turkey wraps with more enthusiasm than any five-year-old Kara’s ever seen.

In essence, anywhere Lena goes, Esme goes with her.

And it. is. adorable.

Kara can’t get enough of it, cooing and aww ing over them every time she drops down into the Tower and finds them sitting together, usually in silence, simply existing in each other’s company. She snaps probably a million photos of them, unashamedly making one her lockscreen, much to Lena’s chagrin, who rolls her eyes in response when she first sees it, although she definitely looks secretly pleased.

“You’re just both so cute,” Kara sighs one day, resting her chin on her hand and staring dreamily across the table over to where Lena and Esme are sat huddled over a colouring book, both scribbling away meticulously at a drawing of two cats with witches hats on.

Lena blushes furiously, face and neck both going an adorable shade of pink that just matches the ribbon in Esme’s hair; Esme, on the other hand, just rolls her eyes, used to hearing Kara exhale this at least twice a day, now bored of the attention.

“Don’t you have some poor, unsuspecting humans to save, Supergirl?” Lena snarks, but there’s no bite to it, only an endearing mixture of embarrassment at the attention and fondness for Kara.

“Nope,” she says with a grin. “Today, I’m on lunch duty.”

She pulls the Big Belly Burger bag out from where she’d been hiding it behind her back, smile stretching even wider when she sees the way Esme’s face lights up. Lena narrows her eyes at her, as if trying to make a silent comment about the fact that this is the third time that week that Kara’s brought them takeout for lunch, but she says nothing, and silently accepts the burger and fries Kara hands over to her; selfishly, Lena’s newfound refusal to make any negative comments on the morality or health factor of Kara’s favourite foods is one of her favourite things about Esme’s appearance in their life, because it means Kara has an excuse to shower them in yummy, greasy food every week.

“Thank you, Kara!” Esme says, smiling like she’s won the lottery, as she grabs at the box of chicken nuggets Kara hands her, munching away at them happily.

“Of course, Es! I have to keep my two favourite girls fed, don’t I?”

At this point, Kara can’t deny that she’s saying this stuff just to get a blush out of Lena. Oh well, who can blame her when she looks that cute?

“Look,” Esme says, mouth still full; Kara doesn’t blink, too used to partaking in the same bad habit, but she can barely bite back a smile at Lena’s barely concealed wince at the sight. Esme pushes the colouring book across the table so that Kara can see it properly. “Do you like our cats?”

Kara squints suspiciously down at the page. “Wait, you guys did these? Are you sure?”

Flummoxed, Esme frowns. “What do you mean?”

“I could have sworn these were Monets?”

Clearly pleased, Esme snorts, grin on her face. “Really?”

“Yeah! You’re telling me you guys managed to make something this awesome, not a professional artist?”

“It was really us, Kara! Tell her, Lena!”

When Kara looks over at Lena, the raw adoration displayed on her face is so strong that it knocks the breath out of Kara’s lungs for a second.

“It was really us,” Lena says, voice so soft. “Although I see where you’re mistaken -- it is pretty amazing. Especially this cat,” she says, pointing at the little ginger cat Esme’s been colouring in.

Esme beams, puffing her chest out with pride. “It is kind of good.”

“Good? It’s great! I want ten of them right now, to hang up in my apartment!” Kara exclaims, which has Esme bursting into a fit of giggles, squirming happily in her seat.

They eat the rest of their lunch together, chatting away happily as Esme shows Kara all the other colourings her and Lena have done, and Kara regales her with exaggeratedly heroic stories of her day, until Kara is pulled away twenty minutes later by J’onn informing her of some issue or other through her earpiece. Esme looks like she wants to complain, ask Kara to stay, but one comment from Lena about how Kara has to go in case she might have to help another little girl just like Esme, and she’s nodding determinedly, letting Kara go without another complaint.

Over the next few months as Esme settles in more, she starts making less and less daytime appearances at the Tower, all the way up until she’s back doing full time schooling. This does nothing to dampen her burgeoning obsession, however — every Wednesday night finds her curled into Lena’s side on the couch, a new and seemingly permanent addition to their previous two-man game night team.

Kara can’t complain, however, because where Lena lacks proficiency in -- if she’s being brutally honest -- most board games, Esme seems to excel, somehow managing to pick them all up instantly despite having had no concept of them before. They win almost every game they play, to the point where their friends start to teasingly accuse them of cheating or somehow rigging it, to which Esme always delights in denying, declaring that it’s not her fault she’s so clever!

In the months after Esme first comes to live with Alex and Kelly, they as a group form such a perfect little harmony that Kara wakes up every day feeling luckier than she has in years to get to live the life she does, surrounded by the people who have become her family. They spend most days together, whether it be cramped together in Kara’s tiny apartment, or all spread throughout Alex and Kelly’s; she hardly spends more than a few hours alone these days, and where she might have found it exhausting at one point, she thrives off it now.

It’s been months now since they’d pulled her from the Phantom Zone, and while she knows logically that she’s safe now, secure at home and at no risk of being stuck there again, that knowledge does nothing to stop the nightmares or flashbacks that plague her at the most random and inconvenient of times, seemingly popping up out of nowhere just to remind her that she’ll never truly be free of the memories.

This, though, this new sense of security and warmth they’ve developed since welcoming Esme into the family -- it feels healing , more so than any other remedy she’s tried. Watching Alex live out her dream of becoming a parent, finally having everything slot into place with the perfect woman and the perfect child; witnessing the bloom of Brainy and Nia’s relationship as they get back to where they had been before; getting to spend almost every waking minute with Lena, basking in the mundanities of life together, reveling in the simplest of things, like making dinner together and going shopping for new cosy winter pyjamas… all of it feels ten times more effective than the therapy sessions Kelly’s had her trying out, where she can’t bring herself to open up about anything without feeling an overwhelming sense of dread at just trying to get the words out.

It’s natural, of course, that it’s on one of these game nights that Esme makes one little innocent comment which brings their newfound harmony tumbling down.

“Did you know it’s my mommies’ anni… anni… ann’versary today?” she asks, reaching across the coffee table where Kelly is currently setting the Monopoly board up to grab the Scottie dog, the cat and the penguin, silently handing the cat to Lena and the penguin to Kara, keeping the dog for herself.

Kara can’t help but beam at the matching looks on Kelly and Alex’s faces at the moniker; it’s a new development, and they’ve been trying their hardest not to make a big deal out of it, lest they scare Esme off, but their delight at the term could probably be seen from space.

“I did!”

“That’s when it’s been a year since you’ve been together, by the way. Or two or three, or other numbers. N’case you didn’t know.”

Lena’s barely managing to bite back her laugh. “Thank you, Es,” Kara replies, elbowing Lena in the ribs playfully.

And then, without even looking up from the figurines in her hand, which she’s reclaimed from Kara and Lena in favour of engaging them in some kind of roleplay, Esme asks, “When’s your ann’versary?”

Kara’s brow furrows. “Whose?”

Esme sighs, like Kara is being interminably stupid right now. “You and Lena, duh.”

All of a sudden, the room goes deathly silent. Nobody says anything, nobody moves, it feels like nobody breathes. Kara certainly can’t, and if how hard Lena’s heart is thudding against her chest right now is any indication, she’s pretty sure she won’t be either in a second.

Esme finally looks up, having sensed a shift in tone, and looks up at them with wide eyes. “What?”

Kelly’s the first one to speak. “Uhh, sweetie… why don’t we go get a snack from the kitchen?” she attempts to redirect, but Esme’s having none of it.

“What?” she asks. “No, I wanna know.”

Kelly goes to say something else, but Kara cuts in, voice strangled as she attempts to get the words out. “Um, Es, Lena and I aren’t together.”

Poor Esme has never looked more confused in her life.

“What are you talking about?” she asks. “Yes you are!”

Kara honestly thinks a return trip to the Phantom Zone might be preferable to having this conversation. Or death.

“No… no, we’re just best friends.”

Esme’s brow furrows. Seemingly baffled, she scrunches her face up in disbelief. “But… but that makes no sense! You act just like my mommies. I don’t believe you.”

“Esme…” Alex interjects softly, looking at Kara with a mix of pity and apology. “Maybe we should leave this for now, okay? We can talk about it later.”

“I want to talk about it now!” she huffs, indignant.

All of a sudden, before Kara can even really process what’s happening, Lena’s standing up on shaky legs, walking backwards away from the coffee table, eyes wide and holding an almost wild look to them.

“Lena…” she says, but stops when Lena shakes her head, cutting her off.

“I’m sorry, I can’t do this right now,” she says, turning sharply on her heel and stumbling out of the apartment.

She looks up to find all of their friends staring at her, obviously waiting for her to follow after her; she gets to her feet without saying anything else, running out the door after Lena wordlessly.

Kara finds her standing just outside the building, looking smaller than ever and absolutely crushed; she’s got her back pressed against the wall and is rubbing fiercely at her eyes with the heel of her hand, as if trying to either forcibly stop herself from crying or punish herself for letting the tears fall in the first place.

“Lena?” she says, voice gentle, too afraid to speak any louder in fear she’ll somehow make things worse.

Lena looks over to her then, and she just looks absolutely crushed. The sight of Lena so distraught makes Kara feel like her heart’s broken in two, and all she wants to do is go over and comfort her, take her into her arms and get to the root of what’s upset her so much, but instead she remains where she is, rooted to the spot.

“I’m sorry,” Lena says, voice watery. “I didn’t mean to make a scene, walking out like that, I just… I couldn’t, I—”

“Hey, it’s okay. Don’t worry about that, nobody else is. We’re just worried about you.”

“I’m sorry,” she repeats again.

Kara feels the need to hold her like a physical ache. “Please stop apologising, Lena, you haven’t done anything wrong.”

“I’m so embarrassed. I shouldn’t have left so quickly -- I mean, she was only asking a question, and God, she’s five years old, obviously she didn’t know…”

“That’s what’s got you so upset?” Kara asks, inching closer to her. “What Esme said? About… about me and you?”

Lena snorts, and gives a self-deprecating roll of her eyes. “Isn’t it obvious?”

And the thing is, Kara wasn’t expecting this to hurt quite so much.

She’d known, obviously, that the chances of Lena feeling the same about her had been slim to none, especially after everything that had happened between them last year, but she really thought that she’d been prepared for what it would feel like to have it confirmed for her. She wasn’t ready to feel it like a stab to the chest, or like Lena had just submerged her in a pool of ice cold water with no warning.

“Oh,” she says involuntarily, voice despondent. She stops moving towards Lena, staying as still as she can.

She knows she’s not doing a good job of masking the pain in her voice, can feel Lena looking at her curiously, but she can’t help it, all of a sudden feeling like she’s been physically gutted at the idea of Lena being so upset over just the mere suggestion of them in a romantic context that it would drive her to tears.

“Sorry,” she says. 

“Why are you-”

She’s just confused right now, but I’ll fix it. I’m sorry she thought that, I… I’ll ask her why she did, and if it was something I’ve been doing, I’ll stop. I don’t want to make you uncomfortable, or-”

She sounds utterly befuddled.

“I mean, yeah…? Why else would you be so upset at the idea?”

Lena lets out a disbelieving little laugh, like Kara’s being utterly ridiculous right now, like she can’t quite wrap her head around what she’s saying. “Kara, I’m upset because… for the past five years, that’s been all I’ve wanted, and I know it’ll never happen, and that’s- that’s fine, I’ve come to terms with it, I know it’s my fault, but. Hearing Esme say that… that this whole time, she’d thought… it was just too much for me, I needed a minute.”

“God, Kara, please can we not talk about this. I know you probably already knew, and think I’m pathetic, but I just-”

“I didn’t,” Kara blurts. “I don’t. Know. I don’t know anything! I had no idea about any of this, Lena, I thought…”

“You thought what?” Lena’s voice cracks, and the need to have her in her arms returns with such a ferocity it almost knocks Kara off balance.

“I thought it was just me,” she says, voice not much louder than a whisper. “This whole time, I-I thought I was the only one who wanted…”

She captures Lena’s wrists in her hands, holding them as gently as she can, moving even closer when she can feel Lena tremble. “I love you. Like, I really, really love you.”

She opens her eyes again, staring up at Kara with all the intensity of someone who thinks she might finally be getting exactly what she wants and needs to make sure it doesn’t slip right through her fingers. “After everything I put you through? I was so scared to say anything in case it ruined our friendship, and I knew that I couldn’t say anything before I told you about me being Supergirl… and then after, when I’d betrayed your trust like that? Cheeks flushed, hair suddenly messy, lips swollen and eyes wide and innocent, like she has no idea the effect she’s having on Kara, Lena nods. “Yes, please.”

“Yes,” Kara says, soothing her with a quick, chaste kiss. “I’ll be right back, okay? They’re probably worried. Or about to come out looking for us. Either way, I should go.”

Lena rolls her eyes, but nods, taking the keys from Kara’s hands and trudging off to the car. “You better be back quickly.”

“As soon as I can, baby, I promise.”

She knows how she must look right now, definitely not like someone who hasn’t just spent the past twenty minutes making out with her best friend, but considering Lena is in the car right now waiting for her to come take her home, she can’t find it in herself to care very much.

“Hey, guys,” she says when she opens the door, barely sticking her head in, too desperate to leave to even cross the threshold of the apartment. By the way everyone is staring at her, they know exactly what she’s just been doing. “Uhh, everything’s fine! I’m just gonna… Lena and I are gonna go back to my place and um. Talk. Yup. Talk. And uhh… we’ll see you tomorrow?”

“I didn’t mean to upset Lena, I promise. Is she mad at me?”

Once again, Kara’s heart breaks, for her niece who reminds her so much of the woman sitting outside waiting for her, of herself at thirteen, too afraid to do or say the wrong thing or upset the wrong person, in case it disrupted the newfound equilibrium of their life.

“Oh, Es, sweetheart, you didn’t upset her. She was just a little turned around already, and what you said made us realise we had to talk about some things. But she definitely doesn’t hate you, silly girl. She loves you!”

Esme’s eyes are wide and unblinking, so purely innocent, when she asks, “Do you promise?”

“I promise. In fact, I think that you might have actually helped us.”

Her face lights up then. “Really?”

“Really!” She moves further into the room then and crouches down so she’s on Esme’s level, leaning forward to whisper conspiratorially in her ear. “And, y’know, I think I might actually have an answer to your question now.”

Her brow furrows. “Question?”

“About our anniversary. I’m pretty sure it’ll be one year today.”

Esme looks so overjoyed then that Kara can’t help but pull her into a tight hug, overwhelmed by love and happiness and everything good, everything she thought not so long ago she might never have again, but now the idea of being without feels so foreign to her.

“Yay! I’m so glad!”

“Me too, Es. But hey, I have to go now, okay? Lena’s waiting for me outside.”

“Okay! Tell her I love her, please.”

“Of course I will,” Kara says.

She presses a kiss to Esme’s cheek and then swiftly super speeding away back to the car, where she does indeed find Lena waiting for her. She can’t help but grin at the sight, of Lena looking so at home in her car, waiting for her, and she allows herself one moment to savour the sight and the feeling of everything finally clicking into place, before finally going to join her.
