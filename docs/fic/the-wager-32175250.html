This is my second piece for the SCRBB2021 and a surprise addition because of a writer needing to drop out. Once I started writing I learned that the idea would have made a better multi-chapter fic than a one shot. Sometimes it be like that, I guess.
The piece is inspired by Darbypea's art from CH1, but I wasn't able to fit that scene into this fic. I'm hoping to do another piece from this universe and include that image specifically.

Anyway, here's a serving of sweet and helpful Kara, touch-starved and lonely Lena, and an Alex in need of some perspective.

(I know nothing about hospitals, hospital directors ((except what I found on Wikipedia)), and other such things. Suspend your disbelief and/or your expertise. It's just a story. ☺)

“First round’s on me, Schott!” Alex calls out over mocking laughter at the fact that James just drew the short straw and has to stitch a laceration on a frat boy’s asscheek. Apparently, it’s best to be sober when attempting to vault over a four foot rusty chain link fence.

“As if the Evil Queen will let you out of here on t-”

Kara turns at the choked-off rejoinder to see Winn cowering slightly under the pointed green glare of the woman everyone in the ER referred to (when she was out of earshot, of course) as Maleficent, Morgana, or a host of other pejoratives. Being a trauma surgeon, she doesn’t directly report to the Director, so she sees little of her on the regular. The ER physicians, nurses, and EMTs complain about her all the time though, and their tendency to nickname her after various fairy tale villains is telling. Kara just tries not to refer to her as anything at all. There’s a little spot in her chest that twists uncomfortably when her friends revert to schoolyard viciousness. 

Dr. Lena Luthor. Daughter of Lionel and Lillian Luthor, top donors with two wings named for them, sister of the disgraced former hospital director, Lex Luthor (currently serving time in prison for insurance fraud,) and youngest director in history of the ER here at National City General Hospital. Kara’s read her published articles; she knows the good doctor is whip-smart and decidedly progressive in her thinking. Her research into the usage of nanobots in medicine is particularly fascinating. Kara thinks the other woman is on the edge of a breakthrough, and Kara is desperate to talk to her about it, but the icy glares, furrowed brows and imperious chin lift form an impenetrable wall with a locked door that Kara is too afraid to approach. She’s seen the Director release the dogs on those that wandered too close, and the tearing of those wanderers, limb from limb, isn’t something she’s keen on happening to her.

“I have no bearing on whether or not Alexandra leaves on time.” The Director’s voice is syrupy sweet, belying the hard emerald of her eyes. Winn ducks and scurries away while Kara cringes at Dr. Luthor’s indirect acknowledgement of their mocking of her and her usage of Alex’s full name. The best way to infuriate her sister is to ignore her title and call her by her full name. Whether or not the woman did it on purpose, Kara isn’t sure, but best clear out either way. Alex’s face is flushed and her jaw is clenched, and Kara decides she’s going to head back to the surgeons’ quarters before the two women square up and thrown down.

“ Alex ,” she tries to emphasize as subtly as possible, “I’ll see you later, ok?”

She realizes the Director knew exactly what she was doing when a slight smirk tugs at the corner of ruby red lips. Her pale eyes glint as she turns on Kara and lifts her hand in a wave that’s impressively dismissive. “Don’t leave on my account, Dr. Danvers.  I’m sure your presence here isn’t at all distracting to anyone, or impacting the care of our patients.”

Kara grits her own teeth and takes a breath. She’s about to call Dr. Luthor out on her passive-aggression when she sees it, a tiny falter, a flash of regret and uncertainty in that beautiful face. It’s gone as soon as it arrived, replaced by the sharp edge of her jaw and lift of one dark eyebrow. ‘Oh there is something there behind the cutting remarks and that slash of blood-red lips,’ she thinks to herself, and takes the high road, giving the Director her sunniest smile and a playful wink as she walks away towards the elevators. She doesn’t stay to see how the other woman reacts, but she has a pretty good idea from the barely audible huff aimed at her back. Eliza would tell her to kill with kindness, a talent that leapt over Alex and settled into Kara as a young girl. As she waits for the elevator, she hums a little tune, smiling a bit at the idea that a little kindness usually goes a long way.

Kara just waves at the bouncer at STAT! before walking past him and the line to the doors; they’re here every night when they’re scheduled for the noon to midnight shift and have immediate entry privileges. The bar is so loud when she pushes through the doors that she checks her watch in confusion. Nope, definitely 01:30, thirty minutes to last call and definitely ear-splitting. Kara stuffs her earbuds into her ears to dampen some of the overwhelming noise -she’s always been sensitive to sensory input- and pushes through the crowd to her friends’ regular table in the back corner. Alex is already there, Kelly perched in her lap and knocking back a bright green shot. There’s a line of them across the table, and Kara’s stomach turns until she sees the two vodka tonics next to “her” empty chair.

“It’s about time, Dr. Danvers!” Alex trills out over the cacophony of James, Winn, and Nia laughing as Kelly shoots another and sputters when Alex jostles her while trying to pull out Kara’s chair. “Don’t tell me Maleficent got her claws in you when that car crash came in.”

Drunk. Alex is absolutely smashed, and Kelly looks to be well on her way. There are 9 more shots on the table, Alex has a finger of scotch in the glass in her hand and another glass on the table with at least two more. Nothing good will come of the look her sister is giving her, sly grin sharp below bleary eyes, so she gulps her drink in three swallows as she settles down in her chair. They give her a hearty cheer and slam back more of the noxious shots before Alex leans toward her conspiratorially.

“So, we’ve decided we’re going to do something about Cruella.” Alex slurs out loudly and Kara chokes on her drink. “A friendly wager just between us, someone will turn out to be a big winner, but in all actuality, we all win in the end.”

“Uh, that doesn’t sound like a very good idea.” Kara pushes the second drink into the middle of the table. Her friends are obviously too far gone to be making judgement calls if they’re plotting murder. “I’m pretty sure if you just keep your head down and do your job, she’ll leave you alone.”

Alex’s drink stops midway to her mouth and she focuses enough to glare at Kara over the lip of the glass. “Don’t defend her. That bitch dressed you down in front of everyone tonight, have you forgotten?!”

“It wasn’t everyone. James, Mike, and Brainy weren’t there. Neither were Sam or J’onn.”

Alex’s eyes roll so hard, Kara’s tempted to check and see if they’ve fallen out under the table. “ Anyway , she called me Alexandra. That’s asking for a fat lip right there, and she does it on purpose ! She knows I hate it.” The second glass of scotch is in her hand now, and she’s using it to punctuate her statements. Kara makes a mental note to get up at 6:00am tomorrow and ensure both Alex and Kelly are miserable by calling them every fifteen minutes. They’re both on-call so they can’t turn off their phones.

“We’re not going to hurt her, god, Kara.” Alex’s eyes are suddenly lucid and intense, “we just want to get that stick out of her ass. A friendly wager, remember? Are you in?”

Kara chews on her lower lip and fiddles with her empty cup. Behind her, the bartender is ringing the bell for last call, and the noise from the bar has died to a low hum with her earbuds in. She wants nothing to do with this so-called wager, but she’s more afraid of what her sister and friends will do without a level-head steering the ship. Since the trauma surgeons aren’t under Lena’s purview, she, Sam, and J’onn don’t typically interact with Dr. Luthor. Sam seems friendly enough with the director, or at least is rarely the target of her ire, and J’onn is old enough to be her father so he’s never singled out either. Kara is blaming her latest run-in squarely on Alex; normally the director wouldn’t say two words to her. At least, if everything goes sideways, she won’t be directly affected. Plus, if she’s in on what they’re planning, she might just be able to prevent anything terrible from happening -like anyone getting fired, or killed by a seething glare and razor words.

“Yeah, I’m in.” 

Alex grins mightily and punches her in the shoulder. As she wipes her face, Kara wishes it was with the hand that wasn’t holding the rocks glass.

The wager is nowhere near as bad as she thought it would be. $100 buy in, winner takes all and to win, the person must get the good doctor to go with them to STAT!, buy the whole group a round of Chemo shots, herself included, and take them together. The part Kara wasn’t so wild about: there was no interest in actually befriending the Director. There had to be something wrong with that, but when she’d cornered Alex about it, her sister had just shrugged and said she didn’t need any more friends, she just needed a boss who wasn’t a bitch. Alex wasn’t generally friendly even at the best of times, but this seemed more callous than Kara thought her capable.

Alex is at the sink scrubbing down after stitching up a screaming toddler when Kara runs into her. She’s coming back from a consultation with James about a nasty pelvic fracture currently in bay 3. It’s been a week since the start of the wager and no one is making any headway. When she’s in the ER at the same time as Dr. Luthor, she can hear how everyone swallows crass retorts. Once, she overheard Winn actually ask her how she was. Their behavior doesn’t seem to be doing anything but make the director suspicious. She remains curt and cutting, but she’s using everyone’s title and has stopped engaging first. 

Kara’s pretty sure that this wager is going to be the death of her, so she’s itching for someone -anyone- to win. She’s even debated bringing in her copy of The American Journal of Medicine with Dr. Luthor’s most recent article, an editorial titled “The Conflict of Public Health Law and Civil Liberties: The Role of Research Data and First Amendment Law” and ask for her autograph to see if that’ll get her in good with the director. Or maybe she’ll just tell her everything, agree to split the pot with her, and then wash her hands of the whole affair. She’s starting to comfort eat to combat her anxiety about the situation and makes a mental note to up her gym attendance.

“You know, after that comment the other day...you aren’t thinking about actually being her friend, are you!?” Alex squints at her suspiciously, “you wouldn’t do that to me would you? You know she’s got it in for me.”

“Oh for crap’s sake, she called you Alexandra once , and THAT’S YOUR ACTUAL NAME!” She can’t hide her frustration with how ridiculous her sister is being. “You guys call her all kinds of terrible things that have nothing to do with her name on a regular basis.”

“She did it three times,” Alex grumbles, “and she can’t hear us.”

“What?” Her voice climbs an incredulous octave. “How can you even say that? We both were there when she heard Winn call her the Evil Queen. It’s hurtful; you are hurting her feelings and then you have the gall to be upset when she treats you in kind.”

Alex looks properly chagrined so Kara turns to walk away, but then she hears her mutter under her breath, “It’s not like the Ice Queen actually has feelings.”

Kara whirls around, eyes wide and teeth bared in a grimace. This is not her sister. Not the Alex she grew up with who got sent home from school at least once a month for fighting Kara’s bullies on the playground. Now, for some reason, Alex is the bully. Kara sucks in a deep breath to stop herself from yelling at the top of her lungs. “What did she ever do...you know what? I’m not going to dignify that with a response. I can’t do this right now. I’ll talk to you later, when I won’t say something I might regret.”

Alex just huffs then shakes her head. “Later then.” She walks off toward the consultation rooms, spine stiff and jaw clenched stubbornly.

‘That’s it, this is done,’ Kara fumes as she stomps off towards the director’s office, throwing her arms around in frustration, ‘I’m putting an end to whatever madness-”

“Oh pardon me .” 

By the derision dripping from the comment, it’s obvious that the person she almost ran into in the hallway in front of the door with a placard that says ER Director thinks Kara did so intentionally and with verve. She looks up into the steely grey eyes and haughty frown of Dr. Luthor. Dr. Lillian Luthor. The woman whose obscenely large portrait hangs, in all its glowering glory, in the lobby of NCGH right next to the matching one of her husband. Kara stands for what might be an eternity, mouth open, until she jump-starts her brain and stammers out an apology. Her cheeks flame under the hard stare of one of the largest benefactors of the hospital.

“No, I’m sorry. I was distracted and didn’t see you, please excuse me.”

Lillian’s lip curls in contempt as her eyes land on the ID card clipped to her pocket. “I hope you’re not this preoccupied in the OR, doctor. Trauma surgery requires laser focus.”

Benefactor or not, this woman clearly needs to be taken down a peg or two. Kara’s one of the best trauma surgeons in the western half of the country, regularly invited to speak at conferences and even more frequently called on for emergency consultations. Her eyes narrow but the retort dies on her tongue as the other woman pushes past her without a backwards glance.

“No need to knock, doctor, Lena isn’t doing anything requiring focus either.” Lillian scoffs over her shoulder as she saunters away.

It’s no wonder the director is as venomous as she is if that’s who raised her. As she puts her knuckles against the door and raps softly, she wonders if it’s a learned or defensive behavior. She waits a moment, but when there’s no response, she turns the knob and pokes her head in to see Dr. Luthor with her face in her hands, shoulders slumped looking small and vulnerable and absolutely nothing like the formidable force that sweeps through the ER on the regular, sending both physicians and nurses ducking for cover.

“Um, Dr. Luthor?” 

The other woman jumps as if shot, practically leaping from her chair and scrubbing at her face with her hands. She turns her back to Kara, and drops her hands to her sides, tipping back her head then dropping her chin in surrender.

“What do you want, Dr. Danvers?” Kara can hear her try to rein in the warble, but her voice breaks and her shoulders sink with a sigh.

Kara has a decision to make: take advantage of the other woman’s vulnerability or make an honest effort to connect. She clears her throat, and decides on the latter. If the other woman lashes out, she can then try the former, guilt-free. Hopefully, the director isn’t protective of her mother.  “Nothing, I just ducked in here to escape the dementor in the hallway. I could feel all my happiness being sucked away, so I had to seek shelter.”

She’s still looking at a stiff back covered by a shining fall of hair the color of a raven’s wing. Wondering idly if it is iridescent in the sun like the feathers, she takes a step into the office and tries again. “Can I close the door? There’s no telling when she might decide to come back.”

“And here I was hoping maybe you could cast a patronus.” The delivery is flat and punctuated with a little sniffle, but there’s no hostility, just sad resignation.

Kara swears she can hear a hint of hope, though, a reaching out, a key to the door in those walls, but still clutched tight and held close.

Kara can see the barbed comments and cutting remarks for what they are now, the first line of defense before the gargantuan walls that this woman has erected around herself. They’re no surprise, especially if the witch she passed in the hallway is who nurtured her. Kara steps deeper into the office and closes the door quietly, reaching out again tentatively to see if the key will be offered instead of just shown. “I unfortunately left my wand back in the OR, looks like we’re going to have to weather this out like muggles.”

Another sniffle and the dark head raises, shoulders straightening. “Dr. Danvers, I’m not sure if-”

“Kara. Please, call me Kara. I think our shared trauma of near-dementor experience allows for the dropping of formalities.” She hears the other woman mutter something that sounds suspiciously like ‘you have no idea’ before she swipes at her eyes one more time and turns.

The puffy redness surrounding her eyes renders them a green so vibrant that Kara can’t look away. Without her fearsome glare, the director is breathtakingly beautiful. She offers Kara a trembling smile, but her sculpted eyebrows furrow with worry. Kara knows the power she has in this moment. The director is never anything but commanding and controlled and as she takes a slow breath in through her nose, Kara realizes her chance at that key is slipping away. “Dr. Luthor-”

“Lena.” Her voice is still rough, but stronger than before. “It’s only fair. Now that we’re on a first name basis, Kara, was it just unfortunate luck running into my mother, or did you have other business to be outside my office door?”

Every minute with this woman is a minefield and Kara doesn’t have a map. If she tells Lena about Alex and company’s scheme, it’ll just add more misery to what looks like a lifetime subscription. What could it hurt to offer the other woman a sympathetic ear or a sturdy shoulder? She probably won’t even take her up on it, but Kara will extend the offer, nonetheless. 

“Unfortunate luck it seems, although it’s panning out to be more fortunate.” She meets Lena’s eyes and smiles, “I was going to go out to STAT! but after that close encounter, I’m thinking something more low key. There’s a small coffee shop three blocks east of here and they have a wonderful, spicy hot chocolate, perfect for restoration after a dementor encounter?”

Lena worries her lower lip with her teeth, her internal debate clear on her face. Kara leans a little harder, “My treat. I owe you for letting me seek shelter in your office. Plus,” Kara lets her eyes laugh while she pulls her mouth into a stern frown, “we should probably get our stories straight for HR.” The exaggerated wink does exactly as she hopes: Lena smiles shyly and ducks her head.

“Hot chocolate does sound good…”

“Wonderful, let’s go.”

The only thing Kara regrets about this impromptu hangout is that it took her this long to initiate something like it. Lena is personable and funny, if somewhat reserved...at first. It’s almost as if she’s unsure of what to say once they’ve finished discussing the menu. So Kara dodges the hot topics of Lillian and work and instead, indulges in her personal fantasy and asks Lena about her research. When Lena just stares, in open-mouthed surprise, for a moment before scrunching her face into a delighted smile, Kara knows she chose correctly. She basks in the enthusiastic lilt and animated gestures as Lena explains her theories and what challenges they are currently facing in this stage of research. The woman sitting in front of her, eyes alight with excitement, is nothing like the ER director whose targeted glare and barked orders send the staff running for cover. 

“What? Do I have whipped cream on my face?” Lena lifts a napkin to dab around her mouth and when Kara doesn’t answer, she swipes at the tip of her nose as well.

She must have been staring, but who could blame her? Lena is gorgeous; Kara can’t believe it’s never registered on her radar before. And while there’s a thread of arousal woven through this tightness in her chest, she realizes she actually just wants to learn more about the other woman, that she’s not ready for this night to be over. Never has she been struck like this, enamored from the moment she met Lena and not Dr. Luthor, and she doesn’t quite know what to do with the feeling. She quickly tries to cover up the fact that she’s still gaping like an idiot.

“Would you want to do lunch tomorrow?” Her mouth is apparently operating without her brain’s permission, because she was actually thinking more about just answering Lena’s question in the negative.

Lena’s eyes go wide and she flushes and Kara is convinced that she has not only put her foot into her mouth, but has swallowed it clear up to the knee. She starts to backpedal, stammering out an apology that her mouth doesn’t want to make but her brain insists. Lena touches her gently on her hand and silences her with a verdant gaze.

“I would really like that. Since I’ve moved to National City, it’s been a struggle to connect with people…”

Kara’s smiling so wide her cheeks ache, “I have no idea why, this has been one of the best nights I’ve had in a long time.” The truth of her statement shocks her; more independent observation from her mouth that her brain can’t deny. “What kind of food do you like?”

“Oh, there’s a new trendy sandwich shop that opened on Elm that I’ve wanted to try...”

“Perfect, I’ll meet you there at noon then?”

Lena beams at her and nods, gesturing at her phone. “Give me your number so I can message you if I get caught up.”

Kara rattles off her number as she collects her bag and pulls on her sweater. Her phone buzzes in her pocket when Lena messages to share her own. She holds Lena’s jacket for her to slip her arms in and hovers near enough to hold the door on their way out, and to open the door to the cab so Lena can slide in.

“Do you want to share?” 

Kara does, but she also knows she desperately needs some time to process everything that’s happened. She’s never been so taken with someone so quickly, which is especially jarring since she’s technically known the other woman for over a year.

“Oh I just live a few blocks away. I’ll walk. Thank you though. I had a great time. Message that you got home safe?”

Lena’s soft smile soothes her immediately. “I will. If you get home before you hear from me, please do the same.”

Kara closes the door and waves, turning around to walk to her apartment only after the cab is out of sight. The night air is cool but comfortably so, and while the stars are mostly lost in the light of the city, the moon shines bright in the cloudless sky.

Just as she reaches the door to her building her phone buzzes again in her pocket. There are two messages there, the one from earlier in the cafe  -a simple ‘Hi, it’s Lena.’- now another:

The key Lena’s offered to the door in those impossibly high walls shines gold and fingerprintless, attached to a delicate silk fob and Kara feels so sure that, although there is no warning that copies cannot be made, this is the only one. So, she tucks it away with care, not quite in her heart, but near enough for safekeeping. Before falling into bed, she types out a brief reply.

The following week, they are practically inseparable. Lunches, dinners, coffee after work, Kara learns that it’s not a fortress of walls that Lena has built around herself, but a labyrinth, and the key only gets her in the door. Lena trails breadcrumbs and lets Kara catch glimpses of her before slipping around a corner with a plaintive look that begs Kara to follow, but wouldn’t be surprised if she didn’t. 

She’s never been this compelled before, never this driven to uncover the correct path. She pays close attention to every motion, every word. She gleans that Lena likes her coffee black but tea must have milk and sugar, that she sneaks trash TV on her phone in her office on days that are especially hard, that her family is anything but since her father died. Lena spins the rings on her left hand with her right when she’s nervous, she bites at her lower lip and keeps her eyes down when she’s unsure, and these observations draw Kara deeper and deeper into her maze of walls. The more she discovers about Lena, the more she wants to know, the further she goes because there is surely treasure at the heart. 

“Ground control to Kara, come in Kara.” Lena smiles playfully -a look that, because of its rarity, Kara would commit murder to keep on her face - and waves a hand in front of her staring face, “Are you alright?”

They are back at the little coffee shop after a shared Sunday day shift. Lena’s black Americano sits in front of her, the cup edged with a scarlet lip-print. There’s a plate of macarons in the middle that Kara bought to share, but so far she’s eaten three of the six and is considering pushing the plate in front of Lena just so she doesn’t eat them all and embarrass herself. 

“Yep, fine! Great! Just thinking.” She smiles back, basking in the soft fondness she can see in the clear green. “It’s been a great week getting to know you.”

Right on cue, Lena worries her lower lip with her teeth and drops her eyes to her lap, murmuring. “I’m waiting for you to realize that dementoritis is a hereditary trait and sic your sister on me.”

A peal of laughter bursts out before she can stop it, because she would never think the woman in front of her is capable of Lillian-like behavior. Lena flinches as if stabbed. Cursing herself internally, Kara reaches for a fidgeting hand and clasps it between her own. Lena tenses even further, sucking a gasp in through her teeth, and Kara realizes how familiar she’s being. Nevermind that she’s been itching to touch Lena, god, since the night they got hot chocolate. She had to stuff her hands into her pockets to keep from guiding Lena through the door with a hand on the small of her back and she’d had to repeat to herself in a litany, Do not hug her goodbye , as Lena had climbed into the cab. Now, she has Lena’s trembling hand, the weight of her wide, verdurous gaze, and absolutely no idea what to do with either. 

“I’m sorry, I didn’t ask your permission to touch you or be in your space. I just grabbed, and now I’m still grabbing, even though I think you want me to let go-” she’s babbling now, Lena’s hand still sandwiched between her own, “but I’m still not. Oh my god, I’m going to let go now, not because I want to, but because I’m making you uncomfo-”

“No, please don’t,” Lena cuts her off with a quiet voice, tightening her fingers when Kara tries to pull away, “at least not if you don’t want to. I’m just not used to -” she hesitates, clearly stuck on how to explain. “people touching me, um, voluntarily and for no reason.”

Kara squints at her, but Lena’s eyes are still downcast. “Lena,” she waits until the other woman looks up to continue, “are you uncomfortable?” 

“Yes-”

Kara takes one hand away, but Lena holds fast to the other. “-but not because of you. I just don’t know how to do this and I desperately don’t want to do it wrong.”

“Do what wrong?”

“Be friends with you. You’re the first person who’s ever expressed an interest in getting to know me since I’ve moved here.”

Kara’s heart twists imagining how lonely she must have been. “You don’t have to do anything special to be friends with me. Especially put up with me in your space. I’m kinda a touchy-feely person and I forget that not everyone else likes that.” 

“It’s nice. I like it. You’ll have to be patient with me, I’m just not used to people wanting to be that close to me.”

Kara tightens her fingers but decides to poke a little fun. “Well, that might be because you’re kinda scary, and despite Alex’s bravado, I’m the bravest of the whole lot.”

Lena just raises an eyebrow.

“Oh come on, you have to notice how the entire ER runs for cover the moment you walk in. They’re terrified of you.”

“They’re not afraid of me, Kara. They don’t like me.”

“You are interpreting their reactions to your presence wrong, I promise. I was terrified of you too.”

Lena purses her lips, still clearly skeptical of Kara’s reasoning. “But you never acted like everyone else.”

She rubs her thumb across the soft skin of Lena’s hand, hoping to keep her from withdrawing. “Well, my go-to when I’m afraid isn’t open hostility, like my sister. I’m more of a duck-and-cover kind of girl. Alex though -Alex gets combative when afraid or intimidated, that fight or flight business? Well, let’s just say Alex doesn’t turn down a battle and you seem pretty perceptive of which of her buttons to push to start one.” 

Lena frowns but nods. “There are times when the Luthor inside gets the better of me. It was just easier, at that point, to respond in kind. I’d already lost control by then, so it was just easier to have them hate me.”

“Well it’s not too late to make a fresh start. Hate is better than indifference, it means they feel something.”

Kara watches her shoulders droop and a look of desperate sadness flash across her face before it’s schooled back into cautious stoicism. Maybe this is the moment the birds devour the trail of breadcrumbs, and Kara will never be able to find her, tucked away deep inside. The thought is unbearable; some part of her recognizes that Lena has been trying to connect in any way she can. Even a negative connection is better than none at all. If only they could see her as Kara did when Lena was explaining her research, passionate and compelling, she knows they’d pull her into the fold. Kara just needs neutral ground, alcohol, and a friendly contest and she’s pretty sure Lena will win them all over. 

“Hey, do you like darts, or pool?”

Lena shrugs. “I’m pretty terrible at darts, but I can hold my own at pool. Why?”

“I’ve the 12/12 shift on Friday, and I know you’re not usually there that late, but maybe you’d want to meet me at STAT! around 12:30?” Kara stuffs a macaron in her mouth with her free hand. She chews while watching Lena deliberate, the sweetness not doing a damn thing to quell the butterflies in her stomach. She feels like she’s forgetting something important and it only adds to her nerves. She grabs a second cookie and pops the whole thing in her mouth, chasing it with the last swallow of her champurrado. Lena sits back, squeezing her hand gently before letting go to push the loose hair around her face behind her ears. Caution still edges the green of her eyes, but those ruby lips tip up in a sly smirk.

“If I say yes, will it be safe to take the last macaron or will I pull back a bloody stump?”

Kara flushes to the tips of her ears and smiles sheepishly as she pushes the plate across the table. “I eat when I’m nervous. Sugar always makes me feel better.”

Lena laughs delightedly then slips the cookie from the plate and takes a prim bite without displacing a single crumb. “I’m supposed to be in at 6AM Saturday, but I’ll shuffle my schedule. Who knows when I’ll have the opportunity again to hustle you at pool?” She grins and pops the rest of the cookie in her mouth, licking a spot of sticky icing from her finger holding Kara’s gaze the whole time.

Kara can only swallow and sputter while the smirk stays firmly planted on Lena’s face. She’s not always the most observant when it comes to social cues, but she’s pretty sure Lena’s flirting with her, and it does nothing to ease the butterflies. Between the flirting and the niggling feeling in the back of her mind, she’s distracted for the rest of their time together. They linger over the dregs of Lena’s coffee and once more, the owner has to shoo them out. The night plays out a lot like the last time they were here, except this time Kara gives in to the voice screaming in her head and wraps Lena up in a hug next to the cab. She almost lets go and apologizes again, at Lena’s initial stiffness, but after just a moment the other woman slips her arms around Kara’s waist and relaxes into the embrace. 

She replays the evening in her head while laying in bed, her phone resting on her chest waiting for Lena’s “home” text, but her reflections are doing nothing to jar that odd feeling of forgetting something loose. So she switches her thoughts to this Friday and how she’s going to ease Lena into her friend group. All of her strategies keep melting away into visions of holding Lena’s hand or sitting snugged tight on her couch, and she falls asleep with a contented smile on her face.

 

s§s

 

She wakes up at 3AM in a cold sweat. The wager. In the whirlwind of her getting to know Lena, her friends’ stupid bet was completely forgotten. The whole point of her going to Lena’s office a week and a half ago was to put an end to the idiotic idea, but it completely left her mind at the sight of teary emerald eyes and sad smile. First order of the day is to tell Alex it’s over, and that she needs to give Lena a chance.

“I can’t fucking believe you.” Alex yanks the curtain of bay 2 around them as if it would do anything to muffle her angry diatribe. “You’re a traitor.”

“Stop it.” Kara’s angry out the gate, and not just at Alex. She’s pissed at herself for ever agreeing to the wager in the first place when she could have shut it down. She’s pissed at Lillian for being, well, Lillian and making Lena cry which in turn made Kara forget why she’d gone to her office in the first place. “And keep your voice down. It’s not like you picked one of the bays with walls and a door.”

Alex just continues as if she doesn’t hear, opening and slamming cabinet drawers before punching the pillow on the gurney. “You’re my sister, you’re supposed to pick me over her.”

It’s almost a whine, and while Kara can hear that there’s still anger there, there’s also a thread of insecurity. She must tread carefully, balancing her loyalties to both women. “Hey, you know if this were actually a choice, I’d pick you every time. But I shouldn’t have to choose, Alex, and you’re not being very fair by making me pick sides. I really like her and you would too, if you just got to know her.”

Alex scowls, but not with as much animosity as she usually uses whenever Lena is concerned. Kara senses an opening and takes it. “She’s absolutely brilliant; the two of you could talk for hours about this new medtech research she’s doing. It’s going to change the face of medicine, and it’s so exciting.”

“Go onnn.” Alex raises an eyebrow in a manner eerily similar to Lena.

Kara rolls her eyes. “She thinks you guys all hate her, so she protects herself as best as she can, by antagonizing. She’s so nice though, and I think she’s really lonely. I need you to be nicer to her, Alex, and not in that patronizing way that Winn and Kelly are, because she’s not stupid. You both have so much in common, and she would really fit in well with our little group. She’s funny and observant, and I really, really want you to try, please?”

Alex’s scowl is gone, but Kara is having trouble interpreting the look she’s getting. Alex’s eyes scan her face until suddenly, she gives Kara a Cheshire Cat smile. “You have a crush on her.”

“What, shut up, no I don’t.” The swoop in her stomach says otherwise, but that’s between her and her gut, thank you very much. Unfortunately, the heat in her cheeks betrays her every time. She can never hide anything from Alex, much to her dismay.

“Oh my god, you DO! You’re blushing!” Alex laughs gleefully.

“Please shut up, remember these curtains block absolutely NO sound. Please. Shut. Up.” Kara is about to lunge across the gurney and clap her hand over Alex’s mouth, but her sister seems content with her current discomfort level. “And tell everyone else she’s coming. I want all of you on your best behavior.”

“I think you should just stick it out, I mean, what she doesn’t know won’t hurt her.” Alex just shakes her head. “I can’t believe you’re giving up a guaranteed win. If you’ve invited her to the bar and she accepted, you’re halfway there. I’m sure if you’re friends ,” Kara doesn’t like the way Alex emphasizes the word with air quotes and an eyeroll, “she’ll buy that round. It’s like $500 you’re throwing away, but whatever. You do you.”

Her own relief is palpable; Kara can feel her shoulders lift and stomach unclench. It’s done, over, finished, kaput -she can put it all behind her. This whole situation was wearing on her more than she thought. She wants to stop by Lena’s office, say hello, and bask in the happy feelings that always arise when she’s with Lena. She glances at her watch, trying to decide when she’ll have a moment, as Alex pulls back the curtain. Her sister’s muttered ‘uh oh’ yanks her from her thoughts.

“Hello Drs. Danvers. I was coming to see if there were concerns that needed to be brought up to the hospital board, but I think, perhaps, I’m interrupting.” 

Despite the obvious tension in the room, a part of Kara is eased just by Lena’s presence. She stands halfway to the bay in a forest green dress and towering heels, arms crossed defensively across her chest.  Ruby lips are pressed in a thin line and her sculpted eyebrows are raised haughtily, but Kara can see the hurt in her stormy eyes. This time the swoop in her stomach is accompanied by icy fear. 

“Lena! You aren’t interrupting.” Kara dreads the response to the next words out of her mouth, “How long have you been standing there?”

“Long enough to know I was right when I said that everyone in the ER doesn’t like me, that it’s not an intimidation thing like you had convinced me could possibly be true.” Lena bites her lip and looks at the floor for just a moment before straightening her shoulders and stepping back into Dr. Luthor. “So, if there are no concerns, I’ll just go back to my office.”

“I’ll walk back with you.” Kara practically shoves Alex out of the way and hip checks the gurney to move to Lena’s side.

“That’s not necessary. I know where my office is.”

Kara’s heart sinks at Lena’s detached tone. If she could just explain... “Please? What you heard, I mean, I don’t know what you heard, but it’s not what you think. I can explain.”

“No explanation necessary. Regretfully, I’m going to have to cancel for tonight. Something’s come up. I’m sorry to spoil your chances of winning.” Lena turns and walks out of the ER, but not before Kara saw the shine of tears. Every click of her heels is a nail in Kara’s heart, and once she can no longer hear them she sinks down onto the wheeled examination stool and rests her head against the metal frame of the gurney. 

“I deserve that. I never should have even considered that wager. I should have told you all how I really felt about the idea, but I thought I’d be able to defuse the situation if it got out of hand, if I just went along with it.” There is a little catharsis in being able to get this all out to Alex, but her chest tightens right back up when she thinks about Lena sitting alone in her office. Who does she have to tell about her awful co-workers? “I need to go apologize. I have to tell her that I didn’t befriend her because of the bet.”

Alex comes around the gurney to squeeze her shoulder. “I would give her some time to cool off. I’m sure she’ll realize that she didn’t have full context and want to talk to you about it.”

Kara’s shaking her head before her sister can even finish. “No. No, I don’t think so at all. She’s so used to people being awful to her -the entire ER staff, her own mother, and now me. I’ve spent all last week getting to know her and finally felt like she trusted me-”

The hand on her shoulder slides across her back and Alex pulls her into a sideways hug and presses a kiss on the crown of her head. “I don’t know how it happens, but sometimes I forget what a truly good person you are, Kara Danvers. If she is all of the things you said she is, I’m sure that she’ll come around. Maybe text her and tell her you want to talk and are available whenever she is, then leave the ball in her court.” When she leans into her, her sister clears her throat and continues, “and I’m sorry that I wasn’t being who I should have been when she was around. I owe her an apology as well.”

“Thank you.” All Kara wants to do is go home and curl up in her comforter and watch cartoons, but she’s got eight hours of her shift left. She’ll go brood in the surgeons’ quarters, play some dumb game on her phone, and mope until midnight, hoping that there’s no serious trauma tonight. All she wants is to be with Lena, but since she’s fucked that up, being alone is the second best option. The path of the labyrinth has drifted and morphed and she finds herself lost, no sign of the trail to go forward and she’s in too deep to go back.  

She tells Alex her plans and on the way back to the surgical floor she sends Lena a text: ‘ I’m sorry. This last week and a half has been so amazing, I never wanted it to end. I value your friendship; I hope we can talk about this when you’re ready.”

Then she slips her earbuds in and works on making a maudlin playlist to suit her mood.

The shift ends with no major trauma call and no response from Lena, so Kara spent a majority of the eight hours wallowing with her playlist and two share-size bags of gummy bears. Nia, Winn, and Kelly have all stopped by at various points and apologized. She sends them away, telling them that it’s Lena’s forgiveness they’re seeking, not hers. Alex shows up just as she’s shoving her things into her backpack, but Kara is already shaking her head.

“I’m not going.” She slings one of the pack straps over her shoulder and pushes past Alex into the hallway, “I’m not in the mood to drink right now.”

“Kar, we haven’t seen you in over a week. Just come out for a little bit, have one drink, and then go home and continue your Lydia Deetz impression.” Alex cajoles, pulling on her backpack and making her veer into the hospital wall. “I’ll even go to the gym with you tomorrow since I’m pretty sure you’re gonna want to try and run off all that candy.”

Sometimes she hates how well Alex knows her. She’s past the stage of wanting to be alone and Alex knows full well. “Fine, just one drink.”

“And a round of darts”

“And one round of cricket, and that’s it.”

Alex grins and hooks an elbow around her neck and pulls her close. “Atta girl.”

When they get to the bar they walk past the bouncer with a wave, Kara already stuffing her earbuds in her ears. The bar is busy, but the wait for drinks doesn’t seem to be outrageous, so she shoves her backpack into Alex’s arms and tells her to go put them in for next at one of the dart boards. She throws a $50 on the bar and asks for a tray of Chemos and a vodka tonic. It’ll be a lot easier to duck out of here if everyone else is drunk. She maneuvers the tray carefully through the pockets of people and across the bar to their regular table in the corner, mind on what episode of Futurama she’s going to watch in bed. One of the sad ones of course, Jurassic Bark or Game of Tones. She’s feeling like a good cry. After sliding the tray on their table without a look, she grabs her drink and pulls her phone from her pocket with the blind hope that Lena has texted her back, but there’s nothing. No text, no DM, no snap, nothing. She sucks in a bereft sigh, then a swallow of her drink from the tiny cocktail straw that Alex always mocks her for using. She doesn’t feel like darts, or even finishing her drink, but she agreed and Alex will hound her until the end of time if she reneges. She scrolls through her social media feeds in the hope that Lena has posted something. At least she’s not blocked yet.

She feels a hand on her forearm right at the same moment that she realizes that everyone around the table has gone strangely quiet. She looks up into Nia’s grinning face, then over to Winn, who looks like the cat that caught the canary. “What?” They both dissolve into laughter and shake their heads.

The hand on her arm squeezes and she’s going to absolutely smack her sister if she doesn’t get some patience. “For the love of all that’s holy, Alex, if you don’t hold your horses-” She whirls around when the hand squeezes again and her mouth drops open in shock. “Lena?”

Her dark head nods, “Can we talk?”

“Y-yes, of course. Do you want to leave?” Kara reaches for her backpack, but Lena’s hand on her arm tightens and tugs her towards the bathrooms. 

“No.” Lena shakes her head, looking at the others at the table who are trying their best to look anywhere else. “Just somewhere a little quieter and more private.”

Kara allows herself to be led into a corner of the alcove at the back of the bar, Lena’s hand wrapped around her wrist. She leans back against the wall, leaving her arm under Lena’s control. She wants to pour out apologies and beg for forgiveness, but Lena is the wronged party here, and deserves her say.

“Remember when I asked for your patience with me?” Lena drops the grip on her arm to fiddle with her rings. Kara puts her hands in her pockets and nods. “It’s going to take a lot of patience, and I don’t blame you if you can’t, or don’t want to give that much. I’m a mess, but I want to try -I’m trying. There are a lot of very ingrained habits that I have to unlearn, and that goes against everything I have lived so far.” She pauses and Kara almost recoils from the pain in her weary but determined gaze. “The last week and a half have been the best in my life, and I speak without hyperbole. I’ve never felt a connection like this before; I’ve never felt so seen. I don’t want to lose you.”

Kara watches the delicate line of her throat bob as Lena swallows then coughs slightly, obviously struggling with what to say next. She wants to pull the other woman into her arms and defend her from a world that has bet against her over and over, but that has lost, repeatedly and without fail. She holds out a tentative hand that Lena takes immediately, flashing a grateful smile. 

“Can I ask you a question?” Kara stares at their joined hands, playing with Lena’s fingers a moment before looking up to catch her dark head nodding. “What changed?”

“Alex. She came to my office and told me I owed you another chance, in that wonderfully polite and undemanding way she has.” A finely manicured eyebrow arches when Lena raises her eyes to meet her own. “And I told her to get fucked, that I didn’t owe anyone anything, and was about to call security when she started laughing. She apologized and explained that you tried to stop the wager, that you are the best person she knows, and that I would be a fool to shut you out.”

Lena sways a moment and takes a deep breath, readying herself to continue. Kara squeezes her fingers and strokes along her knuckles in encouragement, giving her the space to continue. “And then she apologized for how she treated me, and we spoke a little about how we work. I think we understand each other better now. I won’t dread coming to work when I know Alex is working anymore. There is so much more that we need to talk about, but I just wanted to say that I’m sorry for not letting you explain and for not trusting that you might actually have my best interests in mind. I’m not used to being able to trust people, but I want to. I want to trust you and eventually Alex and the others.”

Lena offers a tremulous smile, and Kara counters by letting go of Lena’s hand and opening her arms. Lena hesitates only a blink before she steps into the embrace and presses herself tightly against Kara. Kara can’t help but close her eyes at how perfectly they fit together, she splays her hands out across Lena’s back and hugs her tight, rocking slightly, as she whispers her own apologies in Lena’s ear. “I’m sorry I didn’t stop them, and that I didn’t follow you when you walked away. We’ll both do better next time there’s a miscommunication.”

Lena stays relaxed in her arms for a few minutes before Kara reluctantly releases her and she steps back. “How about you teach me to play darts and we plan on having the start of these talks tomorrow after work?”

“I think that sounds like a plan. Be prepared to earn the ire of my sister in a far more organic way, by kicking her drunk-ass in darts.” She holds out her arm and Lena steps into it, letting Kara drape it across her shoulders and pull her close.

When they walk back out to the table, Winn is singing a song from Fiddler on the Roof into an empty shot glass while Nia applauds, laughing, and Alex and Kelly make out against the wall. The tray of shots that Kara bought is full of empty glasses, and she realizes that she doesn’t want to teach Lena to play darts. What she wants to do is curl up on her couch with Lena in her arms and her comforter wrapped around them both, and she wants it so badly that she just walks them past her oblivious friends, past the dartboard, and right out the door. 

“I really just want to spend time with you with no expectations except that we are just together? I need to decompress; today has been a lot. Would you want to see my apartment? I have the fluffiest duvet, and popcorn, and cartoons.” She buries her nose in Lena’s hair and thumbs through her phone for the Lyft app. 

“I would love that.”

And they stand, wrapped up in each other, as Kara realizes she’s made it to the center of the maze and the reward is far greater than she could have hoped.
