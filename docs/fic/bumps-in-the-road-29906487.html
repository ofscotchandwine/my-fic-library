1. Chapter 1

Author's Note:

Trigger Warning: catcalling/harassment

Kara hopped out of her car, stretching her arms out behind her head as soon as her feet hit the pavement. Of all the days she could’ve picked to pack up and throw her life into the metaphorical wind, this had to be the worst. Tuesdays were just never right for life-changing decisions, especially when they ended up like this. 

Sun beaded down, forming droplets of sweat that lined Kara’s forehead, and caused her hair to frizz up from the humidity. Normally, she didn’t mind the heat, but Kara was starting to think the weather, and the lost road map, and the fact that her car radio had broken down about ten miles back were all signs that maybe she should’ve stayed home. Maybe she should’ve tried to ride it out at work, to fix things with her boyfriend, and every other fuck up she’d spent months trying to handle. Maybe she just wasn’t the adventure type. Some people were built for boring, day-by-day lives with partners they don’t love and jobs they secretly hate. 

Perhaps that was the world Kara was made for.

With a sigh, Kara ran her hands through her sweaty hair and pulled it back into a low bun at the base of her neck. Once she could finally feel the air hitting her skin again, she allowed herself to lean back against the side of her Jeep and do a quick scan of the gas station. 

It was pretty empty. There was a pickup truck and a man in his mid-forties standing by one of the gas pumps, a mini-mart with a lit-up sign at the other end of the lot. Half the letters had gone dark and Kara was unable to make out any shoppers through the windows. Instead, all she could see was the cashier.

Finally, her eyes landed on a young brunette woman. She sat on the pavement, leaning back against the store with one of her legs outstretched onto the road, the other crossed over at the knee. A cigarette sat perched between her index and middle finger, emitting a long line of smoke that clouded up around her face. Sunglasses had been pushed back into her hair like a headband and a navy blue jean jacket was tied around her waist. She didn’t seem to mind the smoke nor the heat. Kara couldn’t help but wonder how long she’d been sitting there, for her to become so unphased to all of that.

With one last pop of her back, Kara began the walk across the near-empty lot, grimacing at the smell of exhaust and gasoline creeping up through her nostrils. She did her best to shake it off, turning her head in the other direction in hopes that it would somehow vanish, but the effort was quickly deemed useless. Instead, she just looked towards the woman and, in turn, made her observance even more obvious.

However, it wasn’t until Kara had already made her way into the mini-mart and was hit with a wall of crisp air-conditioned air that she allowed herself to breathe a sigh of relief. No bad smells, no humidity. Just a cashier and aisles upon aisles of snacks. 

Just what she needed. 

Kara was so caught up in the satisfaction of one thing finally going her way that she didn’t even notice the footsteps behind her, or the cashier grumbling an oddly cheerful hello to whoever had come in after her. It took her all the way until she was standing between the chips and candy aisle that Kara heard somebody clear their throat and tap her shoulder. 

“Hey.” 

Instantly, she whipped her head around, brow furrowing when Kara saw the same woman from outside standing a few feet away from her. She had her hands planted on her hips, chin raised, with the slightest smile on her lips. The cigarette was gone and so were the clouds of smoke, revealing the rest of her face to the world.

She had green eyes, Kara noted. They were narrowed ever so slightly, but Kara could still make out the color, the way the fluorescent lights seemed to bounce off the little pools of honey surrounding her pupils. 

“Hey…” Kara said, slowly setting her bag of chips back down on the shelf. Was she in some sort of trouble? Because it felt like she was about to face the adult version of getting called to the principal's office. 

“I could see you staring at me,” the woman stated. “Outside, I mean.”

It’s a nervous habit, I have this problem where either I don’t make eye contact at all or I just get super aggressive with it and act all robotic. So I stared at you-- but you know that part. But it wasn’t because I wanted to be weird. Or robotic. I just think I’m in the middle of like a quarter-life crisis or something, and you know, when I get nervous--”

“You stare?” The woman finished, raising an eyebrow. 

Kara nodded. She clamped her mouth shut to make sure she wouldn’t get another word out because holy fuck what was she saying. 

“Yeah,” she whispered. “I stare.” 

The brunette smiled, dimples appearing at the edges of her lips. It was a very nice smile, Kara couldn’t help but think to herself. It felt warm like her eyes.

Oh, and how could I forget? You stare when you’re nervous and that’s why we’re here now.” 

Kara just stared again, completely dumbfounded. They’d been talking for all of thirty seconds and this woman was psychoanalyzing her as if they’d known each other for years, and for some reason, was getting everything all of it right. The whole thing was so stunning, all she could do was nod and mumble a quiet “yeah, that all sounds right.”

“Kara?” 

“Nice to meet you, Kara, I’m Lena.”

“Thank you.” 

“So, now that you know every crushingly embarrassing detail about what I’m doing here, what about you? Are you some kind of serial killer who stalks people outside gas stations, comes inside and befriends them Ted Bundy style, only to brutally murder them and stuff the bodies in the trunk of their car once they're done?” 

Lena paused and raised an eyebrow, unsure of how to respond to that. The change in expression was so painful to watch that Kara was starting to consider crawling into one of the ice cream freezers and hiding under frozen Snickers bars and Drum Sticks for the rest of eternity. 

“Oh-- you weren’t joking,” Lena said after a moment. She chuckled nervously and pursed her lips, slipping her hands into her front pockets.“No, I’m not a murderer. If I were though, I probably wouldn’t tell you.”

“But no, I wanted to get away from reality for a bit so I tried to backpack through the country. But all my stuff got stolen about two shady motels ago and the next bus isn’t gonna come by for another day, so I’m waiting it out here,” Lena explained. “I’m not really sure where I’m gonna go, though. It’s kinda hard to figure stuff out when you’ve got no phone.” 

Kara nodded. For a second, she looked back across the store, trying to see her old, beat-up car through the front window. 

She did have extra room-- a lot of it considering she hadn’t packed anything at all. And having someone to talk to might’ve been a nice change of pace seeing as now that the radio was blown out she didn’t have any other way of filling the silence.

But clearly, Kara’s mouth worked faster than her brain because the next thing she knew, she was asking Lena if she wanted to come with her.

I’ve got you. Kara nodded, replaying the sound of Lena’s low, husky voice as she took a step forward. God, this trip was going to be the best kind of torture. 

“Lena, help me out with this.” 

Kara flopped backward onto the double bed, holding up an array of pamphlets and maps she’d grabbed from the lobby over her head. She passed a handful back to Lena, who sat on the edge opposite to her, legs crossed as she went through the plastic bag full of graphic t-shirts and leggings they’d purchased in the gift shop.

As much as she’d tried to pretend to be disappointed when they found out the only unbooked room left was one with a full-sized bed instead of two twins, Kara was still oddly relieved with how things had turned out. This would be her first time in god only knew how long sleeping somewhere other than her own bed. Somewhere where Alex wasn’t only a phone call away and could come over at the drop of a hat, and the reality of getting that key and settling into a room that wasn’t her own meant Kara really had left home. 

She really was alone.

They’d taken a while to settle in. Both tried as hard as they possibly could to push the bed off on the other because ‘ you spent all day driving, Kara, you must be exhausted, you deserve it,' and ‘ but when was the last time you slept in a real bed, Lena? With the sound of Lena’s giggles filling the room like music to her ears, Kara knew for sure that being alone with her made the whole thing a million times better. 

“I’m busy, Kara.” 

“Come on,” she whined. “I’ve got one very important question, then I promise I won’t bother you again.”

“Fine,” Lena huffed. “What is it?” 

“Tell me, if you were spending your weekend in the very beautiful suburbs of Metropolis, what would you want to do first? Visit the world's largest ball of twine? Get a delicious meal at one of the state’s finest Big Belly Burgers? Or uh… interactive math museum?” 

“Hmmm, that’s a hard decision.”

“Yeah, a dire one too.”

Lena took a moment to think, forcing herself not to laugh at the serious expression that had crossed Kara’s face.

“While the world's largest ball of twine does sound very intriguing, this is your trip, so it’s really all up to you.” 

“That’s not true, you’re here too which means it’s up to both of us,” Kara said. 

She extended an arm behind her, lazily feeling around for Lena’s hand and squeezing it once she eventually found it. Through her distorted view, she could make out enough of Lena’s face to see her smile had returned. Dimples pressed into the edges of her cheeks, her eyes scrunching up with joy.

“Really, Kara, I’m fine with whatever.”

“Alright,” Kara said. “While we mull it over, do you wanna see what I picked up earlier?” 

“I thought you just got clothes?” 

“Nope!” 

Kara hopped out of bed, unaware of how Lena deflated when their hands pulled apart. She then made her way over to the wooden desk, where they’d set down the second bag of t-shirts, and began to rummage through it. Lena’s eyes followed the entire time, watching silently until Kara finally spun back around, smiling ear to ear as she held up a black and yellow disposable camera. 

“For memories,” she explained. “When this is all over, I’m gonna make a scrapbook of all the places I’ve been so that I have something to look back on.” 

“Oh, that’s actually a really nice idea,” Lena said, smiling. 

She watched Kara turn around to get a closer look at the rest of the room, holding the lens to her eye to take it all in.

“Can I take one of you?” She asked after a moment.

The camera shuttered, flash erupting through the dimly lit room as she got a shot of the mini kitchen set up. When she turned around, a hopeful smile had spread across her lips, a finger hovered over the red button.

“Uh, I...”

Lena ducked her head. She couldn’t help but wrap her arms around herself at the question, the feeling of Kara’s lingering gaze beginning to overwhelm her. The sensation of her excitement, the sight of her smile, and the way she bounced on her knees with anticipation, it was all so much. So much Lena wished more than anything she could be okay with.

It wasn’t Kara that was the problem. No, she was anything but. The camera, though. The thought that one day her picture would be printed, would be looked at and perceived in ways far beyond her control, was something Lena wasn’t sure she’d ever be comfortable with.

“Please?” Kara pleaded. “You just look so pretty right now, and no one will ever have to see it.” 

Pretty. You look so pretty. 

“I… I don’t know…” Lena’s voice trailed off as she looked down at the ground, at the beige carpeting, the discarded shoes, and tossed out pamphlets.

She’d teased Kara for taking so many, said she didn’t understand why such an unknown suburb would have so many tourist attractions. But as soon as Kara had started reading through them, she hadn’t been able to help but imagine visiting each and every one of them. Sure, they might have seemed boring. Pointless, even. But wasn’t that the whole point of this escape, to indulge in even the most absurd of ideas?

“You’ll just… you’ll keep it for yourself?” Lena asked quietly. “You won’t show anyone?” 

“It’ll just be for us. I promise,” Kara affirmed.

For us. Lena couldn’t help but smile at the way it sounded. Like they’d have something that bonded them together, even as they grew apart in the years to come.

“And when I get them developed, you can keep it. If that’s what you want.” 

“Okay then,” Lena decided after a moment. “You can take one.” 

“Really?” 

Lena nodded. “Just… just one.” 

She kept her arms wrapped around herself, but slowly bent her legs over the edge of the bed so she was no longer balled up like a pill bug. She watched Kara as she moved, lifting her chin ever so slightly so she’d appear as having a confidence that deep down, she knew had never really belonged to her. It was all an illusion but even so, allowing Kara to see her, to capture her forever, felt far more real than, well, anything really. And before she knew it, the flash went off, light erupting and illuminated the room as a quiet click rang out through Lena’s ears. 

“Was that…” She asked once it was done, meeting Kara’s eyes once more.

“It was perfect,” Kara said. “You-- you're perfect, Lee.”

Lena pushed the door shut, keeping her hand still and stiff on the knob as she closed her eyes, releasing a slow steady breath.

Kara had left a few minutes ago, said she would pick up some Big Belly Burgers for dinner, and send a text when she was on her way back. Hardly any time had passed at all, but with every passing second, Lena had to resist the ever-growing urge to call and beg her to return. It was absurd and childish and so out of character, but Lena didn’t care. She needed to see her again. Needed to work up the courage to ask what it meant, to ask why she’d used that name and why it had felt the way it did when she used it.  

Lee. Kara had called her Lee. Three letters that probably didn’t shouldn’t have held any significance at all but seemed to have wrapped themselves around Lena’s throat while they strangled all the air out of her.

They were confusing and terrifying, jumbled her thoughts into a mess of alphabet soup so disheveled that the only thing she knew for sure was that she’d felt so good when Kara had said it. She couldn’t comprehend why, it had just flowed so naturally when it came from her. It made  Lena’s heart skip a beat, let the rest of the world fall away, leaving behind only the gentle syllable echoing out through her being.

It wasn’t supposed to mean anything but somehow, in just a second, it had become everything. 

Lena released a breath along with her grasp on the doorknob and allowed herself to take a step forward, slowly working up the courage to confront her reflection in the mirror. Her skin seemed paler than usual, red blemishes spotting all around her features. It could’ve just been the fluorescent lights, they always made her look smaller. Weaker. Like she was reverting back to the person she’d been before leaving home. 

Stop it, you’re fine , she reminded herself, desperately pushing down the lump in her throat. It’s just a nickname, you’re fine. 

She’d said it so many times these past few days that the words were starting to lose every last bit of their meaning. Lena wanted to be fine, needed to be fine, but the more she thought, the more she looked at herself and the body she was supposed to recognize, the more she knew, that statement couldn’t have been further from the truth. 

Hands drifted up to her face, the icy fingers tracing themselves over the line beneath her cheekbone. They pressed into the hollow indentation, further and further until finally, Lena could feel the pressure again.

“Lee,” she whispered, repeating the word once more. “She called you Lee.”

Something had changed when Kara called her that, something within her shifted. Something Lena couldn’t help but fear had been trying to break free for some time now. 

She ran her hands through her hair and lowered herself to the ground, leaning back against the bathroom door as she dug her teeth into her bottom lip. That flutter in her chest, the way she ached to hear it again, to hear someone, anyone call her that again, had to mean something. She knew it did, knew it wasn’t as irrelevant as she was trying to convince herself it was. 

It caused that same sensation she’d gotten as a child in elementary school that day when she’d tripped into a puddle on the playground. Her teacher had taken her down to the front office in her muddy dress so she could change into a fresh uniform, but all they’d had was the boy’s version. Navy plaid pants and a yellow polo shirt.

She’d thought it was disgusting at first. She’d whined and said she’d rather be covered in mud for the rest of her life than be mistaken for a boy. She hated them, hated anything that had to do with men. But it hadn’t been up to her. So she’d changed into the clean clothes, complaining every step of the way, then reluctantly dragged herself back to class. She’d huffed as she sat down and glared at her teacher, the only thing distracting her from her rage being the girl who sat at the desk next to hers. She’d tapped Lena on the shoulder and said ‘ if we weren’t friends, I wouldn’t know if you were a boy or girl.’

Lena had felt something then. Felt something so big that for the rest of the day, she’d wondered if she was allowed to do that, to become something that wasn’t a boy or a girl. Of course, Lillian had instantly shut her down, but Lena wasn’t sure if that thought, that tiny spark that hovered in the back of her mind, had ever really left.

Even now, she wasn’t sure what to label it, but she knew it had to be the same thing that had erupted within when Kara gave her that nickname. 

As Lena let her head fall into her hands, she heard the door to their room shutting then locking behind her. It meant Kara was back. She was there with the food, expecting to hang out and have fun-- expecting Lena to be fine even if she was the furthest thing from it. 

“Lena?” Kara’s voice called out. “Are you still here?” 

Lena froze in place, eyes closing as she forced herself to swallow the heaviness seeping through. She could do this. She could pull herself together, plaster on a smile, and put on a shot the way she’d done so many times before. 

Except this time, it would be different. Because now, she was with someone she wanted to enjoy herself with, someone who, against all reason, she wanted to be friends with. And Lena sure as hell wasn’t going to allow some anxiety or discomfort to ruin it for her.

“I um, I’ll be out in a sec,” she responded.  

Slowly, Lena picked herself up off the floor and took just one more minute to examine herself in the mirror. She looked at the pale skin and pink blemishes. Wide eyes and rigid jawline. And as she picked it all apart, allowed herself to whisper the nickname one last time. 

Lee. Lena. Lena? 

Perhaps she never would’ve had the chance to sit with that feeling back in Metropolis the way she was now. To dissect the discomfort and euphoria that seemed to mix together, battling for constant dominance within her body. But things were different now, she was alone. Alone with Kara but still, out on her own. So maybe this was her chance. She’d be allowed to examine it, to put a name on that ache that had always been so desperate to break free. Now, she could allow herself to wonder why she’d never let it break free. 

Eventually, Lena drew in a breath and opened the door back up, emerging once more into the motel room. Kara was sitting on the bed, humming to herself as she unpacked enough take-out to feed a small army. 

“Hey,” she said when she noticed Lena standing in the corner. She looked up at her, grinning. “I wasn’t sure what you wanted so I might’ve gone a bit overboard.” 

“Don’t worry about it,” Lena said chuckled. “I’m sure we’ll go through it all eventually.” 

“Yeah,” Kara nodded. “We’ve got all the time in the world.”
