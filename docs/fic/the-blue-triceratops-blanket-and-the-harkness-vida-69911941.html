    Chapter 1

    “Stay still,” Rio instructed, trying to put the poor rabbit into the special shirt she bought. The little pterodactyl wings horning the back, and the cursive letter best big brother written in the front.

    Señor scratchy was less than pleased with this idea. His back leg stumped loudly on the hardwood floor.

    “It’s for Agatha! Not me!” The rabbit looked back at her less than convinced, “You know how tired she is. Do this for her!” Eventually, with a resigned puff and an indignant flick of a whisker, Señor Scratchy allowed the shirt.

    Earlier that day Agatha had officially hit her limit. Between the relentless nausea, the swollen ankles, the hot flashes, and the little feet constantly practicing kickboxing drills inside her ribs, she was done. “Rio,” she groaned from the couch, fanning herself with one hand. “Go. Leave. I cannot look at your smugly happy face right now. Go buy baby clothes. Go anywhere. Just—out.”

    And Rio has the amazing father that she is. Did just that. Baby clothes….

    Standing in front of the racks and racks of clothing, it feels almost foreign now. They looked more like doll outfits than real garments. She swallowed hard, brushing fingers over a pair of socks so tiny it felt illegal for them to exist. Rio never imagined she would be a mother, or have a caring wife. But there she was, standing in the middle of a store, almost crying over a dinosaur patterned socks.

    Her throat tightened.

    Her baby. Their baby. Their family.

    Rio almost cried right up this instant. Breathe, Rio, you can do it. That baby needs clothes. And they’re going to have the cutest clothes imaginable. As she got down the aisles, she saw a onesie. White with brontosaurus stegosaurus patterns. Perfect. The design was less than accurate but she had to admit the cuteness element added weren’t displeasing.

    After five hours running from store to store, several bags in each hand and probably a negative bank account. Rio finally got home pleased with her collection…. And a brand-new outfit for Señor scratchy!

    “Sorry my love for sending you away… I’m just- “Agatha stopped speaking when she saw her. “Don’t tell me this is all for us.”

    Rio smiled, shifting the bags onto the floor before proudly lifting Señor Scratchy from one of them. The rabbit was wearing his best big brother’s shirt with all the rage he could manage to show.

    “Look!” Rio said brightly. “The store embroidered it for free!” Agatha gasped, hands flying to her mouth. “Oh Señor! How guapo you look!” She practically snatched the rabbit into her arms, hugging him like the most precious gift.

    Rio tilted her head, “…Agatha. Are you crying?”

    “No!” Agatha swiped at her eyes far too dramatically to be convincing. “I just have… dust. An overly aggressive dust. Leave me alone!” Rio laughed as she looked through the bags. “Well then, the dust better prepare itself, because I found treasures.” She pulled out a tiny rain jacket complete with a bright green stegosaurus hood. “Look! dino raincoat!” Next came socks patterned with triceratops, hair clips shaped like pterodactyls, sleep sacks with cartoon tyrannosaurs, and finally, a soft jumper printed with bunnies. “This one,” Rio glanced up with a knowing smile, “is because I knew you’d like it.”

    “And last but not least…” Rio reached into the final bag and pulled out a small plushie. It was a security blanket: a smiling blue triceratops head attached to the corner of a soft cotton square. She held it up proudly, shaking it a little so the tiny horns wobbled.

    Agatha let out a tearful laughter, as she took the security blanket in her hands.

    And for a moment Rio looked at her wife. All of her. Her blue teary eyes, her brown hair falling off her clip, her belly button peeking out from under the too tight shirt. Her eyes settled for a moment on her belly, her child. Their child. The one they wanted for so long. The sleepless night after each round of ivf, the cries when it worked… and when it didn’t …. All of it. She remembered their wedding day, but also their fights, Agatha’s gifts, but also Agatha’s worries… of being a good wife… of being a good mother. She not only looked at Agatha, but she looked at her souls, their bond, the blue triceratops blanket clutched in her hand, their baby.
