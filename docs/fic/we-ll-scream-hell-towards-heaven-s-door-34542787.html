“Make way! Kneel for the Mistress of Hell, the Shepherd of Fire!”

 

Lena strode through the burning wasteland she now called home -- that she had called home for nearly four centuries. Passing through the kneeling masses of demon underlings, she did not acknowledge them. They were here to serve her. They were not her friends -- her people. She had lost them all long ago when she had been cast out of her real home.

 

Her boots barely made a sound as she picked her way across the rocky cavern. Flames shot up in volcanic eruptions on either side of the trail that led to her throne. 

 

Her throne was the one thing in Hell that she actually liked -- well, that and her two Head Demons, Sam and Andrea. The throne reminded her of the one from that Earth television show, Game of Thrones, all iron and spiked and imposing. When she sat on it, she knew just how intimidating she looked, and though she took no pleasure in damning souls for the rest of eternity, when she came across a particularly sadistic one -- someone who truly deserved to burn -- she enjoyed the fact that she looked the role of Queen of the Damned.

 

Lena loved Earth television and culture. It was her guilty pleasure. Whenever there was a soul in the land of the humans that needed to be brought below, she always chose to collect it herself in lieu of sending one of her minions. She couldn’t explain what it was about the human world that entranced her so -- their silly customs, their race against the inevitable passage of time, their laughable philosophies -- something about it was so quaint and wholesome when contrasted with the black pit of decay she called home.

 

Sam and Andrea stood on either side of her great Iron Throne, rigid and formidable. They were her bodyguards, her confidants, her right-hand fiends. They were also a couple, which Lena thought was absolutely adorable. The thought of two wretched souls still finding mutual comfort in a cursed existence such as this one showed her that hope could still be found in the darkest of places.

 

Tucking her black, leathery wings behind her, she smoothed her obsidian silk suit as she sat down. 

 

She nodded to Sam and Andrea, perching her arms on the coal-black iron seat regally. They each nodded to her in turn, eyes glowing crimson in the dim light of the fire.

 

“Mistress,” they murmured with a bow of their heads. 

 

Lena sighed. For over four hundred years, she had served as the ruler of all unredeemed souls, purged from the universe and destined to live in torment until the end of time. 

 

It was not exactly a job she relished. Nor one that she had applied for. No, much like the damned who knelt in front of her or burned below her feet, she had been cast down here, forced into her plight by her mother, Lillian, ruler of all the universe and creator of the heavens.

 

What humans referred to as “God.” She snickered to herself. What a charming notion. Humans really were such a delight when they weren’t murdering, raping, and pillaging each other.

 

“Bring in the first sinner,” she sighed, crossing her left leg over her right and sitting up straight. 

 

A door opened to her right and she heard the tell-tale sound of clinking chains, breathless begging, dragging feet. A pair of demons, winged and terrible, deposited a creature at the foot of her chair, a blubbering mess that looked somewhat like a wild boar, only with too many eyes and several rows of teeth.

 

“Articus,” she began coldly. She had to be cold. It was the only way she had figured how to get through damning others for the rest of time. “You have been brought to me under judgement from Queen Lillian, that you shall suffer punishment until the universe burns out, for your crimes in the land of the living.”

 

“P-Please, please, Mistress,” the hog-like creature wailed, “Have mercy, my lady. Have mercy!”

 

“A moral universe requires judgement and retribution,” she replied icily, “not mercy. For your crimes, there must be punishment. For your violence against others, I hereby sentence you to the seventh circle. Take him away.”

 

Gesturing to the demons flanking the piggish creature, she sat back in her chair, drained from the interaction. She always felt so drained after a sentencing. She needed a vacation from the Underworld. She perked up slightly. 

 

A vacation sounded lovely, but where could she go? Purgatory was too cold this time of the millennium. She couldn’t show her face in Draco II after that mishap with the Dragorian Queen…they were still finding pieces of her scattered across Ursa Minor.

 

It came to her suddenly. Earth! Of course! She could go up to the land of the living, to the pale blue dot, and live amongst the humans for a while! Travel around, catch some sin, ruin a few ladies for humans after she had been with them...she giggled at the thought.

 

Rising quickly from her throne, she marched through the antechamber to her left, which led to the Portal of Existence -- the gateway to the living. Sam and Andrea marched quickly behind her.

 

“Mistress,” Andrea called, her boots thumping against the magma-riddled stone. “Mistress, is everything alright?”

 

Lena grinned as she reached the portal, turning the dial to Earth. 

 

“Everything is fine, my fiends.” The portal glowed red and she turned to face the pair. “I’m taking a vacation. You guys keep an eye on the hellhounds, and don’t let the succubus out without a silver stake on hand. And if you drink the last of the holy water? Please replace it. Even the Mistress of Hell likes to get drunk sometimes.”

 

And with that, she stepped backward through the portal, leaving Sam and Andrea looking shocked in her wake.

 

 

Lena landed in the heart of National City. There was something about this place that had called to her. L.A. was great for sinning, New York was good for entertainment, but National City was right on the water and had a gorgeous art scene -- and a rather large gathering of Satanists. If she was going to be in the land of the living, she might as well go where she would be welcomed, if her true identity was found out.

 

Speaking of which, she thought, closing her eyes briefly. Her wings grew invisible and she felt her eyes burn as they lost their amber glow. Checking in the mirror of a parked car next to her, she saw that she hadn’t exactly gotten the transformation right. One of her eyes was green and the other was blue. Oh well, she thought with a shrug. Humans weren’t perfect anyway.

 

Now, she just needed a set of wheels…

 

Buttoning her suit jacket, she stepped out into the street. This was the business district. If she was going to find a vehicle worthy of her, it would be here. Sure enough, amidst the honking of horns, a sleek black Audi R8 rolled up to the stop sign next to her. Smirking to herself, she made her way to the driver’s window and knocked.

 

A man in a business suit turned down his classic rock as he rolled down the window. 

 

“Can I help you?” He asked, looking her up and down ravenously.

 

“Yes,” she replied, her voice like tinted glass as she looked him in the eye. “You want to give me your car.”

 

The man’s eyes glazed over and his mouth hung open lazily.

 

“I...want to give you my car,” he replied listlessly. 

 

“Aw, thank you, that’s so kind!” Lena replied cheerily, opening the car door so he could step out. The light turned green as she slid into the driver’s seat and she gunned the engine, leaving the man standing idly in the middle of the street, a dumbstruck look still plastered to his face.

 

 

Lena woke the next morning between two women from the nightclub she had attended last night and she immediately dressed and left the penthouse she had “borrowed” from the gentleman in the VIP section who had had the stupid idea to hit on her. She wasn’t fond of morning afters, awkward small talk over coffee, or the walk of shame. The trysts meant nothing and so she could not be expected to treat it like more than that. 

 

As she strolled down the streets in the early morning light, black sunglasses fixed to her face, she caught sight of a cute blonde girl, carrying a cupholder full of coffee in one hand and texting furiously with the other. Her black framed glasses kept sliding to the end of her nose as she stared down at her phone and she kept pushing them back up with the thumb she was using to text.

 

As the girl approached the crosswalk across the street from the Catco building, she started to walk into the street as a large dumptruck sped down the street toward the intersection.

 

For fuck’s sake, Lena thought, rolling her eyes. Another one of her Father’s ‘brilliant’ creations he had been so proud of, about to meet her end with a splat.

 

She sighed. She was a sucker for a pretty face. 

 

Spreading her wings, black and colossal, she flapped her way into the sky, racing towards the oblivious girl, who looked up as the dumptruck started honking and froze in the middle of the street.

 

A screeching sound followed as the truck tried to slam on its brakes and Lena grabbed the girl out of harm’s way as the massive vehicle slid past in a hale of smoke from the tires.

 

Lena heard the girl screaming over the rush of the wind and the heavy flapping of her wings. Landing gently in a park not far from the Catco building, where she assumed the girl worked by the badge on her cardigan, she set the girl down softly in the grass.

 

“What -- what are you?” The girl asked shakily, taking a step back from Lena as she folded her wings neatly behind her and disappeared them.

 

“You’re welcome,” she replied, dusting herself off with a roll of her eyes. Ungrateful, the lot of them. 

 

“Oh gosh, I’m so sorry,” the girl apologised, fiddling with her glasses nervously. “Really, that was so rude of me. Yes! Thank you! Thank you so much for -- for saving my life. I mean, gosh! That was so close. I would have been roadkill if it weren’t for you…”

 

Lena couldn’t help it. She smiled. The way this girl was stumbling over her words, rambling along, completely forgetting to be afraid in the wake of potentially being rude. It was, well, adorable. Especially when the crinkle between her brows appeared and her nose scrunched up.

 

“...just my boss, Ms. Grant, she’s very demanding and she’s always on me about making sure her coffee is hot and I keep telling her that it doesn’t matter when I buy it or how hot it is when I do if she’s going to let it sit and get cold anyway! -- Well, I don’t actually say that to her -- she’s kind of scary actually -- but I think it at her really hard and...oh gosh, it’s definitely going to be cold now and she’s going to fire me for the third time this week!”

 

Lena laughed loudly. Humans were such amusing creatures -- worrying over the temperature of coffee of all things. Continuing to giggle, she reached out and popped the lids off the cups and blew a breath of fire into their depths until they started to steam and bubble. Then, she replaced the lids and stepped back from the girl, who was staring at her in amazement.

 

Lena realised the girl wasn’t afraid of her, and it filled her with warmth. 

 

“Who are you,” the blonde asked breathlessly.

 

“Well that’s an improvement over ‘what are you’,” Lena said with a mischievous arch of her sharp brow.

 

Stepping forward, she reached out a pale hand to the girl.

 

“Lena Luthor, Mistress of Hell, Queen of the Damned, Shepherd of the Eternal Flame. And you are?”

 

The girl was silent for a moment, mouth hanging open, before she reached out and took the offered hand.

 

“Um. I’m Kara -- Kara Danvers. Journalist, amateur painter, and Eater of the Potsticker.”

 

Lena threw her another flirtatious smirk. “It’s nice to meet you, Ms. Danvers.”

 

Kara just continued to stare at her in disbelief. Lena could appreciate it though. It wasn’t every day that the ruler of the Underworld came to Earth and saved your life.

 

Lena leaned in conspiratorially, lifting an eyebrow. “Didn’t you have a boss to deliver those coffees too?” She asked in a voice just above a whisper.

 

Kara jolted at the reminder and began flushing a pretty pink. “Oh gosh, yes! Ms. Grant is probably waiting...So sorry, I don’t know why I was staring like that. You just -- you’re very pretty.”

 

She gathered up her things and started to walk away, still looking at Lena.

 

“Is that like, a part of the whole devil thing? You look good so people can’t resist you? Because I think--”

 

“Kara, watch out!” Lena called, as the blonde nearly backed up into the fountain at the center of the park. Kara turned and dodged just in time with a loud, “Whoops!”

 

“Sorry,” she called, with a wave. “I’m okay!”

 

Lena snickered and sauntered over to the girl, falling into step with her, side by side. 

 

“How about I give you a lift to your job?” She asked, offering her arms. 

 

Kara flushed even harder. “Oh gosh, no that’s okay. It’s just across the street.”

 

Lena nodded. “Yes, but I certainly wouldn’t want you to fall down an elevator shaft or step into traffic again. You seem prone to these sorts of incidents.”

 

Kara ducked her head and fiddled with her glasses with the hand holding her phone. She lost her grip on it and it dropped toward the ground. Lena grabbed it out of midair with lightning fast reflexes and held it out to her.

 

“See what I mean?” Lena smirked, opening her arms again. 

 

Kara chewed her lip for a moment, considering. 

 

“You’re not gonna like, drag me to hell or something, right?”

 

A surprise bubbled out of Lena’s chest at the question. 

 

“No, darling,” she said with a snicker. “I’m on vacation, so you’re safe.”

 

Kara climbed into her arms and Lena’s wings reappeared, flapping in the early morning breeze as she rose over the trees and headed toward the Catco building. 

 

“You came to Earth on vacation?” Kara shouted over the wind. “Why?”

 

“I like it here,” Lena whispered into her ear. She felt the blonde shudder in her arms. “The view is spectacular.”

 

Kara looked up to see Lena was staring down at her, a seductive grin on her face.

 

“Oh gosh,” she said, reaching up to mess with her glasses. Lena realised it was a nervous tic. Adorable. 

 

Kara pointed out the balcony nearest to her office and Lena landed with a gentle thump, folding her wings behind her.

 

“Well,” Kara began nervously, ducking her head. “Thanks for the ride. And, you know, for saving my life.”

 

Lena smiled gently. “You’re welcome. I trust you can make it inside without falling off the balcony?”

 

Kara laughed nervously. “Yes! Yeah. Yes, I should -- I should be able to handle that.”

 

Lena reached out a hand and took Kara’s, pressing a red-lipped kiss to the back of it. 

 

“I hope this isn’t the last time we talk, Ms. Danvers.”

 

Kara stared up at her in awestruck surprise.

 

“I hope not either.”

 

 

As Lena frittered away the day visiting museums, reading books, and appreciating all the world of humankind had to offer, she realised that Catco was a magazine, and Kara had told her she was a journalist. What if Kara printed a story about her rescue? What if her identity got out? Oh, Mother would not be pleased about that.

 

She decided she needed to see Kara again to suggest that she not print any mention of this story, nor tell anyone about it.

 

Arming herself with several orders of potstickers, she waited outside of the Catco building at the end of the business day. Why she had brought potstickers along to bribe Kara when she had the literal power of suggestion was something she didn’t want to think about.

 

Ok, so she liked the girl, so what? She was pretty -- cute, really. The way she flushed in embarrassment or rambled nervously. The way her hair glowed golden like the sun and her eyes shone blue like the ocean. The way she fiddled with her glasses and her clumsy nature. The fact that she hadn’t been afraid of Lena, even seeing her true form. 

 

Lena was intrigued by her. She liked her. It didn’t mean anything. And even if it did, fuck off! She was on vacation.

 

The sight of Kara exiting the building with a younger brunette brought her out of her thoughts. 

 

Pushing off of her car, she stepped toward the blonde, who froze when she caught sight of Lena.

 

“Hello, Kara,” Lena greeted with a flirty smile. “I brought potstickers.”

 

She gestured toward her car and the younger brunette’s eyes rounded wide and she whistled. “Wow, sweet ride! Kara, who’s your rich friend and does she need a sugar baby?”

 

Kara opened her mouth but only mere syllables fell out in a nonsensical manner. Lena held out her hand to the girl. 

 

“Lena Luthor. I’m a friend of Kara’s. Not currently looking for a sugar baby, but I’m open to the possibility,” she added with a dark grin.

 

“Nia,” Nia said, shaking her hand. “You let me know if that changes on the sugar baby front.” Turning to Kara, she gave her a hug. “Have fun with your friend, or sugar mommy or dom or whatever she is.”

 

Lena laughed and Kara just stood there sputtering as Nia walked down into the metro station.

 

“I like her,” Lena giggled, staring after the younger woman. “She reminds me of my friend Sam.”

 

Kara finally seemed to gather her bearings and joined Lena by the sleek Audi. 

 

“You know when you said you hoped we would talk again, I didn’t think you actually meant today,” she said with a nervous laugh.

 

Lena opened the car door and Kara hesitantly slid in. As Lena joined her, she started the engine and pulled out into traffic, heading for the ocean. 

 

“I didn’t intend to either,” she replied, eyes on the cars around her. “But it occurred to me that I rescued a journalist for a news magazine and it seemed prudent that I come see you to ask you not to print my identity.”

 

Kara laughed loudly, leaning forward in her seat. 

 

“I’m sorry,” she said, wiping her eyes, “it’s just -- you actually think I would print a story about how the literal devil from Hell saved my life and heated up my coffee with her firebreath?? Do you think I want to be laughed out of my entire profession?” 

 

Lena sagged in relief. Ok, good, she was in the clear.

 

“No, I suppose that would be rather foolish, wouldn’t it?” She replied, parking the car in front of a pier and hopping out.

 

Kara grabbed the bag of food, and Lena grabbed a blanket from the trunk, heading down to the beach. 

 

“So, what are we doing then? If I’m not writing about you and you have nothing to worry about, I mean.”

 

Lena trudged through the sand in her black suit and boots. She probably should have changed. Oh, well. Too late now. 

 

“I thought maybe we could have dinner,” she replied, spreading out the blanket and taking a seat, gesturing for Kara to join her. Kara sat down next to her, opening the bag and nearly drooling at the scent of potstickers that came wafting out. 

 

“Ohhhh you are the devil,” she moaned, opening a container of them eagerly.

 

Lena laughed lightly and grabbed the mei fun she had purchased for herself. (Purchased was a nice way of saying ‘suggested the chinese restaurant make it for her with her devil powers’.)

 

“I must say, you’re taking the whole Mistress of Hell thing very well,” she said, taking a bite of her noodles. “I don’t know whether to be impressed or concerned about your mental state.”

 

Kara shrugged, shoveling more dumplings into her mouth. “The world is already so weird,” she said around a mouthful. “Why wouldn’t there be demons and gods and devils and ghosts?”

 

Lena stared at her in amazement. She had spent the last 400 years observing humans, and nothing in her studying had prepared her for the simple acquiescence to the existence of an Underworld. Sure, man liked to say they believed in gods and monsters from below, but in the face of their existence? Complete and steadfast disbelief. Yet here was a girl who was wholeheartedly accepting it with a shrug and just. Sitting here. Eating Chinese food with the actual Princess of Darkness. Lena couldn’t believe it. 

 

Warmth filled her chest. Not only was she accepting Lena, but she actually seemed to like her? Or it seemed like that with the way she was chatting about life and her job and anything that seemed to come into her mind, smiling all the while.

 

“So,” Kara said suddenly, startling her from her thoughts. “You’re on vacation, right? What are your plans? What do you want to see? I mean, like, what are you doing this weekend, for instance?”

 

Lena shrugged. “I don’t really know. I just kind of take it day by day. See where it takes me.”

 

Kara brightened considerably. “You should hang out with me! My sister and I host a game night with all of our friends, and I feel like you would probably dominate at trivia challenges, considering you’re this all-powerful otherworldly being. You can be on my team and there’s music and drinks and good company -- it’ll be great!”

 

Lena opened her mouth to accept -- any excuse to spend more time with this fascinating woman was fine with her -- when Kara seemed to get self-conscious and began furiously backpedaling.

 

“Unless, of course -- that’s probably not your scene, is it? I mean, game night? Pfft. That’s -- gosh, that’s stupid. You probably, like, go to fancy clubs or maybe concerts or the opera or -- or like, sex dungeons, probably, right?”

 

Lena giggled, her nose and eyes scrunching in a way that they hadn’t since she’d been cast out of Heaven. This girl was something else. 

 

“While I have been known to visit the occasional well-appointed sex dungeon, game night actually sounds very lovely and low-key. I’d love to come.”

 

“Really?!” Kara squealed in disbelief. “Oh wow, okay, cool. Cool, cool, cool. That’s really just so--”

 

“Cool?” Lena offered with a grin. “Just remember, no one can know who I really am, do you understand?”

 

“Got it. No problem.”

 

 

“Hey! It’s Kara’s mommy-domme everyone!”

 

Nia’s voice rose high above the din as Lena stepped through the door and into Kara’s apartment. A group of people were spread out over couches, chairs, and on the floor in the living room as Lena made her way over to the empty seat Kara showed her.

 

A woman sitting across from her with warm brown skin gave her a brilliant smile and reached across the table to shake her hand.

 

“Hi, I’m Kelly. This is my wife, Alex.”

 

Lena smiled, opening her mouth to reply when Kara dropped onto the couch next to her. 

 

“Alex is my sister,” she explained. The woman in question, Alex, was staring at her suspiciously, probably wondering where the hell Kara had found her and what she wanted with her sister. Given her true identity, Lena could hardly blame her and she suppressed a smirk, offering instead a polite nod toward the other woman.

 

“And you’ve met Nia,” Kara continued, gesturing toward the excitable young woman tucked onto the floor in front of the table, sifting through the games, a tan man sitting next to her quietly. “That’s her boyfriend Querl, but we all call him Brainy on account of the fact that he’s, well, brainy.”

 

Lena smiled at him and he gave her a curt but polite nod back. Not a particularly loquacious type, she thought, though something about him felt kind and accepting -- almost brotherly, as if she’d known him her whole life.

 

“Guys, this is Lena,” Kara said, addressing the room as a whole. “And no, she’s not my dominatrix or my sugar mommy, thank you very much Nia for putting that idea in everyone’s heads. She’s my friend, and I invited her here to meet all of you so be nice!”

 

“Where did you two meet?” Alex asked suspiciously, her eyes narrowed at Lena. 

 

Kara looked to Lena helplessly. “Uh…”

 

Clearly the blonde hadn’t actually thought of any kind of cover story or explanation as to where Lena had come from or how they knew each other. Lena almost put her head in her hands at the terrified look Kara was sending her. If they didn’t think Lena was some kind of sexual deviant in control of Kara before, they probably did now. 

 

Lena figured honesty was the best policy in this situation.

 

“I saved her life, actually,” she offered coolly, taking a sip of the wine that Kelly poured for her. 

 

Kara nodded vigorously. “Yep! That’s -- you guys know how I’m always on my phone for Ms. Grant in the mornings, right? Well, I nearly went splat in front of a big truck, but Lena -- she, well -- uh...she... pulled me back at the last second.”

 

Lena had seen how close Kara had come to forming the word ‘flew’ and breathed an internal sigh of relief that the blonde had managed to cover for it.

 

“Wow,” Kelly breathed in concern. “Kara, you’ve got to be more careful! What if Lena hadn’t been there? You could have died!”

 

Kara nodded, looking at Lena. “But luckily, my knight in shining -- uh -- silk, was there to rescue me.”

 

There was a warmth in Kara’s eyes, something beyond just gratitude. Lena felt it in the way the other woman reached out and squeezed her hand. It sort of looked like -- but that wasn’t possible. 

 

Lena was a demon, the literal goddess of the grave. There was no way that soft, gentle look in Kara’s eyes was meant for her, no matter how badly she wished it was.

 

 

 

As the night wound down and the guests began to make their way home, Lena walked out to Kara’s balcony to stretch her wings. They got cramped if she kept them folded for too long, and she hummed gently as they stretched and extended, before folding themselves in again.

 

The door to the balcony slid open and Kara joined her, rubbing her arms against the cold of the night. Lena waved her hand, and suddenly between them there was a bright orange flame -- a gentle ball of balmy fire. 

 

“Thanks,” Kara said with an awestruck smile, putting her hands up to the flame to keep warm. 

 

“Careful you don’t burn yourself,” Lena warned, well aware of how clumsy the other girl was.

 

“I’m not afraid,” Kara replied quietly, and suddenly Lena wasn’t sure they were still talking about the flame. Clearing her throat, she sat down on the bench and looked out at the city below. Kara followed her and sat down next to her, the little ball of fire trailing behind her.

 

“So how did you become the Mistress of Hell? You don’t seem particularly evil to me.”

 

Lena leaned back and crossed her legs, one over the other. She thought about delivering a funny quip, something about Kara not knowing how evil she could be or whatever, but something about the earnest look in Kara’s eyes -- the warm comfort of her company -- made Lena want to be honest.

 

“Truthfully? I didn’t pick the job,” she began with a sigh, settling back against the bench. “Frankly, I...I don’t like it. But I was next in line to rule after my mother passed -- Father said my brother, Lex, didn’t have the temperament to be in charge of the cosmos -- and he didn’t like that. So, he framed me for something. And I was cast out. Mother wouldn’t listen. She had always favoured Lex over me, and she never liked that Father wanted me to replace her when the time came.”

 

Kara sat quietly, taking in every word with rapt attention. “What did he do? To frame you, I mean.”

 

Lena bowed her head. “He killed an angel. A lovely girl named Eve. To kill an angel is the highest crime in Heaven. The only reason I was able to escape being atomised is because of who my parents are. So instead, I was forced from my home -- forced to an eternity of damning souls to pain and suffering until the end of time.”

 

Kara reached out and took her hand, squeezing it gently. “I’m so sorry. That’s horrible!”

 

Lena nodded. “It’s not all bad, I suppose. I have a few friends down there -- fellow demons. And my hellhound, Jack. Small comforts in a place where comfort is hard to come by. And of course, with access to the Portal of Existence, I can travel anywhere I like, just so long as I don’t stay gone for too long.”

 

Kara shook her head, puzzled. “The Portal of Existence?” 

 

“It’s the doorway from my world into the land of the living,” Lena explained, stroking Kara’s hand with her thumb absentmindedly. “Through it, I can travel to any realm, any world, any universe, any time I like.”

 

Kara’s eyebrows shot up. “That sounds amazing! You must get to see all kinds of cool stuff!”

 

Lena tilted her head back and forth. “Yes and no. Mostly I’m there to collect the souls of the damned and they’re never too happy to see me, as you can imagine.
