Summary

Lena allowed herself to fall in love.

Fully and wholly.

No boundaries or obstacles put in place. No need to earn or prove like Lena had demanded of so many others after being burnt too many times to count.

Lena fell in love with a perfect stranger on a crashing plane.

Chapter 1

“I love you.”

It was this declaration, more than her impending death on a plane that she wasn’t supposed to be on, that truly shocked her. 

(Lena fleetingly acknowledged how this revelation would probably horrify her therapist and provide more than enough material to fuel her weekly therapy sessions for a good year - though judging from the smoke billowing off the engine out the window it was unlikely that she would be spending anymore time unpacking the trauma of her childhood.)

It took about five seconds for Lena’s brain to reboot; blinking rapidly at the blonde woman sitting next to her in the aisle seat, the middle seat empty - Lena had been denied first class so had treated herself to an additional seat next to her own to ensure she wouldn’t have to socialise. 

The blonde smiled warm and bright at her - it was a smile that tempered the fear and adrenaline pounding through her veins. It was a beautiful smile that on any other occasion would have turned Lena into a giddy, flushing gay mess that would have Sam, Jess and Andrea rolling their eyes and muttering a ‘useless lesbian’ under their breaths. It was a breathtaking grin that would have, for the blissful seconds that it was directed towards her, cast out all the dark shadows that had accumulated inside the youngest Luthor. 

But it wasn’t just any other day. 

Today, she was going to die.

It was a nice view to look at on her way out though, which is why Lena forced out a response, voice coming out high and strangled as the plane shimmied and vibrated, battered by the wind currents as the pilots attempted to glide the plane safely in its unplanned descent.

“Excuse me?”

“I love you.” The blonde repeated, blue eyes twinkling with sincerity.

Lena’s heart fluttered in her chest at the sight of those blue, blue, blue eyes, feeling for a flash of a second something different to dread. 

“Did you hit your head?” Lena inquired, leaning forward to check for blown pupils or any other signs of concussion. There had been a particularly bad bout of turbulence when the engine had ‘exploded’, and Lena worried that the tossing and turning had led to her companion whacking their head too hard.

“No.” The blonde laughed, the sound stilted and strange, not just because of how it contrasted with the panicked din of distress that pervaded the cabin, but because it didn’t fit the blonde. It ended too quickly, wasn’t as full bellied as Lena would expect from someone with eyes that crinkled at the sides with such joy and smiled like it was her default setting. “I have a suggestion. A kind of crazy one.” 

“Crazy ideas are the best kind of ideas.” Lena replied, quoting the brother she had once loved from her childhood. “Make your pitch.”

The blonde’s brow furrowed heavily, her lips pursing into a thin line - half due to concentrating on her next words and half due to the horrendous shudder that ran through the plane. 

“We’re most likely going to die.” Lena’s companion declared.

“Not the best of opening lines,” Lena tsked even as her fingers curled around the edge of her seat’s armrests, nails digging in - falsely believing that holding onto something would ground her despite being thousands upon thousands of feet in the air. “I would suggest you stay away from sales.”

“You’re funny, I love that.” The blonde chuckled, short and sweet but far more genuine than her previous attempt at displaying humour. The blonde licked her lips, glancing only for a second towards the window where the grey smoke had turned a shadowy black. “We’re most likely going to die and I will be honest it’s not something I’ve really thought about.” The blonde winced suddenly, shaking her head in self-admonishment, “Actually, that’s a lie. I’ve thought about it a lot. Didn’t think it would happen this way. Air travel is the safest way to travel.”

Lena quirked an eyebrow at the blonde’s rambling monologue and forced honesty that was successful in distracting the youngest Luthor from her current situation to a certain degree. She didn’t forget that she was on a crashing plane or was probably minutes away from her own demise - it’s just that it all shifted into the background. It was merely the backdrop or setting to this nervous blonde with dazzling blue eyes and shy words.

“ Anyway ,” The blonde continued, dragging out the word to emphasise her return to the point, “when I do think about it, I had always hoped I would be old and that I would be…” Lena’s companion let out a shaky breath and whispered, “in the arms or at least with someone I loved. No one wants to die alone. And most want to die with someone they love. So… I was thinking… why not?”

“Why not- what?” Lena prompted, unable to connect the dots that had been thrown haphazardly out by her companion.

The blonde shrugged stiffly - she was sat upright, shoulders pressed into her seat as if to embed herself into it, the shrug was therefore filled with effort as it rebelled against the natural push of her muscles. “Love someone. We’re going to die, why not die with someone you love?”

Lena’s jaw dropped for the second time in less than a minute, if she had felt a smidge more comfortable prying her fingers away from the armrest she would have pointed an accusing finger at her own chest. “Me, you mean?” Lena questioned in complete disbelief.

Another bright smile that was veering dangerously towards charming. “If you’re comfortable with that.”

“You can’t just decide to love a stranger because you’re about to die.” Lena retorted with a weary shake of her head, “No one can love someone they’ve never met. Someone they don’t know.”

The blonde’s smile faded at that, blue eyes turning sharper than anyone would expect from a beautiful young woman that emanated an air of hope and naivete. 

“I can love you.” The blonde said so seriously that Lena’s breath caught in her throat.

“You don’t know me.” Lena muttered, though it was half-hearted.

“So what? I love you.” The blonde proclaimed with her now signature sincerity. “ I mean it. In this moment, right now, I love you. I love you with everything I have and will ever be. I will love you for the rest of my life.” 

Lena’s gaze shifted away from those blue eyes that seemed to see her in a way no one else ever had, unable to bear how her soul felt exposed under the blonde’s eyes and how those declarations of love left her flushed and gave her a sense of security she could never have anticipated. 

Lena could see glimpses of the people in the surrounding couple of aisles, snapshots of them, their relationships and their final moments. 

In the row across from her was a family: middle-aged parents wrapping protective arms around their children, a boy and girl that didn’t understand what was happening other than they were seeing genuine fear on their Mum and Dad’s faces for the first time in their lives. In front of them was an elderly couple, holding hands and resting their heads against one another. Behind them was an older mother with her daughter, they were talking into a phone held between them - wanting to share their last minutes with whichever last piece of family they were missing. 

 “Now, isn’t that comforting to hear?” The blonde asked.

And Lena… Lena couldn’t deny that it was. It was comforting to be loved. To know someone loved her. That she wasn’t going to die with a random stranger but with someone who loved her. 

It was a crazy suggestion but it was also the single greatest gift Lena had ever received.

Lena took a deep breath, eyes moving back to meet curious and (Lena could see it now) fearful blue. Gathering her courage, Lena released her white knuckle hold on her armrest and reached out for the blonde’s hand that met hers exactly halfway over the empty seat between them. Their fingers tangled together effortlessly, palms pressed against one another with ease despite how both of them trembled with terror.

Lena smiled at the blonde, stopping her honed emotional guards from surging up. Instead, she allowed herself to experience wave after wave of appreciation for the woman across from her, permitted it to rise and swell. She encouraged it to grow, nourished it with thoughts of affection and images of her companion’s smile. She prevented her usual boxing process from swinging into action, forcibly shutting down the factory and granting chaos freedom to reign supreme.

Lena allowed herself to fall in love.

Fully and wholly.

No boundaries or obstacles put in place. No need to earn or prove like Lena had demanded of so many others after being burnt too many times to count.

Lena fell in love with a perfect stranger on a crashing plane.

“I love you too…” Lena murmured, surprising even herself with how earnest she sounded, though her conviction wavered with the realisation she did not know the name of the love of her life. “Uh….”

“Kara. Kara Danvers.” The blonde supplied, seamlessly intuiting what Lena required.

“Lena. My name’s Lena Luthor.” Lena replied, squeezing Kara’s hand in her own and feeling the writer’s callus that marked the fingers she was intertwined with. “I love you, Kara Danvers. With everything I have and will ever be. I will love you for the rest of my life.” Lena repeated the sentiment truthfully and with her own soft timbre. 

Kara beamed at her, immediately taking to stroking Lena’s knuckles with her curled thumb when the plane suddenly dropped for a disorienting number of seconds.

Screams and yelps suffused the air, prompting Lena to close her eyes and clench her jaw, breathing harshly through her nose.

The fear returned then, though its hold on her wasn’t as strong and overwhelming as it had been prior to having Kara’s hand in her own and declarations of love echoing in her ears. The plane halted its freefall but the terror that had spread through the passengers didn’t dissipate - everyone acutely aware of how fast the ground was rushing up to greet them and welcome them back with an unforgiving embrace.

“Tell me something about yourself, darling.” Lena pleaded, needing Kara's voice again.

“What do you want to know?” Kara asked in one rushed exhale that would have been unintelligible if Lena wasn’t tuned into her so intensely.

“We’re going to die so... Something you’ve never told anyone.” Lena requested through gritted teeth and sharp breaths to control her growing panic. She needed Kara to speak again, needed to be able to focus wholly on her again, to give the person she had promised to love her full attention again. “Something you’ve never shared as you thought people would judge you for it. Something that scares you about yourself. Tell me something with the full knowledge that I will still love you afterwards.”

For a long beat, Lena was left only with the sounds of the plane and its people. The lack of Kara’s response was far more frightening and climactic than Lena expected the plane’s inevitable collision with the Earth would be. Losing her one source of comfort was far more cruel than not having it to start with.

“I still haven’t forgiven my cousin for abandoning me after my parents died.” Kara’s whispered admission was filled with so much heartbreak and shame that Lena’s personal worries vanished in an instant. Lena blinked her eyes open as her head turned sharply to look at the woman she loved. 

“I wanted my family, I wanted to be around someone who knew them and would help me remember them and he… wasn’t there.” Kara murmured, curling forward on herself, her hand going limp in Lena’s as if she wanted to pull it in and create a protective ball - Lena gripped her hand tighter in turn, lifting it up to place a tender, encouraging kiss to the back of it. Kara let out a shaky breath at Lena’s action, lifting her gaze from the floor to meet Lena’s affectionate green eyes. “I know he was young and had his own life and his own family that he wanted to start. But I never felt more unwanted or worthless than when he handed me over to a different family. I simply wasn’t worth the pain of keeping.”

Lena inhaled a shaky breath at that, her heart breaking in a thousand unfathomable ways at Kara’s confession even as every single piece of it yelled that it would happily be broken in a billion other ways for the mere chance to be cherished by the blonde next to her. 

“Kara,” Lena said slowly, her tone firm and clear, lacking all the undertones of fear that had marked her previous words. “I love you and I would take all the pain in the world to keep you for a moment longer.”

Kara’s mouth opened and closed, blue eyes tearing up with relief and joy that made any verbalised gratitude completely unnecessary. 

Lena placed another kiss - this one to convey understanding and acceptance - to Kara’s hand before returning their twined fingers back to the mid-space between them, restoring their equilibrium.

“Your turn.” Kara declared, her thumb returning to its soothing circular motion. “I promise I will love you just as much afterwards.”

Lena doubted that, every time she had bared her soul and its dark, sharpened edges they had merely served to sever her relationship with whomever had been exposed to it. Lena didn’t want that. Not this close to the end. 

Lena intended to offer up something half-hearted, some vulnerability she had discussed with friends and her therapist, one that she understood on a logical level wasn’t damning or incriminating.

She opened her mouth ready to share her rehearsed story but open blue eyes killed it before a single syllable could be spoken. Kara smiled at her just as soft and magnificent as before, this time Lena knew it was for her, knew that smile would be hers at the end and no one else’s. It was empowering in a way that the billions in her bank account could never be but it was also a double-edged sword. The power gave her courage, made her strong which meant the walls she had already pulled down to ensure she could love a stranger were ground to fine dust, leaving her most vulnerable parts bare. 

The dark edges of her soul were visible for all to see.

Therefore when Lena spoke it was not the rehearsed safety net story she had intended that came forth but a truth she had shared with no one. A truth she had never even consciously shared with herself. 

“I hate my mother for killing herself.” Lena declared.

The second she made her confession, Lena wanted to slap her hand over her mouth and never speak again but the dam had been ripped apart and the torrent of water was already sweeping through. 

“I hate her for leaving. I hate that she did it in front of me. I hate that she made me feel so powerless. I hate that she made me carry this guilt and trauma since I was four years old. I hate that she made me afraid of open water. But what I hate most is that she made me hate her,” The stream of words was slowing now which gave time for a choked sob to escape between clauses, “that I can never just think happy things about her because she did this thing that ripped me apart before I had a chance to form.”

Lena’s lip trembled and she blinked rapidly, desperately attempting to keep her tears from falling and her despair and self-loathing from consuming her.

“Lena,” Kara breathed, tugging gently at Lena’s hand that had, at some point, been placed in Kara’s lap and was now held by both her hands, two sets of thumbs working to ease the tension. “I was wrong, I don’t love you just as much.” 

Lena’s breath faltered, a hollow vacuum punched into her chest and on the precipice of turning into a black hole that would destroy everything that Lena was when- 

“I love you more.” Kara murmured before bending her head to press her own kiss to Lena’s pale hand - the adrenaline already pumping through Lena’s system kicking up a gear at the featherlight touch of Kara’s lips to her skin. Lena’s hand tingled and she was surprised, when Kara returned their hands to the seat between them, that there was no discernable mark where Kara’s lips had made contact.

“God, I love how cheesy you are.” Lena announced, only blushing slightly at her own honesty.

Kara’s entire expression brightened impossibly further at the compliment. “I love your green eyes.”

“I love your smile.” Lena returned.

“I love your honesty.” Kara shot back with a pleased twinkle in her blue eyes.

“I love your courage.” Lena muttered softly, so unbelievably grateful that Kara had spoken to her, gifted her this comfort in her final few minutes. 

“I love you, Lena.”

“I love you, Kara.”

And then everything went black.

Chapter 2

Lena awoke to a terrible headache and an agonising pain in her left shoulder that radiated right down to her fingertips. She awoke to a bright light beaming into her eye that made her wince and jerk away. She awoke to buzzing activity, shouts, hollers and constant movement.

She awoke - Lena quickly realised - in a hospital bed in the emergency department of National City General. 

She was still in the same clothes she had worn on the plane: dark jeans, black shirt and her favourite blue jacket that was a tad too long in the arms, coming down to cover her palms. She had hastily shoved these clothes on when she had decided to grab the earliest flight possible back to National City foregoing her business attire for the rare indulgence of practicality and comfort. 

She was actually meant to fly out that evening; she had even had her standard first class ticket already booked - having given up her private jet, handing it fully over to her mother a couple of years ago to further emphasise her stance on turning L-Corp into an environmentally friendly company. 

Lena, however, had finished her business deal a day early and was already missing her own bed. She had no one to stick around for and no one to particularly rush home to so it was merely creature comforts and the rationalisation that if she flew home earlier she would be more effective at work the next day. 

Lena was on the plane by a cruel twist of fate. 

If the deal had taken a little longer, if she had decided to actually be a tourist rather than rushing back home, if she had been more snobbish and refused the only economy class seats left, if, if, if… she wouldn’t be here, blinking up into the serious face of the emergency room doctor.

“You’re awake.” The doctor proclaimed unnecessarily with a dour and uninterested expression. “How are you feeling?”

“Like I’ve been in a plane crash.” Lena replied snarkily.

The doctor pursed his lips, unimpressed.

“My head and left arm hurt.” Lena sighed.

The doctor nodded agreeably at this, moving forward to carry out a quick assessment on Lena’s head and arm upon being given a consenting nod from the belligerent Luthor. Upon completing his inspection, he grabbed the record at the end of Lena’s bed and scrawled a few quick notes on the pages.

“You were knocked unconscious when the plane landed…” The doctor explained, pausing here to contemplate his choice of words. “Crash-landed.” The doctor amended before continuing, “Your arm is broken in two places, it will most likely need surgery but we’re in triage stage so it will be bumped until later. Another doctor will be along to set it, put it in a temporary sling and explain what to look out for in case of concussion, then you can be on your way. Once you’ve given your insurance details, of course.”

Lena blinked at the rapid-fire information and it took her a second to realise that the doctor before her was preparing to leave, satisfied that there was nothing additional requiring his attention.

“Wait!” Lena called out, grateful when the harried doctor stopped to turn back. “There was a blonde woman sat next to me - Kara Danvers - is she… please, tell me she’s okay…” Lena begged.

She knew she should probably ask how many survived or should stop wasting this doctor’s time so that he could get back to saving and treating those that might need it more than she did. But even as her mind swirled with pain and disconnected thoughts, there was only one thing that she could focus on and that was the woman that had loved her as the grim reaper came to take his due.

“I can’t discuss other patients.” The doctor replied with a blank face.

Lena inhaled sharply, pushing herself upright with her good arm, intent on getting up and searching every bed herself, whilst also throwing her name around and wielding the mighty power that came with it. 

They were at National City General Hospital, Lena could discern that much, L-Corp was one of the largest investors here and she had been given a tour of this very department two years ago when she had just taken over as CEO. One utterance of her name and Lena didn’t doubt that the whole hospital would snap to attention.

“Though…” The doctor murmured, the one word sufficient to halt Lena’s movements. “I can tell you that you were carried off the plane by a blonde woman who insisted that the paramedics take you to the hospital before running back in to save more people.”

If Lena was linked up to a heart monitor the doctor would have had physical evidence proving it was possible for a heart to skip a beat. Lena had been able to tell that Kara was kind and brave from their brief interaction but this… half of her couldn’t believe it and the other half was unsurprised. Kara was heroic and if Lena hadn’t already fallen for her this would have finished her off, would have ensured she hit the ground that marked her as in love .

“Did she… Is she…” Lena stammered.

“She pulled another five people from the plane before the flames became too much. She was brought in and rushed to surgery with severe burns to her right arm and shoulder from shielding the last person she saved. I can’t tell you anymore than that.” The doctor’s expression softened then, and Lena could now see that the tragedy of today had taken its toll, the doctor was detaching themselves to keep their head above water but they weren’t as untouched by what was happening as Lena initially presumed.

“Thank you.” Lena breathed out gratefully, even as her stomach twisted itself into knots with anxiety at Kara’s condition.

The doctor departed and Lena was left alone for the next handful of hours. Her phone had been in her bag on the plane, permanently lost now, leaving Lena untethered from the rest of the world and her mind unoccupied, only able to watch people rush to and fro in the gap of the curtains that kept her tucked away from sight in the emergency department. 

She considered going out and finding someone to give her a phone or telling them who she was but Lena didn’t have the strength in her to do so and she didn’t want to distract anyone whilst they were dealing with far more important matters. 

She was intermittently checked on by a nurse to ensure she wasn’t suffering a concussion until a far younger doctor appeared to set her arm and put it in a sling. He gave her some forms to fill in for her insurance and then asked the dreaded question, “Is there anyone we can call for you?”

Lena considered this. 

She had options… none of them, however, were particularly appealing.

Lena and her adoptive mother had still not mended the burnt bridge between them following Lena testifying against Lillian’s favoured child. 

With Lillian out of the running, that then left her four closest friends.

Jack, who she hadn’t spoken to in the last year - both of them too busy with their respective companies to stay in contact - he also lived on the other side of the country, making him the most impractical choice.

Sam was the most obvious person to call but Lena knew her best friend would be picking Ruby up from school at this time and she was loath to disrupt her routine. The youngest Luthor had already upturned Sam’s life less than a year ago by asking her to be L-Corp’s CFO and Lena couldn’t bear to make any further demands on the woman.

Her next best option was Jess, her secretary who had over the last year transitioned into a close friend and confidant - joining Lena, Sam and Andrea for drinks after work more often than not. Lena was acutely aware of this newly formed friendship and was anxious about destroying something so fragile. 

Then there was Andrea, her kind of ex and oldest friend. They had technically never been together but they had come close on a number of occasions. Each time, however, Andrea had run from her with cold feet and into the arms of another. Andrea had broken her heart more often than Lena dared to admit and the trust between them was tentative at best, which removed her as an option as well.

With a heavy sigh of acceptance, Lena had shaken her head before asking them to call her driver to pick her up.

 

 

Lena returned to the hospital the next day, dressed back in her business casual armour, her surgery arranged for a couple of weeks to sort her arm out. She strode back into the hospital, head held high and totally unrecognisable from the woman that had been tended to in the emergency department the day prior. It took merely the single raise of an intimidating eyebrow for her to discover the room allocated to Kara Danvers and a single call to the hospital chief to ensure all of Kara’s hospital bills would be taken care of.

Lena hesitated outside the room, tugging on the sleeves of her jacket and intermittently running her hands down her skirt to confirm the scarcity of creases. 

‘Was it weird to visit?’ Lena pondered. 

She had no frame of reference for her relations with the other woman - they were for all intents and purposes strangers with a level of intimacy that she shared with no one else in her life. 

Should she treat her like a one night stand? 

They had shared their night together and now it was time for her to gather up her clothes, unbare herself and escape into the early morning light before the alarm - never to be seen again. 

Or was escaping without a word too cruel after everything they had been through?

Even if after this visit they never saw each other again, at least they could both rest easy - their what ifs and questions about each other put to rest before they slipped back into their lives, this traumatic event merely a blip that would make an interesting story at dinner parties…

Then again: ‘Hi, I nearly died in a plane crash’ wasn’t the best small talk Lena admitted with a wince before shaking her head, pushing the door open and stepping inside. 

The room was clean, white lines and inoffensive decorations, the furniture practical and unassuming - lacking even the smallest modicum of character to match its current occupant. A small cot was set up in the corner, positioned parallel to the hospital bed, and occupied by a red headed woman, dressed in jeans and a dark shirt, snoring heavily - positioned to face the blonde woman in the centre of the room.

The woman Lena had fallen in love with, barely over twenty-four hours ago, was lying in the light blue hospital bed. Her gown was askew so that the red, exposed skin of her right shoulder and upper arm were easily accessible to whatever healthcare professional came to check. The wound was visible under the transparent bandage - unable to risk any fibres mixing in - the ripped rawness of it in complete opposition to Kara’s golden skin. She was hooked up to an IV and various monitors. Her expression was pinched, jaw clenched and lips a firm line. Her eyes flickered open intermittently, taking quick assessment of her surroundings before falling closed - it was the sound of the door clicking shut that had blue eyes snapping over to Lena - a little hazy but soft, attentive and exactly how Lena remembered them.

“Hello.” Lena greeted gently, stepping timidly further into the room.

The lines that marked Kara’s face vanished in an instant to be replaced by a relieved smile. “Lena, you’re… you’re alright.”

“Thanks to you.” Lena replied, sidling up to the side of the bed, fingers of her unbroken arm tracing the edge of the tucked in bed sheets as she wondered if it would be okay to take Kara’s hand in hers again.

“It was nothing.” Kara assured, head dipping forward, chin pressing into the top of her chest.

Lena arched a formidable eyebrow, “Are you equating saving my life to nothing?”

“No!” Kara squawked utterly aghast, “I mean… that saving you was… that for me…”

“Darling, I was just teasing,” Lena rushed to reassure, all sense of hesitancy thrown out the window at the sight of even the slightest discomfort from the blonde - pale fingers immediately moving to wrap around fidgeting ones and squeezing them gently, “please calm yourself.”

“Oh right, sorry.”

“And please don’t apologise.” 

“Okay.” Kara’s cheeks blushed a pretty pink, blue eyes wandering to their joined hands as she flipped her own hand over under Lena’s so that they were palm to palm. “You came to see me.” Kara whispered, thumb instantly picking up where it had left off on the plane - stroking round Lena’s knuckles in a soothing yet random pattern.

“Of course.” Lena declared, lifting her chin as if her attendance was never in question. “Did you think I wouldn’t?”

“I hoped you would,” Kara admitted shyly, “but I didn’t want to assume.”

“I believe I promised to love you for the rest of my life.” Lena reminded her softly, before shrugging helplessly, “You’re the one to blame for my life being extended.”

“I’ll happily take the blame for that.”

“Good.” Lena asserted, ducking her head, “How are you feeling?”

“Like I’ve been in a plane crash.”

Lena couldn’t prevent her rather unattractive bark of laughter, and she wouldn’t have wanted to even if she could - the way Kara’s entire expression lit up with pride and delight at her reaction was worth any embarrassment.

“I said the exact same thing to the doctor yesterday… he wasn’t amused.” Lena revealed with an exaggerated roll of her eyes.

Kara frowned childishly, “Clearly he has no taste.”

“You’re biased.” Lena tsked, as her laughter faded away.

Kara raised a curious eyebrow at her, “Because I love you?”

Something caught on the inside of Lena’s ribs, sharp but sweet, like her heart had slammed against its cage and was now melting into a puddle of warm goo. The blush from earlier came back like a wildfire, her whole face turning crimson to such a degree that a doctor would consider admitting her for some yet undiscovered facial rash. 

“Uh…” Lena coughed, “I was going to say because you clearly have the same sense of humour as me.”

“That too, I guess.” Kara accepted with an easy-going nod of her head, though the slight uptick to the corner of the blonde’s mouth implied she was more than pleased with Lena’s reaction to her words.

“Seriously, though,” Lena began, squeezing the other woman’s hand in hers, “how are you?”

The openness and effortless charm to Kara’s attitude vanished in an instant to be replaced with that too wide, too big to be real smile that Lena knew in an instant was fake. “My arm kinda hurts.” Kara huffed, attempting to underline the seemingly superficiality of her injury by shrugging her un-injured shoulder.

“Kara…” Lena murmured, slowly dropping to her knees at the side of the bed - uncaring of how inelegant her descent in a skirt and heels was - so that it was now Kara staring down into her eyes, “I’ll still love you, no matter what you say.”

Blue eyes went wide, equal parts terrified from exposure and yearning for the safe vulnerability offered. Lena held her breath, waiting to see which side Kara would give into.

Kara swallowed thickly, shuffling closer to Lena, head dropping down so that their faces were close together - the secrets they shared only for one another. “It really hurts, Lena.” Kara confessed, almost inaudible, “And I’m scared.”

“I’m sorry, darling.” Lena soothed, squeezing Kara’s hand so tightly it almost hurt, “It will get better, I promise and just like before, you’re not alone.”

“Really?” Kara whispered hopefully.

“Really.” Lena insisted, prompting Kara to smile for real. 

Carefully Kara shifted back to rest against the pillows, her movements exhausting her far more than she clearly expected. Lena watched the blonde worriedly, on the precipice of suggesting she take a nap or offering to call a nurse when the sleeping redhead in the corner let out an almighty snore. 

“Umm… out of curiosity, who is that?” Lena inquired.

“Oh, that’s Alex, my sister.” Kara explained, gaze filled with affection even as her sister did a terribly good impression of a jackhammer. “She’s been here since I went into surgery. I should probably let her know I’m awake.”

Once the worst of it had passed, too late for them to even consider boarding the plane, they had retreated back to the passenger lounge. Kara slumping forward in a seat, flooding over with ashamed tears, whilst Lena didn’t hesitate to kneel in front of her providing gentle touches and tender words. 

“I want to do this so badly.” Kara muttered, utterly distraught.

“I know, darling.” Lena replied, she had made it abundantly clear over the last few months that she didn’t care in the slightest if Kara never got on a plane again, happy for them to vacation around America or even drive down to Mexico. 

She didn’t bother repeating all of that this time; Kara knew it and that was enough. Lena refused to invalidate Kara’s own desires and wishes by framing it around their relationship. 

Kara wanted to travel and see the world as much as possible. Being able to get on a plane was a goal she had set herself and Lena had promised to be there every step of the way. She didn’t care if they cancelled a holiday every other week as long as Kara believed she would always be there for her.

“I love you so much.” Lena said instead, kissing the salt off Kara’s steadily drying lips.

Kara’s smile was small but honest in return. “I love you too.”

 

 

“Uh… Lena?” Kara called out curiously, watching with a small amount of alarm as the raven-haired genius lugged the suitcase they had still yet to unpack to the front door. “What’s going on?”

“Road trip.” Lena sing-songed back to her, dropping the bag with a thud before striding back to their bedroom presumably to grab their other belongings.

“Wait... what?” Kara choked, nearly spilling the mug of coffee she had been leisurely sipping from as she moped on the couch, mourning their failed departure yesterday. 

“Come on,” Lena ordered, reappearing now with two rucksacks slung over her back and two tote bags overflowing with Kara’s favourite snacks. “Move that fine ass, you’re wasting time. I want to beat the morning rush.”

Kara spluttered in response, but dutifully got to her feet, hurrying over to her girlfriend to alleviate her of the heavy load. “Where are we going?”

Lena’s grin was wicked and her green eyes crinkled at the edges with excitement. “It’s a surprise!”

They loaded up the CEO’s car with their luggage; Lena gave Kara exclusive control of the music knowing it would keep her occupied and happy. She didn’t bother with the SatNav having memorised the route they would be taking. 

Their first stop was a county fair a few hours outside of National City, they spent the entire afternoon on the rides, playing rigged games (though Kara refused to concede defeat on the basketball throwing one and eventually snagged a cuddly Owl that she dramatically bequeathed to Lena) and eating junk food in its purest form. 

Kara took over driving for the rest of the evening, Lena providing directions until they found a decent motel to stop at for the night. They travelled around for the full two weeks they would have been abroad; Lena directing them to various sights she knew Kara would love to see. 

They joined a cave tour one day - Kara gasping in awe over the stalactites and using her puppy dog eyes to devastating effect to convince Lena they needed to purchase an array of mineral rocks as travel gifts for all their friends.

They visited a vineyard another day, the wine tour leaving them both happily drunk and grateful that there was a guesthouse on the property that they could rent and recover from their hangovers in.

They visited the world’s best zoo, Kara taking enough photos to fill an entire memory card.

They also made stops at the more cliche road trip hotspots: largest ball of yarn and biggest wicker basket being Kara’s stand-out favourites.

It was a jam-packed two weeks, though the highlight was most definitely the Grand Canyon which they reached just as the sun began to rise, early in the second week of their adventure.

“Thank you for this.” Kara murmured, referring to the trip as a whole, resting her head on Lena’s shoulder as they gazed in wonder at the blushing sky on the precipice of being set ablaze. 

“So good surprise?” Lena checked knowingly.

“Good surprise.” Kara confirmed, her arm that was wrapped around Lena’s waist pulling her tighter against her side.

They fell into any easy silence as the molten orb broke through the horizon - pink giving away to crimson and then into a lighter blue. 

“I wasn’t supposed to be on the plane.”

Kara twitched at her side at the confession, head perking up to reveal a curious expression, “Lena?”

“I…” She hesitated for a beat, breathing carefully through her nose, “I had a ticket - first class - for the flight that evening. However, I decided to leave early.”

Kara’s brow furrowed, lips pressed tight together in confusion, “Why?”

“Because I wanted to ensure I got back to work as quickly as possible.” Lena smiled ruefully, “I had no interest in taking my time, in seeing the city and what it had to offer. I had no reason to…” She inhaled the clear air, enjoying the tickling warmth of the new day’s sunbeams on her skin. “To just be for the simple joy of… enjoying the view.” She turned her head so that she could admire her girlfriend’s beauty, “I do now, though.”

“Lena.” Kara breathed, though it sounded a lot more like thank you and I love you all rolled into one.

Lena reached out and tenderly carded her fingers through blonde hair that was slightly knotty and on the verge of turning greasy - the result of two days without a decent shower from a run of less refined lodgings. 

“You told me once that you thought you weren’t worth the pain of keeping.” Lena murmured, tone affectionate and full of love. “I need you to know,” Lena leant forward, pressing their foreheads together, “that I will never regret getting on that plane. Never .”

Kara sucked in a sharp breath, prompting Lena to run delicate fingers over a well-cut jaw, finding it clenched tight and requiring light touches to ease away the tension below the warm skin.

“Because it meant I met you. Meant I got to fall in love with you.” Lena continued, pressing kisses along the path her fingertips explored from the corner of Kara’s jaw, over the apple of her cheek to the outskirts of her lips. “You are worth it, Kara. Worth it all and so much more.”

 

 

Lena carried around the uniquely crafted engagement ring that she had designed with Alex’s input for four months, biding her time only to be beaten to the punch at the last second. 

She had finally given up waiting for a spontaneously perfect moment to occur on its own and decided to craft her own instead. 

She bought out an entire florist, taking a sneaky day off work to arrange the bouquets around their apartment and place photos that hallmarked their relationship along the walls. Her initial plan was to meet Kara at a hole-in-the-wall thai restaurant that Kara had been desperate to try. She would then walk them back through Kara’s favourite park where she had asked that the Christmas lights be put up a couple of days ahead of schedule - calling considerable number of favours to do so - knowing how much the reporter adored them. They would arrive back at their apartment, Kara would walk in first - awestruck by the transformation that had taken place - and when she turned around to ask what was going on she would find Lena already down on one knee.

Obviously, it didn’t happen that way.

The apartment was all set-up ready for the reveal but it was tipping it down with rain, drenching the city in a deluge of water. The walk in the park was out of the question but Lena rallied once Alex spent a good hour reassuring her that Kara was crazy about her and would definitely say yes - park or no park. 

Lena arrived at the restaurant early, sitting down with restless legs, her hand stroking the velvet surface of the box in her pocket for comfort. Kara was late by five minutes and she swept inside absolutely soaked through and leaving puddles on the floor, rapidly spitting out half a dozen apologies. All of Lena’s nerves went straight out the window, her love for Kara taking away all of her anxiety and setting her in motion. She swept Kara up into a reassuring hug, pressed a kiss to the side of her head and guided her into the bathroom. She helped her dry off and warmed her up by cuddling close. 

Kara asked her to marry her right then and there. 

Half-naked in the bathroom of a random restaurant, blue eyes blazing with adoration and smiling perfectly sincere.

Lena swore loud and chagrined, before jumping into Kara’s arms and shouting a resounding yes. They took the food to go and Kara cried at the sight awaiting her when they got home.

Lena wouldn’t have changed a single thing about that day - except maybe the excessive teasing she got from Sam, Jess and Andrea about it for the months that followed.

They married rather quickly, neither keen for an extended engagement. Lillian walked Lena down the aisle - something Lena had once thought as unlikely an event as an alien invasion. The wedding was well attended; Lena discovered she had more friends than she believed possible. 

They were set to fly out the day after. They were doing an extended tour of Europe, both taking a full month off work for it. Kara was confident she could do it this time; Lena didn’t question it, though, she had an alternative holiday planned if they were limited to car travel.

They made it through check-in and security without any mishaps, boarding went alright as well, though Kara needed to go for a walk before they committed to crossing the bridge. Lena secretly expected the worst if they managed to get on the plane. 

Surprisingly, Kara seemed to settle once they were seated. As if having Lena sat next to her, holding her hand reminded her that even if disaster occured it wasn’t all bad. 

Lena kept Kara distracted through take-off with discussions of when Alex was going to propose to Kelly; intermittently checking in with a squeeze to her wife’s hand (Lena would never tire of referring to Kara as such). Once they were in the air, Lena coaxed Kara into watching cheesy rom-coms with her - sharing a headset between them. The flight passed relatively seamlessly, a mild bout of turbulence over halfway nearly spiraled Kara into a panic attack but she breathed through it with only minor support from Lena needed.

They landed in Austria, going skiing for the first few days and consuming chocolate by the bucket load. They continued onto all the cities Kara had craved visiting: Prague, Berlin, Budapest and Paris. They finished their vacation in the Emerald Isle, exploring Lena’s forgotten home.

“Here?” Kara asked, voice almost whipped away by the winds and the dull crash of waves.

Lena shoved her hands into the pockets of her dark coat, chin jutted out defiantly as she stared out across the steely-coloured water. “Yeah.” Lena replied in a rushed exhale whilst trying to convince herself that her eyes were stinging due to the salt-water spray hitting them and not due to the dull ache in her chest. 

The sand crunched behind her as Kara slowly approached, arms moving to wrap round her middle from behind as she whispered softly in her ear, “What can I do?”

Lena swallowed thickly, hands settling over her wife’s, “Just let me be sad for a while.”

“Alright.” Kara hummed, hugging her close and protecting her from the chill of the wind and the ghosts of the past.

 

 

“I don’t know who is more excited, Kara or Lori.” Lena drawled, watching her wife and daughter practically skip up the boarding bridge in front of her, as if they believed the sooner they were seated the sooner they would arrive at DisneyWorld.

At least four different voices behind her immediately called out, “Kara.” 

Lena laughed at the response, casting a glance behind to see her family wearing equally amused expressions. Sam and Ruby were walking next to her, Alex, Kelly and Esme were behind them and Lillian and Eliza were bringing up the rear at a more sedate pace.

Lena finally caught up with her family, who were being directed to turn left at the door prompting Kara to turn around sharply and tug on the sleeve of Lena’s jumper in the exact same way Lori had taken to doing when she wanted attention.

“Lena, Lena, Lena.” Kara chanted, eyes wide and lip caught between her teeth.

“Yes, darling?” Lena inquired, overtly innocent. 

“These tickets are first class!” Kara sputtered out, failing to limit her volume.

“Yes, and?” Lena prompted whilst gently nudging her wife forward and towards their seats to prevent a queue from forming behind them.

“I thought you said economy was good luck?”

“That was before we had a four year old with your attention span.” Lena replied pointedly, jerking her chin to their daughter who was practically vibrating with excitement and was only being stopped from running amok by Lena slipping a hand around her small palm. “Now unlimited movies and games are good luck.” Lena paused for a beat, before frowning in concern at her wife and earnestly offering, “Do you want me to see if I can swap it?”

“Oh, hell no!” Kara replied immediately before wincing apologetically at the exuberance of her response. “I mean… come on, it’s first class.”

“You have become spoiled.” Lena tsked, helping Lori get settled and stepping to one side as Kara stowed away their coats and hand luggage above them, the rest of their party catching up and situating themselves in their own seats.

“And whose fault is that?” Sam cackled from the row next to them, “You bought her a company for a wedding gift.”

Lena blushed, pouting petulantly, “That was an investment, it had nothing-“

“Bullshit.” Alex sniggered as she flopped into the seat behind Kara’s.

“Please refrain from using such uncouth language around my granddaughter.” Lillian demanded haughtily, stepping elegantly around Lena and Kara as they lingered in the aisle with Eliza following in her wake. The grandmothers paused to press a kiss to Lori’s head before finding their own seats in front of the Luthor-Danvers clan. “She’s an impressionable young mind.”

“Thank you, Mother.” Lena acknowledged gratefully. “It’s nice to have back up once in a while.”

“Oh, I wasn’t backing you up about CatCo.” Lillian chuckled. “That was most definitely all about Kara.”

“Traitor.” Lena grumbled under her breath before Lori tugged on her jumper.

“Mommy, come sit with me.” Lori pleaded with a pout ten times more lethal than her Mama’s and blue eyes that twinkled effortlessly between blue and green. 

“Of course, sweetie.” Lena replied, shimmying by and collapsing into the window seat as Kara took the aisle - their greatest achievement and love safely ensconced between them in the middle seat.

“Mama hold my hand too?” Lori requested, tiny fingers waving in the space for Kara to take.

“Always.” Kara promised, pressing an affectionate kiss to Lori’s small hand and sharing a soft smile with Lena.

“You okay?” Lena mouthed at her wife over their daughter’s head as the engines began to thrum to life.

Kara grinned at her, “Never been better.”
