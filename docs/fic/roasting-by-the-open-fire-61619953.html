    Chapter 1

    Of all weather-related nuisances, snow was probably the most difficult. It was incredibly hard to sneak through, each step a soft crunch noticeable to people trained to keep on high alert. And that isn’t forgetting the hidden sheets of ice under the crunchy blanket. It's a minefield of nature, a pretty one at that.

    Still, Yelena trudged through it, not at all minding as her feet went numb from the cold. She knew it was her fault to wear canvas this late into December. Besides, her excitement warmed her as she got closer and closer to the house.

    Barton Farm was busy this year, with Barton’s extended family staying over alongside Clint’s chosen family. They stayed in the barn he had renovated into a cosy home for those staying, currently hosting Laura’s parents and her brother, along with his family. But Yelena didn’t care about them, as cruel as it was to think. Inside the house, she knew she would find her sister, certainly joined by her partner, the dogs, and most important of all, Yelena's favourite person in the world.

    And no, she didn’t mean Nate.

    The ex-assassin pushed through the door, lifting the handle to avoid the carefully placed creak in the wood which would surely alert the entire household. It slid open easily, and Yelena scanned the front room.

    There was a fireplace under the TV, which had died down to a smouldering pile, but she could still make out the figure opposite it, lounging on the couch.

    The flickering of the coals warmed the woman, soft embers lighting up her cheeks and casting shadows over her face. She leaned back in the seat, her head resting at an awkward angle, with one foot propped on the footstool in front, the other on the floor. She wore a Christmas jumper, of course she did. A thick purple one with impressive patterns swirling around a reindeer, making her cheeks a little flushed from the warmth. Her chest rose and fell with deep, exhausted breaths, which was unsurprising as she had spent the day helping with the kids and keeping them entertained, no doubt.

    But still, she sat there, waiting, the clock ticking closer to midnight. Yelena wanted to go to her immediately, but she held off for a moment longer. Instead, she gripped her bag in her hand and hurried up the stairs, artfully dodging another well-placed creak.

    She did wonder if all these creaks were well placed, or just signs of a well-lived-in house. Not that it mattered. She held onto the tinsel-wrapped bannister and continued further up to the second floor, moving silently.

    Yelena reached the top floor with a soft sigh. She opened the door to her room, tutting at the cat on her bed. It was black, curled into a tight ball, and snoring away in the middle of her bed.

    Oh great, Natasha brought her.

    Under the cat was the ugliest Christmas sweater she had ever seen. A swirling mass of snow with a green backdrop, two penguins leaning in for a kiss across the chest. It was so horrid, she loved it.

    Now to get it out from under the cat. A daunting task, as Yelena was much more of a dog person. Maybe she could just… pull it free? Like a table cloth, the cat wouldn’t even know what happened to hit her, she’s sure.

    After checking the time, which was uneasily closer to midnight, Yelena loomed over the cat, taking a steadying breath, and reached for the sleeping creature.

    “I wouldn’t do that.”

    Yelena didn’t jump at the sudden voice, as a part of her knew Natasha was there. Instead, she resisted the full body flinch, along with the knee-jerk reaction to aim her Bites, and rolled her eyes before they stopped on her sister.

    “Why not?”

    Nat was leaning on the door frame with a wry smile, wearing pyjamas Yelena knew would be matching with Maria.

    Loser… I want to do that.

    She walked into the room and nodded down at Liho.

    “If you do, you'll be spending Christmas patching up wounds.”

    Yelena scoffed, “I could beat a cat in a fight.”

    She could, but she wouldn't feel good about it. Nat shrugged a shoulder. “Well. You’d be fighting me and my cat,” she drawled, counting off on her fingers, “Which would make Kate get involved and then Clint, and then Laura would have something to say which means Maria would get huffy and then-”

    Yelena groaned and waved her hand.

    “Bozhe moy, shut up. I just got back from breaking into an organic matter factory. You know how bad they smell?”

    Nat grimaced and nodded.

    “Don’t need to guess. Do you even know what a shower is?”

    Yelena rolled her eyes and turned around, dropping her bag on the floor. She heads to the closet, subtly sniffing her collar. She had showered before arriving and scoffed when all she smelt was mango and passionfruit.

    Zhopa.

    The wardrobe was already full of clothes, a mix of Kate’s and her own. Yelena smiled at the sight, running her fingers down a familiar hoodie hung up.

    It was Kate’s before Yelena had borrowed it indefinitely about a month into their relationship. It made her feel warm whenever she wore it, even more so now she knew Kate had packed it just for her.

    She wanted to wear it now, but knew the law stated what she must wear, so close to Christmas day.

    “Did you really leave your bed to bother me?”

    Natasha lifted Liho in her arms, freeing the sweater and tossing it at Yelena’s back, kissing the cat on her head, all while raising an eyebrow at her sister.

    “Well, you were so loud coming in you woke me up. Thought like you wanted me to come say hi.”

    Yelena scoffed and pulled her jacket off, draping it over a chair, “Well I don’t want that, I want to wear my Christmas sweater and sleep.” She quickly changed into some jogging bottoms and fresh socks, Yelena was tired, and she wanted to go hug Kate Bishop.

    Nat kissed the cat again and dropped her on the bed, immediately she curled up and started purring again, twisting onto her back, showing her soft tummy.

    Yelena tsked, holding the sweater in her hands, frowning at the cat hair interlaced in, plucking some out before pulling it over her head.

    “That looks so ugly.”

    Yelena flattened out the sweater to her sides. It fit well, which was unsurprising. Kate was very good at buying her clothes. Maybe it was the silver spoon she had in her mouth since birth, but Kate Bishop understood style, even if she preferred sensible pants and a hoodie on most days.

    “Shut up, you’re just jealous Maria doesn’t do cute things like this with you anymore.” Natasha looked as though she slapped her, which was impressive.

    “Huh. Rude and wrong. We do cute stuff all the time. Cuter than this, even.” Yelena snorted, ruffling her hair and turning around, crossing her arms over her chest with a self-assured smirk.

    “Oh yeah? Like what?”

    Natasha matched her pose.

    “She left me a note with my breakfast yesterday before she went to work.” “Doesn’t sound like she packed your favourite hoodie, the one you stole from her, just in case you’ll want to wear it? Because Kate did that for me.”

    Natasha scoffed, “No, she didn't do that, because we’re not in middle school anymore.” Yelena pouted with disinterest and shrugged.

    “You didn't know her in middle school.”

    “That’s not what I meant. I was burning you, you’re burned right now.” “I’m pretty sure we were mid-indoctrination around middle school age.” Her sister sighed, scratching Liho under the chin.

    “Alright, smart ass.”

    “So you agree I’m smart?”

    “And an ass.”

    “I am taking the compliment, and myself, to Kate Bishop now.”

    Yelena is sure Natasha made another snide remark as she walked past on her way to the stairs, which she almost managed to descend when a hand grabbed her shoulder. She turned and gave her an exasperated expression, one foot still in the air.

    “Do not damage my Christmas sweater, Natasha.”

    Her sister rolled her eyes and let her go, leaning on the railing and smiling down at her as she began her descent into the house.

    “Glad you’re back in one piece. Ya skuchal po tebe.”

    Yelena turned back around and hopped up the last step, wrapping Nat in a tight embrace. “Tozhe skuchal po tebe," she whispered against her shoulder, squeezing tight one more time before turning and silently rushing down the stairs. The clock was ticking, and she had a promise to keep.

    Kate hadn’t moved much since she had been gone. Her knee was a little more bent and her head was leaned further back, eyes shut but facing the ceiling. Yelena silently walked towards her, wondering how she could approach the woman.

    She could wake her with a soft kiss to her lips, she could tap her shoulder, flick her forehead, or jump next to her on the couch. Before Yelena could settle on a choice, Kate sighed in her sleep. Her jaw tightened for a moment and her eyebrows screwed together, shuffling uncomfortably.

    “‘Elena…”

    The sound of her name hushing from Kate's lips broke the stillness, and Yelena's choice was made for her. She was around the couch in a moment, settling next to the other woman on the cushions.

    Yelena held her jaw in one hand, gripping her arm in the other, and leaned up to kiss her face, just shy of her lips.

    After a second she placed another kiss on her cheek, slowly making her way up to the woman’s ear.

    “Kate Bishop…” she whispered, grinning as the archer shifted closer to the blonde in her sleep, “wake up, moy yastreb.”

    Kate moved her head and softly groaned, her face a glow from the fire, on the brink of waking up. Yelena let her eyes close and leaned down, kissing her lips. She felt the moment Kate woke up, a swallowed gasp, and then she was immediately pushing back against her, fingers resting on her lower back, tugging at her sweater, gripping it gently. Yelena pulled back and leaned over her, arms on either side of Kate’s shoulders. Her head tilted, hair falling around her face as Kate beamed up at her.

    “You're here!” Kate gasped, now wide awake, sitting up and grabbing Yelena in a huge hug, “You made it!”

    Yelena laughed and looped her arms around Kate's shoulders as the archer surged up from the couch, standing and spinning them around.

    The blonde squeezed back just as hard, clinging to Kate Bishop like her life depended on it, wrapping her legs around her waist and getting as close as possible.

    “I said I would.”

    Kate pulled her face out of Yelena's shoulder just enough to kiss her. Yelena lost her fingers in Kate's hair, deepening it with a touch against her lip, and sighing into her mouth. She pulls back and rests their foreheads together, Kate's cheeks red and her smile so wide. Yelena missed her so much. Kate gently tugged at her sweater, feeling it between her fingers. Yelena played with the collar of Kate’s in return.

    “Your sweater is adorable.”

    She smirked and Kate swayed them a little.

    “You have to say that, you picked it out.”

    Kate sniggered and kissed her again, stepping back and slowly settling on the couch. The blonde hummed and shifted her weight, setting her knees on either side of Kate's hips, leaning into her embrace, and sharing gentle touches.

    A part of Yelena wanted to bury her face in Kate's shoulder, to feel the embrace she had so dearly missed and sleep in her arms.

    Another wanted to hold her face and never look away. Kate kissed her cheek and tugged her impossibly closer, holding her in a tight hug.

    “I missed you,” she whispered against Yelena’s neck, she ran her fingers through Kate’s hair, scratching the base of her head.

    “I missed you too, moya lyubov'.”

    Kate pulled back and held Yelena’s face, marvelling at the sight of her flickering in the warm light of the fire, kissing her nose.

    “Cuddle me?”

    Yelena had wanted to go upstairs, to sleep on the bed and cuddle until Barton’s kids dragged them out to be social. But Kate’s lip pouted, and what hard was a few minutes of cuddling? “Da,” she replied, and let Kate move them.

    She lay on her back across the couch, with Yelena sprawled on her chest. Yelena let her somewhat cold hands drift under Kate’s sweater, making her shiver as they ran across her stomach and settled on her side.

    Yelena knew her plan would fail the moment she rested her head on Kate’s shoulder. Being a trained assassin meant nothing when faced with the blanket of comfort Kate brought her. The clock ticked in the kitchen before letting out twelve short but melodic chimes. Her partner silently counted them, Yelena watched from lidded eyes as her mouth curved around each number. She held her tighter as they got to twelve, and the lips pulled into a smile. “Merry Christmas, Yelena.”

    She looked at Kate’s profile as she smiled down at her, fingers running gentle patterns across her back, causing her to shiver from the touch. Yelena felt her heart melt at Kate’s approach to being so gentle with her, despite everything reminding her how dangerous and formidable Yelena could be.

    “Merry Christmas, Kate Bishop.”

    She loved her. Yelena’s mind shifted to the ring in her luggage and promised herself that this would be the day she got down on one knee to ask. She just hoped Kate said yes. Yelena shuffled closer and relaxed even deeper into her warmth, with the fire at her back and the jumpers keeping all their heat in.

    It made her so sleepy, and she fought off a yawn.

    “Maybe we nap for five minutes,” Yelena mumbled into Kate’s throat and felt the archer’s lips on her head.

    “That sounds amazing,” Kate replied, and almost immediately went slack as she succumbed to her tiredness. Yelena stared at her profile for a few moments longer, until she also stepped into sleep.

    Yelena’s eyes snapped open at the sound of a camera clicking, and she snatched for the phone. Her sister was entirely smug as she stepped out of smacking range, waving her phone at them.

    “You’re getting sloppy.”

    “Ya naderu tebe zadnitsu.”

    Natasha smirked, “That’s not very festive of you.”

    Yelena glared and leaned up to grab the phone, or attack her sister, when Kate woke up, tightening her arm around the blonde’s waist and sitting up with bleary eyes. “What's happening?” she mumbled, and Yelena sighed, kissing her on the cheek, which was enough to bring alertness to her eyes, “I suddenly don’t care.”

    Yelena grinned and leaned down to kiss her again when a pillow smacked against her head. She huffed, took a calming breath, and grabbed it to launch back at her sister, who cackled and ducked under it.

    “I’ll be right back, I just need to kick my sister’s ass.”

    Kate sniggered and Yelena lunged for Nat, their voices flowing through the house as Yelena hunted her down. The archer sat up and stretched, surprised her back didn’t hurt from sleeping on the couch.

    “Mornin’ Hawkeye.”

    She twisted around and waved at Clint, the sound of Yelena and Nat's fighting being ignored by them both. He had his Christmas sweater on with a mug of coffee in his hand, sipping it with a loose smile.

    “Gooood morning, Hawkeye!” she replied, standing with a big stretch, “How’s your Christmas? Where’s Laura and the kids? Is that breakfast I smell?”

    He tsked and waved his hand, pushing off the wall as a loud thump and a “Hey!” came from the garden, it was unclear which sister.

    “So far so Christmassy. Laura’s waking up her family with Nate, Cooper is in his room and Lila is probably watching your girlfriend fight her sister in the garden.” They hear another oof, and then “Don’t put rocks in your snowballs!” followed by Lila’s laughter from above. Clint shook his head and sighed greatly.

    “She’s gonna corrupt my kids.”

    “Which one?”

    He chuckled and sipped his coffee.

    “Both of them. You best go get ready for when they’re done messing around.” Kate finger gunned him and pulled out her cell, heading for the stairs when she froze. Her lock screen had been changed, and now was the photo Natasha had just taken of her and Yelena.

    “Hey,” she called back, hanging off the bannister with one hand, “did you change my lock screen?”

    Clint held a thumbs up.

    “Yup.”

    She went down a step, staring at her phone and then into the kitchen with bemusement. “... When? And how?”

    Clint turned with a spatula in hand, waving it back and forth with a shrug. “Don’t think about it too hard.”

    She stared at him for a little too long, slowly putting it in her pocket. “... Right…”

    With one last confused look, she rushed up the stairs, taking the steps two at a time before getting to their room. She smiled at the extra bag in the closet, and pat Liho on the head as she passed the small cat.

    “Hey, bud.”

    The cat grumbled but didn’t swipe at her, which was progress. Kate took the phone out of her pocket to look at the lock screen again and felt her entire body erupt with tingles. She was on her back with one arm behind her head as a pillow, the other holding Yelena to her chest. The ex-assassin was fast asleep, buried in her embrace, looking entirely peaceful. Her heart swelled, and Kate locked her phone, threw it on the bed, and looked over her shoulder, straining her senses before she reached into her bag.

    Hidden in a compartment, disguised in an empty deodorant can, she found it. A delicate ring, purple and green diamond interlinked with gold. Her hands shook a little with excitement and nerves, and she slid it into her pocket, now more determined than ever. This will be the day, she promised herself, patting the shape away and pulling her sweater over it, this is the day I ask her to marry me.

    “Kate! Breakfasts ready!” Clint's voice called up the stairs, and the archer squeezed her hands into tight, excited fists.

    “Coming!”

    She picked up her phone and shoved it in her back pocket and rushed down the stairs with excitement.

    She knew, without a doubt, this Christmas was going to be amazing.
