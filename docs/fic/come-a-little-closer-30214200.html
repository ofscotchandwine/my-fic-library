Lafayette County wasn't too far out of the way. I-70 bore a straight through the middle of the county, right along a fat offshoot of the mighty Missouri. The river runs deep and muddy here. It's a humid floodplain topped with fertile red dirt. Every summer, fields grow heavy with corn and soybeans, while ancient oak trees dutifully shore up the banks of Camp Creek. Nearly every morning this time of year brings rain from out West. Something in the wind just carries those heavy clouds into the river valley and encourages them to pour. It falls to the ground in blinding sheets, and like clockwork, by ten am, it ceases. Leaving the sawgrass and the bull-thistle glistening in the hot sun. As time creeps toward September, the holler smells more and more like pungent Paw Paw, and every night the sounds of squalling animals waging war over the too-sweet fruit echoes in a cacophony over the woods.

The Luthors owned half of Odessa. The steel mill, the duck blinds, the heavy equipment auction house, and about three thousand acres of soybean fields Northeast of town. Kara was just a small time farmer’s daughter, they always did alright for themselves, getting by with a few heads of beef cattle and a healthy rotation crop that kept Kara busy planting and harvesting whenever she wasn’t assisting the local vet, her mother, Dr. Danvers.

In a town like this, you couldn’t help but know everyone else, only two years separated Kara and Lena in school after all. Despite being amicable, they never knew each other except in passing. Occasionally they’d see each other at a mutual friend’s bonfire. Occasionally they’d line up at the only stoplight in town, the one right in front of the Methodist church, and Lena’s little white Audi would dust Kara’s ‘92 F-150. Occasionally their friends would attempt to set them up on a date.

There never seemed to be much of a point in them getting to know each other, though. Despite the fact that they were the only openly queer women in town under the age of 45. Kara had always been primed to take over the family farm, finish her agricultural science degree at the small college in the next county over, maybe even go to vet school in Columbia. And Lena was always destined to get out. To go to an Ivy League school on the coast. To never come back. Separate destinies prevented them from ever wanting to pursue their mutual attraction.

It was no secret that Lena spent an awful lot of time, certainly more than she should, watching the vet tech as Dr. Danvers tended to her show horses. She always appreciated the gentle way in which Kara calmed down her high-strung companions. Many lost patience with the fickle, nervous creatures. But never Kara. Kara cooed to them, coaxed them into relaxing, cradled their slumped heads when the tranquilizers took hold. She was not afraid of the animals like the other techs often were, afraid of hurting something so expensive, afraid of their tempers, afraid of Lena's incensed yelling when her babies were twitched by uncaring hands. The Luthor heiress had certainly put Mike in his place when he had tried to put a lip chain on one of her more reactive horses. He hadn't stepped foot on the Luthor property since then, it always had to be Kara. Lena had insisted it was the only way she'd be comfortable. It certainly had nothing to do with the way Kara's shoulders rippled with effort when she held one of the straining, simmering beasts in position while her mother gave them their injections. It certainly never sent shivers down Lena's spine, it certainly never made her think about the other things Kara could do with those muscles.

It was no secret that Kara went out of her way to see Lena whenever she came back into town. It wasn't like she was stalking Lena, but if she happened to time her trips to the feed store to line up with Lena's, or her grocery store jaunts so she could pull up next to Lena’s little white car at the light and wink. Even that time Eliza had yelled at her for running out of the clinic in the middle of a spay surgery because Winn Schott had called to say Lena had just pulled into the gas station. But what could they do? Lena wasn't staying and Kara never wanted to leave.

So when Lena had come home from college for the summer, an obligation more than anything, as she was about to begin her senior year at MIT. She kept to herself, or had been staying with friends in some wealthy suburb of Kansas City, no one was really sure.
