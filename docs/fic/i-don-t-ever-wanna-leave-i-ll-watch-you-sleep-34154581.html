The sleepovers begin early on in their friendship.

It’s not planned, the first one, which happens maybe two months after they first meet. They’re in Kara’s apartment, lounging on the sofa, boxes of takeout left abandoned on the coffee table and the third movie of the night playing on screen. If she’s being honest, Kara’s not even sure what this one is, having given up trying to keep track of what they were watching somewhere around that weird Home Alone remake Lena insisted they hate-watch. They’re both wrapped up in their own little blanket nests, surrounded by mountains of throw pillows that Kara had produced from the closet; they’re sat close enough together that if they just moved slightly they’d be touching, but neither has quite managed to work up the courage to break that barrier yet, although Kara has found herself wanting to with an almost desperate fervor all night.

She’s almost drifting, eyes half-lidded as she burrows further into her blankets, when her phone, resting beside Lena’s half-finished orange chicken, buzzes, snapping her out of it. She blinks the sleep out of her eyes and reaches over to grab it, sighing when she sees it’s just her Co-Star day at a glance notification; she can’t help the furrow in her brow when she sees it says Telling someone you have feelings for them is an act of bravery.

She swipes the notification away, deciding not to think too hard about that one, but not before noticing the time, the little clock on her lockscreen ticking over from 01:17 to 01:18 in the time it takes her to do so.

When she looks over to the other side of the sofa, she finds Lena fast asleep, curled up in a little ball with her head resting on the arm of the sofa, clutching a pillow Eliza once made for her to her chest. She looks so peaceful Kara’s pretty sure it would be a criminal offence to wake her up, so instead she just smiles dopily to herself for a moment, snaps one or two -- okay, five -- photos of her, before tucking her in properly, adjusting the blanket so it covers her completely.

She gets up and takes a moment to stretch her whole body out, before blowing out the candles she’d lit earlier that night, pausing the movie and turning the TV off, and pouring herself a glass of water.

“G’night, Lena,” she whispers softly, the first words she’s said in hours, when she’s finally ready to go to bed herself. She presses a kiss to Lena’s forehead, smoothing her hair back gently as she does so, before retreating back into her own room, feeling lighter and warmer than she has in months.

Of course, after that, it doesn’t take long for the sleepovers to become a planned event. Soon, Kara’s invites morph from asking Lena if she wants to come over to hate-watch the latest Riverdale with her, to… well, she actually still does that every week, but it quickly comes with the added question of if Lena would like pancakes or waffles for breakfast the morning after.

(Waffles. The answer is always waffles).

For the first few weeks, Lena always takes the sofa; there’s never any question or doubt about this, it’s just the way things are -- Kara doesn’t think too much about it, really, negatively or positively.

But, when one morning Kara notices that Lena can’t stop rubbing at the small of her back, from where it had started aching just from sleeping on her frankly aging sofa, she decides it just won’t do, and from there on out, she insists that Lena share the bed with her.

The first time, they’re both a bundle of nerves, and it’s almost reminiscent of that first time on the sofa -- both jittery, too nervous to get close in case they accidentally touch, which leads to them both laying on opposite sides of the bed, backs facing each other, practically falling off in their attempts to be respectful of one another.

“This is weird.” Kara’s the first one to break the silence. “This feels weird to you, right?”

It seemingly eases the tension in the room, because Lena bursts out into a fit of nervous giggles, and says, “God, yes. I don’t know why I feel so nervous.”

Kara rolls over so that she’s facing Lena, and reaches over to poke at her back, causing Lena to squirm, before turning over herself.

“That’s better,” Kara says when they’re finally facing each other, unable to bite back her smile at the sight of Lena all comfy-cosy in her bed, practically drowning in the duvet cover and donning one of Kara’s favourite sleep shirts.

Lena beams back at her, completely un-self conscious for once, before shuffling a few inches across the bed. “This, too?”

Kara follows suit, wiggling closer to her. “Mm, much. Now I can actually see you -- always a good thing.”

Lena blushes, face turning the most adorable shade of pink, all the way up to the tips of her ears, and Kara can’t help but move even closer, almost subconsciously. 

“Now, Lena,” she says, “I have a very serious question for you.”

Lena’s eyes widen a little. “What’s that?”

Kara pauses a little, mainly just for dramatic effect, and also a little because Lena looks so cute like this, all painfully curious, before asking, “Are you a big spoon or a little spoon?”

It’s a question she’s like, ninety-five percent sure she already knows the answer to, but asking it is worth it just to see Lena roll her eyes and shove at Kara’s arm, as if she’d genuinely been expecting her to ask a really serious question.

“Big, obviously,” she says, but not even ten minutes later, Kara finds herself with her arms engulfing Lena’s tiny body, chin resting in the junction between her shoulder and neck, blissfully happy.

This makes sense too, though, because they always shower one after the other, and it just makes it easier for them to tell apart whose towel is whose, to save from any possible mixups.

When Alex questions her on why she has a mug with the periodic table printed onto it hanging from her mug tree, and she’s met with the most dramatic eye roll in the history of eye rolls at her explanation that none of Kara’s mugs are ever big enough to hold the sheer amount of coffee Lena likes to consume, and it’s therefore more sensible to just keep one of her mugs at Kara’s apartment, Kara finds herself questioning the amount of Lena’s stuff she has at her place for the first time, but she easily waves it off.

A few months later, however, when she finds herself truly evaluating the fact that most people probably don’t have a whole drawer dedicated to clothes for their best friend to keep at their place, she’s forced to recognise that, perhaps, they’ve taken it further than most would.

But, if she’s being honest, Kara doesn’t see the problem in all of this.

And, if there’s one thing Kara’s been forced to learn over the course of her time on Earth, it’s that different doesn’t always have to be a bad thing. Sometimes it can be good -- great, even.

She hadn’t thought much of it, at the time -- Lena always had a carefully curated excuse at the ready, one which she’d usually planted the seed for in the days or hours beforehand. All of a sudden, she was always busy with work, whether it be a business call with Japan that required her staying in the office until the early hours, or a stack of reports she had to get to, and every week it meant cancelling on their plans.

It wasn’t that Lena hadn’t had all of these responsibilities beforehand, but instead more like, all of a sudden, she’d started placing them over Kara for the first time since they knew each other.

Looking at it now, Kara realises Lena likely couldn’t stomach the thought of sharing a bed with her again, of having to spend the night snuggled up with someone she thought of as her biggest traitor, someone who had betrayed her and lied to her face for years, someone she now hated.

It’s pathetic, she knows -- it’s not like any of it was ever real, in fact it was all contrived by Lena, set up to cause her maximum pain. But the memories of it are comforting nonetheless, and ones Kara often finds herself turning to when she needs a reprieve from turning the truth of it all around in her head over and over again on repeat.

The only thing she wishes Lena had given her, something she knows she never deserved, was just one more sleepover. One night, spent holding each other in the dark safety of Kara’s room, where Kara could have finally went to sleep knowing that no secrets existed between them, that everything was laid bare.

Even if it had been a lie.

Her apartment feels morbidly empty in Lena’s absence.

All of her stuff is still there -- the purple toothbrush knocking heads with Kara’s, her towel on the back of the door, all of the clothes neatly folded in her drawer -- and yet they remain untouched from the last minute Lena used them, almost as if they’re waiting for her to come back and pick up where she left off, although Kara knows the chances of that ever happening are little to none.

The problem is, everything reminds her of Lena. She can’t stand at the stove without feeling the phantom memory of Lena pottering around beside her, reaching up above her head to grab the syrup for Kara’s pancakes; she can’t sit at the breakfast table without remembering Lena perched on her chair, swinging her legs absent-mindedly as she pored over the newspaper. 

Hell, she bursts into tears one morning when she comes across her now disused waffle iron when she’s rooting around in the cupboard.

She even takes to sleeping on the sofa, unable to face the bed that’s now so inextricably linked from Lena in her mind that she can still smell her perfume on the pillows even after washing and changing the sheets, but even that doesn’t work, because she’s always bombarded with images of Lena curled up there, drowning in a blanket nest and insisting she’s still awake enough to watch the next episode.

Coming back to her apartment at the end of the day feels like coming home to a graveyard, where everywhere she turns another aspect of the most important thing she’d ever had is memorialised in increasingly more painful ways.

The first time Lena finds herself back in the apartment… after, Kara can’t even find it in herself to be glad at the sight. 

For months, it had been all she’d thought about at night, lying awake, poring over every possible scenario in her mind. When would it happen? Would it ever happen? Would Lena be sorry when she came back, or angry? Would she be shouting, or would she be crying? Would she tell Kara it had all been some kind of joke? That the past months of their lives had all been a horrible dream, and she could wake up now, and things would be fixed?

The more time that passed, the more that became uncovered about Lena and everything she was doing to fight against Kara, the less and less convinced she became that the day would ever come, and the more and more she willed herself to stop wanting it so much. It felt pathetic, to be falling asleep every night to silly dreams of a woman who clearly wanted nothing to do with her coming back to say nice words, to try and fix things Kara didn’t even know could be fixed at this point.

She has to hold back from pinching herself several times, unable to process the fact that these words are actually coming out of Lena’s mouth, that this which she’d only allowed herself to imagine in the dead of night, alone with her own thoughts, is actually playing out in front of her.
