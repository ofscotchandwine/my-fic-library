    Chapter 1

    It's not that Kate doesn't remember the Red Room.

    No, she remembers it vividly. The cold tile and the ice in her trainer's eyes when she spoke and the way her limbs shook with exertion until she stopped noticing.

    Maybe this is why Clint takes an interest in her. Maybe her wide eyes that shone with some mixture of fear and determination attracted him, or was it the scars on her wrists from the handcuffs? Maybe it was simply the skill sharpened by years upon years of pain and discipline.

    Kate doesn't remember when she left her home for the red room. She must have been five, six, perhaps. She didn't know her true age for years but she knows exactly who she was and what the world looked like when she got her first period and tried desperately to hide it from her trainers. She was back to weapons training two days later, because if she could hold a gun, she could train.

    There had been glimpses of the famous Natasha Romanoff—just whispers of her name, and the one encounter of her during the Red Room's takedown. She'd been cleared for missions some year prior and had just returned from the German border. Natasha had made eye contact and then suddenly she felt human. Alive. Real, and tangible, and uncontrolled for the first time in years.

    She ran. She doesn't know how she survived.

    "You're Hawkeye," Kate says, because Clint Barton is Natasha's partner and Natasha is free. Free, like her, living her life by herself, not waking up from nightmares and pinching money from strangers to eat, and stealing pizza from the dumpster behind her apartment.

    Something in his gaze shifts. Kate is tense, knows exactly how to get out of his hold and is preparing to do so. “Who are you?”

    It’s sharp, but not hostile. This is why she says, “Kate Bishop,” something fearful she can’t keep out of her voice peeking through.

    “You’re a kid.”

    Kate could laugh. “Hardly.”

    “You’re a kid,” he repeats. “Why does a kid have the suit?”

    Something about the way he says the suit makes her squint. This is personal, somehow, and that perfectly trained part of her begs to know why; it begs to have leverage. Clint doesn’t let her answer, not that she had one to give him. “Look, we’ve gotta get you out of here. Okay? It’s not safe for you to be in this. Do you live near here?”

    “I’ll be fine,” she says instead. “I can get home.”

    “I’m coming with you. That’s final.”

    She wants to push against it, though Kate lets herself be followed home. He has eyes in every direction, as does she.

    “Where’d you learn to fight?”

    Part of her, a big, angry, childish part of her wants to tell him the truth. Instead she answers, “I started taking martial arts at five,” like it’s normal for her to know six ways to take down Clint in the next two steps.

    He nods. She doesn’t think it’s honest. “Pretty good.”

    Kate isn’t afraid to admit that the compliment makes something swell in her.

    It isn’t until everything’s said and done, until she’s exiting a flight landing in Iowa, holding her swiftly packed duffle in one hand, a grocery bag of snacks in the other, and a bow strung over her shoulder, that Kate is forced to confront the red in her ledger and the blood in her nightmares.

    “You’re good,” Clint says as they turn onto the highway. “Too good to be someone just taking Tae Kwon Do as a kid.” Clint shifts, though not enough to crash the rental car, enough to make some semblance of eye contact. “Where’d you really learn to fight?”

    She doesn’t want to face the truth. Kate refuses to makes the eye contact Clint seems to try to. “Does it matter?”

    “Partners,” Clint says carefully, “Need to know the truth about each other.”

    “You start.”

    Clint raises an eyebrow, but doesn’t argue. “Fact for a fact?”

    “Fact for a fact.”

    He drives in silence just long enough to make it to the exit. “My wife, she’s a former SHIELD agent.”

    Kate brings one knee to her chest, promising to dust the dirt off the seat later. “I was taken when I was five, I think.”

    She doesn’t see his shock, but she feels it anyway. “Taken?”

    “Your turn.”

    Clint huffs something close to a laugh. “My first girlfriend was a juggler in a circus. She was twelve years older than me but she looked my age.”

    Laughter escapes Kate’s lips. She forces herself not to think about herself, but about the juggler, as she talks. “The police told my parents I was kidnapped for illegal adoption, but I’d never tell anyone what happened, so they never found out the truth.”

    Clint doesn’t falter at her silence. “When I met Natasha Romanoff, I almost killed her. It was the look in her eyes that stopped me. She looked… Ready to die. Like she’d accepted that this was what she was meant for.”

    “I was in the Red Room for nine years.”

    The words leave her mouth, and she doesn’t hate them. They hurt, though Clint keeps driving, doesn’t falter, doesn’t get angry. Just takes one breath in, one long breath out. He replies, as if she hadn’t said something so devastating, “I have a brother. Barney. He raised me.”

    “I…” Her head hits the headrest. “I took up archery because it’s the one thing they didn’t teach me. I focussed on nothing else, because everything else felt tainted.”

    “I’m running out of deep stuff to trade,” Clint jokes. “I’m allergic to pollen?”

    Kate laughs. It’s not quite real, but it isn’t fake either, and sometimes a victim needs to fake the joy until they can accept that it can be real. “All that’ll give you from me is that I failed eleventh grade geography.”

    “I sleep with my socks on.”

    “My first pet was a betta fish named Scooter. He died in three weeks.”

    “I hate peanuts so much I just tell people I’m allergic.”

    “I have perfect pitch, but I didn’t know until I sang I Saw Mommy Kissing Santa Claus at a Christmas party last year.”

    They’re laughing again. It turns, and as they pull up to the Barton homestead, it feels less like she’s hiding and more like she’s vulnerable. Like she’s taken a step out into the wild and she can live now. She can live as herself without convincing everyone she’s someone else. She’s perfectly raw, and honest, and naked before the world.

    It makes it easier to accept Clint’s help as soon as she has to sit in the living room with the light on for three hours before she convinces herself that she’s not stuck in an endless loop of hypnotized training.

    “You’re here,” Clint says from a safe distance away. The opposite end of the couch is close enough to be comforting, and far enough away that she isn’t suffocated. Like he’s done this too many times, he repeats, “You’re here, in my living room, you aren’t there. It wasn’t you.”

    Who am I? she wants to ask, something stuck in her that begs to be told it’s valid. Who am I?

    The words leave her mouth. She hates them.

    Clint scooches closer. “You’re Kate Bishop, Hawkeye 2.0, and you’re here in my living room. You aren’t there.”

    He repeats it until her shoulders relax and she doesn’t have to face the door to assure herself that she’s alive.

    Kate knows, somehow, that night in his living room with every downstairs light on, that he will not leave her. Not now, not ever, because she isn’t Natasha but she is Kate, and Kate needs him like Natasha did.
