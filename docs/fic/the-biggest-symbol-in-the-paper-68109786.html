    It’s already been a slow day in the DP offices when Clark was preparing for his lunch break.

    He has already unpacked his sandwich and readied a first bite across its top when he suddenly senses a presence approaching him in a stride — and once a pair of brown eyes under dirty ginger hair stare him down with a smile, he knows it’s not good.

    “Yo, Clark! Got the biggest scoop for your main title rations today.”

    “What is it, Jimmy-boy?”

    “Lena Luthor,” Jimmy begins, hands in his pockets as loose fists, “Is dating Superman.”

    Clark has to physically gag himself back down from spewing out his soda gulp, turning with a snap towards Jimmy, who pops both eyebrows proudly and slightly sways back and forth.

    “Shocking, right?” Jimmy suggests.

    “Extremely,” Clark agrees, fading to a cough as he nearly throws the lunch box over his shoulder to return towards Jimmy, who obviously couldn’t care less no matter his response.

    “The hero Lex of LuthorCorp despises with every cell and fiber of his body — dating his surrogate sister. That’s a valuable tip-off right there.”

    “Tip-off? Where’d you get that?”

    Jimmy scoffs lightly. “Like I’m gonna tell you. You’re gonna steal all the credit.”

    Clark sighs out, slow and controlled. “Jimmy —"

    “Well, guess what? I won’t let you get away with that. The only thing I do allow to go forth, is for the article to land in your section. Then, maybe the new yellow ratings will finally go up. You’re a very good journalist; I’ll give you that.”

    Several different emotions brew inside him as an all-out storm, which concludes with a breathy chuckle and a humble nod. “Thank you, Jimmy, but I’d rather just get the information straight.”

    It’s true. Ever since another Luthor stepped up to the company throne, Perry has been preaching for the paper to finally open up a magazine sector — or, “the yellow dumpster quarters”, as Lois calls it. In his pitches, he talked about ‘higher rating disposition’, and ‘total popularity index’, and an entire range of other bullshit metrics he can use as buzzwords to justify exerting an entire sector of the building to maintain what is basically just celebrity gossip and media rumors. Special sights on the Luthor on top, of course.

    ‘Lena is not Lex. She has no platform PR. Superman can’t manage that.’

    Support, his ass. Journalistic integrity is on life support at the moment, and it would’ve been completely deceased were it not to Lois’ expert evidence detection and further bond termination. You can’t get paparazzi pictures or contact off-selling moles when all of them suddenly lose electricity and internet just the week of the posting. Lackluster luck, obviously.

    “What’s better,” Clark adds on further, noting the silver thought blinking through Jimmy’s eyes, “Get a shady rumor, report it as truth, and then immediately lose your media license by the man who despises your voice when it all turns out to be false — or give a bit or two of evidence to your best buddy, so that all of your potential struggle won’t go to waste in turn for credit risk-and-reward?”

    Jimmy seems to consider it, seriously glaring at the ground, hands on hips.

    “Fine,” he says, before roughly unfolding the thick paper from his back pocket and tossing it towards Clark. “Don’t lose your head, Kent.”

    “Can’t do,” Clark responds with an airtight smile, watching Jimmy walk off back to his own desk.

    As soon as he’s out of sight, he nearly damn tears apart the paper as he attempts to open it quickly. Once his eyes land on it, they go wide; cheeks go pale, mouth lips part.

    “Oh, my,” he mutters softly.

Superman is a figure of hope and light, not a celebrity you root for on social media. Even though Clark obviously wouldn't mind it at all.
