Lena sighs, her eyes clouding over. “I’m afraid no one can be told what the Matrix is. You have to see it for yourself. Right now, all I can tell you is that the Matrix is everywhere. It’s all around us. It’s in the air we breathe, in everything we touch…”

Lena ventures a hand between them to touch Kara’s, their hands connecting in the slightest. And even though she knows that she’s not really touching Kara’s hand, her mind feeds her the sensations of it — the softness of Kara’s skin, the gentle press of her flesh under Lena’s fingers.

Lena draws her hand away, and Kara follows it avidly with her eyes. “For you to know what the Matrix is, I have to go back to the beginning. Or at least, to where it begins for us.”

Lena

It begins in the Matrix.

 

The Luthors are a picture-perfect family. Powerful, affluent, and respected. The father, the mother and the golden son. And little Lena - smart, angelic and pretty, the ideal daughter - completes the picturesque family.

 

However, as she grows from toddler to child, it becomes apparent that Lena is not like other children.

 

It's always been clear that her intellect far surpasses people five, six times her age. Lena is sharp and brilliant, able to grasp complex concepts most adults cannot. She seems to see the world around her in a different way, and her keen-eyed perception sees through any trick or subterfuge. She doesn’t miss a thing, this little girl. 

 

The Luthors are no strangers to gifted children; their son Lex was deemed a prodigy at around the same age. At first, Lionel and Lillian take this as yet another proof of how exceptional Luthors are, and Lena is proudly displayed as their indigo child.

 

But Lena's talent develops as fast as she does. 

 

Soon, she begins to exhibit strange, unexplained abilities. An expensive Waterford crystal goblet in Lionel's hand explodes while he’s in the middle of berating Lena at dinner. Once, Lillian walks into her playroom to find Lena having a tea party with her dolls, and when her mother enters, all heads turn to her. Lena's and all four of her dolls. Five sets of eyes stare at Lillian disconcertingly from the middle of the room.

 

Her intellect begins to surpass what defines “normal” intelligence. She successfully foils an assassination attempt against Lionel. She predicts a massive shift in the stock market and saves the company from going bankrupt. She prevents Lex from getting hit by a driver in a car chase five blocks away. She begins to talk of some vague concept she calls the Matrix and compares it to the philosophy of Plato.

 

The last straw comes when Lena finds out that the cleaning lady's ten year old son has cancer. 

 

Lena convinces Alma to take her to see him. Five hours later, a tearful Alma brings the little girl back with something akin to wonder in her eyes. “Your little girl is an angel, Mr. Luthor! Bendecida por la Virgen. She cured my Carlos! She took away his sickness! Ella es un milagro de Dios!”

 

However, far from seeing it as a miracle, the Luthors circle the wagons. The next day, Lena finds out Alma has been dismissed, and a shift occurs in the Luthor household.

 

When Lena's abilities were within the parameters of "normal", they were good — something to be proud of. But now that her gifts have proven to be beyond that, they become alien, freakish. Something to be hidden. People would be asking too many questions, and Luthors do not permit those.

 

Suddenly, instead of being lauded for what she is able to do, Lena is now scrutinized and examined to find out what's "wrong" with her. It begins to strain the family that is obsessed with order and perfection. They take Lena to various doctors and put her through all sorts of tests, but none of them seem able to find an explanation for Lena’s strange abilities.

 

Not all the Luthors are united in their disdain for Lena’s abilities, however. 

 

From the first time Lena had beaten him at chess at the age of four, Lex had been proud of Lena’s brilliance. Now that this brilliance has seemingly surpassed his, this pride is tinged with envy. Yet of all the Luthors, he is the only one who encourages Lena’s gifts.

 

When she brings up Plato, Lex brings her books to read. Descartes. Sun Tzu. Biographies of the world’s greatest thinkers, history’s greatest masters. He fills her shelves with books on philosophy, history, the sciences, and takes out Lena’s favorite novels. To develop her gifts, he tells her, she should be educating herself, not disappearing into a world of fiction.

 

During the family’s winter vacation in Colorado, Lex puts Lena’s talents to the test and has her steal Lionel’s most prized fountain pen — a gift from the King of Jordan — from his study. Lionel is livid, an outcome that pleased Lex, but by the time their father finds out about the theft, Lena is already running across the snow toward Lex, and he helps her climb up the rope to their Treehouse.

 

The Treehouse is where the Luthor siblings hide whenever Lionel loses his temper. In there, Lex is the ringmaster and Lena his perfect audience. The little girl sits, rapt, hanging onto his every word. He pushes her to develop these strange talents. These gifts, Lex tells her, will save them from their volatile household.

 

“What if he finds us?” A frightened Lena asks as they listen to Lionel shouting for her below.

 

“Don’t worry, he can’t climb when he’s drunk.” Lex scoffs as he sits down on the floor next to her. “One day, Lena, we’re going to get out of here. You and me, together. You’re going to set us free, and I’m going to be the most powerful person in the world. Then, I can keep us safe. When we’re on top of the world, no one can hurt us. No one can poke and prod at you anymore, or study you like a freak. Not when we’re in charge.”

 

And because he’s her big brother, Lena believes him.

 

The more time passes without answers, the more strained the family becomes. Lionel disappears further into the bottom of a bottle. Resentment begins to build within Lillian. Lex continues to reassert his dominance. And Lena continues to be othered by her doctors, her family, and now even her classmates at school.

 

Everything changes when they meet Rhea.

 

An elegant and well-spoken woman, Rhea runs an exclusive facility for gifted children. From the moment they first meet, Rhea is fascinated by Lena. Her teaching “methods” seem vague, but out of all the specialists Lena has seen so far, she is the only one who seems to understand and make a connection with her. 

 

At the very least, they seem to speak the same language. Rhea knows about this Matrix Lena has been talking about.

 

“You’re not really a teacher, are you?” Lena blinks serenely at the older woman across the room. She’s seated on a leather wingback chair that’s far too big for her, and her dainty little mary-jane shoes dangle in the air, too small to reach the ground. “And you’re not a doctor. Not like the ones who come to see me.” 

 

“No, I’m not.” Rhea admits easily, watching the self-possessed little creature before her. “If you so choose, you may think of me as a Guide. I can show you the way to go, but you must take the journey yourself.”

 

Lena plays with the ear of the teddy bear in her lap. “You’re not like the others. They all think there’s something wrong with me. They say I’m… different.”

 

“I think they’re right.” Rhea’s smile is beatific as she looks at the little girl. “I think you are very different from everyone else, Lena. You know things. You can do things. Things other people can’t. You're special.”

 

Lena looks down, her little voice lowering to a whisper. “I can tell when people are lying or hiding something. I can make some things move without touching them. Sometimes I can see things before they happen. I can… change them.”

 

Rhea’s eyebrows rise, and she leans forward. “Oh?”

 

“It’s like… everything around me is a dream.” Lena looks up at her with solemn, luminous eyes. “And because it’s a dream, I can change things. I can make things happen or not happen, because none of it is happening at all.”

 

Rhea’s eyes narrow and her smile widens a tad. “I see.”

 

“I know what it’s called. But I’ve never seen it.” Lena’s arms tighten around her bear.

 

Rhea nods. “Would you like to?”

 

Lena doesn’t even blink. “Yes.”

 

With her parents’ permission, Rhea takes the little girl out for another consultation. Except, this one is different. Lena is taken to a tall building in the middle of the city, riding all the way to the top floor with her little hand in Rhea’s. 

 

On the 86th floor, they enter a glass office in which an imperious looking blond woman sits, watching her with a piercing eye.

 

“Leave us.” 

 

The woman orders sharply, slanting a glare at Rhea. She is at least 6 inches shorter than Rhea, even in heels, but her tone and her face brook no argument. Rhea retreats with a seething sneer, but she complies.

 

“Now, you,” the blond woman turns to Lena with a dark look and a raised brow. It fails to intimidate Lena; she is Lillian Luthor’s daughter, after all. “Do you know why you’re here?”

 

Lena merely blinks at her. “Because I know things.”

 

The woman scoffs. “So do I. Doesn’t make you special.” She gestures around her at her office with a spectacular view. “I know things too.”

 

Lena’s eyebrows rise as well. “Not everything.”

 

The woman’s glare intensifies, but Lena stares her down. After a moment, a corner of the woman’s mouth lifts, and she barks out a laugh. “You’re a smart one, aren’t you?”

 

Lena clasps her hands behind her back. “So I’ve been told.”

 

“Do you know who I am?”

 

Lena nods. “You’re the Keeper.”

 

The woman snorts delicately. “Did Rhea tell you that?”

 

Lena regards her solemnly. “She didn’t have to.”

 

The woman’s eyes narrow at her, but Lena says nothing more. She is scrutinized for another moment before the woman smirks. “Alright... Since you’re so smart, why don’t you tell me what you already know.”

 

Lena blinks at her, responding to the woman’s scrutinizing gaze in kind. “I know that you’re not human.”

 

Another laugh, this time louder. Piercing blue eyes gain a twinkle of mirth. “Very good. What else?”

 

“I know that you’re not real.”

 

The woman scoffs disdainfully. “‘Real’ is an abstract concept.”

 

“I know that I’m dreaming, and none of this is real.”

 

The mirth suddenly vanishes from the woman’s gaze, and her blue eyes stare at Lena intently. “What do you mean?”

 

“This. All of this.” Lena sweeps her little arms across the room. The glass-walled office with all of its impressive grandeur. The twenty large monitors, exhibited to project the image of surveillance and omniscience. The expensive art and the throne-like chair where the woman sits, designed to exude power and opulence. Even the view of National City in its vast expanse outside. 

 

“Everything. It’s not real.” The little girl looks the woman right in the eye. “It’s just a dream.” 

 

The woman is leaning forward now. It looks to Lena as if she is holding her breath. “And what makes you think that?”

 

Lena chews thoughtfully on her lower lip, tipping her head in a curious little way. “Have you ever read Plato’s allegory of the cave?”

 

The woman’s eyebrows rise and an amused smile dances over her lips. “Of course.”

 

“It feels like that. Like the people chained to the walls of the cave, watching just shadows and reflections of fake objects. Other people — even my parents, even Lex — they look around them and think that this is the real thing. But all we’re seeing are just shadows. Sometimes it makes me feel confused and blurry, like I’m dreaming, but I can’t wake up.”

 

The woman hums and her hands form a steeple under her chin as she continues to observe Lena. 

 

“In the story, the prisoner who is freed into the sunlight was angry and in great pain after being in the dark for so long. Why would they go through that? Why not stay in the comfort of the darkness that they’ve known all their lives?”

 

Lena’s gaze doesn’t waver. “Because they would finally know the truth. They wouldn’t be living in a lie anymore. They would be free.”

 

A smile spreads across the woman’s face, and the nod she gives is almost approving. “Is that what you want?”

 

“Only if you tell me the truth.” Lena nods. Her wide green eyes are solemn and piercing as she stares at the older woman. “Will you tell me the truth, Keeper?”

 

The woman looks at her silently for a very long moment. When Lena finally thinks she won’t answer her question, the woman rises from her throne and makes her way slowly around her desk to where Lena is standing. Despite her small stature, the older woman still towers over the little girl. “I’ll tell you everything you need to know.” 

 

An elegant hand is extended toward Lena who offers her own. Long fingers wrap gently around smaller ones, and the woman smiles. “And one more thing.... Call me Cat.”

Nothing but a dream, a neural-interactive simulation that was being fed to the human subconscious mind. All while the rest of the human’s body — the body that had never been used, the new fragile little body Lena now occupies — was kept in stasis, in a permanent sleeping state. The pod that each Luthor had woken in after their extraction was specifically designed to generate energy from a human being’s body from birth until death.

 

The real world, J’onn tells them, is a barren wasteland of a planet taken over more than a hundred years ago by an invasive alien race, a Type-III 1-minus civilization with advanced technology known as the Daxamites. Combined with an advanced form of fusion, the Daxamites had found all the energy they would ever need to power their new colony. And the fields, from which Lena and her family extracted, was where they harvested their energy supply.

A lie to keep humans enslaved and complacent.

Amid the hiss and groan and heat and metal of the Daxamite’s underground conduits, these last — and first — humans had forged their home, driven deeper and deeper by the invaders. So deep that Argo had lost all contact with the above-world, had lost its connection to the rest of the humans, save for the Matrix. 

 

This was both a curse and a blessing, for through the Matrix, the survivors had been able to extract other humans from the harvest fields. By hacking into the Daxamite’s mainframe, they were able to gain access to the simulation and plant extraction points to provide the rest of the enslaved humans a way out. Without her parents, fourteen year old Lena is taken in by the crèche where all the orphaned children of Argo live. It’s not a bad place to grow up, considering the tense environment of her home. She meets other children her age here. Some of them were Born in Argo, but most of them were unplugged from the Matrix. 

 

Here is where she truly begins to see Argo as home.

 

The crèche is where she meets Kelly Olsen, whose parents had perished after a Daxamite sentinel had attacked the Osiris. There’s also Jack Spheer — a science prodigy working with nanomachines in the Matrix, he had been unplugged at the age of eleven and had been living at the crèche since. She also meets Andrea Rojas, who had been unplugged with her father when she was a baby. Bernardo Rojas had been Captain of the Gate that served as Argo’s defense, but an accident with one of the robotic armed exoskeletons that defended the dock had left him paralyzed and unable to care for his daughter.

Whenever the Manhunter is docked at Argo, he will train her himself. When she’s ready, he will allow her to come onboard. Basic crew training takes two or three years, and learning to navigate the Matrix usually takes longer. By then, she’ll be of age.

Increasing its speed without sacrificing its fuel or its storage. Modifying its suspension system to decrease the turning radius for better maneuverability. She pushes her own limits during her training. J’onn loads her into the ship’s training program, the Construct, and throws every challenge he can at her. Maybe because they were the same age. Maybe it was because, like Lena, Adam had been lonely and misunderstood by everyone around him, including his own parents. 

 

Whatever the reason, Adam had trusted Lena to lead him out of the Matrix.

 

She and Adam had been surrounded by Agents on all sides, and while Lena had been in and out of the Matrix several times now and she was used to maneuvering them, Adam was not.

 

She could’ve jumped the building easily, but Adam was already terrified of climbing out of the window and onto the scaffolding. J’onn was the one who had provided them a way out. The helicopter was waiting for them, flying dangerously low, but close enough for Lena to jump inside while a petrified Adam clung to her for dear life.

Lena struggles to maintain her composure. “You asked for the truth.”

 

”I didn’t ask for this! ” Adam gestures at the four metal walls of the tiny cabin that had been provided for him aboard the Manhunter . There are angry tears in his eyes. “I didn’t want this!
