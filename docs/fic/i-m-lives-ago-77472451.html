I'm Lives Ago

by midwestprincess_01

    Chapter 1

    An abnormality.

    An obscure outlier.

    An imbalance.

    Souls—not alive, not yet dead.

    Hundreds of them, all toiling away– unmoving.

    Frozen or trapped.

    Cursed or imprisoned.

    A complete anomaly.

    As Death, Rio could sense them, sense the pain and anguish that accompanied. Rather curious as no human or god made tragedy would cause such a thing. But, there was a pull she couldn’t ignore. Death did move in time as humans did, Death was around since before the first human, and would remain long after the last. She supposed it didn’t matter, she had been alone the past two hundred and seventy-three years, and would remain that way until the heat death of the universe. Agatha would always scrunch her face whenever Rio said that, giving a soft kiss and promising she’d be there holding Rio’s hand as the dark cosmos went static. At least, before. Now though, on the exceedingly rare occasion that Rio did cross paths with her wife, there was no kissing, no promises. Rio hadn’t seen her wife in close to two decades, not the longest they had gone, but it left her aching all the same. She knew what Agatha had been up to in the meantime– draining, scheming, scamming, hurting others and herself externally to distract from the internal wounds which could not be mended. Most importantly, hiding behind all that dark magic provided by the darkhold. Rio could no longer feel her wife as she once had, and that made her isolation a lot more…isolating.

    If her wife wasn't a spirit witch, Rio might’ve believed that the mirage of Westview had been Agatha’s. Though, her wife would not use such unbounding power on a pursuit so frivolous. The suburbs, really? Rio tilted her head while studying the sign announcing one’s arrival to the town, such an unassuming exterior for such a sinister interior.

    Welcome To Westview Home: It’s Where You Make It!

    She puckered her lips, brows furrowing beneath the delicate silk lining of her cloak. Apparently, she hadn’t been the only one trying to understand this, if the U.S. military were any indication. In all her endless centuries of existence, she never found herself thinking: “Thank gods the military is here, surely things will be handled with as few casualties as possible.” Because humans were futile creatures, ones who did not strive to think but rather feel. Rage, greed, sorrow, lust. Each one had led to motivation, sometimes all four at once.

    Rio placed her palm on the forcefield, the red instantly crackling in response. Chaos magic. Fascinating. A fence to keep out others, to shield the world from what the town hid– or to keep its residents contained against their will. Either way, it was the most interesting thing she’d come across in the past two centuries, so fine, she’d investigate. Though this did not fall within her jurisdiction, Rio was bored and she’d most likely have to make a trip back here anyway to ferry the victims of whatever malevolent being was causing such an occurrence.

    A quick gash with her dagger and she was in, the red barrier sewing itself back together. She was not visible to the human eye, not by default, so she was able to freely roam the town. Rio made light work, noting the black and white desaturation of this tiny bubble. The people residing in it all pretending it was a different decade, and acting accordingly. But their thoughts were not their own, their actions forced, their true desires and needs locked within them by a strict warden.

    Wanda.

    Please! Release us!

    Wanda.

    Please, I’m begging you! Wanda! Let him breathe!

    Wanda.

    Does anyone know we’re here?! Help! God, it hurts!

    Wanda.

    Rio was not in the business of muddling in human affairs, not after Nicky. Not after her son, not after guiding him along far past his due time. She observed and did her job. Nothing more, nothing less. She was not without emotion, she was aware of these people’s agony of having their agency stolen. But, in the court of cosmic rules and regulations, her hands were tied.

    She left after five minutes, the witch casting this illusion was clearly powerful, perhaps by birth right, but not Rio’s responsibility. There was only one witch she’d consider hers, and she was not the one imprisoning an entire populace, though Rio might find herself slightly impressed if Agatha had managed to pull it off. She might even find herself endeared, if it meant seeing her wife again.

    Just to cover her bases, Rio moseyed over to the military encampment. She was not familiar with what subsection of the military it was, S.W.O.R.D. or some shit. They all did the same thing anyway, why must they continue with the asinine acronyms? The multiple tents contained multiple agents all asking the same questions Rio had figured out, though humans moved slower, so she gave them grace. Her feet carried her to the next tent, abandoned, the only sign of life proving to be an old TV with a repeating broadcast.

    She inched forward, taking a seat at the vacant office chair, picking up the mug of steaming hot coffee. Rio blew on it as the woman and a cyborg man on screen bantered, the coffee tasted of hazelnut much to Rio’s delight. The show was the same thing Rio had just been inside of, the anomaly. “My wife and her flying saucers,” The man quipped, holding a newspaper and wearing a suit.

    The woman in a period-typical dress grinned, her pearls just as dazzling as her smile. “My husband and his indestructible head.”

    Eugh, why would she want to wear clothing from that era again? Rio herself was a big proponent of pants unless she was going for a dramatic effect, humans rules about clothing was as convoluted as their politics, governments, and religions. The laugh track was artificial, this may be a broadcast but there was no TV show.

    “Wanda?” The man asked, the woman in front of the fridge humming in response.

    Oh.

    Well, one mystery solved, the woman was Wanda, though she did not currently scream evil witch enslaving a whole town for funsies. What the fuck was this woman’s deal. Rio gingerly sipped the coffee as she flicked her wrist, fast forwarding.

    Boring. Boring. Boring. She rolled her eyes, wondering if she should get off her ass and actually do some real work. Rio downed the rest of the coffee, the scalding hot liquid not doing much to dissuade her, it kind of just tickled. She rose to her feet, counting this as a dull spark of interest in an otherwise monotonous existence.

    “Oh! Hello, dear.” Rio dropped back down as though she was now magnetized to the seat. Her voice was not her own, it was a performance, one of her wife’s many, but her wife nonetheless. “I’m Agnes-” Liar. “Your neighbor to the right. My right, not yours.” Agatha smiled big, like a predator spotting its prey. Her dress was not something she’d ever actually wear, her hair curling above her shoulders for the first time since the 90’s. Her eyes were still the same, huge and full of grief which she did not dare drudge up. Her face– Rio had studied it, memorized it ages ago. “Forgive me for not stopping by sooner to welcome you to the block. My mother-in-law was in town,” Agatha said, waiting for a beat. “So I wasn’t.” As she handed the plant over, Rio’s stomach dropped. She was still donning the locket, her mother’s, the one encasing their son’s hair like something precious. And to Agatha– it was.

    “I’m Wanda,” The witch said, offering out her fucking hand. “Wanda.” Agatha repeated, fucking taking the aforementioned hand. “Charmed.” Rio picked up in one second that Agatha was running a con, figuring out how to drain the witch, but was all the covert flirtatiousness and touching really necessary? Pretty, naïve, powerful. Wanda checked a lot of Agatha’s boxes for someone to screw and siphon.

    “The only way Ralph would remember our anniversary is if there was a beer named June 2nd.” And the crowd went fucking wild for a fictional man that stole her and Rio’s actual handfasting anniversary. Yet, despite the anger slithering through her chest and into her veins, Rio couldn’t look away. Her wife’s face and voice were too comforting to deny, and she hadn’t realized just how alone she’d been, how alone she’d be. So, Rio watched, she endured the torture of watching Agatha flirt with “a young thing” like Wanda. Agnes was the antithesis of Agatha, soft and matronly where her wife was sharp and vicious. Agnes was a sweet kiss to Agatha’s cruel bite, and Rio loathed it with her entire being.

    Agatha had not always been as bitter as she was now, there had been a time where her wife was loving and gentle beneath a rigid façade. Those days now long gone, that Agatha had been left to rot, the only memory of such a person ever existing confined in Rio’s heart– the one made for Agatha. The organ pumping though it did not have to, an accessory adorning a made up body, beating for one mortal who had just called another woman good girl. As the broadcast came to a close, she swallowed nothing. Choking back the emotions that Death was not made to feel, but that Rio Vidal felt all the time. Agatha spent her whole life chipping Rio away, spent the centuries running and snarling. Her wife, the one whom Rio pledged her undying love and devotion to, doing the same thing she always did– perform. Agnes did not marry Death, did not lose her son to Death, did not love nor fear Death. Agnes had a husband and a life, and though it was a character, and a means to an end, the erasing of any real history was far too easy for Agatha to execute.

    Humans came back into the tent, Rio getting up and waving her hand to teleport elsewhere. “Aw, guys, c’mon.” A woman with long brown hair and glasses huffed. “Who drank my coffee?”

    Rio invisibly spawned on Agatha’s street, finding the old blue and white house, the world now saturated in color. It looked to be the sixties as she shuffled up the walkway, peering into the nearest window. Wanda was not around, so Agatha’s current state would not be broadcasted. She sat on the floor, the Darkhold floating in front of her face, blackened fingers running along the page as she mumbled under her breath.

    Nobody could see either of them, hell, Agatha couldn’t even see Rio, but she preferred it that way. If Agatha didn’t see her, then she would not have to repeat herself. Would not have to utter, in such exquisite detail, just how much she now hated Rio.

    Rio carried that sentiment like a scar etched into her mortal form, always there, always aching. Her wife unceremoniously dropped the book, the leather spine ricocheting off the floor. Agatha’s gaze locked on Rio’s, head tilting as that adorable line in her forehead wrinkled in suspicion. Can you see me?

    Do you sense me?

    Do you miss me, even a fraction as much as I miss you?

    Her wife’s lips parted, and just as Rio swore that she was going to speak, the curtains closed with a wave of Agatha’s lithe fingers. Agatha spent her life scrubbing Rio from her story, Rio spent her life being erased. She stumbled back, the tears pooling, though she would not let them fall. Rio had convinced herself Agatha hurt too, that the pain was mutual. That maybe her wife was just the better actor between the two of them. If she was ever needed, Agatha would call for her, but until then– Rio’s heart would remain as black as the magic currently flowing through her wife’s body. She disappeared again, doing her duty and finding the next soul in need of an usher to the afterlife.

    Death would live until the heat death of the universe, a constant until all the matter was dust and all the energy had vanished. Death would remain, until all the stars went out and the cosmos went quiet.

    But, Rio Vidal– she would die with her wife.

    She would die with Agatha, until her heart no longer beat. Decades, centuries, millennia, or eons.

    Until the end of their time.

    Until her love’s last dying breath.

    Because Death did not, could not love.
