Kara-2 watched as Kara continued to pace, her words sinking in. “Kara, I’m… I’m so sorry.”

Kara looked to the ceiling of the Fortress, tears stinging her eyes. Her voice was watery when she spoke. “What would you do? If your Lena decided to stay? If she made the choice to stay someplace where you could never see her again?” She looked to Kara-2 with such a broken, innocent expression that Kara-2 had to clear her throat to compose herself before she could reply.

“I.” Kara-2’s brow quirked as she thought about it. She met Kara’s eye, clearly hesistant to share what she would do before speaking. “I would pull her through the portal with me and then suffer the consequences.”

Kara nodded, and when she spoke, her voice was small. “What would the consequences be?”

Kara-2 shook her head sadly. “My Lena is different than yours…” Kara nodded her head. “Things between you are new,” Kara-2 said softly. “Maybe if you gave it a little time–”

Kara sniffed. “When she said she was staying, I said I’d stay too, but. She was right, I can’t. Kal-El is off world on my Earth and there is an evil–”

“You don’t have to explain, I understand,” Kara-2 said.

Kara’s face twisted. “I can’t leave without her.” She shrugged her shoulders. “How can I leave without her?”

Kara-2 stepped forward and hugged Kara, and Kara cried.

***************************

Lena-2 looked across the lab at Lena, who was inspecing the last set of simlated test data from the rebuilt build. “You’re stonier than usual,” Lena-2 said as she took in Lena’s cold face. “Everything all right?”

“Fine,” Lena replied coolly.

Lena-2 didn’t look convinced. “Let me guess, you and your Kara fought again last night.”

The memory of them making love rocked Lena. Her cheeks flushed red and her movements momentarily stuttered. “No.”

Lena-2’s body language went from guarded to concerned. “What happened?”

“Nothing,” Lena said, her concentration back on the task at hand.

“Nothing,” Lena-2 said with a smirk and an eyebrow arched. “Because you’re doing a really bad job of–”

“I don’t want to talk to you about it,” Lena snapped, her eyes blazing as she met Lena-2’s eye. “You don’t get my thoughts just because you share my face.”

Lena-2 nodded contritely. Lena stared for a moment and then went back to her work. Lena-2’s communicator buzzed. She pulled it from her pocket and read it, her eyebrows drawing together. She typed a quick reply and then tucked the device back into her pocket, her face growing contrite, a wave of guilt washing over her. She looked to Lena’s profile, Lena who was so obviously keeping busy on the tasks at hand to keep from falling apart. Lena-2’s face fell, her eyes softening as she understood the situation more clearly. “I’ll run the final diagnostic on the formula,” she said quietly.

“Thank you.”

Lena-2 walked to a bank of computers in the corner and Lena dipped her head, her hand grabbing the edge of the table and squeezing almost painfully. She let go and got to work.

***************************

It was just before noon when the two Karas entered the lab. Kara looked like she was going to come unglued at any minute and Kara-2 looked like she wasn’t faring much better.

“Kara,” Lena-2 said as she stepped toward her wife and Kara-2 waved her off, stopping her in her tracks. There was anger etched into Kara-2’s features, and Lena-2’s breath caught at her dimissive gesture. She swallowed, stung by her wife’s rejection, and nodded to herself, understanding. She had set this in motion, and Kara—both of them—were hurt. Lena-2 looked to Kara and then to Lena. She tried to meet her wife’s eye, but Kara-2 deftly avoid it, her shoulder almost touching Kara’s as they stood still off to the side. She addressed Lena when she spoke. “We should give you two a moment alone before–”

“We don’t need a moment,” Lena interrupted. She refused to look at Kara, her words directed to the floor. “We’ve said what we needed to say.”

“Lena,” Kara gasped out, distraut, and Lena turned her back. Kara swallowed hard. “Lena, please don’t make me do this.”

Lena hesistated for a split second, before she reached over and pressed the button activating the portal. The device hummed to life, the once empty space between the metal parts filling with swirling blue energy that made Lena’s pale skin glow bright. She stared at the portal, memory flooding her. She had done this before. Made the hard choice to do what was best for her and move to National City, even if it broke Jack’s heart. She could do it again. She took a deep breath and turned away from the group, walking to the far part of the room, her back to the portal and Kara.

Lena-2 and Kara-2 looked like they were going to burst into tears, Lena-2 looking to Lena before looking back to where the two Karas stood shoulder-to-shoulder.

Kara stared at the portal, eyes wide and stiff as a board. Kara-2 turned to face her, the two lookalike’s meeting each other’s eye. “I’ll take care of her,” Kara-2 said, her voice wobbling with urgency and emotion. “You have my word.”

Kara nodded numbly. She took a step toward the portal before stopping and turning to Kara-2. “If bad things happen here, promise you’ll come get me,” she said, her voice strident, and Kara-2 nodded. The two hugged, using their full strength, and when they broke apart, Kara looked to where Lena stood, her back still to the group. She could hear Lena crying, could hear her heart beating rapidly in her chest.

Kara took a deep breath, then another, and with all of the remaining strength that she had, mechanically stepped through the portal for home.

***************************

The journey home was as fast as the journey there, and when Kara’s eyes adjusted to the dim light in the L-Corp subbasement, she knew she was home. The blue light behind her disappeared, and she was suddenly as alone as she’d ever felt before. Her eyes struggled to focus, the hum of the flourescent lighting along the walls growing loud like an invasion of cicadas and her chin trembled as the reality of her loss, of losing Lena, coated her being like a suit made of Kryptonite.

***************************

The blue glow cut out as the machine was turned off, and Lena-2 looked to her wife. “Kara–”

“No.”

The word was so final. Lena-2 took in a deep, shaky breath and watched as Kara-2 looked at her with a barely suppressed fury. “I will never understand this, Lena,” she said with a sad shake of her head, and she turned on her heel and left. Lena-2 took a stuttered step after her before she froze, realizing there was no use in going after someone who could superspeed away. If Kara-2 needed time, that’s what she would give her.

The door to the room clicked shut and the two Lenas were left alone. Lena-2 went to the bank of computers and pulled up the data, wiping at her nose in an effort to stay composed. “It was a total success,” she said, her eyes skimming the numbers. There was no reply, and Lena-2 nodded to herself. The air in the room felt oppressive, and Lena-2 wasn’t sure her words would be welcome, but she felt compelled to say them. “I didn’t think you’d stay.” She turned to find Lena’s back still facing her. “It’s why I didn’t tell Kara what I offered you, because I thought once you were faced with losing her– I didn’t think you’d actually stay.” There was no reply, so Lena-2 continued. “I don’t know what happened between you last night, but even if it was the worst fight of your life, that you stayed…” She drifted off, running out of steam as the reality of the situation sunk in.

Lena turned to face Lena-2 and she looked like a ghost, her eyes haunted. “Your wife–”

“Is livid,” Lena-2 said softly.

Lena nodded. The silence stretched between them. “She’ll forgive you.”

“Possibly,” Lena-2 said, her eyes turning watery. “I think this broke her heart.” She shrugged slightly when Lena met her eye. “Mine feels pretty broken, so…”

“I left Jack for National City,” Lena said, robotically.

“This is not the same,” Lena-2 said, head cocked, and Lena’s wide eyes met her for a flash before she looked away. They let silence stretch again before Lena-2 sighed. She rubbed at her forehead, trying to kickstart her rational brain. “In the short term you’ll need to stay with us, at least until we figure out–”

“You need to turn it back on.” The words surprised them both.

“What?” Lena-2 asked, and Lena stumbled as she stepped toward the bank of computers, her motions frantic as the enormity of what she’d just done slammed into her. This wasn’t the same as leaving Jack. She–

She had let Kara go.

She had let the woman she loved—fervently, singularly, totally—walk away forever. And for what? To protect herself from hurt, as if what she felt in this moment didn’t dwarf the hurt she’d felt at Lex’s reveal of Kara’s betrayal, as if it wasn’t tenfold the hurt she imagined she’d be saving the world from with Non Nocere. Being around Kara since she learned the truth about her identity hurt, but this? She clawed at her chest, the pain in her heart almost too excrutiating to bear. “You need to turn the portal back on,” she gasped desperately, her eyes so watery her vision was blurred.

“Lena–”

“The portal–” Lena sucked in a wheezing gasp and she felt Lena-2 grab her, pulling her into a hold that stilled her movements. She looked, wild-eyed at her doppleganger.

“Lena,” Lena-2 said strongly. “We never ran simulations for back-to-back uses–”

“I don’t care,” Lena said, her voice desperate. She broke free from Lena-2’s grasp. “We need to turn it back on, I–” she gasped again, the pain crippling. “I never told her I loved her, too.”

Lena-2’s face fell as she pieced together what must have happened between Lena and Kara, what Kara must have confessed, and Lena nodded in confirmation in the way people do when they know they can’t change the past but they’d be willing to give anything to do so.

“We can try,” Lena-2 said, her face turning determined. “But Lena–”

“It’ll work,” Lena said, steeling herself. “I designed it, we, designed it. It’ll work.”

Lena-2 looked skeptical, but turned back to the bank of computers.

***************************

Kara wasn’t sure how long she stood in the subbasement alone. The phone tucked into her boot was blowing up, a week’s worth of worried phone calls and text messages colliding into the device at once, but she couldn’t move. It felt like, if she just stood still, time would stand still, too. And if time stood still, she wouldn’t have to confront a future that didn’t include Lena. She could fool herself that this was temporary. That the pain she felt slicing through her every nerve was temporary.

She was deep in this space, her mind and body frozen, when the room started to glow, and before she could process why, Lena crashed into her back. Kara spun, her eyes wide, just in time to see the portal blink out of existence, Lena bouncing off of her and falling backwards. Kara grabbed her, steadying her and marvelled. Lena was standing in front of her, solid, and real, and panting as if out of breath.

“Lena–” Kara wasn’t sure the whole word made it past her lips before Lena was on her, kissing her, pressing against her so tightly it was like she wanted to become a part of her, and Kara’s arms circled her desperately, her brain kicking in just in time to keep the embrace from crushing Lena’s bones.

“I love you,” Lena gasped when she broke apart, Kara’s eyes desperately searching her face. “I didn’t say it there, and then you were gone–”

She didn’t get another word out before Kara kissed her. While Lena’s kiss had been desperate, Kara’s was grounding, and when they came apart, they rested their foreheads together.

“Please don’t hurt me again,” Lena said, her voice small and she felt Kara nod against her head. They pulled back, ready to speak when the door opened and Alex’s voice called.

“Kara?”

Kara shared a look with Lena and then shifted where she stood, standing slightly in front of Lena and taking her hand.

“Lena,” Alex said slowly, a confused, distrusting look flashing across her face stopping her in her tracks before she looked back to Kara. “Where the hell have you been?”

“It’s a long story,” Kara said. “There was a portal, and–”

“OK, it’s–” Alex interrupted before she cut herself off, her face pinched and drawn. She looked to them again before settling her gaze on Kara. “You need to come with me. Right now.”

“Why?” Kara asked, her body going on alert, her fingers still tangled with Lena’s.

“Something’s happening,” Alex said, and Kara could tell from the worried set to her face that whatever it was scared her. “Something big, a crisis. The Monitor.”

“OK,” Kara said, suddenly undertstanding the gravity of the situation. She glanced back to Lena before meeting Alex’s eye. “We’ll face it together.”

Alex looked to where Kara and Lena held hands before she looked back to her sister, Kara nodding her head in silent confirmation that things had changed while they were away. Alex nodded and started walking toward the door. “I’ll meet you at the DEO, Brainy and J’onn are already waiting.”

“OK,” Kara said, taking a few steps before stopping. “Wait, Alex.”

Alex stopped and turned to face Kara, her expression pleading. “We don’t have time.”

“I’ll be there, just…” Kara said, and Alex frowned before nodding. She quickly exited the room and Kara turned back to Lena. “I meant what I said, we’ll make the life we want, together.”

“Kara,” Lena said, the uncertainty in her voice clear as she looked to where Alex exited and then back.

“Come with me to the DEO,” Kara said, stepping close and resting her forehead against Lena’s again. “Help me solve, whatever crisis this is, so we can go someplace warm and secluded for a vacation, just the two of us.”

Lena couldn’t help but crack a smile before they met each other’s eye. “You and me?”

“You and me.”

Lena nodded. “OK.”

They entwined their fingers and headed toward whatever it was that faced them, together.
