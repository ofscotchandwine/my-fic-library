    FYI: up until the 1800’s, illnesses were diagnosed based on humors: yellow bile (associated to temper and bitterness), black bile (associated with melancholy), blood and phlegm. For more on humorism, see wikipedia.

    Kara found her way much easier to the cabin this time around. It was not so much that she knew the way but that her feet guided her as if by magic. And when she reached the owl, the rest of the path appeared before her.

     

    She’d brought her tools with her this time as well as some wood, glass, and other items, should she need them. 

     

    She knocked on the door but got no answer. She called out for Lena and when she didn't receive a response she decided to get to work on her part of the bargain.  

     

    She climbed up the cabin and repaired the part of the roof that was causing the leak first, which didn’t take her long. She also replaced the broken window she noted the day before. Last but not least, she did smaller improvements in the outer infrastructure and repaired some issues with the fences around the chickens among other smaller undertakings. 

     

    By the time she was done, she sat on the steps by the door of the cabin, waiting for Lena. She realized now it would have been wise to bring some food as her stomach began to grumble.

     

    “You’re early,” a voice from the side of the cabin startled Kara. Lena had arrived on a black horse that Kara hadn’t seen the day before.   

     

    The blonde quickly rose from the steps. “You’re here.”

     

    “It is my home. Where else would I be?” Lena replied as she dismounted the animal. Kara noted she had a basket with her that held some wild fruits, undoubtedly collected in the forest.  

     

    “It’s just that you weren’t here before, but you’re here now, so that’s great,” Kara said a little too eagerly. 

     

    Lena furrowed her brows at the peculiar show of enthusiasm on the blonde’s part. Then she made her way to the door, opened the cabin and led them in as she placed the basket in the kitchen. 

     

    “Your dress,” Lena remarked. 

     

    “What about it?” Kara inquired. 

     

    “You have yet to repair the tear,” she noted.

     

    Kara looked down at her raggedy outfit. The encounter of the day before had left her so stunned that she’d all but forgotten about that. 

     

    “Right, I’m sorry to show up poorly dressed, I don’t have another one,” Kara said, briefly wondering if there was appropriate attire to wear when meeting a witch. 

     

    Lena nearly chuckled at the needless apology. She then whispered some words Kara could not understand as her fingers moved and pointed to the holes in her dress and the garment magically sewed itself up. 

     

    Kara’s breath was cut short at witnessing the fabric move on its own and her gratitude almost went unheard. “I repaired your roof. And your window, and some other things, like the fence for the chickens. The wood was starting to rot. And the sheep was on the verge of escaping so I fixed the pole deeper into the ground as well,” she said all too quickly.  

     

    Lena glanced at where her broken window used to be. “Thank you,” she said. “You needn’t have bothered.” 

     

    “I wanted to repair it. It’s what I’m good at,” Kara said. 

     

    Lena gave no indication that she cared for Kara’s comment and she returned to her usual silence. 

     

    “So… Do we start?” Kara pressed. 

     

    Lena turned around after she’d washed some berries from her basket and placed them on a plate in the dining table, where she sat and motioned for Kara to join her. 

     

    “Yes, now we start,” Lena said. “You may have some if you like,” she told Kara with regards to the fruit. 

     

    “No thank you,” she declined politely although they looked appetizing. “I’d rather not accidentally consume another magical thing without knowing,” she blurted out, immediately regretting it. 

     

    But Lena surprised Kara with her reply. “My apologies for that. But I assumed you were intent on having my help, so I just sped up the process. These berries, however, are perfectly non-magical, I assure you.” 

     

    “Oh, alright,” Kara smiled, and trustingly grabbed a few and ate them, more than happy to satiate her hunger. 

     

    Kara was all too trustworthy, Lena thought. Here she was, taking fruit from a witch recently after having been tricked into drinking magical tea. Had she not heard of what happened when people took fruits from witches? Luckily for Kara, Lena bore no ill will, and the fruits were just that, ordinary fruit. But her suspicions about the blonde’s naivete were proven correct.

     

    “I’m unsure that I can help you,” Lena said straight out. 

     

    “What? But I did everything you asked, and more,” Kara replied in immediate despair, her mouth slightly filled with the fruit. 

     

    “Indeed you have. Unfortunately, your dream was not as insightful as I’d hoped.”

     

    Kara swallowed. Her dream. Her nightmare. Had Lena seen it all? What had she made of that last part?

     

    “So you saw it?” Kara asked. 

     

    “Not as vividly as you did, but yes, I saw it,” Lena stated. 

     

    Kara said nothing in return. So there was a chance Lena hadn’t seen that last part, or at least not clearly. She wasn’t sure what she felt. For a second it was relief and the next, something a lot like… disappointment. 

     

    Lena proceeded after an unusual silence from the blonde. “Most of it was rather average. A father you lost, a childhood friend you miss, a sister you fear losing, and of course, a witch you fear getting into bed with,” she said, in what Kara could only describe as the shortest summary of the chaos that was her dream. The last of Lena’s words send a strange tingle through her body. It didn’t clarify if Lena had seen that part of Kara’s dream. 

     

    “But,” Lena continued, “I couldn’t grasp your real desires or fears beyond that which you’ve already shared, and none of those are of use to me. It seemed to me in fact, that even you are unaware of your own sentiment. Your childlike dream reflects childlike thinking, an uncertainty, a …lack of understanding.”

     

    Kara was about to reach for more berries when she stopped. Childlike? That’s what Lena had to say about what she told Kara would be a reflection of her deepest thoughts and feelings? 

     

    “That’s not fair,” Kara said, though she was unsure if this was a battle worth fighting. She should be grateful that the witch had nothing to hold against her, yet Kara also knew that meant she had nothing to bargain with. 

     

    “It’s not your fault, I suppose. It is easier not to think of the things that trouble us,” Lena said nonchalantly as she grabbed some berries of her own. 

     

    But Kara was outraged. “I am not simple minded, or whatever you’re making me out to be. I have deep thoughts and feelings, I have… desires and fears that matter!” she found herself nearly yelling. 

     

    “I never said you didn’t,” Lena responded calmly.  

     

    “Yes you did. You said they weren’t of use to you. As if somehow all that I am is simply not good enough,” Kara argued. 

     

    Lena took a breath and pursed her lips. “I told you, I use people’s fears to keep myself safe. If you do not have a fear beyond your sister dying and people finding out you came to me, then I have nothing to ensure that you won’t return tomorrow unsatisfied with my help and with a dozen men carrying torches to burn me or worse.”

     

    “But I would never do that,” Kara said, outraged as well by the implication that she’d be the cause of Lena’s death simply because of a personal dissatisfaction. 

     

    “So all I have is your word that you won’t have me killed?”

     

    Kara sighed, nearly defeated. “Please, I’ll literally do anything. I’ll work here for the rest of my life if it means you’ll cure Alex. I can take care of the chickens and the sheep, and care for the garden out back. I’ll clean and cook, I’ll do anything,” she proposed. “Please Lena,” she pressed one more time. The sound of Lena’s name on her lips felt more natural perhaps than it should. Kara’s eyes shone with tears. This was her last hail mary. If Lena couldn’t help, Kara had no other resort. 

     

    Lena sat quietly as if considering the proposition. Her demeanor softened. “Alright, I accept your payment. You may work here, and depending on the gravity of your sister’s illness I will determine such time as I see fit for you to repay me.”

     

    Kara lit up. “Thank you, thank you, I could never thank you enough,” she repeated as she wiped her eyes. 

     

    “Do not thank me yet Kara, I haven’t helped your sister yet,” Lena said. 

     

    “Right, but I have a feeling you will be able to,” Kara replied confidently. 

     

    “You mentioned a pain of some nature,” Lena got down to business. 

     

    “Yes, Alex is in serious pain. She has often pointed to her abdominal area, the whole of it, sometimes reaching towards her chest, and most often it goes to her lower abdomen,” Kara explained, “it lasts all day long, and it keeps her mostly anchored to her bed. She is unable to rise out of it except as if she’s some old lady, moving slowly and hunched back.” 

     

    Lena hummed. “And I suppose you tried to treat her with more conventional methods.”

     

    “I did. Three apothecaries saw her. They all told us that she had an imbalance of the humors, that she had too much black and yellow bile. We tried alcohol and opium, leeches even. None worked and none of the apothecaries could truly find anything wrong other than the pain,” Kara expanded. 

     

    “That is unusual indeed. Any other changes in her I should know? Changes in her diet, activities, anything prior to this pain?” Lena questioned. 

     

    “None. Everything was exactly as it had always been. I even noticed that my sister grew closer with a friend of hers, Kelly, and that made her happy. I’d dare say she was happier than I’d ever seen her until this pain began.”

     

    “Perhaps a husband… transgressed?” Lena carefully asked. 

     

    Kara shook her head, “Alex and I are unmarried.”

     

    Lena nodded. “Alright,” she said as she stood and made her way to the kitchen where she pulled out several small pots from some cabinets and proceeded to remove a handful of herbs from each one and mix them all together. She then wrapped the mix in a small cloth and handed it to Kara.  

     

    The blonde looked at the cloth. “These are just herbs. As I’ve said, I’ve tried going to apothecaries and they’re useless. This can’t be it,” she complained. 

     

    “And the herbs I gave you yesterday that allowed me to see your dreams seemed just herbs as well, didn't they?” Lena was quick to reply. 

     

    “Yes, that’s true but-”

     

    “But what? Do you doubt my help?”

     

    “It’s not that, I’m just… tired of nothing being effective. I’m tired of seeing her in pain, it makes me hurt inside, it breaks my heart… And I’m just tired of not being able to help her,” Kara said in earnest, the tears nearly returning. 

     

    Lena’s hand barely moved from the table, almost as if she was about to reach over to comfort Kara, but it ultimately remained in its place. “That’s an honorable thing Kara. You’re a good sister. Now, these herbs will help me decipher where in your sister’s body the pain is coming from. Then I will do my best to help her. That is a promise. And while I’m aware that a witch’s promise might not be worth much, it is what I have to offer you.”

     

    “Your word,” Kara stated. “I should take yours but you refuse to take mine,” she challenged.

     

    Lena raised her head and tensed her jaw, “I am not the one in need of help, but I am the one in danger simply by providing it.”

     

    “I wouldn’t put you in danger, no matter what,” Kara contended. 

     

    Lena disregarded the blonde’s words. “You are bound to me still. That entails a strong connection between us. If anything is to happen to me, you might just feel it. So I would recommend you keep your promise. And I’ll keep mine.”

     

    “I will. And thank you for helping,” Kara said as she placed the cloth with herbs inside her pocket.

     

    Lena nodded and sat in place, patiently waiting for the blonde to leave, but instead, Kara remained sitting. 

     

    Something about Lena made Kara feel strangely tranquil, though she’d never share that with another soul for fear that they declare her mad. 

     

    “May I ask you something?” Kara asked, tracing her fingers on the edge of the table. 

     

    Lena’s jaw tightened. “You may,” she said cautiously. 

     

    “How did you become a witch? Was your mother a witch and she taught you?”

     

    Lena was surprised by the questions, that much was clear to Kara. But the blonde could discern nothing else.

     

    The blonde was a curious one, but innocently so it seemed to Lena. The brunette had not answered Kara’s earlier (intrusive) question about getting lonely. But the truth was that she was lonely. Not that anyone else had ever bothered to ask, or care. But why should she admit that to the blonde? Why should she answer her at all? 

     

    Except, it had been a long time since anyone had asked Lena about herself. And something inside her pushed her to answer Kara this time. 

     

    “My mother was a healer,” she found herself replying. “She was very spiritual, though not particularly keen on any religion. She had an immense knowledge, passed down through generations, about the power of nature and of plants among other things. And she used that to help people. She healed their minds, souls, and bodies by mixing the plants and converting them to restorative potions, though all these healing acts were later to be labeled as heresy. She taught me marvelous things, but none of them were magical.” 

     

    “She sounds lovely,” Kara said kindly. 

     

    Lena smiled softly. She hadn’t spoken of her mother in years, and for the first time in a long time, a kind word was spoken about her and it meant the world to Lena. But Lena pursed her lips as she knew what was coming. 

     

    “Then my mother was accused of witchcraft. She was taken by men and drowned as a punishment for something she had not done. I, barely grown at the time, was dragged along with her, saved only by the mercy of some women who claimed I was innocent, that I knew nothing of my mother’s crimes against God,” she said bitterly. 

     

    Kara’s face showed sincere sorrow for Lena, and rather than interrupt, she listened patiently and quietly.

     

    Lena went on, “it was those same women who saved me, the ones that took me in, who taught me actual witchcraft. They were the witches. My mother was not. Many of them didn’t have much moral criteria for their witchcraft, I’ll admit. They were happy to wreck families and destroy villages for whatever they wanted in return, oftentimes killing themselves in the process with magic too powerful. But I don’t know if these acts of witchcraft merited drowning them. I have seen men do much worse and go unpunished. Either way, I learned a lot from these witches.”

     

    “You learned these dangerous spells since you were a child? Couldn’t that have gotten you killed as well?” 

     

    Kara’s concern was earnest, and unheard of for witches. Lena let out a little smile at the words. “Well, rather than the complicated spells that would place a curse on someone’s family for generations, I was more interested in the mundane spells that would help me with my daily tasks.”

     

    “Like lighting the logs, and boiling water,” Kara spoke, remembering the magic of the day before.

     

    Lena nodded, “indeed.” The blonde looked straight upon her with undivided attention, and Lena proceeded. “Not that that last part mattered. A witch is a witch no matter what she does with her power. And though we thought we were safe, we were soon to be hunted just as my mother was. I was found out but managed to escape, barely. Eventually I made it to the village I presume you come from. I knew I wouldn’t last long there either, and at the first sight of trouble, I was warned to stay away or else. Being young, alone and weak, I preferred to retreat here. And now it’s been over a decade.” 

     

    Lena had never said that aloud and it only now struck her that she’d spent a decade isolated in her cabin. She’d gotten so used to being alone that she couldn't even recall what it was like to have someone around, to have someone to simply talk to as she was right now with Kara. 

     

    And also, this was Lena’s first confession to being a witch. She’d never explicitly admitted it before. She’d just told Kara more than she’d told anyone in over a decade. She knew what came next: the disgust, the scrutiny, the moral superiority. There was always punishment for speaking so freely. And Lena waited for the judgment from the blonde to come. 

     

    But instead, Kara reached for Lena’s hand and placed it above her own, rubbing her thumb on Lena’s skin. 

     

    Kara was unsure where she’d gained the confidence to touch the witch, but she hadn’t thought, just acted. “Lena, I’m so sorry,” she said. “I can’t even imagine having gone through all that.”

     

    The brunette blew out a short breath. It had been too long since someone had touched her with kind intent. What a strange thing it was to feel Kara’s strong and slightly calloused hands, to feel her warmth on her skin. 

     

    Lena blinked a couple of times, dazed by the unexpected compassion. “Thank you Kara,” was all she could say. 

     

    “I had a foolish question I was going to ask before you spoke about whether it was worth it.”

     

    “How do you mean?”

     

    “I mean… I was going to ask if it was worth it being a witch, when you’d be hunted for it. I heard about the witch trials. And you mentioned the solitude. I know you said you like it, but I don’t know if I could live alone for a decade, let alone the rest of my life,” she said softly. “But I imagine after what you’ve been through, it probably hasn’t been worth it.”

     

    Lena was about to reply when Kara was spooked by a small cat at her feet. “Whoa!” she exclaimed as she looked down and raised her feet quickly to avoid injuring the cat. “Who’s this little guy?” she said as she took the creature in and kneeled down to it. 

     

    “That’s my cat, though he isn’t usually so fond of strangers,” Lena said, confused while her cat rubbed against Kara’s hands and legs, greeting her as if it were her own. 

     

    “What’s his name?” Kara asked as she caressed him, seemingly having forgotten their conversation.

     

    “Cat,” Lena answered drily. 

     

    Kara chuckled. “That’s his name? A cat needs a proper name, it can’t be that,” she pestered. “How about I name him… Streaky?” she said after a quick thought. 

     

    “Streaky? Lena asked dubiously. 

     

    “Yeah, I think that’s a fun name. He showed up by my feet like a cute streak of darkness,” Kara stated as she rubbed Lena’s cat in all the right places, making him roll over and expose his belly to her.

     

    Lena was floored by the behavior never before exhibited by the cat towards strangers. 

     

    “You like that name right little buddy?” Kara asked the cat as she satisfied its need for belly rubs. “See? He likes it,” she contested with an almost infectious smile. 

     

    “We’ll see,” was all Lena replied, perhaps a little fond of the name already. 

     

    “It’s funny that you have a black cat,” Kara said, thinking out loud. 

     

    “What is the humor in that?” Lena asked. 

     

    “Well, you know, people say that witches have unlucky black cats, among other things,” Kara said, focused mostly on the cat. 

     

    “Do they also say that the cats serve the function of clearing any mice in the area? Because that is what they are for. Luck has nothing to do with them or their color,” Lena explained, having heard this ridiculous association between witches and unlucky black cats too often for her liking.  

     

    “No, they don’t say that. But that makes sense.” Kara accepted the explanation easily, something that did not go unnoticed by Lena. 

     

    “What else do they say?” Lena asked. 

     

    “What? 

     

    “You said, they say witches have black cats, ‘among other things’. So what are the other things they say?” Lena questioned. 

     

    “Oh,” Kara said as she stopped pampering Lena’s cat and returned to her chair. “You know, just other things,” she said in an appalling attempt at stopping the conversation. 

     

    “No, I do not know. Go ahead, tell me,” Lena pressed. 

     

    “Well, they say that they wear black hats,” Kara began carefully. 

     

    “Mhm,” Lena hummed, unimpressed and crossing her arms. “I am not wearing a black hat.”

     

    “I know. That one is silly,” Kara said. “And that they carry a broom and fly on it.”

     

    “No house apparently ever needed cleaning before it seems,” Lena replied sarcastically as she crossed her arms.

     

    Kara chuckled, “Yeah, that one never made much sense to me. Less so about flying on them.”

     

    Lena pressed her lips and uncrossed her arms. “Well, in truth, I might have an idea about how that came along,” she confessed. It was odd but she was enjoying this little talk with the blonde. She hadn’t had a decent conversation with anyone for quite some time. 

     

    Kara leaned forward, interested. “Oh?”

     

    “Remember how I told you my mother used to have ample knowledge about plants?” 

     

    “Mhm,” Kara nodded. 

     

    “Well, those plants often gave people transcendent experiences that took them outside their bodies into another world. Not literally, they only made people feel as though they were flying,” Lena explained. “Many people truly believed they had flown to some other place though they had not moved.” 

     

    “And the broom?” Kara asked. 

     

    Lena tried to hide a smirk. 

     

    Kara raised a brow, even more intrigued than before. 

     

    “Well,” the brunette began, “these plant concoctions couldn’t be ingested, rather they were best absorbed through skin contact. But what many healers discovered was that some parts of the body were most sensitive to them and could absorb them best.”

     

    Kara, clueless about where this was leading, quietly let Lena go on. 

     

    “In fact, one particular part of the female body was ideal for absorbing the mixtures. So the women would grab a broom, slither the top with the plant oils and… gently insert it and rub it on,” Lena said. 

     

    “Insert it… where exactly?” Kara asked, quite sure she was on the verge of misunderstanding. 

     

    Lena raised a brow, “into their… lower lips , their genitalia,” she said, with a now evident smirk.

     

    Kara’s face turned an instant red, her eyes opened wide and her lips went dry. “Oh- right - of- of course,” she stuttered. 

     

    Lena, at the show of reaction let out an honest-to-god laugh. Kara hadn’t heard her laugh until this moment and it was the most beautiful melody that had ever graced her ears. Kara witnessed it with such incredulity and she almost felt as if her own soul was about to leave her body momentarily just as Lena described those plants did to people. 

     

    The blonde felt a smile form on her own lips and soon enough she was laughing herself, her reddened face slowly regaining its normal color.

     

    “You have a beautiful laugh,” Kara spoke. 

     

    Lena’s laughter slowly subsided into a smile, and then a look of disbelief, as if she doubted Kara meant the compliment. “Thank you Kara. I hadn’t laughed like that in a while.”

     

    “That was really not what I expected,” Kara said regarding the newly shared information.

     

    “I would imagine not,” Lena said as she relaxed into her chair. “So tell me, anything else about witches I should know of? I wouldn’t want to disappoint any newcomers. Perhaps I’ll get a black hat to make sure I look the part.”

     

    Kara chuckled at the comment, then waved a hand in the air, “it’s all nonsense. I don’t believe it. At least not anymore anyway. I think some things were wildly misconstrued as I now know, and others they just said to scare people off.”

     

    Lena’s brows pinched, “Such as? I can’t imagine a cat, a hat and a broom are scary.”

     

    Oh Lord, Kara did not want to share this thing about witches. It was an injustice to Lena, a blatant lie. And she’d rather do anything than say it. But Lena had a way about her, a look that made Kara want to say and share. Perhaps something about Lena being so withdrawn made it easier not to worry about the kind of things she’d usually refrain from talking about in her village. As if being so sequestered made all that pressure about right and wrong disappear. And of course, there was something spellbinding about Lena’s entire manner that made her helpless against the witch’s wishes, and it felt something like a type of magic.

     

    “They say that witches are the most horrid and grisly creatures one has ever seen,” Kara confessed with a grimace. 

     

    Lena tensed up at the reply, her smile disappeared. 

     

    “But they’re so wrong,” Kara jumped in before the brunette could say anything. “Nothing could be further from the truth. You are the opposite of what they described, you’re… so beautiful.”

     

    In an atypical manner, Lena looked away from Kara’s eyes, blushing. But she quickly regained her composure. “You flatter me,” she said softly. 

     

    “No I don’t. It’s the truth,” Kara asserted.

     

    Lena smiled, almost against her will. 

     

    A quiet settled between them. A comfortable one this time. Kara would take all the time in the world and just stay here and gaze upon Lena if she could.  

     

    “You should leave now if you wish to take this to your sister before noon,” Lena interrupted Kara’s trance as she began to rise from her chair and head to the door so the blonde would follow.

     

    “Oh, right, yes, of course,” Kara said as she stood and headed towards the door. “So, I will see you tomorrow? And by then you’ll know what’s wrong with Alex, right?”

     

    Lena paused and nodded once, “until tomorrow.”

     

    Kara stepped out of the cabin and Lena watched from her window as the blonde skipped away. 

     

    Lena turned to her cat. 

     

    “Meow.”

     

    “I like her too, against my better judgment,” Lena replied.

    Day 3

    Chapter Notes

    Kara was at Lena’s cabin at first light the next day. She was as hopeful as ever. Today, she was confident Lena would tell her what was wrong with Alex, then make something for her to drink or whisper some incantation and her sister would be cured. She could feel it. To think she’d almost not come to Lena for help. 

     

    Before she knocked, Kara heard a sound coming from behind the cabin. She decided to go investigate and there she found Lena kneeling on the ground, working on her garden. 

     

    Kara greeted her politely and Lena returned the greeting. Lena’s face was usually unreadable, but today, there was something the matter, and the blonde noticed right away. 

     

    “About your sister,” Lena began, jumping right in. 

     

    “Don’t worry about that now. We can talk about it when you finish your tasks,” Kara replied. Something told her she didn’t want to hear Lena’s news no matter how eager she’d been moments ago. “Can I help with anything?” she asked. 

     

    Lena thought about it for a few seconds. She’d play along with this delay if it was what Kara wanted. “I suppose you could feed the chickens,” she said.

     

    Kara smiled and nodded. The brunette stood and walked towards a bag nearby, opened it, reached in for some grain and then poured it on Kara’s hand. 

     

    The blonde made her way to the chickens and placed her open hand for them. But the feeling of a chicken clucking on her skin took her by surprise and Kara dropped the grain by accident and retracted her hand. 

     

    Lena chuckled at the scene and soon, Kara too laughed at herself. “Sorry about that,” the blonde said.

     

    “It’s quite alright,” Lena said kindly as she fed the chickens instead of Kara. 

     

    “So what did you name these chickens? Chicken 1 through 4 and rooster 1?” Kara teased. 

     

    Lena hid a smile at the friendly banter. “I didn’t name them, actually.”

     

    “So basically I’m right,” Kara teased further. 

     

    Lena smiled lightly, “I suppose you’re right.”

     

    “Oh, no, that won’t do, we have to come up with names for them.”

     

    “Are you intent on naming all my animals against my will?” 

     

    “Mhm,” Kara nodded, “unless it’s going to get me cursed,” she dared to joke, testing the waters.

     

    Lena smiled, clearly unoffended, and Kara breathed out in relief. 

     

    “You’re awfully tranquil around me considering I am in fact a witch that could curse you,” Lena remarked.

     

    “Oh, it’s customary to meet a couple of witches in one’s lifetime. In truth, I’m not even all that impressed with your magic,” Kara taunted humorously and with a little more confidence now that she’d managed to land one witch joke with no repercussions. 

     

    Lena turned to her in absolute surprise, but her smile grew at the conversation. “Is that so?”

     

    “Oh yes,” Kara continued, “I was hardly affected by your boiling the water in seconds and lighting the logs on fire. I’ve seen that countless times. I’ve seen even more inconceivable things from other witches.”

     

    “Such as?” Lena teased back.
