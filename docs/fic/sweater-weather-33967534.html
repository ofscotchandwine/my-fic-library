There’s so many things Kara loves about the fall, it’s hard to keep track.

She fell in love with it during her first year on Earth, when the leaves of the oak tree in the Danvers’s back yard had gone from green to ochre and umber in the space of one achingly cold night. 

She remembers it still; standing under the canopy open-mouthed, barefoot, wearing faded hand-me-down pajamas from Alex, eyes glued to the thousand different shades of gold and russet red above her head. Against a perfectly blue morning sky, the colors had been so vivid it kinda hurt the eye to look.

At the time, Kara had no name for what she was seeing. No frame of reference. It’s not that Krypton doesn’t — didn’t — have seasons, but they had long been regulated through technology. Made tame.

Temperatures were kept within what parameters best benefited agriculture. It was the same for rain. Snow...Rao, before being sent to Earth she’d seen it only once. Astra had taken her to a glacier, and Kara had been dazzled by the pure nature of its beauty, by how the frozen surfaces burned copper under the sun. But on Krypton the leaves never changed color, trees never went bare. Air lacked a bite in the morning, it didn’t sear against the lining of her lungs. Storms were a rarity and scheduled, always. No sudden gust of wind to steal a hat, or tug at the end of a scarf. No running from an unexpected downpour.

No...unpredictability.

That’s the first thing about autumn Kara falls in love with, but it is not the last. 

The change of wardrobe is another.

She can ditch the dresses and skirts she’s never really comfortable wearing in exchange for corduroys and button-ups. On the weekends it’s soft flannels and faded jeans. A beanie if the wind is particularly capricious. The pair of scuffed up Timberlands Lena has been trying to convince her to swap out for new ones for two of the three years they’ve known each other complete her off-work outfits, which Alex calls the epitome of lesbianism ---whatever the heck that means. Kara likes women (men too, sometimes) but surely, certainly, her wardrobe choices have nothing to do with that .

Then, there’s the holidays of course. Starting on Labor Day weekend, Kara merrily eats her way through Halloween and Hanukkah, Christmas and New Years Eve. Food is good all year round, don’t get her wrong, but autumn food is special . It’s rich and hearty, brims with warmth. Doesn’t matter how nasty cold it gets outside; there’s nothing than a cup of chicken soup or a mug of cocoa can’t fix. 

Besides, it really is the only time of year she can eat her own weight in fall-themed corporate food without getting judgy stares, because everyone’s too busy doing the same.

But clothing and food and dropping temperatures aside, the reason Kara loves autumn the most out of the four seasons is Lena Luthor.

It’s taken her a while to figure it out — the better part of two years to be exact — and the realization crept on her so quietly it took her even more to connect all the dots. Hours, days, months of careful observation, of study to conclude that, yes, she isn’t crazy. Lena’s mannerism changes depending on the season.

Kara loves all four versions of her equally, of course (not that Lena has any idea), with their unique quirks and subtle differences. 

Summer Lena is a creature of wide-brimmed hats and big sunglasses. Catching sight of her in direct sunlight is rare, and when she joins the Superfriends at the shore, the shady spot beneath the beach umbrella is her natural habitat. In the humid heat that sometimes wraps itself too tight around the city, that smothers it under a blanket of smog and pollution particles if the sea breeze refuses to blow, Lena wilts like a plant without water. 

Lena and lethargic are two words that do not go together —- she’s always so busy, always in a hurry — but summer surely throws a spanner in the works. In summer, especially on the days where the sky’s the color of sour milk and perspiration makes everything sticky, it’s actually possible to convince her to take some time off.

Spring is when Lena’s at the peak of her activity, to the point Kara suspects she’s somehow found a way to recharge from it the way Kara does under Earth’s yellow sun. And she likes the changes spring causes to Lena’s attire just as much as those brought on by winter, the soft cashmere sweaters stored away while pencil skirts reappear, the top button of her blouses left open, the cotton hoodies with the faded MIT logo on the weekends.

As for winter Lena, well, in Kara’s ranking she’s the second best, and not just for the aforementioned oversized hand-knitted sweaters. It’s how cold reddens Lena’s cheeks, how she brightens under Christmas lights, whilst pretending she doesn’t really care for the festivities at all. The fact that she readily accepts holding hands with Kara, never outright admitting that she’s cold, but moving closer until they stand shoulder to shoulder, or she’s curled up against Kara’s side on movie nights. 

And, if Kara’s thermostat is always set half a degree lower than is comfortable for human physiology to facilitate the process… what Lena doesn’t know won’t hurt her, right?

But fall Lena, who pokes her head out timidly at the first turn of the weather and curls chilled fingers around her to-go coffee cup outside of Noonan’s in the morning, who initiates their cuddling sessions — she holds a special place in Kara’s heart.

It’s not that Lena isn’t cuddly, but to be fair, it’s hard to be as clingy as Kara is when it comes to physical touches. It’s… she’s always been this way, okay? Not on Krypton, with her mother and father, but the moment she figured out this sort of affection was tolerated — no, encouraged — in her adoptive household? Yeah, sign her the heck up, thanks. 

On her homeworld, emotion had been regulated too. Showing too much was frowned upon in private, unacceptable in public. Astra had been the only one who understood, that encouraged Kara to follow, listen to her heart. The only person Kara knew who had openly questioned Krypton’s need for utmost control.

And it had cost her everything.

Kara’s tried to curb the habit around Lena, has done her best to hide it at the start of their friendship. Right off the bat she’d know, with the sort of gut instinct that she wouldn’t be able to explain even if she tried, that Lena was — is — someone important. That, for better or worse, she’d play a great part in Kara’s life. Hadn’t wanted to scare her away.

She thinks she’s managed, in the first year, when they were still getting to know one another, but the closer they’ve become, the harder it is to remember why she should hold back, exactly. 

It’s like magnetism, or gravity. Kara knows it’s there, knows how it works, has no choice but to obey it. Even with the gift of flight she can’t stay mid-air indefinitely. Sooner or later the laws of physics call her back to the ground. It’s the same with Lena. She’s done all she can to not come off as smothering, but if their fingers accidentally brush while on a walk, the obvious, natural thing to do is grab her hand. 

Lena doesn’t seem to mind, anyway.

So, when on the third year of their friendship the heat of summer fails to dwindle, August stretching well into September, Kara gets a little fidgety.

“It’s just a heat wave.” Alex dismisses her concern during their daily five mile run. They had to move it back to 5am for the past week — later than that and it feels that they’re gasping through water — so the sun isn’t out yet, and the lingering semi-darkness of the park makes exercise bearable. 

“Well, maybe.” Kara’s toe catches against a root she was too worried to see, she trips, and nearly sprawls at her sister’s feet. “But what if it isn’t? It could be something else. Maybe Lex is trying to cook us all to death, or it’s some sort of hostile alien attack, or…”

“Or, and hear me out here, it’s just global warming.” Alex cuts her off as they loop around the lake. It’s still early enough that the geese and swans are still asleep; the lone one paddling across the water looks miserable already. “You can’t do much about that.”

“Just? JUST ?! Every other week a new study tells us that the damage is almost irreversible, and all you have to say about it is “ just ” a heatwave?” 

“Kara.” Alex stops dead in the middle of the gravel path, taking Kara off guard. She has to double back, skidding to a halt by her sister’s side. “It’s not the first time in our life the weather has been weird. Are you sure this is only about the heat?” 

“No! I mean---yes!” Kara stiffens. Looks away and kicks a loose rock into the bushes. “Of course it’s about the heat. Totally. What else would it be about?”

“You tell me. You’re the one acting all weird.” Alex wipes at the sweat on her forehead with a disgusted sound. Overhead, the sky has paled, and humidity lifts in hazy spirals from the grass. It’ll be another hellish day. Too hot, too bright, too everything . 

“I’m not— what you said.”

“Sure you aren’t.” 

“I’m not weird!” 

“I hear you.”

She’s not weird, but in order to dissuade Alex of the notion Kara’d have to talk about Lena. About what she feels, about how her pulse ticks up whenever the brunette is in her vicinity. She’d have to explain her sweaty palms and the persistent heat under the collar. The perennially dry mouth that has nothing to do with the time of year, and… she’s not ready to do that. 

If there’s somebody who’d understand it’s Alex, Kara doesn’t doubt that. But talking about the crush she’s finally realized she has on her best friend will make it real. And if it’s real — and not just in her head, and in her heart, but real as in put into words real — Kara will have to do something about it. Alex might even suggest she talk her feelings out with Lena, which is inconceivable. Because this suffocating heat makes her feel like crap, but the idea that Lena might reject her is ten times worse, hands down.

Two more weeks go by, then three. The end of September looms large on Kara’s kitchen calendar, but still the weather doesn’t turn. The news has picked up on the unseasonable heat and not a day goes by without doom and gloom reports. The river is at an all time low, and the prices of fresh produce have gone up. If rain doesn’t come soon, the mayoral office will have to resort to staggered blackouts. 

Kara isn’t affected by heat or cold, but the current conditions are taking a toll. As Supergirl, she spends her time helping the fire department on the hills around the city, and most nights she flies home smelling of ashes and smoke, falls asleep before her head hits the pillow only to wake up tangled in sweat-soaked sheets.

As Kara Danvers she can do even less. Just watch the sky for any sign of clouds, and watch her friends grow irritable and short-tempered with one another.

Alex is snippy with everyone, Nia and Brainy look about ready to sublimate into vapor. Even J’onn and M’gann suffer, and they’re almost as impervious to extreme temperatures as she. Of Lena, Kara sees little. The AC at L-Corp broke after the last power outage, and repairs are taking longer than expected, so everybody is working from home. That’s not a problem in itself, but between her actual job and her Supergirl one she has zero time left to swing by Lena’s air-conditioned penthouse, and the only temperature-regulating device in Kara’s possession is a rattling old desk fan, so.

Kara’s so desperate for some Lena time that she decides to buy a portable AC unit, but they seem to be sold out across the State. In online stores too, with stock listed as unavailable for the foreseeable future. 

The days blend into a single indistinguishable mass. Kara can’t untangle them from one another, and it’s only because of the date crawling forward on her calendar that she knows time is indeed passing. October fast approaching is why she has the certainty she’s not just a blonder, sexier version of Bill Murray in Groundhog Day.

Her routine becomes more than a habit. An ingrained reflex, a muscle spasm she cannot stop. Wake up, check the weather forecast, head to work or to the DEO depending on emergencies. Help the fire department until she can barely stand, and snatch sleep where she can. Rinse, repeat.

Eventually, for convenience’s sake, Kara just stays at the DEO. There’s showers and beds in the med-bay, and a well-stocked vending machine, even though she’s barely touching food these days. Most nights she’s too tired to even chew, and anyway anything she puts into her mouth tastes ashen. 

Every few days, Alex forces her to leave. Go home and take a break. It’s never long before she has to be called back, and honestly Kara prefers to keep herself busy. Whenever she’s home, surrounded by the quiet stillness of her apartment, her thoughts take a dark turn. She begins to think that maybe this is never going to end, that they’re never getting rain, or snowstorms in December ever again.

Kara is in the middle of another emergency — the fire is so close to the suburbs this time they had to evacuate several streets — when she hears it. The sound she’s been waiting on, praying for every night before she falls asleep. 

Thunder. 

It comes tumbling down from the mountains, and the crackle-snap of nearby fires is so loud that it doesn’t immediately register. But something stopped her in her tracks, and Kara tilts her face skyward, squinting into grey-orange clouds of ash. The sky isn’t visible, hidden behind boiling waves of heavy smoke, and blistering hot winds blows more in her face. 

Her eyes don’t sting, the poor air quality doesn’t affect her all that much, but because of the extreme conditions the powers tied to her vision are basically useless. 

Then, she hears it again, and is not the only one. 

“Did you hear that?” One of the firefighters she’s been working with for the past several hours shouts through her gas mask. “Is that thunder?” 

Lightning casts the scenery in black and white before Kara can reply. So, instead, she tilts her head again, filters out all extraneous noises. The roar of the fire, the hiss-wheeze of people breathing thanks to their oxygen tanks, the wail of far-off sirens. She’s bone-weary and her concentration slips several times, but she finally manages. 

Going utterly still, she holds her breath, counts slowly just like Alex taught her when she first came down to earth and thunderstorms scared her half to death. Can almost hear her sister now, telling her to just keep counting. Her breathing slows; the entire world does. 

Kara’s not even made it to four when thunder booms again, close enough that it vibrates through the soil of the valley they’ve been fighting to save all day. Another peal follows on the tail end of the first, the interval with the next lightning strike reduced to two. And after that, there’s no longer sense in measuring the distance.

The first fat drops of rain hit the ground like bullets, raising little puffs of ash and debris upon impact. The water hisses over the fires, sputters, seems to make no difference to the flames that swallow it and burn on unperturbed. But it doesn’t take long for things to change. 

Suddenly they’re standing in the middle of a downpour, sheets of rain that drive down from the heavens with ferocity to make up for lost time. The fires falter, the wind drops, and someone over the radio starts to celebrate.

It’s too soon to call this a win, probably — fires have a way of lasting against all odds — but a smile tugs at Kara’s heat-chapped lips. If their luck holds, the worst of it is over.

Despite being dangerously close to power depletion, Kara hangs around a couple more hours. The storm quietens down, rain settles in to stay, and as the smoke turns first to vapor and then completely lifts, she gets her first glimpse of the sky in what feels like forever. 

It is with a sense of disconnected shock that Kara discovers another day has gone by. The bruised gloom of a cloudy dusk wraps the valley floor in bluish shadow, and with the fires around her extinguished by the rain, it’s hard to see even for her.

But she can smell it. Fall. The first hint of it is the slight chill the wind now carries, the almost audible sigh of the scorched land at her feet as it soaks in as much rain as it can hold. 

Most of the trees here have burned but Kara doesn’t doubt that, come morning, she’ll spot red and yellow accents on the tallest spurs of the oaks in the park she can see from her kitchen window.

By the time she makes it to bed it’s well past midnight, and come to think of it she should not have taken the long way home, but it felt so good to fly into the cloud cover, to let its watery touch rinse her of the grime from the fires. 

There’s enough energy left in her tank to take a shower, and then she drags herself to bed, partway asleep already. Her bedroom window is open, a crack, but enough to allow the autumnal breeze an entryway, and when Kara wakes up the next morning, the entire apartment smells of rain. Of puddles, wet soil and more downpours to come. 

The sky cleared overnight, but as she climbs out of her blanket nest and opens the window wide, Kara glimpses another storm on the horizon. Black-grey clouds, tinted pink by sunrise, a line of ink alongside the horizon. She pulls in a breath, as big as her lungs will hold, cold air tickling at the back of her throat. A happy smile dawns on her face, and she stretches in the sun, cat-like. Stops herself short of a contented purr and decides, right then, that she’s going to go and wake Lena up with coffee and fresh pastries from the new bakery downtown. 

But first, she’ll go for her morning run. 

As she throws on an old hoodie and laces up her running shoes, Kara debates giving Alex a call. But it’s Saturday, it’s only 6am, and after the couple of weeks they’ve had Alex might decide to murder her. 

Besides, she kind of doesn’t want to share her first fall run. It’s always special. The wind slashing at her cheeks, the crunch of leaves underfoot. The geese begging for scraps by the lake before they take off to spend the winter in warmer climates. 

It’s selfish, maybe, but Kara’s sure Alex won’t mind missing out on it for once.

One hour and another shower later, she knocks on Lena’s door, entirely unsurprised when it immediately opens. Kara doesn’t think she’s ever caught Lena sleeping in, except perhaps when she’s been sick. And even those few times, it was only for a day or two: Lena makes for a horrible patient.

“Hey.” She waits until their breakfast has been safely set on the kitchen counter before pulling Lena into one of the overenthusiastic hugs her best friend never starts but always melts into. 

“Hey yourself.” They sway in place for a few moments before Lena disentangles, looking for all the world like she doesn’t want to. “I’ve missed you.”

“I’ve missed you too.” Kara accepts a vanilla glazed donut and bites into it with gusto. “But there won’t be any fires for a while.”

“God, that was so terrible.” Mid-sip, Lena halts and shudders. “Every time you— I thought—” 

“Thought what?” 

Lena doesn’t reply, eyes fixed somewhere above Kara’s shoulder. Mind lost somewhere unpleasant. She’s gripping her coffee cup so hard the lid pops off, and some of the hot liquid sloshes out onto her hand. On the floor.

“Ow.” 

“Rao.” In a blur Kara’s there, tugging her toward the sink. “Are you hurt? Put your hand under the water.”

“Kara, it’s okay.” Bracketed between her body and the sink, Lena lifts a hand to cup her cheek. Strokes softly. “It’s just a little coffee.”

“It’s a burn !” Turning the water on, Kara guides Lena’s injured hand under the water jet, and glares at the reddened skin as if that’d be enough to chase the pain away. “I’m only bringing you ice coffees from now on. I—”

“God, you’re so warm.” Lena sighs, thumb migrating from her cheek to trace along her jawline, and Kara freezes. She must still be tired, heck, perhaps she’s still asleep and this is all a dream. Because Lena’s just leaned back against her frame, her back flush to Kara’s front, and she’s never done that before. “I’d forgotten how warm you always are.” She shivers, pointedly, and that snaps Kara out of her stupor.

“It’s not that cold, yet.” She deflects, trying to keep her voice steady. “Fall is barely here.”

“You’re kidding right?” Without warning Lena twists around, out of her grip, and they are now nose to nose. So close that Kara can smell her shampoo, something that reminds her of fresh hay and lilies, and it’s tempting to card both hands through the silken, unbound mass of Lena’s hair. “It dropped down by ten degrees overnight!” 

Unprompted, strong fingers curl around the edge of Kara’s hoodie, sneak underneath the fabric, idle loosely on her belly. They’re cold, and Kara jumps, but the chill that spreads from Lena’s touch isn’t the reason. 

“See?” The fingers wiggle their point across Kara’s suddenly tingly skin, and that’s both better and worse. “I’m freezing.” 

“Well, uhm.” If Kara wasn’t sure Lena is none the wiser to her elephant-sized crush, she would say she’s being repaid for all the times she set the thermostat too low. But that’s impossible. Lena loves her, but like, as a friend. And it’s normal for friends to get handsy when cold, right? Certainly not the turn of events Kara had expected for today, but hey. She’ll take it.

“We could, uhm, watch a movie?” Kara’s not sure what that has to do with Lena’s body temperature, but she’ll figure something out. “I mean..” She casts desperately around for something to latch onto, and her gaze roams unhindered out of the open-plan kitchen, into the living room beyond. From where they are standing she has a clear view of the sectional, and there’s several thick-looking fleece blankets folded on top of it. Relieved, she points them out. “You have blankets.” Lena gives her a look, but really it’s a reasonable suggestion. “I’m sure they’ll keep you warm while we watch tv. Why don’t you go ahead and pick a movie while I put the rest of the food on plates?” 

Alex tells her it’s strange that she knows where everything is in Lena’s house, but up until now, Kara’s dismissed the fact without much thought. They spend a lot of time together after all, and when she says a lot she means they end up at one another’s place several evenings a week, so it’s obvious that she’s figured out where things are. Kara likes to help out. It’s a logical consequence. And, she has a good memory. Once she sees something, it takes more effort to forget about it than remember.

“You do at least owe me  a movie marathon.” Lena steps back, making breathing easier. Kara goes from near spontaneous combustion to a manageable simmer. “We can watch anything I want?” 

“Anything you want.” 

Lena nods, turning away, but not before Kara’s seen something like victory glint inside her eyes. 

Kara grabs the kettle, selects one of Lena’s favorite black teas and picks two sturdy mugs from her extensive collection. What coffee is left she pours down the drain; they’ve putzed by the sink longer than she’d thought, since it’s gone from scalding to disgustingly lukewarm. A blue plate accommodates the rest of the pastries she brought, and once the water’s piping hot she arranges everything on a tray for easier transportation.

All in all, the task shouldn’t take more than a few minutes, but Kara makes vigorous use of the time, puttering around the kitchen, wiping away at the coffee spill on the tiles even though she could leave that for later.

She’s painfully aware of the fact that Lena’s staring. Possibly wondering why it’s taking her so long. Frankly, Kara wonders too. She usually leaps at a chance to cuddle with Lena, but today… today the atmosphere is oddly tense, the silence thick, dragging on unless they make a conscious effort to break it. 

Unbidden, her eyes roam to the window. Nope, that isn’t it. The sky’s still clear, the picture-perfect blue lulling her into a sense of false security. But something’s brewing. Something new, and as unpredictable as wind shaking every last leaf loose. Kara’s always loved unpredictability, but when it comes to Lena she finds she much prefers the known and the familiar.

So, this is what she knows.

That she has a crush on Lena. That she’s nurtured it and fed it when perhaps she shouldn’t have, and that Lena doesn’t know. Can’t. Never will, because Kara isn’t about to come clean and ruin their friendship.

She’s in love, but she’s not stupid.

“Kaaaara.” The TV is on, and Lena pouts at her from under a blanket. “Come ooon.”

Okay. She can do this. Just has to keep some distance. 

One steadying breath later and Kara joins Lena on the couch, palms sweating, heart beating hard against her ribs. She manages to stick to her guns for the first half hour of the bland rom-com Lena selected, but no sooner has she congratulated herself over the achievement that things take a turn for the cuddlier . 

It’s a subtle shift, and Kara’s allowed herself to relax so she doesn’t notice right away. Only when Lena’s weight presses against her side, does she realize the chasm is gone, the gap between them bridged. There’s enough free couch to sit three more people at the very minimum, but Lena seems determined to climb into her lap.

“I’m really cold.” Lena meets the rise of Kara’s eyebrows with another shiver. “And I really, really missed you.”

Kara missed her too. The weeks they were forced to spend apart lacked color, joy. Everything. She can survive a couple movies with Lena on her lap. She can .

“Come on.” She opens her arms in invitation, and doesn’t have to say it twice. It takes some maneuvering, more than a bit of squirming and a corner of the blanket in her mouth, but she ends up with Lena sitting in her lap, head tucked against her shoulder.

Things get worse from there. 

For the first ten minutes of this new position everything seems normal. As much as having your best friend’s lips brushing against the side of your neck with every breath can fall in that category. 

Kara keeps her eyes on the TV, but it might as well be off for all the attention that she’s paying it. It’s too quiet in the apartment, and Kara wishes she was the one with the remote. Upping the volume would drown the little noises Lena’s making. Her sighs, the thump thump of her heart (but that could be Kara’s), the tiniest, not-quite-whimper that escapes her when Kara forgets herself enough to start rubbing her back.

“Still cold.” Lena declares matter-of-factly as she slips both hands under Kara’s hoodie yet again. This time, they don’t just rest there, but roam along the waistband of her sweatpants, pause over the crest of Kara’s hip, before stroking lightly back toward her navel. Thank Rao she isn’t ticklish. 

“I can brew another cup of tea?” 

“Don’t move.” As if she could. “ Just hold me? Please?” And it’s unfair of Lena to ask her like that, with her chin trembling slightly, her eyes all shimmery emotion. Kara’s never been able to say no to her before, about anything, and she’s not gonna start now.

“Okay.” Lena leans against her almost boneless, and before her face angles to press against Kara’s shoulder she sees it again. A glint of victorious self-satisfaction. The confirmation of some theory or thought. 

Kara’s eyes narrow. Her brows wrinkle in suspicion, and the crease that appears on her forehead is deep enough it could pass for a ravine.

Well, two can play this game.

On the flatscreen TV, the protagonist — whose name Kara forgot as soon as she heard it — chases his co-star into the driving rain, grabs her by a wrist, and spinning her around, kisses her with passion.

That gives Kara an idea. 

A crazy, insane, demented idea.

Alright, she can be stupid sometimes.

“Lena?”

“Yeah?” Predictably, Lena’s face tilts up. “What?” 

“I was thinking…” That’s a first. “If you’re cold, I can…” Kara leans in, almost but not quite closing the distance. “I could…” Lena is going to jerk back. Laugh nervously. There’s a chance that she will blush, or giggle. Likely demand what the heck Kara thinks she’s doing. 

Honestly, she would very much like to know too.

But Lena, damn her, does none of these things, and now Kara is committed. Unbalanced, she’s leaned too far forward, and sheer momentum propels her the rest of the admittedly small way.

And then they’re kissing.

It’s portentous. It’s nothing like Kara expected and everything she’s dreamt of. Mainly, it’s awkward. Soft gasps and hands fisting, clawing at clothes.
