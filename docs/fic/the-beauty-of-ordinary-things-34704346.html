It started with, of all things, a plum.

Specifically one plum. It could have been any plum. There were others. It wasn’t like it was the last plum in the store or even the only ripe one. It might not have even been the sweetest or tastiest. It was just a plum, sitting on a smallish stack of other plums in the produce section of one particular, organic market in downtown National City.

It could have been any market, of course. There were many. This one managed to be one of only a handful that strived to be zero waste, and it was certainly a quaint market, friendly staff and pleasant patrons. So maybe that was why it was this particular market, on a random weekday afternoon, where one, unassuming plum managed to be the catalyst for a truly remarkable meeting.

Lena Luthor had people to shop for her. Clothes, sundries, groceries. There was an entire personal assistant industry that ran exclusively on catering to the needs of people who were too rich or important to do it themselves. But it was likely that very fact that was the reason she felt compelled to do at least some of her shopping for herself. Life offered so few moments for what one might call normalcy, and the woman was quite content, eager in fact, to enjoy the few that she could.

It was a strange thing, being a Luthor, one Lena had grown used to, if against her will. One that carried a lot of preconceptions behind it. More than a few she had made considerable efforts, as an adult, to counter where she could. Not always successfully, but, still, it was a matter of personal conviction that she at least try to be better than what so many of her family had spent so long making themselves very well known for.

Thus, Lena Luthor was the kind of person to do things for herself, like grocery shopping. And she was the kind of person to try and go anonymous where she could, to try and avoid leveraging, even accidentally, the kind of power her name could wield. She was not the kind of person to boast or brag, and she was definitely not the kind of person to take things that were not meant for her, even if she could. Which was probably why when she reached for this particular plum, after spying it from across the produce section sitting on its little pile of plums, and her fingers brushed those of another person also reaching for this particular plum, her first instinct was to pull away.

“Oh, sorry,” came the reply before she could make her own, stumbling answer of “Please, go ahead,” while ducking her gaze behind shades, an extra means of hiding her identity.

“No, I insist,” the other person replied, a light and pleasant tone to their voice, though Lena still hadn’t looked up at their face.

“No, no, you were here first,” came Lena’s knee-jerk response.

“I don’t actually think I was,” followed immediately.

The inanity of arguing over a single plum, especially when there are other, perfectly good plums to choose from, was overshadowed by Lena’s own, sudden annoyance at the interaction. “Take the damn plum,” she wanted to say, though didn’t but regretted thinking it. She wanted to chalk that up to a very long day at the office, of which this little sojourn into normalcy was a welcome reprieve. Instead of a terse rejoinder, she finally turned to look at her plum rival and was met, and instantly struck speechless, by the most startling blue eyes.

Granted there was a lot to look at, golden hair, broad shoulders, that smile, but it was the eyes that caught her. The moment you caught sight of them you were suddenly very, very thankful that they were looking at you at all, and wondered what exactly you’d have to do to get them to keep looking at you.

“You can really have it,” Lena heard the woman say, though it didn’t quite register at first with those perfect, blue orbs staring at her.

“Wh- What?”

“The plum,” the woman said, pointing. “It’s all yours.”

And it took every ounce of Lena’s willpower to shift away from those eyes and follow the finger pointing out the plum in question, the object of their impromptu tug-of-war, sitting, as previously stated, on a stack of other, fully acceptable plums.

“Oh,” Lena replied, for what of anything to say, hesitating, for just a moment, before turning back, almost afraid to fall into those azure pools again, but not so afraid as to not. “No, it’s fine. Please,” she managed.

“Oh, don’t be silly,” the blonde replied. “Here,” she added and reached over to grab the very fruit and hold it out towards Lena like it was a perfectly normal thing to do.

Lena stared at the plum, back at the woman, back and forth, back and forth as her brain tried to reconcile a universe that now contained one very odd, yet startlingly beautiful woman.

“Uh, thank you,” she finally replied, though without reaching for the plum.

She saw the woman smile and hold the plum out to her, more. Finally, Lena plucked it gently from her grasp, fingers brushing ever so slightly in a way the businesswoman would most certainly claim was accidental if ever asked.

“What are you going to use it for?”

Lena’s eyes darted up from the fruit, once more to be completely entrapped by those eyes of blue. “What?” She replied. She’d be absolutely mortified at her lack of oratory skills if her mind wasn’t elsewhere right now.

“The plum,” the woman asked with that terribly disarming smile, “What are you going to make with it?”

Lena stared at the plum, suddenly bereft of an answer. She had simply meant to eat it, but that felt, somehow, disappointing an answer to give to this stranger who unexpectedly felt deserving of a better answer.

“They make great pies,” the woman added. “Or ice cream toppings. Ooh!” She exclaimed, nearly knocking Lena back with the sheer strength and suddenness of her excitement. “You should make a cobbler! Cobblers are delicious.”

And, despite herself, or despite who she would be anywhere else, who she was not five minutes ago before reaching for this particular plum and meeting this strange woman, Lena found herself very curious about cobblers. “I don’t think I know a cobbler recipe.”

“Well, good news, I have a killer one,” the blonde replied. She glanced at the plum in Lena’s hand. “Of course, you’ll need like 8 or 9 more. Here.”

And the woman pulled out a reusable, produce bag and began taking more plums off the stack, before handing the sack to Lena, who took it more out of reflex than thought, incredibly surprised at what was happening, yet not moving to stop whatever this was.

“You’ll also need apple brandy. Got any at home?”

And Lena made a quick list of the various alcohols she had access to, momentarily felt a pang of shame at the length of it, shoved that down quickly, and simply shook her head.

“Come on, we’ll go find some,” and the woman began walking towards the spirits aisle, presumably to continue shopping for the ingredients of this recipe, as if this were a perfectly normal interaction between people who hadn’t met each other two minutes ago and knew far more about each other than a momentary desire for a single plum. And, as if that weren’t odd enough, Lena Luthor found herself following right after the blonde woman, as if none of the above mattered whatsoever.

The blonde’s name, it turned out, was Kara. She was a cub reporter for CatCo Magazine, had one adopted sister, loved dogs but also cats and also birds and pretty much anything cute and pet-able and many species that were neither, was Bi, was really into N’Sync, and thought curmudgeon was a very funny word.

This might sound like an amount of information that would label someone a stalker simply for knowing it, however Lena learned it all in the span of 20 minutes, quite unprompted, straight from the woman with the startling blue eyes’ mouth.

“So, what’s your story?” Kara asked her newfound shopping companion after laying her full-on life story out.

Lena stammered for a moment. Now that she had some space for her brain to fit its way back in, she was, unfortunately, cognizant enough to be self-conscious about, well, everything. Most of all the fact that she was Lena Luthor, a woman’s who name and face were imminently recognizable and couldn’t but garner some kind of reaction, even from a perfect strange, and, despite being a perfect stranger, less the amount of information Lena now knew about her, the CEO couldn’t help but feel anxiety at the thought of this new woman finding things out about her and, horribly, being disappointed by them.

“I, uh… I’m in business,” she replied.

“Really?” Kara replied, as bubbly as she was blonde. “What kind?”

“Uh… biotech,” was Lena’s answer. Not a lie. L-Corp was in biotech, and a host of other things.

“That’s amazing!” The blonde said, and, despite herself, Lena actually believed she thought it was. The dark-haired woman found herself just a little excited too.

“Uh, yeah,” she replied. “I guess it is.”

“So, do you work in a lab or are you one of those stuff, marketing types?” And Lena might have been insulted at the insinuation but for those blue eyes again, and that twinkle in them, matched by the slight lift of her mouth, that told you the blonde didn’t quite mean it.

“I used to work in the lab,” Lena sighed. “But now I’m more on the stuffy, marketing side.”

Kara hummed at this as they kept shopping, the concept of “they”, so new and surprising, not lost on Lena, though the woman pushed any hesitation aside, unwilling to question it, unwilling to break the spell of this very ordinary, if unexpected, occurrence.

“So, science major, right?”

Lena shook herself out of her thoughts for a moment and looked quizzically at the woman.

“In college?”

“Oh, yes,” Lena replied. “MIT.”

“I bet you never miss a chance to mention that, huh?” The blonde laughed.

“No,” Lena shot back. “If I did, they’d stop inviting me to alumni events.”

The blonde laughed again, and Lena, again quite unexpectedly, thrilled at the sense of accomplishment.

“Liberal arts major, I take it?” She asked the blonde, further reveling in the ease of this conversation. How long had it been, she would wonder later? Since she had been able to talk to someone like this? So easy, with no strings or qualms or agendas? Had she ever? Maybe that was why the unexpectedness, the oddity, of it all didn’t turn her off. In her life maybe the only real conversation she could have was with a stranger she ran into at the grocery store.

“And proud of it!” Kara exclaimed confidently, then waggled her head. “I mean, if you asked, communication majors do see themselves as a breed apart from the, you know-“

“Art history school?”

“Exactly.”

“Ah, but where would we be without comparative superiority?” Lena added, again terribly proud of the dialogue they were building.

“Library sciences,” Kara quickly pointed out.

“Are you kidding?” Lena exclaimed in faux shock. “That country club!”

And the blonde guffawed, momentarily drawing looks from the handful of patrons perusing the market. Lena’s own glee was nearly abated at the thought of the onlookers, but she was soon distracted again by those amazingly blue eyes, shining with laughter.

“You’re fun, I like you,” Kara replied with all the confidence of an elementary school student meeting their best friend on the first day of class.

They wound their way down the aisles, trading bits and pieces, jokes and random takes on food items, until Lena’s basket was filled with all the necessary ingredients for a plum cobbler, and yet none of the things she had actually walked in the store for.

“375 degrees for 45 minutes,” Kara explained. “Golden brown top and bubbling filling.”

Lena nodded along with the instructions as she jotted them down on her phone, fully bought into the fact that she was now, apparently, going to attempt to make a plum cobbler. When she looked up it was into those blue eyes, staring back at her oddly. It suddenly occurred to Lena that they were standing next to the registers, both baskets quite full. Her stomach dropped at the thought of this, whatever it was, had been, or could be, ending.

“Hey,” Kara replied. “What are you doing Friday night?”

And where her stomach had dropped, Lena’s heart now leapt into her throat. No… that can’t be right. Surely she wouldn’t… She couldn’t. People didn’t do that. Or if they did, it didn’t happen to Lena Luthor.

The dark-haired woman sputtered for an answer, finally, as unexpectedly as anything else the last hour had held, landing on, “Nothing.”

“You should come to game night.”

And Lena was confused by the sudden change. Confused and disappointed. “What?” She said.

“Game night,” the blonde repeated. “I have it every week with a bunch of my friends. You should come.”
Lena blinked several times. “Game night?”
“Yeah, you know, board games and stuff?”

“Uh, yes,” Lena replied. “I know what it is. It’s just…”

“What?”

Lena gulped. “You hardly know me.”

Kara shrugged.

Lena gaped.

“Yeah,” the blonde added. “That’s kind of the point of game night: to get to know you. You can tell a lot about a person that way.”

“Over board games?” Lena had to ask.

The woman nodded, quite assured. “Exactly. I’ve vetted like half a dozen potential relationships that way. After all, I’m not going to want to hang out with you if you’re a sore loser at Monopoly.”

And Lena’s confusion at the whole shape her afternoon had taken, her anxiety at the invitation itself, her surprising elation at the prospect of more time with this strange yet intriguing woman, was pushed aside by her own self-assurance. “That’s assuming I’d lose at Monopoly at all.”

The blonde beamed. “Oh, this is going to be fun.”

And she grabbed Lena’s phone out of her hands. The dark-haired woman panicked, any number of uncomfortable possibilities leaping to her mind. After all, Kara couldn’t possibly know who’s phone she had just grabbed. But she managed to school herself from snatching it back in a panic and instead watched the woman pull up the contacts app and open a new entry. After a minute, she handed the phone back, and Lena stared at “Kara” written so nearly there, followed by a number and an address.

“7 o’clock,” the blonde said with that disarming smile. “See you then?”

And, despite herself, despite literally everything else, Lena Luthor nodded in reply. The blonde smiled and stepped towards the registers, only to stop and spin around.

“I didn’t get your name,” she asked.

And the Luthor gulped but steeled herself. “Lena,” she simply said and felt her heart soar when Kara’s smile brightened.

“Lena,” the blonde said. “I like that.”

“Plum cobbler?”

“Yes.”

“You’re making plum cobbler?”

“Yes, Jess.”

“Because a blonde stranger in a grocery store told you to?”

“Well, I mean… yes, but there were extenuating circumstances.”

“Which are?”

“She has the most startling blue eyes.”

Lena would like to say there had been more to the conversation than this, but there really hadn’t. After a very, very interesting trip to the market, for what was supposed to be a few, healthy essentials to make up a working dinner in the office, the CEO had stepped into her office with bags full of exactly none of the things she had planned on getting, right past her assistant, who, to her credit, (she was a very good assistant), said nothing while her boss strode into her office with the most dreamy look on her face, waited patiently while the woman set the bags on her couch, sat down in her chair, and turned to her desk, and watched while that dreamy look seemed to fade into total confusion, before she followed the woman inside to ask what the hell had happened.

Lena, unfortunately, did not have a lot of context to offer.

Jess rubbed her temple contemplatively. Being the assistant to a Luthor usually meant inappropriate advances, verbal harassment, and an unsustainable work-life imbalance, so she had, naturally, counted herself quite lucky to be the assistant to THIS Luthor. She was learning that, while the weirdness was certainly more agreeable, there was still a lot of weirdness to the job.

“So,” the woman said, slowly, trying to order the events in a way that made sense. Failing. “You picked up a woman in the grocery store?”

Lena was apoplectic for all of 5 seconds. “I did not pick up a woman in the grocery store.”

“You were picked up by a woman in the grocery store?”

And Lena was halfway to objecting when her memory flashed back to the store and an image of the blonde reaching for something on a high shelf, her sleeve dipping back to reveal a bicep, and giving the dark-haired woman a lot of ideas.

“I…” she started for something to say, then gave up. “I guess. Maybe?” And she looked up at her assistant, expecting admonition, and was very surprised to find intrigue.

“Did you get her number?” Jess asked.

And Lena suddenly realized she had. She looked at her phone. “She, uh… She invited me to game night.”

And Jess’s face slipped back to confusion. “Game night?”

“Yeah.”

“Like the fun kind of games or the ‘fun’ kind of games?”

Lena’s mouth went fishy. “Well, she said her friends will be there.”

“That doesn’t really answer the question,” her assistant added with a smirk.

“Jess!”

The woman threw up her hands. “Sorry, it’s just as first date’s go, it’s, you know, a little odd.”

And, analyst that she was, Lena had to wonder at that. The strangeness of it all, which hadn’t left her, felt so much more acute now that she wasn’t getting lost in those gorgeous, blue diamond orbs.

“She said she vets potential relationships that way,” Lena commented, trying to parse it all out herself, divine some meaning to what was, increasingly, feeling like an unreal experience.

Jess looked off in thought as well. “Really?”

Lena nodded. “She says you can tell a lot about a person by how they act at game night.”

The assistant had to nod at the wisdom. Her brow furrowed, and she asked the question Lena had been mulling over since the offer had been made. “But what did she mean by ‘potential relationship’?”

“I don’t know, Jess. That’s what I’m trying to figure out!” Lena nearly exploded. Her hands suddenly covered her face, right before she dropped her head to her desk.

Jess slid into the chair opposite. She reached a hand to pat the woman’s shoulder comfortingly. “So,” she asked gently. “Are you going to go?”

Lena’s head lifted off the desk by a degree. “Should I?” She asked. “I mean I hardly know the woman.”

“I think you should,” Jess replied, to her boss’s eyebrow raise. “Ms. Luthor, I know it’s not my place to say this as your assistant, so that’s why I’m going to say it this way, so you know it’s coming from a friend.” She smiled gently at the dark-haired woman. “Lena, I think you need to get out more.”

And the kindness of the statement, and the spirit with which it was offered, settled over the CEO like a warm blanket. She sighed. “That bad, huh?”

Jess could only shrug. “You’re a really great person. I mean that not just as someone whose checks you sign. I mean that seriously. You’re kind. You’re smart. You’re funny. And if I swung a certain way, I’d say that hourglass figure belongs in oil paintings.”

Lena had to choke and laugh at the same time.

“If you ever want to grab a drink, I’m always up for it,” her assistant continued. “But you also need friends who know you outside this place.” She waved an arm to the office, the building, the whole L-Corp schtick. “The real you.”

And Lena had to smile. “Thank you, Jess.” She sighed again. The woman wasn’t wrong, but the suggestion of “real” friends brought to bear a set of facts the Luthor had quietly pushed aside since she locked eyes with the stunning blonde. “But she doesn’t know the real me,” she said sadly. “I don’t think she even knows who I actually am.”

Her assistant met her doubt with another smile. “Perfect,” she said.

Lena’s eyebrows met the ceiling again. Her assistant leaned over the desk as she rose.

“Game nights are for figuring people out, remember?” She replied. “So go show her the real you.”

And, with that, Jess turned and marched back towards her desk. It amazed Lena, the luck she had finding Jess. So much so she was starting to wonder if Jess found her. If maybe that was what her life was. Since she seemed so against finding people herself, they just came knocking at her door. Running into her in the market.

She sighed again. It was still a lot, but it felt lighter. It felt less like a wall now and more like a door. One she found herself desperate, excited, to step through. She turned to her assistant before she stepped out of the office.

“What do people bring to game nights?”

And, so, on a Friday night following a very interesting week, the highlight of which was the very interaction that brought her here, to a stranger’s apartment, Lena Luthor found herself knocking on this very strange, yet somehow oddly charming, door after only 5 or 6 minutes of really psyching herself up. The dark-haired woman shifted the bags between her hands. It had been something of a point of anxiety, (well, the whole damn thing really, but this had become a bit of a fixation), as to what people were meant to bring to game nights. Jess’ simple answer “themselves” not being adequate, her assistant eventually supplied that food and drink could hardly be turned down.

Lena breathed. She caught the wisps of laughter beyond the door and tried not to think if it would absolutely come to a halt the moment she stepped inside. It suddenly occurred to her that, while her host did not seem to recognize her, there very well may be a room full of people behind this door that would. Lena breathed again.

There was the soft padding of footsteps, and then the door was flung open, and Lena’s entire gaze was blonde hair framing an adorable face and the most striking blue eyes the woman had ever seen.

“Hey!” Kara exclaimed. “You’re here!”

“Hi,” Lena replied.

She stared. Blue eyes stared back at her, and Lena might very well have been content standing in that gaze, if her own internal alarms didn’t alert her to the awkwardness of standing in the doorway of a woman you’ve met all of once staring at her in silence.

“Uh,” the, at certain times, quite well spoken CEO replied. “I brought wine,” she added, holding up the cloth bag in one hand. She spun it to show the second bag in the same hand. “And beer.” She held up her other hand. “And a cheese plate.” She looked down, searching desperately for what else because, somehow, that didn’t seem enough. “Oh!” She exclaimed and held up the last bag. “And plum cobbler!” She smiled what she was sure was the most awkward smile in existence and stared at those blue eyes, who just stared back at her. Again, she could probably have happily died here, but it kept occurring to her that no one else was saying anything. “Is that…?” she stammered. “Is that too much? Should I not have brought anything?”

And the blonde woman suddenly beamed back at her, and, correction, Lena could definitely happily die here. Kara laughed. “Clearly, you have never been to a game night.”

Lena’s smile faltered. Her eyebrow raised in question.

“No one turns down free food and booze,” the blonde replied before reaching over and taking half the bags from the dark-haired woman’s grasp. For a moment, her fingers warmly brushed Lena’s and the Luthor thought she was going to melt.

Her impromptu host led her inside a very cozy, open-floor loft that, despite knowing very little about the woman, seemed to perfectly suit Kara in its light and warmth. The place was so homely. Lena couldn’t help but feel invited in. She was tempted to exult in that feeling, relax even, until she was reminded they were not alone as she suddenly became aware of five pairs of eyes now trained on her, all seated on comfy-looking couches and chairs around a coffee table in the living room at the center of the apartment.

Lena froze.

“Hey, everybody, this is Lena,” Kara introduced her casually. “And look, she brought food!”

The offerings seemed to momentarily distract the group, two of which got up to help take in the food and alcohol. Lena found herself quickly introduced to everyone.

There was Nia and Querl, called Brainy, coworkers of Kara at CatCo. James, another co-worker/“boss”, though Kara said that with air quotes and looks to Nia and Brainy like this was probably a recent event and none of them could stand to take him seriously. There was Kelly, James’s sister, and girlfriend to probably the most important person in the room, from what Lena could gather, mostly because she had been staring at the dark-haired woman quite thoroughly since she had stepped through the door, Kara’s sister, Alex.

The greetings were warm, mostly. Somewhat clipped from Brainy and James, but Lena thought she could chalk that up to neurodivergence from the former and just general awkwardness from the latter. Nia was nearly as bubbly and welcoming as the blonde, and Kelly was, by the far, the most comforting, if only for the look she gave Lena that seemed to communicate she was very aware of how uncomfortable the woman must feel. And there was Alex, who offered a handshake and a quick, flat, “Nice to meet you,” before going back to staring. Thankfully, the booze was quickly poured and everyone got distracted by the cheese plate.

So, Lena Luthor tried her best to get comfortable in this place, which was both not hard and hard. Hard because of, well, so much, but not hard because it seemed her grocery store acquaintance had no intention of letting her feel unwelcome at all. Kara got Lena a drink and guided her to a spot on the couch, sliding right next to her such that there thighs touched, and the dark-haired woman felt like she could very much let the world fall away if they kept that up.

With booze and food happily flowing, the game night got started in earnest. Lena recalled her college days, where nights like these certainly weren’t a foreign concept, but Ivy Leaguers have a tough time ignoring competition of any variety, so those tended to devolve into all sorts of unpleasantness or mere excuses to be drunk. This was categorically different, and Lena quickly began to realize why. The game, or games as they made their way through several, were secondary to the actual point of the night, which was just to be in the same room with all these people.

And, despite herself, or maybe because of the blonde woman sitting next to her, Lena found herself become more and more comfortable as one of those very people.

They wound their way through several means of passing time, starting with Jenga, which quickly gave way to Trivial Pursuit, which quickly became very heated as everyone realized the aptly-named Brainy finally had a rival in the newest addition to the group. Kara announced to the whole apartment that Lena was on her team from now on, no arguments, and everyone agreed if only to make sure Lena was not on Brainy’s.

They were just wrapping that up, the group ready to continue on for a couple of more hours, when Lena got up to get a refill. She was opening a new bottle when she heard it, a bit of conversation between Kara and her sister, the redhead having taking the lull in activity to pull the blonde aside behind the room divider that blocked off the bed space.

“I realize this is about 2 hours later than I should have brought it up, but Kelly wouldn’t let me,” Alex said. “So let me just ask you what the hell is going on?”

“Huh?” The blonde replied.

“You have brought some interesting people to game night before, and I use the term loosely,” her sister went on. “But this really takes the cake.”

“Seriously, Alex, what are you talking about?”

“When were you going to tell us that the woman you met in the grocery store was Lena freakin’ Luthor?”

Lena froze. Somehow, in her comfort, she had forgotten, of course, the very obvious subject of conversation.

“Do not act like you didn’t know who she was.”

Silence. Lena, long since having given up any pretense of looking like she was pouring herself another glass of wine, stood with bated breath. Waiting.

“Of course, I did,” came Kara’s reply after what felt like an eternity.

“Then what the hell, Kara?”

“What?”

“What do you mean ‘what’? Why did you invite her to game night?”

“Because I thought it would be fun.”

Alex let out an exasperated moan that the dark-haired woman figured was decades-old with practice. “God, Kara. You didn’t even warn us.”

“Why did I need to warn you?”

“Because you invited a billionaire to game night, you nerd!”

“I invited a friend to game night, and you could at least be nice to her.”

And Lena really should have been pleased with the fact that the woman with the startling blue eyes was rising to her defense with, seemingly, no provocation, but all she could focus on was the word ‘friend’ and turn it over and over in her mind and wonder at the implications.

“You are unbelievable,” the redhead groaned back.

The blonde made no more reply, at least not that Lena could hear. The conversation had grown a bit louder than intended. The rest of the room, possibly out of habit whenever the siblings got going, had picked up their own conversation in volume to compensate. The dark-haired woman caught nothing more than a few brief whispers that sounded heated. Then Alex came stomping out, with Kara a step behind her. Lena desperately tried to look like she had been doing anything else, purposefully avoiding the blonde’s gaze until she was in the room, so as not to make herself look suspicious. When she did look over, Kara only gave her a smile. Lena wanted to believe it was genuine. She wanted to believe a lot of things, most of all that this might be the first of many game nights, the first of many visits to the blonde’s apartment, but doubt sat quietly in her brain, and it held a sign that said “Luthor”, which made any fantasies seem unlikely.

The night resumed. Monopoly was brought out, and, to Kara’s delight, it turned out Lena was, as promised, not a sore loser as she dominated. No one made the obvious joke, but Lena thought she could hear it nonetheless. Then, too soon, they were all done. Brainy bundled a sleepy, slightly inebriated Nia downstairs to get a cab. James let himself out. Alex and Kelly made a show of helping to clean up, before Kelly tugged her girlfriend to the door, to Alex’s obvious objection. Lena could guess why.

The dark-haired woman let herself linger long, unsure why, just that she didn’t quite want the night to be over yet. Probably because it was going to be all that she was going to get. Finally, good sense and courtesy meant she couldn’t stay a minute more.

“Thanks so much for coming,” Kara said as she followed her to the door. “Did you have fun?”

Lena sighed. There was a lot she wanted to say, ask, but she decided she could simply be honest. “I did. Thank you.”

“Great!” The blonde beamed. “Can’t wait for next time.”

And Lena wanted so badly to believe there would be. But she just sighed and stepped out of into the hallway.

“Hey, what are you doing tomorrow?”

Lena stopped and turned quickly to see the blonde leaning against the doorway to her apartment. She stammered. “Uh, I… didn’t have any plans.”

“Want to meet me for coffee?”

And Lena froze again. What was it about this woman that she seemed so willing, so eager to offer Lena everything she couldn’t possibly have wished for? What was it about those startling blue eyes that made the CEO believed she could deserve it?

“Sure,” she replied, her voice squeaking slightly.

“Awesome. Noonan’s at 2. I’ll see you there?”

And the dark-haired woman nodded. The blonde smiled back, waved, and closed the door.

Lena had hardly slept, getting home late, bone tired, in a good way, was about the only thing that got her down, but, even then, her brain was awash in thoughts. Not new thoughts. All the same ones that had plagued her since she had first met the overly friendly, blonde woman with the startling blue eyes.

It wasn’t meant to be, she decided. It was a depressing thought but a true one, a realistic one, anyway, and Lena Luthor was not the kind of person to shy away from reality. Certainly not the kind of person to indulge in a fantasy that couldn’t help but hurt them both.

There was just too much against it. Even if you didn’t count everything her last name brought with her, there was just the fact that she didn’t fit in. Here Kara had this, probably not perfect but downright lovely, little life, with her job and her friends and that homely apartment and, let’s be real, more than likely a long line of charming, and certainly willing, suitors, a good portion of whom probably didn’t come with the kinds of complicated baggage Lena did. All of whom would likely fit into the blonde’s simple life much better than she.

And that was really it. Simple. Not in a bad way, either, Lena could readily admit. Simple like game nights and plum cobblers and making friends in the grocery store. Coffee and cuddling on the couch. Simple in that warm and inviting way that so little of her life was. And as much as she could envision herself in it, she found she couldn’t commit. It seemed so foreign a concept to her, and her to it. Lena Luthor did not belong there. Her being there might just upset the whole, perfect picture.

Nevertheless, Lena Luthor was not the kind of person to miss an appointment, and a part of her knew the blonde deserved to hear it in person, so the next day, she showed up at Noonan’s, early to get a quiet table towards the back, and waited for her new, soon to be former, friend.

She had it all planned out: what she would say, how she would say it, even how she would calm herself and wait until she was in the car back to her apartment before bursting into tears. She had it clear in her mind. And, perhaps entirely expected, the bubbly blonde was making it very hard to go through with it.

First, Kara had practically skipped into Noonan’s after catching sight of the dark-haired woman sitting in a booth in the back. (It had seemed only right to get there early, to scope out a good spot, far enough away from the crowd, and, Lena could admit, she needed time to psyche herself up.) Despite what she was there to do, the Luthor found herself rising to receive a very warm hug from the blonde, letting herself linger just a moment, breathing in a faint scent of peach and kicking herself for wanting to hold onto it as long as she could. After they sat down and drinks were ordered, Lena was all ready to break the news. Only for her companion to instantly jump into a story about an article she was working on at her day job, which was becoming something of a nightmare. That then served as a perfect segue into how grateful she was for game night on Friday and how much fun everyone had, (which felt like such a knife to the heart, despite the blonde’s enthusiasm). Kara was well into a story about her first cat, Streaky, black, practically feral, but that just made a very young girl all the more determined to win him over, god, the woman could talk, when Lena wondered how exactly she was going to derail this conversation now, or if she even wanted to.

“Hey, is everything all right?” Kara asked, seemingly out of nowhere.

“What?” Lena replied with a shake of her head, so lost in the blonde’s retelling, and, let’s face it, startling blue eyes.

“You haven’t touched your drink.”

The comment brought Lena’s eyes down to the cappuccino currently growing cold between her hands. She sighed. Well, there really wasn’t any avoiding it, was there?
“I…” she stammered for a moment, then sighed again. “Kara, there’s something I need to tell you.”

She looked up into blue eyes, which only stared back in genuine curiosity, completely unaware of the bomb about to be dropped. God, Lena hated herself right now.

“I really appreciate… everything,” and that really seemed the best word for it. It was certainly the truth. “But, um, I don’t know if it’s a good idea for us to see each other anymore.”

And there it was, in all of its devastating realism. Lena took a deep breath and stared back at Kara.

“Why not?” The blonde asked, nonplussed.

Lena stared back, harder. “Because…” Funny how she’d had all this planned out. What the hell was she going to say again? She sighed again. “Because I’m me,” she said and hoped that did it.

It seemed to as the blonde’s brow suddenly furrowed with worry. “Oh, god,” she said. “What did Alex say?”

Lena shook her head in confusion. “What?”

“I told her not to say anything,” Kara went on. “She does this, you know. Ever since we were kids. I have a right mind to call Mom. You know what, better idea, I’m gonna call Kelly.” And she was pulling out her phone even now when Lena reached across the table.

“No, she didn’t say anything,” the brunette replied, quite out of her wits. She really had seen this going differently. “Kara, you know who I am. I know you know.”

“Yeah,” the blonde replied, again with more nonchalance than Lena might have expected. Way more.

Lena felt the need to point that out. “Why didn’t you say anything?”

Kara shrugged. “Did you want me to say something?”

Lena’s mouth went fishy. Damn, she had worked out her replies and everything but somehow did not expect any of this. “Well, I… No, I guess,” she finally admitted.

“Then what’s the problem?” Kara asked. “Are you seeing someone else?”

“No,” came the reflexive reply.

“I’m not your type?”

“No, quite the opposite,” she replied again.

“You don’t want to date, you just want to be friends?”

“No, Kara, I-“ And it was right about then that something occurred to Lena. “Wait a second.” She looked down at her coffee, as if the answer was there, as if it had been staring her in the face this whole time. She darted back up to the blonde. “Was this a date?”

“Yeah,” came the reply that, once more, seemed entirely less dramatic than the situation called for.

“You asked me out on a date?!” Lena repeated, at a volume that really didn’t care if people heard.

Kara nodded. “Game night then coffee date. It’s my standard approach.”

And Lena was agog, flabbergasted, and several other words for surprised. Here she had been quite certain what the problem was, and a good portion of it involved her not thinking the fantasies in her head had any possibility of being realities, and here the blonde seemed willing to grant her wish, quite without her asking. “You want to date me?” She had to ask.

“Of course!” Kara replied, as if it was the most obvious thing in the world.

“But…” And Lena knew there was a but. There had to be. She had come into this conversation with several, very well thought out arguments to that end, but they all seemed to melt before those blue eyes, until all she had left was, “But I’m me.”

And this, surprisingly of all, seemed to be the thing that finally struck the blonde. Kara’s face fell slightly. “Oh,” she said, and Lena again felt like kicking herself, but this is what she had come here to do. “I see.”

“I’m sorry,” the dark-haired woman sighed. “I really am. You are amazing and so many things that I could want, but I don’t know that I fit in your life.”

She watched those startling blue eyes, brimmed at the edge with tears, but there was nothing for it.

“My life is so much…” and she simply sighed. “And I could never, would never want to bring any of that into your life, which is beautiful, by the way. Really, it’s amazing. You and your friends and, well, you. You are amazing, Kara. The way you talk. The way you are. Forget dating, I would be blessed to have a friend like you, to come to game nights and coffee and all that beautiful normal. Even just to have had it for a little while was more than I could have asked for, and I will always be grateful for that, but I have to face facts: I’m Lena Luthor. And it’s a little silly to think that I could fit into your life.”

And there was the truth of it, laid out on the table between them. Lena let her eyes drift down as the silence settled. When she looked up, it was to startling blue eyes looking back at her with surprise and confusion.

“Oh,” Kara replied, somewhat less devastated than Lena might have expected. “Is that the problem? You think you’re too complicated for me?”

And, really, did the woman have to make it so hard? Lena nodded with another sigh. “Yes, Kara, I do.” And then something occurred to her again, written all over the blonde’s not-really-all-that-upset face. “Wait, what did you think it was?”

“I thought you thought I was too ordinary.”

And now it was, again, the dark-haired woman’s turn to be perplexed. “Too ordinary?”

“Yeah.”

“Kara, you’re not too ordinary. That’s not… I’m not even sure that’s a thing.” And something once more occurred to the woman. “Wait, aren’t I too complicated for you?”

And the blonde simply shook her head. “No, why would you be?”

And Lena could have sworn she had an answer for that ten minutes ago. “Because I’m me.”

“So?”
“Kara, I’m Lena Luthor. I run a billion dollar company. My relatives regularly end up dragged before the SEC. I’m… complicated.”

“So?” Was the blonde’s only rejoinder.

“You don’t…” Lena stammered. “You don’t think that’s a problem?”

The blonde shook her head, and Lena felt very much like asking- scratch that, absolutely had to ask, “Why the hell not?”

Kara shrugged again. “Because I like you. You’re funny and you’re really smart, and you’re a lot of fun to have at game nights. You’re also super hot. That’s not the only reason, but I think you should know that.” And the blonde could only stare at her and smile. “You’re nice to talk to.”

There had been arguments, there had been a whole speech, full of well-thought-out examples, facts, figures. It was, now that Lena thought about it, a very complex answer as to why this wasn’t going to work, but, whether it was fate, or happenstance, or just life itself, Lena found all the complications swept away by the sheer, blessed normalness of it all. Of blue eyes that she finally realized were staring at her, that she could finally let herself believe were startling because they were looking at her.

She could almost cry, but there was one more question. “Why did you think I would think you were too ordinary?”

And here the blonde finally looked away, almost ashamed. In answer, she motioned to herself, as if it was right there, as if she was some, boring thing, in her slacks and Oxford shirt with the collar poking out over her cardigan and those glasses that sat just slightly askew on her face, all of which made her just about the most adorable thing Lena thought she had ever seen.

“My life’s not all that interesting,” Kara added. “It’s fun, and I like it, but nothing glamorous. Game night is about the highlight of most weeks.” She shrugged sheepishly. “I just wondered: how could you want all of this?”

And that Lena found she had the courage to answer. “Kara, all of this is exactly what I want,” she said, spreading her arms to take in the two of them, the coffee shop, everything. “I want game nights and coffee dates and walks in the park and… and…”

“Grocery shopping?” Kara smiled.

“Yes!” Lena exclaimed. “Grocery shopping and holding hands in the aisles and arguing about what to buy for snack foods and going home and making dinner. Doing the dishes. Sitting on the couch, watching the sun go down, memorizing the way the light shines in your eyes. Waking up to you in the morning. God, Kara, I want all of that. It sounds like bliss. It sounds like all the things I always wondered if I really deserved. And maybe I don’t. Maybe I’m fooling myself. But, god, do I want to try! I want to try so much it-“ And she had to sop. She needed to stop. Talking no longer seemed to hold any purpose as the blonde was now in her space, having crossed to her side of the booth and slid in alongside Lena, Kara’s thigh pressed to her own like she had wished for the night before, the blue-eyed woman’s hand suddenly tracing Lena’s cheek.

“You want all that?” Kara asked.

And Lena nodded, as she reached a hand to hold the blonde’s in place, to feel the warmth of her all through her. “Yes,” she said. “And you’re okay with a woman who’s life is so complicated completely normal things feel like a vacation?”
Kara smiled, she beamed. “Yeah.”

And Lena found herself smiling, something blooming in her chest that was so new and foreign it felt almost painful until she realized it was relief, it was joy. For the first time, she felt like she could actually deserve it, but more than that she realized it didn’t really matter. Deserve as a word no longer seemed important.

“So, normal?” The blonde said curiously.

Lena nodded, though she raised an eyebrow.

“Just normal?” Kara repeated. “You don’t want anything extraordinary? Because I can do extraordinary.”

Lena felt herself laugh, and she had to admit the idea was intriguing. It was certainly giving her ideas. Nevertheless, she asked, terribly curious, “Like what?”

“Like kiss you until every doubt in your mind washes away,” the blonde replied.

“Oh, god, I wish you would,” Lena said, and Kara accommodated.

It wasn’t everything, but it was good. It was, as promised, extraordinary, but the dark-haired woman was starting to see it’s outstanding quality didn’t really have to do with anything particularly outstanding. It simply was and had to be just because both of them had decided it should.

And that really was it. That was and is everything. The beauty of ordinary things. Life doesn’t need a rubric or scale to tell us when we should be amazed, we just get to be amazed, mesmerized and astounded, by all the beauty around us. Like plums and game nights and kisses that take your breath away.

Because ordinary doesn’t have to be ordinary.

Sometimes ordinary is anything but.
