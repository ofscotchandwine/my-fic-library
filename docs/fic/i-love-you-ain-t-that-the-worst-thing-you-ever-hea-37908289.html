I love you (ain't that the worst thing you ever heard?)

There is something to be said about powerful men. The kind that spend more time in their study than at the dinner table. The kind that appear cold and unrelenting and who never need to raise their voice to get their point across because everyone already knows: Their word is absolute. Lionel Luthor was all of these things and more.

Like his father before him, he sat in a heavy oak chair that creaked far too much, paper weights dusting in the corner of his desk from inuse. The shuffle of papers could be heard down the hall during the day and if someone were to walk by late in the evening sometimes that sound was accompanied by the clinking of ice against delicate glass. This is how Lena always preferred her father, loose after a scotch or two, inhibitions lowered enough that the cloud in his mind could part to allow interactions that didn’t involve business. This is how she learned about coal mines, right there in that very office, her fathers big hand wrapped around her middle as she sat perched on his lap, his other hand directing her eyeline towards the blue glow of the desktop, the drink slowly dribbling down the glass with every movement as he excitedly pointed out different people on the screen. There was her grandfather and his father and on and on it went, generations of coal miners that slowly became supervisors and then worked their way up to owners until the LuthorCorp empire was built, all of it stacked on the foundation of efficient and clean fuel, long before anyone knew any better.

The day her father died, the earth stood still. The quiet of that hallway reverberated across continents. The board members went wild and ravaged, their ten year plan clutched in a tight fist with no one to commandeer it. 

This was the lineage that was bestowed upon her brother Lex, when he was young faced and stupid enough to still believe he could do it all. Lena expected the curse to take him on, how he would grow tired in a creaky chair with a glass of scotch and a mountain of paperwork on the thirty-first floor of the LuthorCorp building in the heart of Metropolis. She expected to see the transformation everytime she came home during the holidays, back ramrod straight in the dining chair because Luthors didn’t slouch, shoulders waning and bursting and wishing to be free of the stiff dress her mother plied her into. She expected to see it during her internships in the summer, her only reprieve from boarding school. She even expected to see it in the letters they would write to each other when she was across the globe at university, Lex in a snazzy suit sitting in on some board meeting, Lena in her dorm, a smattering of textbooks and notes for the upcoming final left unattended in favor of ripping open the eggshell colored envelope. It would start to show in his tone eventually, the letters would grow dull and short, surely.

Except… except that never happened. He remained his maniacal, obstinate self, ever the easy going sibling who waved off concerns like they were nothing but an insistent fly. The tabloids flourished during his reign, trashy gossip lining grocery store aisles and the local dentist office. “Loony Luthor does it again” hits the front page during his first year on more than one occasion. On the anniversary of his second, The Natural Science magazine prints “The downfall of Lionel Luthor’s Orchestra: His son” in big yellow font splashed against a dark vinyl lined cover. Lena is fresh out of MIT at this point, elbows deep in some mechanical robotic arm inside her oldest friend's basement. Jack slaps the magazine down on the table right by Lena’s work and announces she’s officially no longer allowed to call herself inept to the Luthor heir. She rolls her eyes and tells him limbs don't grow themselves and to get back to work. The phone rings against her ear the next room over, a greasy rag slung over her shoulder. She’ll allow herself this break, just this once. When her brother answers the phone, Lena asks him what the hell he’s doing. There is silence on the other end; he has no idea.

Day 1

The podium is encased in burning light, the glow of the lanterns following with every step. She steps up to the mic and clears her throat, a smattering of flashes from the first row assaulting her senses. There's a small murmer in the crowd that dies out the longer they wait. And then she speaks.

“LuthorCorp started eighty-three years ago in a small town in Michigan and even though our means of efficient and sustainable fuel have changed greatly, our means have not. This company was built on the backs of hard work, dedicated minds, and unrelenting force. At least that’s what my father would say if he were standing here tonight. It’s no secret that my brother was always meant to take the helm but I like to think I hired quite the clean up crew-” the audience laughs, Lena gives the camera a smirk of her own in response.

“-the improvements we’ve made this past year are going to leave a lasting impact for generations to come. I would like to take a moment to thank our associates, Spheerical Industries and Lord Technologies, we wouldn’t be here without them.”

She steps down from the podium and the crowd goes wild, reporters waving a notebook here, another motioning a recorder, individual questions drowning out anything that could be construed as an intelligible sentence. Distantly, Lena can hear one of the public address announcers breathe heavily into the mic that she won't be taking any questions at this time. Nevermind that, she's half way down the ramp and to the corridor that leads straight to her town car and Lena will be damned if she’s deprived another second from her eighties collection of Van Winkle.

She shuffles into the backseat, grateful that even during these times she still had the common sense to request the side street be blocked off. It’s blissfully silent for all of two seconds when the door she's just shut jerks itself open again, the head of LuthorCorp legal gesturing for her to slide over. Lena sighs and unbuckles, her mood suddenly turned sour again.

“Thirty-first and Wallace, please-”

Lena turns to look at him sharply. The audacity of this man. She turns back to the driver, her voice terse. “No, my loft. I still have things to wrap up.”

Stanley sighs like he's the one being inconvenienced. “Honestly Lena, after that stunt you just pulled in there, you can't just skip the conference, reporters are already lining up outside.”

Lena crosses one leg over the other and nods at her driver through the rearview mirror. The car makes a right at the corner and merges into traffic. There’s a horde of reporters standing outside the venue she’s just left hoping to get a peak of her. The notorious Luthor, here to mop up her brothers mess, and there they all are, standing at attention and ready to attack at the mere sight of her, the fucking vultures.

“That’s precisely why I won't be there.”

The car pulls up in front of her building. Lena unbuckles, reaches for the door handle.

Stanley makes to speak but Lena simply holds up a hand. “The board made a convincing argument trying to hire me on, I'll give you that much. But if you think even for one second that I am going to grovel to a group of festering and simpleminded journalists just to appease your head of PR then you’re more foolish than I thought. I am here to run a company, no more, no less. Do remember that.”

She steps out and shuts the door a little more forcefully than necessary. She could really do with that glass of whiskey right about now.

“I’m not,” she says.

They all praise Supergirl mindlessly, solely based on some good press, the same way they vilify her family mindlessly, based on some bad press. Based on word of mouth or lackluster rumors, not ever stopping to question for themselves if it’s been deserved. If it’s been earned. The elite technological society of hearsay, her brother always said.

“Don’t be ridiculous,” she says, admonishes really, because when you’re talking to Jack Spheer there is always some level of warning to her tone, try as she might.

Lena asks him why she bothers bringing him to these events as if his name isn’t in the invitations handed out to every available news outlet and local science committee, same as hers. Leaving him for dead in that basement of his was the best thing Lena ever did, she insists even now. He would never have worked up the courage to launch the company had they still been dreaming behind closed doors.

The minute Jack occupies himself with some gratuitous flirting, some correspondent in a red number, Lena takes the opportunity to step out. There’s a balcony that usually remains unoccupied near the stairwell if her memory serves her right.

The second fresh air hits her, she starts to relax. She leans against the railing overlooking the city, twinkling lights reflecting off the glass of tall buildings. Lena sets her drink down on the edge of the railing, allows herself a moment to slouch, the muscles in her neck popping when she turns it this way and that.

“Oh.” Someone says behind her, “Looks like we had the same idea.”

Lena immediately stiffens, is already thinking over a reason to excuse herself. The woman walks up and places her own drink next to Lena’s, leaving an appropriate amount of space between them. Lena braces herself for the inevitable, some rookie journalist who stumbled upon Lena Luthor and couldn't let the opportunity pass without hounding her over a question or two. She waits for a beat and is surprised when the woman simply turns her head, gives Lena an easy smile before turning back to the view before them. Lena takes a moment to look her over, from the casual way she's crossed her ankles - a couple well placed scuffs on the brown leather of the dress shoes showcasing how loved they are - to the way the material of her suit jacket stretches across her shoulders, elbows resting precariously against the railing. Lena wonders if this woman knows she's wearing that suit better than any man in this building. She’s indulging herself in these thoughts as she watches on in silence, the way blonde hair gets tucked behind an ear, the ice in her drink rattling when it gets raised.

“Scotch?” Lena doesn’t even know why she’s asked, only that she can’t remember the last time she’s shared comfortable silence with someone, much less a stranger, and that it’s beginning to unnerve her.

The woman hums, plasters on another smile. It’s so unlike the kind Lena is used to seeing, typically laced with some sort of undercurrent or implication. Not this one, instead there’s something almost sincere about it, like it’s given away freely and often. “Macallan. Single.”

“Good choice,” she says, not surprised in the slightest that someone wearing that suit would have adequate taste.

The woman takes another sip, turns her body more fully towards Lena with only one elbow resting against the railing now. She extends her unoccupied hand, friendly sparkle not once leaving her face. “Kara Danvers,”

Lena reaches for Kara’s hand, considers for a moment lying about her name before realizing how utterly ridiculous that would be.

“Lena,” she settles on.

For a second it seems fine, and then there’s a moment of recognition that passes over Kara’s face. Lena resists the urge to cringe and instead prepares herself for impact.

“You’re Lena Luthor,” Lena groans internally the second the words are out of Kara’s mouth, unhelpfully reminds herself she wouldn’t be in this position if she had left the balcony the moment the other woman entered.

“You stood up my interview.” Kara continues, the smile on her face not once faltering. It takes a moment for Lena to register the words and when she does, she realizes with only a hint of embarrassment that they’re still shaking hands.

Lena removes her hand and straightens herself out a bit hoping to gain some leverage, only for Kara to do the same and emerge a few inches taller than Lena herself now that she isn’t leaning against the railing.

“Excuse me?” she says, not kindly.

“Stan told me you weren’t taking sit downs at this time,” she says, as if somehow that’s explanation enough.

Stan..? Lena vaguely remembers turning down Stanley's request to humor one more reporter. Of course he’s set her up.

“And yet here you are,” Lena can’t help the clipped way it comes out. Kara puts up her hands in a show of surrender, as if she wasn’t the one who came out here primed and ready to pounce.

“I didn’t come looking for you, if that’s what you’re thinking.”

Lena wants to believe the sincere way Kara says it but she’s been burned one too many times to know how these things usually go. She shouldn’t have come here tonight. Dinner with her mother suddenly doesn’t sound so bad.

“How convenient that we both ended up on the same balcony, then.” She says, if only because the disarming smile Kara keeps on her face is starting to piss her off.

“I like to think of it as lucky,” Kara takes another sip of her drink, gives Lena a long look over the rim of the glass.

Lena can’t believe what she’s hearing. She scoffs and reaches for her clutch off the railing. “You’re full of it,”

“I’m guessing that’s a no to the interview?” She hears Kara call out when she is already halfway through the door, the exterior sliding glass doors and about a hundred other people in this building are the only thing separating them. If she hurries up Lena is sure there’s a crowd she can get lost in.

Day 2

Lena feels like she’s going stir crazy. Between the mountain of paperwork that only seems to keep stacking up and Jack’s ongoing commentary in the group chat, she’s about ready to snap.

The shipyard fire that Supergirl put out only served to cause damage to the warehouse right next door that was being used as storage for some of LuthorCorps retired patents along with shared space with Lord Technologies. Of course, the side of the building with the most significant damage was the west wing which just so happens to be Lena’s side of the building. Naturally, this has now become her problem.

That’s how Stanley finds Lena, hunched over her desk and fingers massaging her temples. He slides a folder over, sticks his hands into his pockets and hovers for a moment.

“...yes?” She asks, already annoyed that he made the trek from the fifth floor where his office is held to deliver a set of papers that any intern could have taken care of. He has her on a leash and is too stupid to realize Lena knows.

“I’ve arranged a meeting with the CFO, she’ll be here any minute now.”

Lena squints at Stanley, watches the way he stands there twiddling his thumbs through his belt loops. He has the gall to look innocent. She swipes the file off her desk and begins to flip through it. “Why do we need finance? I can authorize these expenses myself,”

Stanley tells her to look at page three so she does, her eyes going wide at the chart. One of her subsidiaries in Northern Asia has had two transmission towers go down due to an earthquake. Lena can feel a tension headache coming on the more she reads.

“A russian team called it in an hour ago,”

“This could potentially shut down the city,” she says, setting the folder aside.

“Which is why we need to get it fixed asap,” says Sam, walking into the room. She looks impeccable as always and her presence is enough to make Lena smile just a little bit. Working with friends has its perks.

They share a quick hug, a nod reserved for Stanley, and then Sam is handing Lena another folder. Oh great, more paperwork.

“Why hasn’t this been dealt with already?” She directs the question to Sam but it’s Stanley who answers.

“We don’t have the equipment,”

Sam quirks an eyebrow at the man, the same question on her mind that Lena’s had all morning. Why the hell is legal here? If they were in better company Lena would scream at the top of her lungs that she does not need a babysitter but it appears that even the most reasonable of Luthors can’t be trusted nowadays.

Lena looks over the documents Sam has handed her one more time, uncaps the nearest pen on her desk to sign off. “Well then, we’re gonna need one hell of a crane,”

She wordlessly hands it over to Stanley. He’s already here, might as well make him useful. He makes to leave but Lena stops him when he’s halfway out the door. A thought has occurred to her from last night seemingly out of nowhere.

“You go by Stan?”

Stanley stops in his tracks, hand still grasping the doorjamb, a small secretive smile forming on his face. Sam watches the interaction with amusement, perches herself on the edge of Lena’s desk.

“You haven’t replied to the group chat,” is all Sam says when Stanley is clear of the room.

Lena rolls her eyes and rounds her desk so she can finally sit down. “Why? So Jack can tell me how many days I’ve been single?”

Sam frowns, takes a seat opposite Lena’s desk and crosses one leg over the other. “He put a lot of work towards that, there’s a website and everything. Anyways, that’s not why I’m here. I need you for a double date tonight.”

“Evidently, that is why you’re here,” Lena gives Sam one of her sterner looks and hopes it’s enough to deter her but she doesn’t falter even a little bit. “I’m busy tonight, Sam.”

Her friend lets out a laugh as if the idea of Lena having plans is hilarious. Which, okay, Lena isn't necessarily well known for being socially rounded outside of work but still.   “We both know that’s not true. Besides, you like Alex and we think you and her sister will get along. She just moved back from Metropolis, she’s a reporter.”

Lena lets out a bark of laughter and she has to stop her typing momentarily. “The last thing i want after last night is to be around more reporters,”

“She’s blonde,”

“I hate blondes.”

“No you don’t! Look, it's two hours and all the drinks I’m willing to buy you, please?”

Lena sighs and resigns herself, sets her ipad to the side. “You’re not doing a very good job of selling this,”

“I’ll pick you up at eight?” She asks, already collecting her things.

Lena mulls it over. Truthfully, the only plans she had for tonight was a new book and that bottle of cabernet that’s been calling her name for a few days now. She’s only been around Alex a handful of times but they’ve always gotten along fine and drinks with her best friend to wind down from this exhausting day doesn’t sound half bad.

“What the hell,” she says, a half smile already forming on her face. “Fine,”

Sam lets out a noise that sounds suspiciously like a squeal, “thank you, thank you! This is gonna be great,”

Lena watches her retreating figure through the glass of her office. Her ipad pings and she looks down to see twelve more emails than she had just a few minutes ago.
