all you

 Lena stares blankly at the fire raging in front of her.

 “It's cold mother, at least give me some form of heat.”

 “You’re leaving tomorrow. I think you can survive one more day. Besides, there are plenty of blankets in the house.”

 The wine glass in her hand contrasts with the heat of the blaze. Her apartment is normally sleek and white, just another space to get work done. Cold, she thinks. But right now, it feels warm. Maybe it’s because it's six o’clock and she's on her third glass of wine, but still, the buzz in her body won't drown the fact that she's never felt more alone in her life.

 Her father is dead, her brother is incarcerated. Lord only knows what they did to her mother.

 She looks out the window. The snow has started coming down lightly. They're small snowflakes, probably won't stick, however, it’s still a welcome sight. She's always loved the snow.

 “Lena, I told you to stay inside! Now look what you've done, the carpet is soaked!”

 It doesn't remind her of happier times exactly, but they sure were better. She had a family. She had friends. She had Lex. She wants to laugh at the absurdity of it all. Aside from her brother, she’d have run far away from the Luthors if she could. Now she's sitting here wishing she could have just one more moment with them. She looks down. Her wine glass is empty again.

 She gets up from her seat by the fireplace and heads into the kitchen. She has a large collection in the wine cabinet, but at this point she's looking for something a little harder. She grabs the whiskey off the top shelf and is screwing off the cap when suddenly, there’s a knocking on her door. It startles her.

 She walks over to the door and tries not to think about how disheveled her apartment looks.

 “Lena, I told you to put these clothes away last week. What are they still doing out?”

 “I forgot, mother.”

 "You don't forget to be clean, Lena. I want to see that room spotless within the hour. Messy people are unproductive people.”

 As she reaches the door, the amount of wine she drank begins to hit her. Her footsteps feel heavy and her head is pounding, though she’s not sure whether the latter is from the wine or the chaos in her mind. Could be both.

 Not really caring to see who's behind the door, she skips the peephole and yanks it open. Her eyes widen in surprise when she sees her guest.

 “Kara,” She clears her throat, “What are you doing here?”

 Kara shifts uneasily and speaks quietly.

 “I-I brought you the latest copy of CatCo.” She holds it out to Lena. “I thought you might like to see it.”

 Lena stares at the magazine in Kara’s hand for a moment.

 “You don't need to be reading those trashy magazines, darling. Get a publication with some actual value instead of finding out which celebrity is checking into rehab again.”

 She hesitantly grabs it. She looks up at Kara who is giving her a concerned look. The girl hesitates before deciding on her words.

 “Are you okay?”

 It's a loaded question, both girls know this, but Lena just gives a somber smile and nods anyway.

 “Can-can I come in?”

 Lena is silent for a moment before making a decision. What does she have to lose?

 “Sure.”

 She turns away from the door and heads into the apartment, and Kara follows. Lena notices that her trail of alcohol is a lot bigger than she originally thought, and she can't help but wince when she hears Kara let out a soft, “Oh,” most likely at the cracked family portrait Lena had hanging on her wall. The frame is split and the entire thing is resting on the floor now, a pile of wood splinters and glass chips in its wake. She vaguely remembers throwing the television remote in that direction after turning off the news.

 Lena clutches the magazine and hastily walks over to the kitchen to start clearing empty glasses and bottles.

 “I-I’m sorry about the mess,” Lena sniffs, “I wasn't expecting any company. And I just-” She trails off and grips the kitchen sink. After taking a deep breath she continues.

 “I just lost track of time.”

 Lena picks back up, rinsing out glasses and bottles that had accumulated on her counter. Kara eyes her.  

 “I-I didn’t mean to interrupt,” She takes a step closer to Lena. “Do you want any help?”

 “Luthors don’t require help, Lena. They work hard until everyone else is begging for theirs. Have I not taught you that?”

 “No, that’s alright.”

 Lena is furiously scrubbing at a glass. It’s well past clean and she’s one hand squeeze away from having a shard-filled palm, but she doesn't really know what else to do. Concerned for her safety, Kara ignores Lena’s response and places her hands over Lena’s. The brunette sucks in a deep breath.

 “Why don’t you go sit down by the fire?” Kara says softly, “I’ll clean up.”

 Lena shakes her head and looks away.

 “It’s really okay, Kara.” Her voice is wavering. “I can do it myself.”

 Kara runs a gentle hand across Lena’s back and finishes with a light squeeze to the shoulder. Lena turns her head to face Kara’s. Kara gives her a small smile.

 “You don’t have to.”

 Lena’s eyes dart for a moment, wishing away tears she feels welling up, before they lock with Kara’s. She backs away from the sink in surrender, a silent agreement flowing between the two girls. She dries her shaking hands with what little composure she has left, and stalks back over to her spot on the couch. She picks up the, now crinkled, magazine on the way. She nearly plops onto the couch, the exhaustion of the day finally catching up with her and she takes a quick look back at Kara. The blonde has begun drying the dishes, and Lena doesn’t understand what she’s ever done to deserve a friend as good as Kara. With a sigh, she finally looks at the magazine.

 She gasps.

 On the cover is a picture of Supergirl and Lena side by side with a headline that reads, “Supergirl Saves the Day...With Lena Luthor!”

 She’s been on covers before. Probably too many to count. It’s not her first and it won’t be her last. It’s not even her first cover on CatCo. Regardless, she doesn’t think that any cover or article written about her could ever make her feel the way that this one does. All she’s ever wanted is to be recognized for good.

 Not for Lex and his crimes, or her family’s company. Not even for her own business ventures. She just wanted to do something impactful enough for the world to see that she’s not a bad person. And now she has.

 She can’t help the cry that escapes her body.

 She hears a clunk from the kitchen and a small, “Lena?” before Kara is rushing toward her. She sits down next to Lena, worry etching her face, and immediately begins consoling her.

 “Lena, what’s wrong?”

 “I didn't make it into the science journal.”

 “You should have revised your thesis like I told you to. Lex just made it into the Bloomberg. They did a piece on his start-up. I was hoping you’d have an accolade to match.”

 Lena holds the magazine toward Kara.

 “Did you-” her voice cracks, “Did you do this?”

 Kara’s expression floods with understanding and she smiles.

 “No, Lena. You did that. That was all you.”

 Lena takes one look at Kara and immediately engulfs her in a hug. She’s full on sobbing at this point, and Kara just accepts it graciously and holds her back as tight as she can.

 They spend the rest of the night like this, Lena in Kara's arms, until they eventually both fall asleep.

 When Lena wakes, her head feels like the inside of a grandfather clock, but she's not alone.

 And that's all she can really ask for.
