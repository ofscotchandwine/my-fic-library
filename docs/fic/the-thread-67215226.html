the thread

 

 

Since Kara landed on Earth, protecting her identity has been drilled onto her like a mantra. She appreciate it, really. Her adoptive family willingness to protect her even at the cost of their own safety. She was too young when they took Jeremiah to do something, anything.

But now they were taking Alex for not revealing her human identity… And that's a line she's not compelled to stand by.

Kara had somehow managed to convince Lucy to tell her when and how they would be transferring Alex. She had roped Winn into creating false ID's for Alex, she had planted a car on the route the bus transferring her sister would be taking. She was going to rescue her, help her hide for a while, until they could figure it out.

But somehow she had tipped her hand too soon or too strong. She followed the transport from a distance, until they got to the deserted highway where she'd parked the escape car. Maybe she got to eager, maybe they were counting on her to show up, because after a brief chase, her on a motorcycle, just to not be the flying alien. She felt like she crashed into an invisible wall, that propelled her backwards, the bike exploding and turning into a wild mass of fire.

She felt like her skins was peeling off her bones, she almost thought they had managed to hurt her with kryptonite, but the only glimpse of green she saw just before loosing consciousness, were the worried eyes of a woman.

Kara groaned as she woke up. She had rarely felt so much pain in all her years under the yellow sun. There were not very many things she was vulnerable to, and she managed to crash into one, while trying to rescue her sister.

She opened her eyes, she was in an unknown bed, under unknown fairy lights. Kara sat down, looking around her, sore and disoriented.

"You should be dead," a voice said.

Kara turned, enough to see a figure standing at the door, leaning on the frame.

"E—Excuse me?"

"The magic spell… the force of it, when you crashed into it…" the woman whispered. "You should be dead."

Kara looked at her, she was beautiful, and she seemed concerned, worried even.

"I'm stronger that I seem," Kara whispered instead. "Where am I?"

The woman wrung her hands together.

"My apartment… One of them, anyways."

Kara took a moment to get her bearings back. She'd failed to rescue her sister, she didn't know when she would be able to help her. Or even if she could. And yet, the more curious thing, is that she was calm. She was curious about the woman in front of her.

"My name is Kara," she said, slowly. "Thank you for helping me, but why didn't you take me to a hospital, if you thought I should be dead?" She asked, curiosity, getting the better of her.

The brunette lightly blushed, rubbing her left fourth finger, "I had a feeling you would rather not."

Kara observed her for a moment, it was probably a few seconds, but it felt as if time had stopped for Kara to admire her.

"Do you know the spell they used?" She asked instead.

The woman nodded, gulping, "It's very powerful… I created it, many years ago. It was… stolen from me."

It clicked in place, then. The woman was a witch, strong enough for original spell-casting, seemingly. And yet, there was something so… familiar about her.

"Do I know you?" Kara asked, even if she knew the answer, she had eidetic memory, that's not a face she would've forgotten.

"I'm Lena," the woman said, but didn't respond, she only kept rubbing her left fourth finger, somewhat anxiously. "Why were you following that bus?"

Kara didn't respond immediately, but she felt the pull to tell the truth. Maybe the woman was enchanting her to do so.

"Rescuing my sister," she said, simply. "She was arrested for protecting me, and now I don't where they are taking her."

Lena didn't say anything for a few seconds, she was seemingly observing Kara.

"You are an alien, yes?" She asked at the end. "You are Supergirl, aren't you?"

Kara froze up, pretending to be an average human was what had kept her safe for the years she's been on planet Earth. But there was something about Lena, something that push her to tell the truth.

"I am," Kara replied softly. "That's why they took my sister."

Lena nodded to herself, "that explain why you don't see it, then." She said, barely audible, still wringing her hands. Kara wasn't even sure if she was supposed to hear that. "Did Cadmus take her?"

Kara tensed up, even more. Who was that woman who could know so much without any prior contact between them?

Lena seemed to understand her apprehension, "they are the reason I'm here, actually. I think I know where their base of operations are. I need to do something to dismantle them."

Kara blinked in surprise, what were the odds? "Why?" She observed her, and noticed that one of her eyes was more blue than the other.

Lena sighed, a sardonic laugh escaping her lips. "I should say it's because what they are doing is wrong. And it is, don't get me wrong. But I am doing it because of a more selfish reason, I don't want to be judged by their actions, just because of the family I was adopted into."

Kara didn't understand, really. Maybe she couldn't connect the dots because her brain, her powers even, were still scrambled because of the magic. Lena seemingly took her silence for an opening to explain.

"I'm a witch, and so was my biological mother. The family who adopted me was… complicated. They were more of science, technology; and don't misunderstand me, I'm excellent at it. But as a kid I wanted to be accepted, and that gap was bridged when my brother started to be interested in my magic." Lena said.

Kara just kept looking at her, she was still nervously wringing her hands. Kara's heart was beating fast in her chest by the mere anticipation, she remained quiet.

"He challenged me, made me feel stronger, more powerful in my magic," Lena continued. "With time, even my adoptive mother started appreciating my magic, while also encouraging me to keep up with science and tech. It took me years to understand that they were using me, to create spells, potions... to further their agenda."

Kara gulped, nervous about Lena's words. She felt some sort of doom coming her way.

"My brother, he got it in his mind that aliens are the enemy," Lena resumed. "And that might be true in some cases, but not all. He got it in his head that Superman was his enemy."

A gasp escaped Kara, surprising her.

"I see you've put it together," Lena smiled, somewhat sadly, nodding. "My name's Lena Luthor. Lex is my brother, my adoptive mother is the leader of Cadmus. But here is the kicker, Kara," when Lena mentioned her name, in that accent that Kara couldn't quite place yet, her heart hammered in her chest, but in a different way. "Because you are not human, you can't see the red string connecting you to me."

Air remained trapped in Kara's lungs, she could not breathe. She knew about it, of course, Alex had hers. Kara was able to pretend to be human without the red string connecting her to someone, because while most humans had soulmates, not all of them did. She didn't thought she had one, because she was Kryptonian, and Kryptonians had the matrix, and Kara was the only survivor, except for her family. Her mate should've be long gone. But then… maybe her mate was actually in front of her. By mere chance… And she was the sister of her cousin's nemesis.

"You don't believe me." Lena affirmed.

Kara wanted to say something. She wasn't sure she believed it… But it was more that she didn't want to hope. She has wanted a perfect partner since she was a kid, she gave it for lost when her entire world exploded, light years away, over two decades now.

Her silence gave Lena an answer, even if it wasn't the correct one. But Lena nodded to herself, and started saying words, in a language Kara could not identify.

Slowly, a golden glowing thread started to reveal itself. Tied around Lena's left ring finger and flowing until it ended at Kara's left ring finger.

Kara tried to touch it, and while she couldn't feel a thread in her finger, she could feel its warmth.

"I do believe you, I did before you showed me." Kara confessed. "I just… I thought my person, had died with my planet, I've expend half my life having to come to terms with that. But… It's a welcomed surprise, Lena. I promise."

Lena stopped whispering in that secret language, and the thread connecting them disappeared from Kara's view. But now that she knew it was there, she could feel it, pulling her towards Lena.

"And I do believe you want to stop your mother and your brother too," Kara continued. "I know you are going to testify against him, for the red sun incident."

Lena nodded.

"I'm sorry you got saddled with me as a soulmate, though," Kara smiled.

Lena laughed, and Kara decided she wanted to hear that sound everyday. "Are you kidding? Someone good, who gives hope speeches for a living and is a superhero? I think whatever force decides these things, knows I'm a very glass half empty kind of person, and needed someone who could make me see another perspective."

Kara kept her smile, "I can do that, yes. I'm also a reporter, though. Hope that doesn't bother you."

"My relationship with the press is a bit contentious at the moment, but I suppose arrangements could be made."

It was so easy, the banter, the connection. Kara had never felt like that with anyone. Not Kenny, not James, not Lucy… She couldn't wait for the moment cynical Alex felt something like this. Kara bit her lip, feeling the worry about Alex again.

"Do you really think you know where they took my sister?" She asked.

Lena nodded, "If we play of cards rights, we can get your sister, and everyone they have detained too, legally."

"How?" Kara frowned.

"We have to expose them," Lena said, as if it was very simple. "You have to be a bit patient though. It won't happen today or tomorrow."

Kara worried about Alex, maybe she could rescue her first and then do whatever Lena was planning.

"I know what you are thinking," Lena said, surprising Kara. "But rescuing her too early would tip our plans, and we know they have at least one Homo-Magi, which you are vulnerable against."

"How do you know what I'm thinking?"

Lena walked closer to Kara, "I think you are just very bad at lying," she said. "I could see your worry about your sister in your face, you risked rescuing her once, not difficult to deduce you would want to try again."

Kara stood up, slowly, still sore. "I worry about her."

Lena shook her head, "I understand. But we still have a few days for them to try and interrogate her. They will leave her alone first, isolate her, psychological manipulation. That's my mother's way of breaking people."

Kara felt there was so much story in that sentence, but she could wait for Lena to open up on her own time.

"Okay then, what do you need me to do?" She asked, instead.

"I think you being a reporter will come in handy, actually." Lena smiled. "We need more information."

"I can do that," Kara nodded. Took a single step towards Lena and extended her hand. "Partners then?"

Lena smiled, taking her hand. And maybe Kara fell in love a bit.

"Partners."
