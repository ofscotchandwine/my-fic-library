The peach.

It starts with a peach.

What does one give to a witch? Kara doesn’t know. And in any case, she has nothing else of value to give. Anything she has is not her own, and anything else she can claim as her possession, she cannot part with.

And yet she knows that one cannot approach a witch’s door empty-handed.

It’s remarkably easy, where everything in her life thus far has been so hard. Clark and Lois retire early enough, her cousin being exhausted from his labor in the fields, and his wife exhausted from the labor of the home and carrying his child in her belly. Kara can feel the outline of the folded letter in her pocket, reminding her of her purpose. She spares one last glance at the empty bed next to hers, and steels her resolve. She blows out the candle and slips out quietly into the night.

The branches of the old peach tree creak as Kara climbs nimbly upward, one branch at a time. It’s familiar and easy, her muscles remembering how she and her sister used to climb trees all day as children until Eliza called them in for supper. It’s so easy, she doesn’t even tear her dress.

The wind whispers by softly, rustling leaves and dislodging errant wisps of blond hair from her white cap. Kara ignores it as she selects the ripest fruit from a cluster hanging from the highest branch she can reach. The one she chooses is firm enough for her to hold in her hand without turning into mush, yet soft enough that it yields and bruises with the slightest pressure of her fingertips.

Kara brings the peach up to her nose. Its scent is sweet and delectable enough that it almost tempts her to take a bite. The ripe fragrance of it is such that Kara can almost taste it, sweet and tart on her tongue.

She pulls it away before temptation can get the better of her and slips it carefully into the pocket of her dress. Slowly, she makes her way down the tree and lands in a crouch on the dirt.

Once safely on the ground, Kara looks around. The little village is silent and deserted, the whole world seemingly deep in sleep. Her crime has gone unnoticed.

Reassured and determined, Kara sets her sights on her new destination.

Every so often, Kara picks at the thread she had loosened at the hem of her sleeve. The night is full of ominous sounds. Leaves crunch beneath her feet like delicate bones. The trees themselves seem to creak and groan, their branches reaching out toward her like snatching claws. In the distance, an owl hoots a warning of danger. It brings back childhood nightmares of being lost in the forest, of being alone in the dark, of trees coming to life to devour her.

Forgive me, I can’t go back. I love you, be well.

There must be. Alex wouldn’t just leave her like this.

“I am… not….” Kara swallows and finds herself struggling to lift her gaze away to meet the witch's eye. “... feeling virtuous at the moment.”

The silence that greets her statement takes a while to dawn on Kara, and it’s only when she manages to look up at the witch’s face, with her raised eyebrow and surprised smile, that she realizes what she’s said. Kara’s eyes widen and she coughs and sputters.

“I- I didn’t — I meant—” Kara flails her hands helplessly, her face burning with embarrassment. “I’m angry! I went to see my sister today.”

The amused smirk remains on the witch’s face and her green eyes shimmer with a knowing look, as if she knows what Kara had been thinking. But she turns away, giving Kara reprieve. “I take it your reunion did not go well.”

Kara huffs, her shoulders sagging with the weight of the memory. “She would not come back with me, and she would not say why. She said I wouldn’t understand.”

The witch seats herself on her bed unhurriedly, her fingers resuming the work that Kara must have interrupted. She reaches into a small glass jar, and her hands come away slick and smelling of fragrant oil. As she smooths it into the rich dark fall of her hair, the witch turns back to Kara, head tipped and one eyebrow raised.

“Perhaps she is right. Perhaps you would not understand.”

The witch’s hands still, and her eyes meet Kara’s with a clear, unreadable gaze. Kara feels her pulse rise under such enigmatic scrutiny.

“You wish for me to bring your sister home.”

It’s not a question, Kara knows. But she nods anyway and holds out the parcel in her arms toward the witch. “This is the most expensive piece of finery I have. Is it payment enough?”

The witch’s fingers trail over the lace on the bodice. “The woman who made this for you, she loved you well.” A faint smile ghosts over the witch’s lips. “I can feel her love for you in every stitch.”

A lump forms in Kara’s throat as she looks down at the dress. “Eliza. She was my foster mother. She raised me from the time I was a young girl.”

The witch nods as her hands explore the blue fabric reverently. “She wanted you to be happy. Every stitch of the lace is a wish for your joy.” 

Green eyes flick back up to Kara. “And you’re certain you wish to offer this as payment?”

Kara tightens her jaw and nods back. “Eliza would want Alex to come back home.”

The witch sets the dress aside carefully before turning back to look Kara straight in the eye. “Understand that any spell I cast, no matter how powerful, will not change your sister’s mind if she has already made her decision.”

Kara swallows, but she nods in agreement again. The witch exhales and closes her eyes, murmuring words under her breath that Kara can’t understand. The air around them tingles again, just like before, charged, like the air before a lightning strike.

The glowing hand drops, and the tiny stars forming her sister’s outline shudder and disperse, dying a spectacular death in the middle of the circle. The golden line around the witch vanishes, and her eyes return to their green hue. The room darkens with the dying of the light.

Finally free of the spell that had kept her rooted across the room, she surges forward to the spot where Alex’s glowing body had been. “No! What happened? What does that mean?!”

The witch stands before her with gentle, almost pitying eyes. “It means that wherever Alex is, that is her home now. I cannot bring her home because she is home.”

“No!” Kara cries, reaching out to the spot where Alex had stood, as if she could still touch her sister this way. Her hands find nothing but empty air and a sob claws its way up her chest to her throat. 

“Bring her back, please! Please! ”

The witch shakes her head. “She will not come, and I will not force her.”

“But- but... “ Kara chokes on her desperation. “You are an enchantress! Surely you, of all people—”

The witch’s eyes flash, and for a split second, Kara sees the shimmering gold overtake the green. Kara blinks, and the gold is gone, replaced by the glittering green of the witch’s hard gaze.

The witch’s firm words silence her and cool her ire. Now Kara is perplexed. Surely these are not the words of a demon?

Kara exhales slowly, feeling the fight leave her body. She just feels… tired and drawn. And heart-broken. 

She no longer has her sister. 

Tears fill Kara’s vision and spill unhindered down her cheeks. She looks back up at the witch with defeated eyes. “Forgive me, I-I shouldn’t have— I’m sorry. I just… Alex...” another sob hiccups out of her.

The other woman’s eyes soften, and she nods. With a flick of her hand, one of the table chairs appears behind Kara. It still jolts her a little bit, these tiny mundane acts of magic, but she supposes they’re not any more extraordinary than seeing her sister nearly appear in a cottage in the middle of the woods. 

Nearly.

Kara drops down heavily onto the chair, her head in her hands. She feels brittle and dry, as if everything has been wrung clean from her, and all that is left is sorrow clinging to her parched frame.

Vaguely, she hears the sounds of the other woman moving around, but she cannot find the strength to lift her head. If this witch truly is the devil, then she can take Kara, she has no fight left.

After a while, Kara smells the pleasant aroma of herbs, and feels something soft brush her forearm. The witch touches her arm gently, as if Kara is a delicate thing that might break if she approaches her with anything but the lightest of touches. She takes another sip, and finds the same experience, warmth building slowly inside her. The tea is pleasant and soothes Kara from within.

She looks up at the witch with a small, grateful smile. “Thank you.”

The witch smiles back with a gentle nod. It’s the softest smile Kara has seen on her yet, and strangely enough, it gives her comfort. “Rest, Kara.”

Kara pauses over her cup and looks up as the witch draws nearer. The way the witch says her name is gentle and hushed, like a secret. Kara bites her lip, unsure of what to make of the tingling across her skin at the sound of her name from that mouth.

The witch leans closer, and Kara lowers her cup, not caring if it would spill. The witch lifts a hand towards her. It doesn’t feel like a threatening gesture, and so Kara does not move to stop her. A lone finger traces her cheek, its touch delicate and oh so light. Kara can feel it following the tracks her tears had made on her face. 

Kara’s eyes can’t help but close at the contact.

“Sleep, Kara. Tomorrow will be a new day.”

When Kara opens her eyes again, she finds herself in her tiny bed. Sunlight streams through the open window. 

It’s morning, and her limbs feel lighter. There is warmth inside her left over from the tea from last night.

When she looks in the mirror, she sees nothing on her face, but Kara would swear, she can feel her skin still tingling where the witch had touched it.
