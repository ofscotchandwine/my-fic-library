    Jack nods. “You got it.” He reaches for her bag—which leaves both of his hands full, the left with her suitcase and the right with Lena’s—and then sighs deeply, a shining smile spreading on his face. “Anyway, let’s get going.” He starts to turn, looking back at them to add: “You two ready for Australia?”

    Kara’s mouth opens to answer. She’s incredibly excited to spend a weekend in virtually the beach alone. The little splinter of her rift with Lena that’s still poking at her chest, however, impedes her from getting out her usual chipper answer.

    But they’re two adults, she reminds herself. Two adults who are mature and capable of understanding when their mutual disdain for each other has to bubble on the down low, for the benefit of everyone else around them (even if Lena doesn’t seem to show it very well). So… maybe leaving their history in the States for this party will be the best course of action. After all, if they’re going to spend this weekend together, they might as well begin to at least try and be civil to each other for good, right?

    She nods lightly to answer to Jack, and then she turns to Lena and shoots her a quick, tight-lipped smile. Lena answers by rolling her eyes, just before she starts walking to catch up with him.

    A crinkle appears between Kara’s brows, heat simmering low in her chest.

    She’ll have to take a lot of deep breaths this weekend, that’s for sure.

    ***

    Kara’s grateful that Jack’s sleek Mercedes is a convertible—feeling the tight pressure of high-speed wind on her face was certainly what she needed to start her flight recovery.

    As he drives them down the Harbour Bridge—him in the front, extra bags sitting passenger, Lena and Kara together in the back—Kara lets her mind wander as she watches the urban blur of glittering blacks, grays, and blues through the amber filter of her aviators.

    She can’t wait to see Eliza and Jeremiah’s house after all these years. It’s so full of warm memories, she’s sure it’ll cheer her up as soon as she steps a foot in their lawn. Plus, the two of them, Alex, Sam, and Ruby are already there, which means there’ll probably be some delicious dish on the table with her name written on it in bold when they arrive. Before she can start to daydream the rumbling-stomach-fueled iterations of what it might be, though, Jack grabs her attention.

    “A pointer,” he says loudly over the Dominic Fike song playing low on the stereo and the wind that surrounds them—a thought, casually popping into his mind. “Alex has been readying your rooms like crazy, so you’d better love the personal accommodations she’s made when we get there. I have a feeling she’ll snap if you don’t comment on her efforts.”

    Kara’s mouth opens to retort with a fond acknowledgement of Alex’s hysteria, but the implications of Jack’s notice hit her like an overachieving child does a whack-a-mole. He just said Alex had been readying their rooms. Rooms.

    “Wait, we’re all staying together?”

    Jack nods, exhaling amusedly, like he’d been waiting for the question. “Your parents’ idea. They said it was important to keep the wedding party all in one place.”

    Kara rolls her eyes, leaning her elbow on the window ledge as she looks out at the glistening cityscape again, knuckles against her temple. She knows and appreciates that Eliza and Jeremiah are all about interpersonal connection and strengthening bonds, but come on, Kara’ll have to deal with Lena twenty-four-seven, now, and not simply gather enough bearings over the weekend to deal with her at the party?

    God, why did Alex not tell her anything about this?

    “Of course they did,” she laments under her breath. Below them, across the sea, she can see the crisp outlines of the Sydney Opera House.

    Lena perks up beside her, leaning a little to aim a frown at the rearview mirror. “That seems like a piece of information that could’ve been shared a little earlier.”

    Jack just scoffs, smooth and urbane. “They think you two hate each other. They didn’t want to fuel the fire.”

    At that, Lena and Kara both get the urge to look at each other. It only lasts a second, but when her gaze falls into those clear, green eyes, framed by a deeply annoyed frown, Kara can’t help the blazing spark that shoots from deep in her chest all the way to her belly, fluttery and electric and utterly awful. Huffing to snuff it out before it can grow into something inextinguishable and catastrophic, she rolls her eyes and looks out at the view again, jaw set tight.

    If all else fails, she figures, brow furrowing at the steaming sizzle still dancing in her chest, she’s pretty sure she could get away with convincing Alex to tell Sam to tell Lena to get an Airbnb. Or whatever.

    ***

    The fresh smell of oceanwater. A malleable pillow of smooth sand under her feet. Sunlight warming her severely under-tanned winter skin and a surfboard, secure under her arm.  

    Finally, Kara thinks as she takes in a big inhale of salt air. Finally, things are turning around.

    She’d been right—when they arrived, after the mandatory hugs and kisses of warm welcome from the family and a tackle hug with a heart-melting Aunt Kara! to boot from Ruby (who is running now, how crazy is that?), she got to dig into a brimming plate of fruit salad and a salmon and cream cheese bagel, along with the power mocha Jack had promised. They chilled for a beat, allowed Kara and Lena to regain her bearings, before Alex suggested they all go inaugurate the weekend by going out to freshen up.

    So now, changed into swimsuits, hats, and sunglasses, they’re headed to the section of beach that’s attached to Eliza and Jeremiah’s property, Kara and Alex ready to fight over the best waves like they did when they were kids and Sam and Lena happy to lie under an umbrella to watch and read some scientific article, respectively.

    Sam and Alex walk a couple steps in front of Kara and Lena—who is smothered in sunscreen and sporting a flowy cover-up and the biggest floppy hat Kara has ever seen—holding hands and commenting on how gorgeous the view is: rocky mountains in the horizon, bring green flora teeming with life around them, and deep cerulean water that matches perfectly with the clear sky above.

    Kara’s fixing the long sleeve of her surf shirt when Sam catches her attention by turning around, walking backwards to look at her and Lena as she asks: “So… have you guys made up yet?”

    Kara takes in a breath, then stops with her mouth open for a second before deciding to settle with a perfectly neutral, totally not wedding-ruining statement. “Uh, well, we—"

    Before she can finish telling them she and Lena will just focus on making their wedding the star of this weekend, Lena makes the wholly sound call to cut her off. Of course. “We’ll be fine,” she says, reaching up to fix her Prada sunglasses on her nose, because clearly she never learned to wait for her turn as a child. “As long as she stays away from me.”

    Kara’s brow furrows for a second. Is Lena seriously not familiar with the concept of expected civility? She’s on the board of a multinational. “Well, I don’t need an invitation for that,” she does counter eventually, though, soft but laced with budding frustration, “that’s for sure.”

    “I think you do,” Lena counters, voice brimming with animosity but gaze still fixed on the sandy path ahead. “For some odd reason, you can’t ever seem to leave me alone.”

    “You came to me during—"

    “Okay!” Alex cuts her off before this can escalate into a full-on diatribe, gesturing widely with her free arm at her side. “Clearly, you guys have buried the hatchet and will behave like the two full-grown women that you are, and we fucking appreciate it. Thank you, let’s move on.”

    Sam snorts quietly, and then, after a second, she perks up like a lightbulb has just lit up inside her mind. “One last thing, though: we did have a tiny, little last-minute snafu,” she tells Kara and Lena, turning around to face them again once they stop at the start of the beach. “You guys are gonna have to share a room. Yeah, there’s only one bed, but we hung a shower curtain in the middle, so you—"

    “She’s kidding! She’s kidding,” Alex cuts in, placing her hands on Sam’s shoulders to fix her with a pointed look—a look that Kara recognizes, that says, we’ll talk about this later (but that is also extremely amused by this diabolical prodding).

    Kara just shakes her head with a sigh, a little mirthless smile of defeat pulling at her lips as she adjusts the lift of her board in her arm. “Let’s just go ride some waves.”

    The ocean gives Kara a rush of endorphins so big, she can’t even remember why she’d been moping earlier anyway—the waves are tall, fast, and smooth, and she and Alex have so much fun racing each other on top of their white, foamy crests that is isn’t until when Sam waves at Alex to tell her they need to get back to the house soon when she realizes they’ve been at it for almost two hours.

    She and Alex end up paddling over to a rocky cliff that provides partial shade from the sun, just to unwind for a few minutes before they head back to the coast. Riding the high of her post-surf bliss, Kara stops to catch her breath for a second as she pulls up to straddle her board—she’s grateful she feels good-tired, because cardio will never not be the death of her, but at least this kind is always enjoyable. She faces her sister, who has one leg bent on her board and the open sea framing her silhouette from behind. After a moment of silence, which Kara takes to savor the soft roar of the waves crashing and the feeling of the sun against her wet hair, a thought she’d been meaning to voice pops back into her mind.

    “How have Eliza and Jeremiah been?”

    A fond smile tugs at Alex’s lips. “They’ve been good. Mom has been loving having a toddler around again, and dad has obviously not been pointing out every single new mineral he’s found on the floor.”

    Kara chuckles playfully. “A very big surprise.”

    Alex nods, playing along with the joke, but then she licks her lips, a crease appearing between her brows. Kara knows that look—she’s weighing the pros and cons of whatever she wants to say. “They’re also, uh…” she starts eventually. “Well, they’re worried about how you’re gonna take all of this.”

    “What do you mean?”

    Alex shrugs, clearly trying to be more casual than she actually feels. “Everyone always thought you’d be the one to get married first.” She looks down at her leg, smiling softly as she picks at the ankle strap of her leash. “Remember Halloween in eighth grade?”

    Kara snorts with fondness, stretching her leg so she can hit Alex’s shin with her toes. “I mean, mummies are, like, insanely creepy. A veil was a much niftier use for that kit, and you know it.” But then, she bites her lip, the heaviness of her decision rushing back up to her heart as she fiddles with her shark tooth necklace. “But… I don’t think that’s really me anymore. I mean, I still want to have that big storybook wedding, but I’m just… going with the flow, now, you know? Free and deprogrammed, or whatever those people who don’t believe in soulmates say.”

    Alex grunts at that, almost wincing. “You sure?”

    “Yeah!” Kara assures earnestly. “You’re my sister, I’m ecstatic for you; it doesn’t matter what bullshit I’m dealing with.” She kicks the water to get closer to Alex, reaches a hand out, and lets it drop on her knee—she even adds squeeze, for good measure. “I’ll get to have my moment someday. This weekend is yours.”

    Alex squeezes her hand back. “Okay.” A beat. “If you wanna talk, though… about anything, I’m here. Always.”

    Kara bites down a smile. She appreciates the concern, but Alex will definitely not be available this weekend, that’s for sure. “I know,” she says instead, though, not wanting to fuel the fire any further. Then, a surge of movement to her right catches her attention.

    A great wave is heading towards the shore and, if she books it now, she’ll be able to catch it and ride it on her way back. She doesn’t even stop to hesitate—she’ll use the oldest, most trusted tactic in her book to steal it away.

    With a theatrical gasp, she points at the rocky wall behind Alex and says: “Oh my god, look, a whale!”

    Of course, Alex isn’t phased in the slightest. The last thing Kara sees before she drops to her stomach to paddle towards the wave is her rolling her eyes, utterly unimpressed.

    “You’re such an ass!” she yells fondly, nevertheless, and Kara lets out a bright laugh, feeling the water break beneath her hands.

    ***

    They’ve been back at the house for all of about twenty minutes, a bowl of crunchy green grapes by Kara’s hand as she looks at Twitter on her phone, when the front door creaks open.

    She looks up to see who’s just walked in. It’s a man she’s never seen before—around their age, tall, dark skin, kind eyes. He’s carrying a heavy-looking duffel bag on his shoulder, and a large, lidded glass Tupperware full of what, from her faraway spot on the breakfast nook’s little couch, looks like some sort of pasta salad. Most importantly, though, when he sees Lena—who is sitting on the kitchen counter, looking at her computer—his entire body lights up like the entrance of the Tunnel of Love at a carnival.

    Kara’s eyes widen, in tandem with the knit her brows acquire. What the hell?

    “Lena!” he says with a kind of warmth that makes Kara’s stomach twist into an uncomfortable knot, for some inexplicable reason, as he walks over to the counter to wrap her in a tight one-armed hug. “What’s up? How are you? I thought you wouldn’t be here yet.”

    And then, as if this completely off-the-wall display of casual affection with the woman who certainly wouldn’t hesitate before sticking out a foot to trip anyone who walked by her path wasn’t enough to raise Kara’s alarm bells, Lena ducks her head, bites down a smile, and, in a tone that almost feels flirty, answers: “Well, I’m sorry to disappoint.”

    As the crinkle between her brows deepens, Kara stretches out her leg and pokes Alex with her toes beneath the table to catch her attention. When Alex looks up with an annoyed frown (yeah, Kara might’ve gone a little overboard with the strength, there), Kara stage-whispers: “Who’s that?”

    Alex follows the direction of Kara’s referential side-eye, then looks back with understanding. “James Olsen. Sam met him when she was doing social service.” Lena laughs—laughs—at whatever James just told her, and Kara’s lips part with utter appalment—it’s sweet, it’s charming, and it reminds her of—

    No. Oh, no, she’s not doing this right now. She reaches out to grab another grape.

    Alex snorts softly at the reaction, understanding. Then, she continues: “He and Lena had a thing a few years back, not that serious. He met someone else, really broke her heart for a while.”

    Kara rolls her eyes, chewing with dismay. As if Lena even had one. “Right.”

    At the counter, Lena is leaning forward and resting her chin on her long fingers—a move that, even with Kara’s limited back view of her, makes something hot start flutter from her chest to her belly. “How’s life treating you?” she asks James, smooth.

    James doesn’t seem to be affected by the action or by the lush tone of her voice. When she manages to pull herself out of her horribly uncalled-for reaction with a sharp breath, Kara frowns at that.

    “Come on, we’re at a wedding. Loosen up,” he jokes instead as he leaves the Tupperware by the sink. “It’s been fine. I’m glad to see you.”

    Kara swallows, hoping that the heat she feels on her cheeks isn’t visible to Alex when she turns back to add: “Guess they’ve kept in touch since, huh?”

    “Yeah. Ever since he broke up with his ex, they met up again a couple times,” Alex informs her, voice slow and deliberate—she knows, and she’s reveling it. Traitor.

    But it doesn’t matter. Though apparently her body may not agree, Kara really has no interest in getting on Lena’s good graces ever again. She made her stance on her opinion of their date clear that next morning, and Kara has no intentions to try and change it. So, who cares if James is jacked and handsome and not a disaster, by the looks of it? He’s the kind of dude that Lena would go for, clearly—and a mile away from what Kara is. Good for her. She should grab the chance while it’s hot.

    Kara reaches for another grape. “Uh-huh.”

    “What, you’re jealous?”

    Kara almost chokes on her bite. Between badly held-back coughs, she counters: “You’re so— weird, I— She can do whatever she wants, I don’t care.”

    Whether or not she means it is irrelevant—Alex lets out a squawk that rings through the kitchen. She covers her mouth instantly, eyes wide and almost shocked by her own volume, but the damage has been done. Lena looks back to see what the commotion is about, brows knit together with slight confusion in the sharp way only her looks can be. She stops for a minute, takes in Kara’s stiff form—which Kara only catches a glimpse of before her eyes dart to a random groove in the wooden table—and thankfully doesn’t comment on the bright flush that’s surely spreading all over her cheeks right now. When she finds nothing else, she turns back to James, and Kara kicks Alex in the shin (with intention, this time). Alex only shakes her head, scoffing before she fondly mutters fuckin’ mess as Kara runs a rough hand through her damp hair, slumping back into the cushions with a pout.

    “I know,” Lena answers to whatever James had continued telling her, voice still slow from her distraction. “And I’m glad to see you, too.”

    James smiles softly—suggestively—at that, and Kara crosses her arms and looks back at her phone. Usually, the picture of a bright-white kitten that pops up as soon the screen comes to life again would suffice to distract her from the inopportune thought she can feel sizzling its way out her entrails. She’s not quick enough to stop it from climbing out of the darkness this time, though. Could that have been them, if she hadn’t walked out that morning? Could she have been the one making Lena laugh, standing on the receiving end of her dimpled, bright smile?

    “Well, I gotta run back out,” James says, thankfully cutting the useless hypotheticals in the bud. Kara doesn’t care—why on Earth does she feel this way? “The appetizers won’t pick themselves up.”

    He takes the duffel bag off his shoulder and leaves it under the counter, and then turns to walk out the left archway that leads to the garage door. Just when he’s about to cross it, he turns back and finishes the conversation with a suave: “That looks good on you, by the way. Red’s nice.”

    He’s right, is the worst part—Lena’s bikini compliments her skin perfectly. She bites her lip, rolls her eyes, and shakes her head as he walks out, and the way she does it, so completely devoid of annoyance and instead brimming with a kind of long-standing affection only fuels the scorch that’s gathered deep in Kara’s chest, makes it travel down to her belly.

    So Lena has an ex that’s totally open to rekindling the fire during this wedding weekend. Cool! Good—

    Seriously, good for her. Kara stuffs a grape into her mouth and looks at her phone again.

    ***

    Kara has just booted up her laptop when an email notification pops up in the lower corner of her screen, accompanied with an impersonally modern chime to boot.

    METROPOLIS UNIVERSITY SCHOOL OF LAW – NOTICE OF WITHDRAWAL

    Slumping forward, Kara buries her face in her white comforter and lets out a long, suffered groan.

    It’s certainly ironic to receive this email now, she thinks. Here, in her time -capsule summer bedroom that still houses plushies on the dresser, old sketchbooks piled on top of shelves, and many a One Direction posters plastered on its walls. Back when she still came here yearly, freshly eighteen and feeling lost and scared about the vastness of her future, she never would’ve guessed she’d come back and feel the same way, a whole seven years later.

    But she can’t help it. There’s no going back now—she’s a dropout, jobless, and all she has in her head as an idea for her next step are visions of sugar plums and paintings. Seriously, what is she doing with her life?

    Well. C’est la vie, right?

    She’ll just have to store her fear away until she’s back in Metropolis. She’s supposed to have fun this weekend, she reminds herself for what feels like the thousandth time. She can do it! Just breathe in, breathe out; take in confidence, let the uncertainty go…

    Luckily, she can’t wallow in her dread for long. A knock on her door forces her to straighten up again.

    She runs her hands through her hair, tries to look less like a mess. “Come in.”

    The door opens to reveal Eliza, who’s carrying a small bowl full of trail mix, with Jeremiah standing just behind her. Eyes widening, Kara rushes to slam her laptop shut.

    “We thought you’d like to have something to pick at,” she tells Kara as she enters the room, leaving the door open. “Dinner’s still gonna take a while.”

    Kara lets out a little chuckle, still riding the wave of her close encounter with apprehension. Then, smiles as she takes the bowl. “You’re the best. Thank you.”

    Eliza sits at the opposite side of the bed, and Jeremiah remains standing, right next to Kara’s packed nightstand and the hanging model of the solar system she made when she was fourteen.

    “So, how’s our future lawyer doing?” he asks, dad-casual, as he crosses his arms.

    Fun. Kara takes in a big breath, mouth hanging open in search of an answer that won’t give her crisis away. “Great,” she starts with. “I…” After a second, she settles on something true. “I missed this place.” Before they can inquire anything further, she segues into: “But how’s your trip been?” She regards Eliza. “Alex tells me you’ve been enjoying having Ruby around.”

    Eliza smiles warmly. “Oh, she’s lovely.”

    “She is.” Kara looks down at the bowl in her hands, a tender look softening her features. “I’m really happy for her.”

    Jeremiah tilts his head at that, brows knitting together. “You are?”

    Kara looks up at him, a little puzzled. “Yeah,” she answers. Then, she shrugs, because really, this should be obvious. “She’s my big sister.” Plus, though everyone seems to keep forgetting, she was the one who called off the wedding, after all.

    “Nothing underneath the surface?” Eliza asks, still, with that caring look of hers that indicates she’s gone full analytical.

    Kara throws her head back, a smile of disbelief blooming in her face. “Why is everyone so concerned with my feelings right now? You’re supposed to be all over Alex, you know?”

    “We’re just trying to see if you’re going to need any extra support,” Eliza explains, an understanding, fond smile pulling at her lips. When Kara frowns slightly, she adds: “You tend to compare the pace your life goes at with others’.”

    Kara looks down at the bowl again, biting her bottom lip as a slight frown darkens her face. The words are meant in a compassionate fashion, she knows, but they sting like a punch to the chest. In her head, they morph like the ominous shadows of a sleepless night, turning into an excruciating whisper she’s heard a million times before since Alex and Sam announced their engagement. Could’ve been you.

    “Well… I have thought about what could’ve been,” she admits quietly, grabbing an M&M and rolling it between her fingers. Then, she looks up at her adoptive parents with resolve. “But I promise I’m fine. I’m really sure about my choice.”

    “Good. That’s good,” Jeremiah says, voice a little too light to be truly casual. “We’re here if you need to talk, too.”

    Kara smiles softly, popping the chocolate into her mouth. He spent a heated hour trying to get her to change her mind when she told him and Eliza about the canceled wedding back in the fall, and he’s been dropping little cutting hints every once in a while ever since, to try and steer her back into the right, secure path (as if her brain didn’t cause her enough distress by itself). The air around her sort of feels like a taut slingshot. “Thank you.”

    “We can even bring Mike here, if you need it.”

    Ah. There it is.

    Kara makes a sound that’s somewhere between a scoff and a whine. “Please don’t do that. I really don’t think he’d like to see the girl who broke up with him twice during her sister’s wedding weekend.”

    “Yeah. But he was at our house practically every night since you guys were thirteen. He was family.”

    “He still is,” Kara assures wholeheartedly. “Just because we’re not together doesn’t mean we stop being friends.”

    “Well, has he called? Have you heard from him lately?”

    “Jeremiah,” Eliza interrupts, before Kara can start to crumble under the weight of his expectations.

    “Can you— Can you please just forget about it for this weekend?” Kara gets in when she confirms he won’t push it this time, shutting her eyes tight with frustration. “I thought I could come here to take a breather before going back to… you know, face it and figure out my next steps. So.”

    Eliza gives Jeremiah a pointed look, to which he tightens his lips self-reflectively. “Right,” he says, looking down for a second. At least he does look apologetic. “Right. Yes, sorry.” A beat. “I just want both of my daughters to be happy.”

    “I’m very happy,” Kara promises. “Just… not when you do this.”

    “Just trying to nudge you in the right direction.”

    Eliza lets out a sharp exhale. “Honey, can you please go check on the ribs?”

    Jeremiah catches on with a sigh of his own. He raises his hands at his sides in a truce as he straightens up again. “Alright.” He turns on his heels and heads out the door.

    Kara chews at her lip, listening how his heavy steps get more muffled as he goes down the stairs with a small frown. She tucks her hair behind her ear as she looks back down at the bowl, next to her closed laptop over her comforter. “Thank you.”

    “It’s your life, sweetheart,” Eliza tells her, voice softer—Kara can tell she’s trying to reach her further beneath her words; to tell her that she really is here to help her, and that she supports her choices, whatever they are, as long as she’s sure of them. The gesture sparks a seed of warmth in Kara’s chest. At least someone doesn’t think she’s completely stranded.

    Eliza reaches out a hand, tilts Kara’s face towards hers. “Hey. I’ll talk to him. He’ll come around.” Then, she leans forwards and presses a kiss to her forehead, rubbing Kara’s cheek with her thumb a couple times as she says: “See you at dinner. 7:30.”

    Kara nods, a little smile pulling at the corners on her lips, and Eliza gets up from the bed and walks out of the bedroom, softly closing the door behind her.

    Once her footsteps have faded into the staircase, Kara grabs a pillow from behind herself, buries her face in it, and lets out a groan that almost teeters into yell territory.

    Actually, she doesn’t have visions of sugar plums and paintings—she scratches that as she shuts her eyes and reaches up to wipe the tears that have welled up in them off her lashes, before the can slide down her cheeks. The only thing she can truly hold onto is the hope that, five years from now, she’ll be at a place so stable that she’ll look back at this dreadful uncertainty with fondness.

    ***

    The first thing Kara sees when she comes out to the deck is Lena’s dress.

    By now, the formerly eggshell-blue sky has darkened, painted a rich navy specked with a smattering of stars. The shore comes and goes with a watery drone in the faraway view, and the air around them all is sultry and humid—it smells faintly of salt and strongly of barbecue ribs; of summer. That, plus the string of fairy lights that Alex and Sam requested be hung on the open beams overhead, makes the atmosphere feel warm as honey—but Kara’s eyes are focused on the right side of the deck.

    A tepid breeze blows, and the off-white linen of Lena’s dress ripples softly along with it. Under the golden lights, it makes her look like a figure from an Impressionist painting, all silky hair and milky skin. It makes Kara stop to wonder for a moment is she’s underdressed in her own loose denim shirt, chinos, and Birkenstocks. Then, as if the picturesque image of her holding a wine glass limply at her side and chuckling at something Jack said wasn’t enough, Lena turns. It’s casual, a natural gesture she probably doesn’t think twice about, but, by some opportune coincidence, she meets Kara’s eyes when she does. Her soft smile fades, replaced with slightly parted lips and a look penetrating and sharp as always, as her gaze lingers on Kara’s form—for a second too long, in Kara’s opinion: long enough that she notices again since that fateful night how Lena has a very alluring trail of dark freckles going down from her jaw to her collarbone.

    Crackling warmth starts to bloom through Kara’s belly. Of course she remembers those freckles now, which she’d been so tempted to—

    Of course! Just… lovely.

    Honestly, she has no right to look this beautiful when she’s… well, Lena.

    With a frown, Kara deviates her gaze to the ocean at the opposite side of Lena’s location as she starts to walk further into the deck. Who cares that Lena still is the most attractive person Kara has had the misfortune of knowing? Really, she should classify the low simmer she’s feeling all over her torso right now as justified rage: Lena’s certainly outshining Sam, the bride to be, and what kind of friend knowingly does that at a pre-wedding dinner? Plus—

    Kara grunts when Alex greets her with a bear hug (tackle, really), with an effervescent Kara! to boot. She’s tempted to answer with a flippant complaint, playing up the hit Alex’s hand just gave to her shoulder as it passed it, but Alex is so happy and comfortable that, like the first sip of iced tea after an early morning surf, her energy overrides all of Kara’s preoccupations and she just starts to laugh as they sway together to regain their balance.

    Her sister’s timing is impeccable as always, Kara figures as she eagerly accepts the plate of grilled shrimp and marinara sauce that Alex hands her after they part—it reminds her that there are more pressing matters to focus on right now than Lena’s horribly insolent fashion choices and… overall flawlessness. Forgetting about her crisis, for one, as well as celebrating her sister’s tremendous milestone. Most important of all, she tacks on when she lets out a moan of pleasure once the first bite of tangy, charcoaly goodness reaches her mouth, the food.

    “She told Jack,” Eliza clarifies, voice soft with knowing resignation, “Jack told us.”

    Lena’s eyes widen. “Oh my god,” she mutters to herself.

    As if on cue, Jack walks into the deck then, with a plate housing a half-eaten sandwich in his hands. “What did I tell you?” he asks, a light tone that hits the air like a drop of water falling on a scorching grill.

    Kara’s gaze has molten into something penetrating, stuck to Lena’s like white-hot daggers. Lena’s expression seems to fill with fear, as if she were desperate to keep whatever had sparked between them from being fucked up any further—as if that whatever still existed to her. “I-I did tell him,” she gets out, “but not— That wasn’t my intention.”

    “What did I say?” Jack repeats, this time puzzled by the tension.

    “Oh, yeah?” Kara scoffs, reaching up to rub at her cheek feverishly. “Did you just feel like airing out all my shit for fun, then?”

    “Kara,” Eliza reprimands.

    “I— I’m sorry. I mentioned it in passing, I didn’t think—”

    “Exactly! Exactly!” Kara interrupts, her brows scrunching up deeply with the tears she can feel bubbling up the back of her throat. “You didn’t think about how your actions would affect the people around you, did you?”

    The sheer fervor of her voice betrays a second layer of meaning to her words, more intimate and vulnerable than anyone but she and Lena could know. Lena lets out the softest puff of air as she catches on, but her expression doesn’t turn shameful and apologetic, like Kara would righteously expect—instead, her brows furrow with outrage, as if Kara was the unreasonable one.

    “They were going to find out eventually,” she counters, crossing her arms across her chest as an wry, affronted smile starts tugging at the corner of her lips.

    Kara scoffs—almost a disbelieving laugh. “But that’s not for you to decide!”

    Jack’s voice grows stronger. “Find out what?”

    “She quit law school!” Alex, Sam, Mike, and Eliza all clarify in frustrated unison.

    Jack’s eyes widen. “Oh,” he says, quieter, as the gravitas of his past actions dawns on him like an anvil of lead. “Oh, Christ.”

    Kara lets out a shallow breath. “What’s funny,” she tells Lena, voice thick with still-trapped acid tears, “is I really thought we’d gotten somewhere together.”

    Her chest rises and falls heavily, now, and her hands are tightening into fists at her sides—an attempt to maintain whatever’s left of her steadiness. She bets the jaws of everyone around them have gone slack, either dumbfounded by the seemingly total shift of her and Lena’s heretofore successful liaison or questioning why two virtual strangers could be having such a heated argument to begin with, but she can’t bring herself to care. Right now, the world only consists of her and Lena—Lena, whose face grows more and more indignant with every word that she says.

    “I couldn’t tell this to Alex,” she goes on, voice sharpened by Lena’s growing fire, “I couldn’t tell this to Mike, and then you go and you— You…”

    A thousand different slights crawl up Kara’s throat, a multitude equipped with pitchforks and torches, but she can’t bring herself to let any of them out—instead, her breath just hitches. Truthfully, she figures in that split second where all she can do is look at Lena, slack-jawed, all of this is just sad. Two years of back and forth, and, still, she can’t even bring herself to send a simple, idiotic insult Lena’s way? We were in this together, is all she really wants to tell her. What happened?

    “You know,” she manages to get out in its place, crossing her arms tightly to try and mask the quiver in her voice, “there’s a reason why you’re alone.”

    “And what’s yours?” Lena asks, scorn dripping from her voice.

    Kara shrugs carelessly, heart squeezing so much it cracks. “People like you.”

    “Sorry to break it to you, darling,” Lena answers, taking a step towards Kara as pure venom emanates from her expression, “but everyone comes with long, tangly strings attached that you—”

    “Okay, okay, look,” Jack interjects, shouldering his way through the small crowd to stand between Lena and Kara with his arms out in a mediating signal of peace. “Hey, let’s just— I made a mistake, I’m sorry; you don’t have to do this because of me.”

    “Why don’t we all go out to clear our heads?” Eliza suggests, only a little too enthusiastic. “Maybe early drinks?”

    The casualness of her tone, the cloying nature of how light her voice sounds—it all makes Kara want to storm out of the room and run through the packed crowds of the city until her lungs burn and her mind is blank with exhaustion. How is Eliza trying to fix this shitshow when every single person involved has somehow made sure to demolish all of its possible solutions?

    “No, this is— Guys?” Kara all but yells, not helping the pathetic laugh that escapes her when she turns back to address the whole group. Fuck it, everything’s on fire—she might as well finish the job, right? “Guys, I am so sorry, but your plan is all ruined. We are not together.”

    She aims her next words at Lena only, white-hot daggers pointed right at her glassy green gaze to prove just how little she cares about this, too. For a split second, Kara’s body’s overtaken by the eclectic emotions that have been growing inside her for the past two days, forming a visceral need to make Lena feel the same piercing embarrassment she felt when she woke up alone. With their fuel, she spits out:

    “We were faking it the whole time.”

    A cutting shadow crosses through Lena’s features—her heated expression falls with a barely-perceptible, stomach-churning pang; with an uncharacteristic kind of heavy sadness that, yeah, takes Kara right back to last night, silent room and empty bed. Instead of making her feel vindicated, though, the sight only shatters her heart more.

    Alex’s brows scrunch together. “You knew?”

    Jeremiah’s eyes flicker from Kara, who’s crossing her arms tight over her abdomen, to Lena to Alex. “What are you talking about?”

    Alex’s mouth opens, but she stays quiet for a second—maybe too embarrassed, now that she has to relay the plan out loud like this. “We, uh, may have tried to soften the energy in the house by orchestrating a… a union. Between them.”

    “Okay,” Jeremiah answers, frown and voice deepening, “who’s we?”

    “Everyone but you, Mike, and them, dad; just—"

    “So you lied to me?” Jeremiah asks roughly, turning to Kara with a look you’d only aim towards a troublemaking kindergartener in his eyes. “What have you been honest about lately? First it’s breaking off your engagement, now it’s setting your entire career ablaze— What’ll it be when we—"

    His voice goes on, grating and violent as a sledgehammer, but Kara’s mind can’t process it—how could it, when it’s already full to the brim with years and years’ worth of those very same slates and reproaches? The tightness in her throat is growing; the blazing sting in her chest is rising and spreading—she’s not sure she’ll be able to sit and bear Jeremiah’s humiliating disapproval without bursting into uncontrollable sobs of helpless rage. She knows she fucked up big time over the years—she can admit it, much to her white-hot chagrin—but she’s an adult, for Christ’s sakes: does her daring leap of faith really warrant the kind of lecture she’d have gotten in high school?

    She forces herself to try and steady her erratic breath, gathering all the will she has to just get through this asinine earful one more time, but she can feel Lena’s angry gaze piercing her skin and the mortifying stunned silence of everyone else that’s witnessing this scene, and, even though she could probably withstand its crushing weight on any average day, her metaphorical armor is in fucking shambles today. Jeremiah’s spear words settle inside Kara like the angry drops of a rainstorm that overfill a precariously cracked dam—her last shed of self-control snaps.

    “Oh my god, can you seriously not leave my life alone for a goddamn second?” she finally cuts in, shutting her eyes and dreading the uneven heat that’s fully covered her cheeks, now, as Jeremiah’s voice stops dead. She can feel the collective held-back gasp around her—she doesn’t care. All she wants is for this hellish scene to stop and die and combust into pieces of dust so small they can’t even be seen by a military-grade microscope.

    “Have you ever thought about why I never tell you anything I want to do until after I do it? I can’t— You don’t let me think, you just throw all these preconceived life paths at me and— and expect me to be happy following them! Do you remember how freaked out you got when I told you my major? You practically stopped talking to me for a week—how do you think that made me feel? Do you think I built confidence?”

    A muscle in Jeremiah’s jaw tightens; he crosses his arms over her chest defensively.

    “I can’t hijack the rest of my life just because you can’t fathom me living it in a way that doesn’t fit your perfect little vision of a family,” Kara goes on, voice rising with adrenaline. “I can’t! I— I know it’s terrifying—believe me, I’m the first person to doubt every single fucking thing I do, but— But I won’t ever find what’s right for me if I don’t… If I don’t fail looking for it first. Okay?”

    There’s a long moment where Jeremiah’s eyes pierce inro Kara’s, shaking his head softly. Are you done? The air is thick around Kara’s neck; the only thing she can hear is her shallow breathing, juxtaposed with the wide-eyed stillness of the rest of the group. After furrowing his brows, steely and distant, Jeremiah simply asks: “So you don’t want to be a lawyer.”

    “No,” Kara reiterates, voice lilting intently.

    “And you’re not even gonna try to reenroll.”

    “No.”

    “And… you never dated Lena.”

    Kara’s stomach twists at that. “No,” she lets out a second too late to seem determined, voice quiet and muffled as she reaches up to wipe away the stray tears that have finally fallen from her face. “No, I could never be with someone like her.”

    A soft, distinctly earnest scoff sounds to her left. “Because I fucked it up.”

    “Yes!” Kara hisses, directing her fed-up gaze toward Lena again. “You fucked it up; what do you call sneaking out in the middle of the night after we finally—”

    “I was trying to—” Lena starts, her brows tilted with the last straws of her desperation, but, at the last minute, a flip switches in her mind. It’s as if, in a second, all the value she saw in Kara just vanished. “You know what?” she says instead, low and cold and awful. “I don’t care. I don’t give a shit, go back to Metropolis or National City or wherever the fucked-up vestiges of your intuition lead you and waste your life away yearning for everything that never was.”

    “Oh, that’s rich coming from you,” Kara sneers back, voice tightening up again.

    “Excuse me?”

    “You heard me,” Kara nods, raising her brows to drive her statement home. “I’m not the one who gave up my brilliant, promising life to keep beating the dead horse of a company that my brother left in ruins after a psychopathic fit.”

    Lena’s face seems to mirror the awful, knotty feeling in Kara’s chest—all these insincere tries at hurting Lena’s feelings, they feel like stepping harder on a splinter jammed on her bloody foot. They can’t be together, they can’t get fulfilling lives, not even apart—why can’t Kara just take it in and live with it once and for all?

    “Fuck off,” Lena finally answers, tense and cutting, as she shakes her head with what looks infuriatingly close to true pained outrage.

    And then, before Kara can think of anything else to do besides run away and cry, a plasticky, muffled, wet grumble pulls her attention to the right.

    “Ruby!” Sam yells, before bolting towards the table to snatch her toddler up before a wedding cake almost twice her size crushes her like a behemoth of a foot would an ant. In her tiny hand, a mangy stuffed lion, clutched with the might of a too-proud, blissfully oblivious hunter. James must’ve set the cake on top of it; she must’ve felt big enough to try and get it on her own.

    Kara’s heart drops to the floor with a chilling splat. As she watches the scene—Sam, holding Ruby tight to her chest as she tries her best to explain why what she did was dangerous without bursting into an adrenalin-fueled earful; Alex, staring daggers so sharp at Kara that she’s eerily reminded of the laser-like contempt she used to send her way back in their middle school days; Lena, jaw clenched tight and breathing roughly as she rushes to kneel before the cake and try to salvage its splattered remains with Eliza’s help—the simmering, open space left behind by her outburst fills up with a slew of guilt so dense it turns her insides into white-hot ice.

    Fuck this, she thinks as her breath sharpens and her throat closes tight tight tight. Fuck this—this weekend is a laughable shitshow and her entire life is a cosmic joke and she will never be able to get things right because, somehow, she always manages to make the exact choices needed to screw everything up. Hell, she’s truly ruined her sister’s wedding, and for what? Her aimless eyes land on Jeremiah’s tilted brows—budding remorse, painfully late—they land on Alex, who responds to her helplessly penitent expression with a sharp, seething exhale, and the cold dreariness that started to eat away at Kara last night voraciously threatens to swallow her heart whole. Right now, most of all, she cannot bear to be on this godforsaken deck a second longer.

    With a shaky, self-berating breath, she turns to rush towards the back door that leads to the stairs that go up to her room. She makes it past the tear-blurry figures of shell-shocked people in what feels like just a few strides, but just before she can shut the sliding door behind her, she hears familiar footsteps hurrying in her wake.

    “Kara,” Mike calls urgently, with that same warm voice that has soothed night terrors and fits of rage and healed some of her darkest, deepest conflicts throughout the years, and, just like that, he’s got his arms open and she’s stopping at the foot of the stairs and shuffling forward for a hug instead.

    They stagger together towards some corner—Mike murmuring sweet words of comfort into Kara’s hair—where they’ll be able to stall without being noticed, and, with her face buried in the crook of his neck, she finally shatters. A quivering lip, a broken whimper, and then she finally lets herself cry—uglily, helplessly cry.

    She figures, as she tries to resist the sobs that still manage to escape from her against the softness of the collar of Mike’s shirt, if she focuses hard enough on the tender way his sturdy hands are rubbing up and down her back, she’ll be able to forget how Lena made her feel as if she could truly rule her own world.

    ***

    It takes forty minutes—with the help of the best stilted advice Mike could muster with the little knowledge he had of the situation and the dusty meditation app she downloaded some hazy moons ago—for Kara’s rage and sorrow to settle enough so she can pick herself up from where she’d been curled up in her bed, grab the bouquets of flowers that Mike and Jack were so kind to pick up for her, and head down to the patio to freshen up the A and S arrangement she’d charred in the firework incident.

    Replacing the crumbly, black remnants of flowers with lively-colored chrysanthemums and black peonies—for lack of dahlias at the flower shop, Mike informed her in a tone that managed to amuse her exhausted mind with its similarity to the wariness an unexperienced zookeeper might adopt while speaking to a lion with a recorded temper—turns out to be more meditative than breathwork, and she finishes the task with the weight on her shoulders lifted, even if just by a hair.

    She goes back up to her room, pulls her dress out of its garment bag and lays it on top of her bed, and heads to the shower—the official start for her pre-wedding doing up ritual. When the bathroom is all steamy and citrus-smelling and she’s standing in a towel in front of the mirror, reaching across the sink counter to connect her blow drier to the outlet, she hears the muffled murmur of an exhausted voice coming from the floor below.

    “—okay? Nothing is fine. All we asked them to do was come out here and celebrate with us and they are fucking ruining it!”   

    Alex’s tone borders on desperation. The blow drier in Kara’s hand freezes mid-air as the dense, heavy whirlwind of shame that’s lingered through this whole day returns to her stomach. With a crinkle of concern between her brows, she leaves the drier on the counter and slowly gets closer to the window. Through the cracks in the shutters, Alex and Sam sit on opposite sides of an outdoor loveseat.

    Alex is leaning back on the fluffy couch cushions with her feet up on the coffee table, and Sam is cozied up against the armrest, her legs crossed. They’ve had their hair and makeup done already—Sam’s look warm and glowy, Alex’s dewy and natural—but they’re both wearing robes over house clothes, as if they paused their getting ready to try and ease some of the tension winding up the air with a talk. Gesturing widely with her hands, Alex goes on:

    “I-I mean, all weekend, I’ve been chin deep in fixing all these asinine messes and rescheduling appointments and trying to figure out whatever’s going on with Kara because she won’t talk to me, and, honestly, I just—"

    “Well, have you even stopped to think about what’s going on with me?” Sam interjects, her brows furrowed earnestly, heavily. “All you’ve concerned yourself with this whole time is your planning going exactly how you want it. Are you marrying me? Or are you marrying your itinerary?”

    There’s a pregnant pause. Alex leans back on the chair; she goes to run a hand through her hair but reconsiders, seeing as it has just been stylishly slicked back for the event. She brings her hand down to pick at her un-pristine sweatpants instead.

    “Well, if that’s how you feel,” she murmurs eventually, “then why are we even doing this?”

    After another long beat, Sam lets out a sigh that cuts right to Kara’s core. “I don’t know.”

    She continues doing her hair and makeup with the feeling of an ever-growing rock filling up her gut. There is just no way she can allow the universe to go on as if nothing had happened—as if her sister’s wedding was a bothersome chore they all need to get through just because of commitments and formalities. This day is special. This day deserves love and thrill and tenderness, and she sure as hell has neglected giving that energy to it—which, she reminds herself, is kind of a big part of being the best woman—by getting caught up in all this idiotic tomfoolery.

    But it all ends right now. It has to. Even though all the things that concern her life balance might look darker than an abandoned house at midnight (and she doesn’t see them brightening up any time soon), and even though Lena might not even care to do reparations in favor of just getting through the damn thing and going back home where she can avoid Kara all she wants, she can do her part and take action to avoid their horrid case of bone-deep rancor from ruining the lives of two of the people they love most. She can do her part and set things right, for good. After all, third (or fourth, or fifth, or… a millionth) time’s the charm, right?

    When she’s done with her wavy do and minimal gold-toned makeup, Kara heads down the stairs to the lounge area and takes a right turn instead. Down the hall, the door to Lena’s bedroom is shut and shadowed, despite the midday sun seeping in across the open floor—it looks hostile, menacing. Still, despite the pang of dread that rises through Kara at the sight, she takes a breath and walks towards it. She pauses, bites her lip, furrows her brow in one last begrudging second of consideration, and then lifts her hand and knocks on the carved panel.

    It takes half of a second for Lena to answer. When she does, Kara is hit with a now familiar but still equally frustrating reminder of just how beautiful she is. She’s dressed in a wisteria gown that clings to her body as freely as the flowers themselves; her hair is pulled into a half-updo that falls over her shoulders like silk, and her eyes—though just as pained as Kara feels—are rimmed with a line of kohl so black it makes the green of her irises look translucent as a freshwater sea. Kara’s heart almost skips a beat, but her stomach twists tight. God, if things were different…

    With a huff, she pulls herself back to the present, to her resolve. Meeting Lena’s gaze only a tad apprehensively, she pushes past the simmering in her chest to mumble:

    “There’s one last thing we need to fake.”

    Lena just looks at her for a moment, cutting, with her jaw set tight. After letting out a breath, though, she rolls her eyes and opens the door wider, so Kara can walk through.

    ***

    By the time Kara and Lena make it to the driveway, the whole group is already nearing the motorcade that’ll lead them to their pièce de résistance, to the destination they all came here to see. All of them are dressed in shiny tuxes and lively gowns, and they make casual, light conversation, laughing and congratulating the soon-to-be newlyweds, Kara notes as she takes a furtive glance of Lena—still walking stiffly by her side, shoulders tense and brows awfully knit together—with the one-last-time type of effort needed to try and evaporate the remains of any noxious energy that might’ve clung to their minds.

    By a stroke of luck and fate (Kara chooses to believe, because do those things even exist, anyway?), they approach Alex and Sam just as their lovesome conversation with Jeremiah and Eliza is ending.

    “Sam, Alex,” Lena says as, quickening her gait a little so she can get to them before they get the chance to escape in Eliza and Jeremiah’s wake with the excuse of protection from any further idiocy. As a testament to the kindness and integrity of both, they do turn towards her and Kara, faces stoic and wary, but soft in a heartwarming way that shows they’re willing to give another chance. “We’ve been complete assholes. We’re truly sorry.”

    “I’m an awful sister,” Kara adds, an earnestly and genuinely remorseful pang in her belly, as she slows down next to Lena, “and she’s an awful best friend, but we’re not gonna ruin your big day.” She turns to Lena, who puts on a little, tight-lipped smile to strengthen Kara’s next words: “We promise.”

    Both Alex and Sam stay silent, appraising Lena and Kara as if, burrowed beneath the layers and layers of spiteful dishonesty they’ve piled on top of themselves, there were peeks of pure gold shining though. Kara can feel the brush of Lena’s arm against her own, the tingly energy of her fingers less than an inch away from her own, but, despite the fact that holding her hand or putting an arm around her shoulder would make their argument as solid as lead, she resists the urge and tightens her arms around her abdomen instead. She doesn’t know where they stand, after all, now their boundaries have been so blurred.

    “Thanks,” Alex lets out eventually, not the least bit convinced (but still appreciative, in the buried, buried way only Kara and Sam have learned to spot).

    “FYI, though,” Sam adds, raising her brows and letting go of Alex’s hand to cross her arms over her chest, “if you fuck up today, my something borrowed will be a knife.”

    A wry smile pulls at Alex’s lips; her eyes narrow. “And it’s shiny and new.”

    Kara chuckles half-heartedly (because, knowing her sister, there is a metaphorical knife she and Sam have agreed upon using if anything goes wrong) and Lena closes her eyes and lets out a good-natured huff of air though her nose. With that, after a pointed look from the both of them, Alex and Sam turn to go board their ornamented eggshell-blue pickup Jeep, leaving a pressing expectation floating in the air behind them.

    You’d better keep your word.

    When they’ve fully turned their back to her and Lena, Kara feels her smile drop—the darkest of gray clouds blocking whatever last dregs of sun had been left in her. She looks at Lena, and a biting mass of sorrow floods her insides, cold and deadening as an ice avalanche. What would it have been like, to be her date tonight; to be inundated with the nourishing fulfilment that she could have something right after all? Lena’s eyes are charged with something seethingly disbelieving, and they linger on her for just a beat too long before she directs them to the cobblestone below, flaring her nostrils the slightest bit, and turns to meet James, who has been waiting for her by the Jeep.

    With a sigh, Kara takes a second to recenter her energy as best she can, to let go of the weight in her chest and the tension in her shoulders and nourish herself with the joy and excitement she’s harbored for this day since she and Alex started daydreaming about it, back in their shared bedroom in Midvale. Then, she looks up and meets Mike’s gaze at the other end of the driveway. The smile he shoots her as she walks to him, soft and knowing and ready to support whatever feelings arise in her today, makes her mirror the expression with a little true one of her own.

    She gets in the car, Mike turns the engine on, and then they all get on their merry way.

    ***

    “Love is intoxicating,” Jack says, standing in front of the flowery altar at the very tip of the fairy-light-filled deck Sam and Alex had chosen all those months ago, his well-combed hair blowing softly in the wind as they stare at each other with so much affection in her eyes that Kara couldn’t help the tears that started to fall silently down her cheeks a few minutes ago. “It envelops you, it tickles you, and it makes you feel like you can walk on air. It’s like this wind. It’s in your bones, it’s in your breath. It’s in every beat of your heart. You can’t look for it, you can’t describe it, and you can’t escape it.”

    “Sam,” Alex says, a tender grin lighting up her features and the diamond-y sheen of tears in her warm russet eyes. “I love you so much, and I don’t think I say it enough. You are the yin to my yang. The jelly to my peanut butter,” she laughs wetly, tightening her hold on Sam’s hands. “And I’ll love you forever and always.”

    She knows she shouldn’t give in to her most primal urges, but Kara can’t stop herself—Lena’s right there at the other side of the altar, and, when she lets her eyes fall on her for just a split second, she blinks, a little stunned by the fact that Lena had been looking at her already. She’s quick to divert her gaze, of course, but Kara returns her attention to the ceremony with the hum of a flush warming up her cheeks and the lightest crinkle between her brows. Was she thinking the same thing as her? Was she trying to find the best way to go up to her during dinner and apologize?

    “Alex,” Sam goes on, the sweetest of tones in her voice. Her cheeks are dewy with the tracks of tears she’s been allowing to fall for the entire ceremony, and Kara’s belly goes fuzzy at the sight. How lovely it is, that her sister has found someone who can compliment the hollow spaces she has; who, time and time again, has proven to truly be the love of her life? “I love you with so much of my heart that none is left to protest.”

    “That was very good,” Jack remarks, a playfully raising his brows.

    Sam shrugs, impish. “I just made it up.”

    A wave of chuckles passes through the clothed tables that form a couple of parallel rows in front of the altar, enveloped in an intimate, golden-blue atmosphere with silky strips of fabric blowing softly from the wooden beams that stretch out overhead and mellow cerulean waves that frame them on three sides. Kara bites down a poignant smile as she reaches up to wipe a new tingly tear that’s fallen from her lashes, trying her very damn best to ignore the prickling sensation of green eyes scanning her profile’s outline.

    “Now,” Jack calls, playfully adding an exaggerated lilt to his voice, “if I could please be handed the rings?”

    Right on cue, a smiling Ruby dressed in a flowy linen dress and holding a little wooden box with a geometric pattern carved on its lid, followed by a heartwarmingly amused Eliza, appears running down the flower-carpeted altar towards Sam and Alex. They accept the rings with wide, fond smiles on their faces, thank Ruby profusely before she returns to her seat beside Jeremiah and Eliza in the front row, and put them on each other’s fingers with so much love and care one would think they were made of the most delicate of porcelains.

    “Alright,” Jack says brightly when Alex and Sam lock eyes again, already pulling each other close. “I now pronounce you wife and wife!”

    The kiss brings a burst of claps and cheers that fills the deck like honey tea. Kara’s smile widens with the sheer, bursting pride of seeing her sister live through such a monumental moment in her life, and as she watches the small crowd—friends, aunts, uncles, cousins—sharing the sentiment, a kernel of something squirmy and fluttery appears in the pit of her gut.

    What would it have been like, to wear a flowy white dress and stand under a candlelit altar and tie her future to someone she wholly, soundly loves in a snowy November day? Despite everything, it astounds her how she still hopes she gets to know soon.

    Certain.

    ***

    If there’s anything Alex Danvers and Sam Arias are known for, that is throwing one hell of a party.

    The reception lights up the atmosphere as the sun starts to go down around it. There are assorted drinks and groovy beats playing and the most opulent looking hors d’oeuvres displayed on several lovely grazing tables, and Kara certainly tries her hand at enjoying the amenities with gusto. That is, at least, until the guests all settle at their tables or scattered seats and she’s left alone with her mind and Mike, who, despite his best efforts, is kind of failing miserably at getting her to kick all thoughts of their failed wedding to the curb.

    He’s tried all the kinds of small talk he’s got available—and the most effective ones, given the fact that they’ve known each other since they were thirteen: he jokes about the responses to the latest pieces she’s put up on her “blob,” he brings up the newest episode of The Bear—he even reminds her of that time he stepped onto the lectern for his final debate championship with his shirt on backwards, which, Kara can’t deny, does bring the shadow of a smile to her face. But, as she pushes around the pieces of croquembouche she’s picked off with her fork on her plate, the humming void in her gut only grows stronger and stronger. She can’t help but recall the feeling, as she watches Alex and Sam dance, unencumbered, to Remi Wolf on the dance floor, that emerged in her heart after Eliza so thoughtfully pointed out her penchant for comparison during the first afternoon they all spent here.

    Could’ve been you.

    “—do remember Barry, right?” she hears Mike ask, to which she responds with a halfhearted nod, smushing a croquembouche ball in half. “Well, he comes over to me then and he just— Shit. Parents, three o’clock.”

Her brows knit together instead, getting into her head for a moment as if to compute the claim that has just been made. “What are you talking about?” she finally gets out, straightening back up to get closer to Kara. “We were never gonna call off the wedding.”

    “Uh, yeah, you were,” Kara doubles down earnestly just before taking another bite of cake. She continues, no-nonsense crinkle between her brows, waving her ganache-coated fork in the air to enhance her dessert-muffled words. “The day of the ceremony, after everyone found out that I had quit law school, I heard you talking to Sam. You were saying you both were all pissed because of our pettiness and you didn’t even know if you wanted to go through with the party because of it.”

    A pause. Alex blinks, deepening her frown to try and remember better, and Sam’s eyes flicker down as she presumably runs through the entirety of the past year in her mind, but the analysis doesn’t even last a full second—just like that, a lightbulb in her head lights up, she looks up at Kara with a smile blooming through her face, and then she bursts out laughing.

    Kara’s face scrunches up into a full-blown frown.

    “Oh my god,” Sam gets out, her grin lighting up her features by the second. When she turns to Alex, just the look spreads the enlightenment—her eyes widen, and she starts to laugh, too. “We were— No, we—”

    “We staged a fake fight so you guys would feel bad and make up,” Alex finally supplies, voice barely intelligible through her chortling.

    Kara’s face drops. Her lips part and her brows shoot up, in tandem with the spark of outrage that rekindles in her gut. “Are you kidding?”

    “Oh, it really was good,” Sam reminisces warmly, before putting on a clearly truthless frown and turning to Alex to ask: “Are you marrying your itinerary or me?”

    Alex scoffs and looks away, trying to tamp down her smile to be able to play along. “Well, if that’s how you feel, I don’t know why we’re doing this anymore.”

    “Fuck you!” Kara reproaches, dropping her fork fervidly (though the smile pulling at her lips betrays an honest air of respect to their commitment) as Alex grabs Sam’s hand to do an impromptu half bow with her. “I could barely make it through the ceremony without crying with shame, you assholes!”

    “Puppets,” Lena laments playfully, shaking her head as Alex simply shrugs, not a single drop of remorse in her being. “That whole weekend, we were nothing but puppets.”

    Kara lets out a rough sigh, crossing her arms as she slumps against the back of her chair. “You can say that again.”

    Alex opens her mouth to tack on some annoyingly big-sister remark or other, probably, but before she can speak, the oh-so-familiar plucked guitar of Natasha Bedingfield’s biggest hit starts to surround their space.

    Kara’s whole body lights up from the inside, a huge grin spreading through her face as she pushes her plate of cake aside and gets up from her seat. Biting down her smile, she turns to Lena expectantly, only getting a single arched eyebrow in return.

    “Come on,” Kara goads, reaching her hand out and opening and closing her fingers a couple times, to enhance her enticing. “You know you want to.”

    Lena’s eyes narrow as she brings her hand up to take a sip of champagne. She savors it and swallows it and, even though her gaze is as sharp as ever, it’s clear she won’t be able to resist her fond smile much longer. After one more second of holding the stare, her playful resolution breaks—she rolls her eyes, grabs Kara’s hand, and allows herself to be pulled up as the loveliest of lights spreads though her features.

    “For old times’ sake,” she adds, looking into Kara’s eyes with a kind of warmth she still can’t believe gets to be hers.

    Kara cheers as Sam and Alex laugh with affection, and then she leads Lena out to the floor, walking backward and starting to dance to the pre-chorus. She revels in the way Lena giggles at her moves, in how her white dress flows as they sway to the lively groove and how, when she spins Lena out and then holds her close, the grounding pressure of her body infuses her own as if the essences inside them were entwined into a single one.

    The moment she dips Lena to press a kiss to her lips, Kara can’t help but glow at just how true the words that permeated that weekend turned out to be.

    Today is where your book begins. The rest is still unwritten.
