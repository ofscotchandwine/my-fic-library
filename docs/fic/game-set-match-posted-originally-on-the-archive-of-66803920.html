    RIO The job, for all intents and purposes, should have been a straight forward one.

    And it had been, up until the most crucial moment, that is. The instructions, as they always did, came through her door disguised as a birthday card from a distant relative.     Agatha didn’t live off the grid, and all of her records appeared without delay or error whenever Rio ran her name through the database. Social security – normal.

    DMV – an unusually high level of parking tickets and speeding fines, but nothing that screamed dangerous criminal who would need additional measures to get the job done.

    Medical Insurance – a lapsed policy, currently uninsured. But again, not particularly unusual or alarming in the current economic climate. And at first, it was hard to understand why someone had paid to have this, put bluntly, rather unremarkable woman killed.

    Yet the moment Rio ran her hits name through the Tor browser instead, intent to see what the dark web might have to say about one Agatha Harkness, the reasoning behind her employers choice to end her life became exceedingly apparent.

    Because Agatha Harkness was a hacker. A damn good one too. A hacker who used her impressive skillset to steal all she could from whoever was unfortunate enough to cross her pixelated path. She was intensive, she was relentless, and her work was spotless, making Agatha Harkness the online equivalent of a cat-burglar, one who left neither a wayward hair nor a rogue fingerprint behind whilst committing her crimes.

    And once Rio scratched that surface, which turned out to be an iceberg tip hiding a colossal mass beneath dark water and foaming waves, the true extent of Agatha’s criminal history became crystal clear. Fake app’s; trojan horses designed to scan for banking details and passwords; phishing attempts across multiple communication and social media platforms; keylogger viruses disguised as coupons; false text messages and emails from insurance companies sent to unsuspecting victims.

    You name it, if it pertained to online crime, Agatha Harkness had done it. And it was clear that Agatha’s intention, at least to start with, had been purely financial, as the cyber-crumbs her online actions left scattered across the internet all pointed towards good, old-fashioned monetary theft. But recently, Agatha appeared to have turned a corner, pivoting towards an entirely different sort of prize. One that, once won, could be utilised again and again to wring her victims for cash whenever she so desired. Blackmail.

    Agatha Harkness was blackmailing people.

    And not just your everyday, run-of-the-mill, average Joe who might have a few skeletons stuffed in their closet that they want to hide from a nosy parent or a misinformed spouse.

    Oh no.

    Agatha Harkness was blackmailing the mob. The mafia. The family. So, enter Rio stage right.

    Who had been hired by said mob to take out the risk.

    The risk who had been threatening, rather indiscreetly at that, to make their far messier, and far more amateur, cybercrimes visible to the feds if they didn’t provide her with the financial security she was demanding to keep her mouth shut.

    And even after learning who Agatha Harkness was, and at last understanding why Rio had been tasked with wiping this particular player from the board, the job had run surprisingly smoothly. And on looking back, Rio could kick herself for not picking up on quite how simple it had all been.

For two, the room her computer was set up in was set toward the back of the house and her desk, the one where she committed all of her decidedly devilish crimes, was positioned in front of a window.

That particular moment had occurred whilst Rio was laying flat on her stomach in the hay loft, binoculars in hand. And to start, all she had seen was the shadowed glide of a person moving past a window.

From there, it had been a domino-line of observations, each one as disarming as the next and adding to the tally of just why Agatha Harkness was the most beautiful woman Rio had ever seen.

    Her body: illuminated by the glow of the lamp in the corner, highlighting the captivating exposition that existed between the curve of her waist and the bow of her hip.

It was important, after all, that she gave herself enough time to double check all of the delicate parameters a sniper must consider when aiming to kill.

Then she tested her scope against both the weather and the angles, giving herself breathing space to adjust it for any other unseen variables that could affect her shot like a rogue branch, or the sway of a curtain caught in a breeze. Next, in silence, as the weak autumn sunlight was quickly overpowered by dusks broad shadow, Rio waited for Agatha to sit at her desk by the window, as she did every night.

Still, the shot explodes from the rifle, shattering the window, and echoing through the valley Agatha’s farmhouse was cradled by, bouncing off thick tree trunks and causing a murder of crows to take flight, their dark bodies mere shadows in the moonlight flittering between branches. Rio’s breathing quickened as her body stilled. She stares through her scope with an urgent eye, trying to see whether her shot, even with the electricity cutting out at such an ill-timed moment, had been true and hit its intended target.

That’s what we’re going with? Not even the ‘I was hiking and needed shelter’ trope, but straight into sniping vermin through an unsuspecting, innocent woman’s window.”

That every visit she had made, sneaking through the woods under the cover of night, Agatha had been watching. And was obviously very amused too, by Rio’s incorrect belief that she was undetectable.

Albeit slightly dirtier, and slightly more charred than she had been before.

And Rio, high off the adrenaline mixing in her blood, and more than likely concussed from being flung from the barn like a ragdoll, couldn’t stop herself from grinning at the sight of her.

    “I bet.”

    “I like the headset. Its very sexy-receptionist.”

    As horrifying a job as it was to most, designing the best ways to kill folks for money, it was, in truth, her life’s work. And Rio was proud of her life's work.

    But, as with all day-jobs, it had in recent years lost some of its splendour. And Rio had found herself, at times, merely existing through the motions of what it entailed.

    She would receive her orders, research the mark, complete recon, and bang. Job done.

    But now she had discovered something grand.

    Something wonderful.

    Her very own golden goose.

    Because Agatha Harkness wasn’t just brilliant and she wasn’t just beautiful. She was something Rio had always wanted but had never found in the underbelly of the world she worked in.

    She had found an equal, an adversary.

Because I do genuinely think, in different circumstances, the two of us could have had some real fun together.” Rio leant back on her elbows, feeling for the position of her rifle beneath her before remembering the knife she always kept strapped to her ankle for these sorts of occasions.

Without delay, Rio scrambled to her feet and sprinted for the treeline, shouting over her shoulder as she fled, “Raincheck?”

    And as the thick woodland covered her flight, she couldn’t help the crazed laughter that exploded from her mouth as a roar of fury erupted behind her. Clearly, Agatha had just learned what Rio already knew.

    That she had met her perfect rival that very night.

    After one short visit to the emergency department and one lie about having fallen down a flight of stairs, the days that followed that initial extremely explosive meeting blew past in an uneventful march.

    And those following days felt far duller now too.

    It was frustrating, because ever since she had gotten a taste of what it felt like to go to head-to-head with someone like Agatha Harkness, which could be aptly described as a bolt of lightning to the stomach, it suddenly felt as though all the colour in the world had been drained like water down a plughole, leaving behind only the blandest of tones like tapioca or beige.     In truth, it had been the running theme of her life and infected every aspect of it: she would discover a topic that caught her interest and read of nothing else for weeks; or she would hear a song once, and find that no other melody could satisfy her; or she might get smiled at by a pretty stranger in a coffee shop, and suddenly no other coffee tasted as good, nor anyone else's smile felt quite as satisfying.

    No, it wasn't a secret that a handful of her more innate impulses couldn't be considered reasonable nor particularly sane. But Rio had learned, over time, how to separate her more obsessive compulsions from her regular ones, keeping herself on a tight leash whenever the more demanding of the two became louder and more abrasive.

    Though, she didn’t work to unpick those addictive threads from her personality entirely, because in truth, they were the main reason she was so damn good at her job.

Somebody wanted their mother-in-law taken out, but also wanted it to look like an accident – Rio would stage a terrible car accident. Another hated their boss and wanted their job for themselves – Rio would brush passed them in a bar, dropping something deadly into their mimosa or beer.

And as her high school councillor, someone who had known her before she had ever lifted a gun or a knife to end someone’s life, had phrased it, Rio could be 'extremely intense. Unnervingly so.'
