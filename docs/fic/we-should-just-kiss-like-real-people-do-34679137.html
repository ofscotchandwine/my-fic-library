“Captain on deck! At attention, maggots!”

 

Kara threw her scrub brush into her pail and hopped to her feet, pushing her shoulders back and standing at attention in line with the rest of her crew. Her sister, Alex, pushed aside another crew member so that she could stand next to her and Kara stifled a smile. Captain Edge trudged down the line, measuring up the sailors as he went.

 

“The Isle of Gollerus is upon us,” the captain said, pacing up and down the line of sailors. “By order of King Lex, you are to go ashore, find the precious green rock which he seeks, and return to the ship. Do not fail, or on your head be it.”

 

Stomping down to his quarters, the deckhands returned to their duties. 

 

“The Isle of Gollerus?” Private Nia Nal asked quietly from her place next to Kara. “Isn’t that supposed to be full of mermaids? This is to be a suicide mission.”

“Don’t be ridiculous,” Alex said from where she sat carefully tying darning a torn sail. “Mermaids aren’t real. Those are just stories meant to scare gullible crewmen like you.”

 

“I hope they are real,” replied Petty Officer Matthews, who was lazily cleaning his nails with his knife. “I want to be the first man to kiss a mermaid.”

“And you should get back to scrubbing my decks,” he spat, kicking over her pail and spilling dirty water everywhere. “You’ve missed a spot.”

“Got something to say, Danvers?” He jeered, stepping into her space. She opened her mouth but a hand on her arm stopped her. It was Nia, shaking her head to stop her from doing something that would earn her a lashing or a trip to the brig. 

 

“It’s fine, Kara,” she whispered, pulling her back to her station. “Just let it go.”

Nia grinned. “You don’t mean that.”

 

“I do,” Kara huffed. “I really do.”

 

 

The sailors climbed into the dinghy and Kara grabbed an oar as the remaining crew lowered them into the water. It was a small group of them setting sail for the island, only Kara, Nia, Officer Matthews, and a handful of others. The vessel rocked and bobbed on the choppy sea as they paddled toward the island, a dull grey mist hanging in the air over the green of the sea. 

 

As they approached the inlet to the island, Matthews set down his oar, stood up, and began to sing. 

 

“What are you doing, man?” Nia cried, pulling him down to his seat. “You’ll sign our death warrants!”

 

Matthews ignored her and piped up again, singing about some bonnie lass and her brave soldier.

 

Kara looked at her puzzled. “What do you mean?” 

 

“Mermaids are drawn to singing,” Nia explained, looking through the mist around the boat nervously. “He’s going to draw them here and then we’ll all be lost to Davy Jones’ locker!”

 

A thump against the side of the boat cut Matthews’ song short. Kara stilled. Nia sat next to her, praying silently over shaking hands. As the crew looked around, a deckhand -- James Olsen -- pointed quickly over Matthew’s shoulder.

 

“There! Look!” He cried, his pointing finger shaking slightly.

 

The sailors turned their heads to see a beautiful woman bobbing in the water, her arms folded on the edge of the boat next to the officer. 

 

“Hello,” he said with a smug grin, pulling himself closer to her. “What’s your name?”

 

“I’m Imra,” she said quietly, a British accent lilting her words as she lay her head on her folded arms. “Was that you I heard singing?” She batted her eyelashes at him. “Are you my brave soldier?”

 

Mike took off his hat and ran a hand through his cropped hair. “Aye ma’am, that would be me indeed.”

 

He leaned toward her, his eyes fixed on hers. 

 

“Mike!” Nia hissed, reaching toward him. “Don’t! It’s a trap!”

 

“Shut up!” He hissed back, teeth bared. “I’m going to kiss me a mermaid!”

 

Turning back to the sea maiden he smiled, leaning toward her. “You wouldn’t hurt me, would you?”

 

Imra leaned up, reaching back to cup the back of his head.

 

“Not my brave soldier,” she whispered.

 

Then with one quick motion, she pulled him out of the boat and he was lost to the depths.

 

“Mike!” James shouted, looking over the edge of the craft, but there was no sign of him. A sudden thumping caught their attention and within seconds, a gathering of fins and long hair was circling the boat, reaching up for the various crew members. They battered them with their oars and fired their pistols into the water as they began to beat a hasty retreat to the ship. 

 

James grabbed a net and tossed it at one of the mermaids, wrapping her in it and pulling it taut. 

 

“What are you doing?” Kara cried, jabbing her oar at a particularly vicious blonde siren. 

 

“Captain Edge won’t believe us!” James cried, tying the net to the stern of the dinghy so that it dragged behind them. “We have to bring him proof or he’ll have us all thrown into the brig!”

 

They rowed with all of their might until at last they reached the mouth of the inlet and the mermaids began to fall away, unwilling to hasten after them in the open sea.

 

 

When James had finished telling the story of their narrow escape at the hands of the sirens, he and another sailor dropped the net containing the captured sea maiden at Captain Edge’s feet. The captain cut the net open with his knife and Kara’s breath escaped her.

Wide green eyes looked up from a terrified face that looked as though it had been carved from marble by the Lord herself.

 

“Well well,” the captain sneered, watching the blue-green scales of her fins slap around on the deck. “Lucky for you chaps, King Lex has long wanted a mermaid for his castle. Their tears are said to contain healing powers. She’ll fetch a pretty penny, she will. Put her in a basin in the brig.”

 

Kara darted forward. “But Captain! You can’t just take her --”

 

Captain Edge turned on his heel to face her. “You dare to address me like that? What is your name, sailor?”

 

“Danvers, Captain,” Kara said with a blush. “Private First Class Kara Danvers.”

 

“Well, Private Danvers, you’ve just bought yourself mop duty below deck,” he sneered, turning back to the rest of the crew. “Get this fish off my deck.” He clomped off to his quarters, telling his first mate to set a course for the Port of Metropolis.

 

“Tough luck,” Nia said from beside Kara as she watched Olsen and another officer lift the net off of the mermaid to transfer her to a washtub. “Mop duty in the lower levels…” She trailed off with a disgusted look. 

 

“It isn’t right,” Kara said, clenching her fists. “What gives him the right to take something from their natural habitat for the pleasure of the King?”

 

Nia shrugged. “It is not for us to question.”

 

As the water drained away from the net, leaving the creature dry, the scales on her lower half began to fade in the warm sunlight, transforming into a perfect pair of legs. 

 

The crew gasped at the heavenly sight before them, as the naked woman clung to herself as though to hide from their gaze. Shrugging off her coat, Kara darted toward the woman and threw it over her, shielding her from the hungry gaze of the men. 

 

“Help me get her into the basin,” she yelled to James, who stood a foot away, gawking at the siren. “James! Now!”

 

He leapt into action, helping Kara lift the creature into the wash basin that had been filled with seawater, and then helped her and a few other sailors carry the basin below to the brig, where Alex locked the door to the cell, stowing the key on its ring at her belt.

 

Kara gazed through the grates of the cell, watching the woman sink into the water to hide from the hungry looks from the sailors, and she knew she had to do something. It wasn’t right to take someone from their home like this. Not for all the gold in the world. She may not be human, but she was a living creature, surely with thoughts and hopes of her own. What hope had she now but to become an exhibit in a miniscule tank in the Castle of Lex Luthor? What comparison was that to a life in the open sea? Kara had to free her. She must.

 

 

Kara lay awake until she heard the loud snoring echoing around her in her bunks that let her know that the crew was asleep. She would have to be silent to avoid the officers on deck, but the coast should be clear to the brig.

 

Rolling out of her bunk, she knelt down to her sister’s bed which lay beneath hers and quickly, but quietly relinquished her of the key to the cell where the mermaid was being kept, then she quickly crept down to the lowest level of the ship, opening the door to the cage. 

 

The woman appeared to be sleeping and Kara tiptoed into the cell, pulling the door closed behind her so that it would not creak with the rolling of the ship. As she drew closer, the maiden opened her eyes wide and threw herself against the back of the basin, trying to get as far away from Kara as possible, sending water splashing loudly to the floor.

 

Kara threw up her hands in a placating gesture and then pressed a finger to her lips, shushing her quietly.

 

“Shhh,” she whispered, kneeling down beside the tub, “I’m here to help you. I’m going to set you free, do you understand?”

 

The woman looked at her for a moment and then nodded curiously. Kara smiled in what she hoped was a reassuring manner.

Again the creature nodded, and Kara tried not to be distracted by just how beautiful she was. Her eyes pierced through Kara’s skin curiously, their trail setting the blonde’s skin on fire wherever they went. 

 

Reaching into the tub, Kara carefully lifted her out, setting her on the bench against the wall as the water slid down her fins and they began to transform into legs once more.

The girl frowned and shook her head. 

 

“You can’t?” Kara asked, fear slipping in. She wasn’t sure she could carry her all the way up without being caught. “You can’t walk?”

 

“I don’t know how,” the girl responded and Kara was lost for a moment in the rich chocolatey sound of her voice, dark and smokey like aged mead -- deep and full and with the hint of an accent. Gorgeous.

Her arms began to shake as she rushed towards the ledge of the ship, and in her hurry, she tripped over a set of casting lines, falling face down on the deck and sending the mermaid flying onto the hardwood.

“What do we have here?” Captain Edge said, standing over her angrily.

 

A pair of crewmen grabbed the woman and took her down to the hold and Kara climbed to her feet angrily.

 

“You can’t just take her, Captain! It isn’t right!”

 

Edge backhanded her across her cheek. “You would defy my orders -- the King’s orders -- commit treason, for a fish?!” He spat on the deck. “You will hang for this,” he growled, turning to the crowd that had gathered on the deck. Kara picked out Alex’s worried face in the throng.
