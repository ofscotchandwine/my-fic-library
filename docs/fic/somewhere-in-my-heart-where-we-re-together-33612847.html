somewhere in my heart (where we're together)

The first sign that something was off went easily ignored in the mental fog and bleary eyes that accompany Kara when she first wakes.  If she’d have paid closer attention, it would have registered as strange when the curtains blowing in the open window were a light shade of pink instead of their usual cream, the very light shade of pink she’d been wanting to switch to for the past few months.  She’d never got around to it.  Superhero life is busy, after all.

 

Kara rubs her eyes, not stifling her yawn, and sits amidst her blankets in a daze.  The wind shifts her hair.  If she were normal, she might have shivered, even in the warm glow of the morning sun.

 

It’s the very same sun that makes her so incredibly not normal.

 

She crawls toward the foot of her bed and over the seat, continuing as her hands touch the rug, followed by her knees, then finally stands up.  It’s a comical way to get out of bed, and Alex would make fun of her had she seen it, but she’s too tired to care.

 

It’s when she’s made herself an iced cold glass of water and settled on one of the chairs across the couch that she notices something out of sorts.  On the end table, which had been bare except for a vase of fake flowers and a book the night before, now housed a trio of picture frames.

 

Now, she could just use her super eyesight to zone in on the pictures from her comfortable position across the way, but where’s the drama in that?  She sets her glass on the little table between the chairs and moves towards the pictures.

 

The first one she’s seen before, a picture of teenaged versions of her and Alex in the middle of a younger Eliza and Jerimiah.  But, how did it get here, framed on her table?  It was in a box of old pictures and mementos she keeps tucked safely away under her bed.  Maybe Alex thought it would be a good idea to come decorate while Kara was asleep - an absurd sort of option, but a plausible one.  She’s seen weirder things.

 

The next picture she doesn’t recognize, but it’s not like she remembers every picture she’s ever been in.  It’s her and Lena, arms wrapped around each other and cheeks pressed together, bright smiles on their faces.  Through the cloud of confusion swirling in her head, she can’t help but think that she’s never seen Lena happier, never seen her cheeks so big and eyes so bright with joy.  Maybe the picture was taken on a night when one of her friends convinced her to drink Aldebaran rum.  It could explain the missing memory.  (She has no proof it was Nia’s doing, but somehow she just knows).

 

The third picture, however, is much harder to find a plausible explanation for.  Alex and Kelly look incredibly happy, not unlike her and Lena in the other photo, but in the middle of them is a little girl, maybe four or five years old, that Kara’s never seen before.  She’s incredibly adorable, skin like Kelly’s and hair tied up in two little buns on top of her head.  But who is she?  And where did the picture come from?

As if on cue, Kara’s phone lights up with Alex’s contact picture.  She hurriedly answers.

 

“Alex!”

 

Her sister sighs in a way only a sister would, not even bothering with a hello.  “Where are you?”

 

Kara feels the crinkle in her brow form (and not the first crinkle of the morning),  “Huh?”

 

“You were supposed to come eat breakfast with us, remember?”

 

Kara doesn’t remember.  She must have forgotten.  “Oh, shoot!  Um, I’ll be right over, okay?”

 

There’s a bit of a commotion in the background as Alex speaks.  “Hurry up!  Kelly won’t start without you.”

 

They end the call and Kara gets dressed at superspeed, not bothering to do a hairstyle - it’s just breakfast with her sister and her sister’s girlfriend, anyway.  She whips her glasses off so her suit will materialize, and then she’s soaring to Alex’s apartment.




Huh.

 

Kara hovers across from Alex’s floor of her apartment building.  One of the windows to the apartment is open.  Someone is sitting there, reading a book and sipping from a mug.  Someone that isn’t Alex.  Or Kelly.

 

She uses her x-ray vision to see through the walls.  The apartment looks nothing like it did before.  It’s decorated differently, the furniture isn’t the same, and the food she can see in the pantry isn’t anything that she knows Alex likes.

 

Fear grips her suddenly, anxious thoughts filling her mind.  Is Alex alright?  Why isn’t she here?  Other thoughts battle those.  Of course she’s fine, you talked two her two minutes ago.  She would have told you if something was wrong.

 

Shaking all that away, she closes her eyes.  The sounds of the city and beyond its limits fill her ears.  Cats meow, people laugh, cars honk, and waves in the harbor spray mist upon the rocks.  It doesn’t take long for her to find her sister's voice.  Opening her eyes, she takes off.




The house she descends in front of is beautiful.  Not too showy, but comfortable.  It reminds her of their old house in Midvale.  It’s almost the same shade of gray, with white shutters and plenty of windows (bulletproof glass, no doubt).  Alex busts through the front door as Kara’s suit dematerializes.  She either doesn’t notice or is too distracted to comment on the disastrously confused expression on Kara’s face, the questions that are flashing through Kara’s mind and coming out of her mouth in the form of jumbled syllables.

 

“Wh - how - house?”

 

Alex grabs her wrist and pulls her to the porch.  “Hurry up!  I’m hungry!”

 

She doesn’t have time to fully take in the interior of the house.  Someone slams into her, wrapping their arms around her middle with impressive strength.  It takes a second for her to realize the person is small , that the person is a child.

 

Brown eyes look up at her.  The same ones from the third picture on her end table.

 

“Aunt Kara!” she exclaims before releasing her grip on the Kryptonian.  “You’re here!”

 

Aunt...Aunt Kara…

 

More jumbled syllables fall from her lips.

 

Alex picks the girl up, who gives a delighted squeal.  “Yeah, now we can finally eat!”

 

“Mommyyyyyy!” The girl sounds so happy.

 

“Mom…” Kara mutters, “Mommy…”

 

Kelly shows up then, linking her arm with Kara’s as she guides her to the dining room.

 

“Now you know,” her voice is stern, but playful, “that Kara is more important than breakfast.”

 

Alex doesn’t try to be quiet as she sits the girl at the table.  “Barely.”

 

Kara doesn’t hear her anyway.  She’s spotted more pictures, more pictures that make no sense and that she has no memory of.  Alex and Kelly at a wedding, a wedding that looks suspiciously like it’s their own.  Kara and the little girl at Christmas time.  Kara, Lena, Alex, and Kelly at the wedding.  Kara’s wearing a blue dress she doesn’t remember owning.  Lena is in red.

 

She almost jolts when she feels Alex’s hands on her shoulders, guiding her back to the dining room.

 

“Sit.  So we can eat.”

 

“Someone’s testy today.” Kelly says as Kara is put into the chair beside the little girl who Kara is slowly coming to the conclusion is Kelly and Alex’s daughter .  A daughter that didn’t exist last night.  Who lives in a house that didn’t exist last night.  With parents who weren’t even married last night.

 

“I’m hungry!” Alex defends herself as she helps her wife(?) place food on the table.  “Kara understands.  She’s about the hungriest person in the universe.  Need I remind you that she threatened to melt my face if I ate the last potsticker?”

 

The familiar sound of J’onn touching down from flight distracts Kara.  She knows he’s there before the rest of them do, of course, and when he walks into the room the girl is on her feet in an instant.

 

“Grandpa J’onn!”

 

Kara stares in a daze as J’onn picks her up and swings her around like she’s a spaceship, or a plane, or a Martian for that matter.

 

“To Kara’s credit,” Alex says, “she isn’t the only one who’s late.”

 

J’onn laughs as he lands the girl back on the ground, feet first, thankfully.  She runs back to her spot beside Kara.

 

“I’m sorry.  M’Gann and I were working on a lead for a missing alien.”

 

“How is it that it’s the superheroes with powers that are always late?” Kelly asks, placing the last plate of food on the table.

 

“The tortoise and the hair!” The girl adds.

 

“Perfect analogy, Marci.” Alex coos.

 

Marci…

 

It takes her a second before it hits her.  James and Kelly’s father’s name was Marcus.

 

Everyone’s seated now, filling their own plates up, looking so happy and content that Kara doesn’t have the heart to break the moment with all her questions.  Her movements feel like she’s on autopilot, grabbing biscuits and bacon and hashbrowns and taking a gigantic gulp of orange juice.

 

She’s on another Earth.  But there is no other Earth.  She’s...she’s...in a reality conjured by the Black Mercy.  But there was only one Black Mercy, and it died.  Are there more on Earth?  She’s dreaming.  She’s in...the future?  She’s…….completely and utterly lost.

 

A little hand takes hold of her own.

 

“Are you okay, Aunt Kara?” Marci’s sweet voice asks.

 

She feels everyone’s eyes on her now.  Looking up, she sees the concerned look on all their faces.  All traces of the light atmosphere start to wither as they realize that something is wrong.

 

“Kara?” Alex asks, voice soft.  “Are you alright?”

 

“Oh.” Kara says, looking at each of them.  She shakes her head free of the thoughts threatening to drive her mad, if something else already hadn’t.  “Yes.  Yeah, I’m alright.  I just…”  Marci’s eyes are so adorable, so caring.  “I just need to get a taste of these breakfast potstickers!  Did you make these?”

 

Marci giggles like Kara is the most ridiculous, entertaining person in the world.

 

After breakfast, when Marci is settled on the couch watching a documentary (Alex, really?), the three other adults ask after Kara to make sure she’s okay.

 

She’s not okay.  She’s very not okay.  But she assures them otherwise, that she’s just a little out of it today.  She’s not sure if she fully convinces them.

 

She finds herself back at her loft, cape spread out under her and her booted feet propped on the arm of the couch.  She’s anxious.  Dumbfounded, that’s for sure.  She flops face first onto her bed.  When she lifts her head and notices the pink curtains, she’s quick to go to the kitchen, but her thoughts follow her everywhere.  It’s when she’s lying on the floor, arms and legs spread out like she’s mid snowangel, that she looks back at that end table.  And then she’s crawling again, lifts herself on her knees in front of the frames, grabbing the picture of her and Lena.

 

Lena.  Her genius, wonderful, forgiving best friend.  Maybe she can shed some light on the situation.

 

She makes it to L-Corp in record time.  But isn’t it LuthorCorp now?  And didn’t Lena quit?  But no.  The sound of Lena’s heartbeat led her here, hovering in the sky and looking down at the balcony she’s landed on so many times.  She can’t see Lena at her desk, but she can hear her voice.  She’s so focused on her voice that she doesn’t hear the others.  When she lands and walks into the big office, she sees Lena talking to two other people.  A man and a woman, dressed professionally, who notice her first.  Their eyes widen.

 

Lena turns around then, confused but happy to see her, it seems, which is such a stretch from what they’d turned into that it makes Kara’s heart melt.  Things really are getting better between them again.

 

“Supergirl.” She says.  “To what do I owe this surprise?”

 

It takes a lot of strength for Kara to hold her shoulders back in that classic Supergirl pose, to not let clumsy words fall out of her mouth like she so wants to.  She clears her throat.

 

“When you are done, Miss Luthor, I’d like to speak to you.”

 

Kara tries to convey her emotions subtly through her eyes, though not too strongly.  She doesn’t want to worry her.

 

Lena looks a bit concerned nonetheless.  “Of course. Miss Barlow, Mr. Madden, and I were just finishing up.”

 

Kara nods, allowing them to say their goodbyes, and then Lena shuts the door.

 

“Kara,” she says, not too loudly, in case the guests had lingered, “is everything alright?”

 

She doesn’t know where to begin.  It’s like her mind is a tornado of questions and theories and she just wants it to stop.  Lena’s hand is on Kara’s then, grabbing hold tenderly.  Kara looks into her green eyes, then, so full of care and compassion and something else she can’t decipher, and it all does stop then.  The thoughts and the questions.  They blow away like the morning fog.

 

In that moment, to her surprise, everything does feel alright.

 

But the moment doesn’t last, not completely, as the memories of this confusing day play in the back of her mind, present enough to let her know they’re there.

 

“I just…” Kara sighs, still looking into those eyes, those eyes still looking into her own, “...feel strange.”

 

Lena leads her to the couch and when they sit, she’s still holding her hand, but now her other hand is resting on top.

 

“You fought a very powerful alien last night,” she says, “It’s okay if you feel a little off.  Even the Girl of Steel has her moments.”  Her last words are said with a smile.

 

A very powerful alien.  One she doesn’t remember.

 

She doesn’t feel like talking about any of that, suddenly.  Doesn’t want to burden Lena with her problems, or remind herself of them.  She just wants to sleep, wanting to curl up with her head in Lena’s lap and drift away for a little while.

 

“Hey,” Lena says, and her hand cups Kara’s face.  She’s never done that before, but it’s not unwelcomed.  “Don’t be afraid to feel what you feel.”

 

Kara stares at her, mouth moving to form words but instead just looking like a fish plopping its mouth open and shut.  She presses her lips together.  Nods.

 

“How about movie night at my place tonight?” Lena asks.  This brightens Kara's mood instantly.  She nods her head more fervently, eliciting a laugh from Lena.  “Perfect.”

 

Supergirl .

 

It’s J’onn’s voice in her earpiece, which she technically doesn’t need, though it does make the job easier.

 

“J’onn?” she asks, fingers pressed to her ear.

 

“Your mother is calling.” he says.

 

“Got it.”

 

She hears J’onn disconnect.

 

“My mom’s calling me from Argo.” Kara clarifies.

 

“Tell her I said hello.” Lena says.

 

“I will.” Kara gets up from the couch and Lena follows.  They hug goodbye, and Kara cannot even begin to describe how much she missed this feeling.  Lena’s nuzzled comfortably against Kara, her hold tighter than she ever remembers it being.  She must have missed this just as much as Kara has.  It’s a notion that sends a swell of emotion through her, melancholy and happiness all in one at what they’d lost and what they’ve started to gain back.

 

Lena’s hands slide down to Kara’s hands, giving them a squeeze that Kara returns.

 

“You’re not cancelling a hot date to watch movies with me, right?” Kara’s joking, but she doesn’t want to get in the way of any plans Lena’s made just because she’s having an...indescribable day.  A day that seems a little less so in the presence of the best friend she’s missed so much.

 

This world she’s woken to isn’t so bad.  She can wait to find answers tomorrow.

 

“Well, we were going to get dinner tonight, but I think pizza, potstickers, and Disney movies are way better, right?”

 

They were going to get dinner!  Just like they’d done so many times before things went wrong.  Yes, the answers can wait until tomorrow.

 

Kara nods.  “Right.”

 

She’s forgotten that they’re still holding hands, but Lena seems unbothered.  Then she remembers her mother.

 

“Guess I gotta go.” Kara says.

 

One more squeeze from Lena and her hands are let go.  She instantly misses the contact.  It’s been so long since things were this good.

 

Kara walks to the balcony doors, then stops as a thought occurs to her.  Turning around, she points to herself.  “I was the hot date, then, huh?”

 

Lena rolls her eyes.  Is Lena blushing?  She’s not blushing.  She’s definitely blushing.

 

“The only hot thing about you, Kara Danvers, is your heat-vision.”

 

Kara’s mouth drops, followed by a playful scoff, and maybe a little bit of stuttering.

 

“Now, that is rude.”  Kara points her finger at her friend, a super cheesy smile plastered across her face.  “I am...I am dashing!”

 

Lena’s smile, to be fair, is just as cheesy.  “And humble too.”

 

Kara takes a step forward to argue some more, but Lena shoos her off.

 

“Go talk to your mom!”

 

“Bossy!” Kara exclaims, takes one last look at Lena, who looks extremely amused, and shoots into the sky.

 

The interaction had turned into a good enough distraction that she had forgotten how everything was turning upside down, that is, until she spoke with her mother.

 

It had started normal, the hologram broadcasting from Argo showed her mother looking as beautiful as ever, so happy to speak to her daughter.  The happiness was mutual.  She’d even forgotten to mention all the strange happenings she’d experienced.  And then her father stepped into frame, greeting her with a pleasant Hello, my daughter, and How are things on Earth?

 

“Father,” Kara is taken aback, not for the first time, “but...but...you were just on Earth.”

 

“Yes,” Zor-El says, eyes scanning her like they used to when she’d get up to mischief as a child, “a month ago.  To visit.  But I couldn’t leave your Mother for too long.  She’s hopelessly in love with me.”

 

Alura rolls her eyes, smiles as she pushes Zor-El against the shoulder.

 

Since when did her father move to Argo?  He’d barely adjusted to life on Earth!

 

Answers.  Tomorrow.  Not now.

 

They bid goodbye, not without asking her if she’s okay, and it’s a question she very well should be asking herself at this point.  Her father even asks how Lena’s doing, which seems strange.  They haven’t gotten a chance to know each other very well.

 

Answers tomorrow.




Kara zooms through her bedroom window, changing into her civilian clothes in an instant.  She should have noticed even before landing in her room that someone else was in her apartment.  When she’s in her living room he sees him, lounging on the couch.

 

“You!” Kara gasps.

 

The Music Meister looks way too comfortable in a home he’s just broken into.

 

“Me.”

 

“I knew it was you!”  Her pointed finger is accusatory.

 

He shakes his head.  “No you didn’t.”

 

“I-” Kara stops, huffs, crosses her arms.  He smiles at her.  “Don’t smile at me!  You almost had me killed in that musical...world!”

 

“But you lived.” He stands up, roams around the room, touching this and that.  He points at the pictures that started this whole day.  “Cute.”

 

“What did you do to me this time?”

 

“Remember that I helped you last time.  Helped you to forgive that guy you were dating.  That was a dark time - probably shouldn’t have enabled that relationship.  Anyway!”  He spreads his arms like someone asking for a hug.  “I am here to help again!  With what, you ask?  Let me tell you.”

 

Kara stares at him.

 

“Instead of a musical,” he starts to explain, “you’re in, well, sort of an ideal world.  You’ve gotten everything the way you want it, well, within reason.”

 

Kara feels angry, or, angrier.  “And who are you to tell me what I want?”

 

He laughs loudly, gesturing around them.  “This is your brain, honey!  Not mine.”

 

Kara huffs.  He isn’t deterred.

 

“But I do have eyes.  And a brain of my own.  And I know I shouldn’t meddle in other people’s lives, but it’s what I do .  And there’s something out there in the real world that you want, and it’s manifested itself here.  Which has totally proved me right, by the way.  It’s your job to figure out what that is.  Then, dear Kara Zor-El, you can wake up.  Oh, and Mxy says hello.  This was his idea, anyway.”

 

She turns away, rubs at her temples, then turns back to yell something.  But he’s gone.  Of course he’s gone.




Things she’s found out today: she has new curtains, Alex and Kelly are married with a child, her parents are living happily on Argo and visit frequently, and she’s the head of the Journalism department at CatCo.  In this world, Lena owns CatCo.  Andrea is no longer the boss.  Brainy and Nia are still happily together.  Olivia Marsdin is the President.  Kara is still Supergirl.

 

James is still running the newspaper in Calvintown, mentoring kids, and he’s brought the business up to be so much more of a success than it was before.  He visits often.

 

Even Winn stops by from the future, she’s learned.

 

Answers tomorrow have turned into answers today.

 

But she still doesn’t have every answer,

 

She’s learned all these things, things she already knew she wanted, so what’s the hold up?  Why isn’t she back in her real world?  What is it that she wants ?

 

What she does want, she thinks, as she’s preparing a bag of snacks, is to forget about all this for a while, to go and spend time with her best friend like they used to.  

 

But not exactly as before.

 

Now, Lena has the knowledge of Kara’s identity, and she’s not angry anymore!  In this world and in the real one.  Kara takes a deep breath, feeling extremely grateful despite the circumstances.

 

It could be much worse.  The Music Meister didn’t give her any reason to think that she could be harmed in this world.  In the last one, he’d told her and Barry point blank that if they died there, it wouldn’t end well for them.  She has her powers here, so at least she can’t get shot again.

 

And Mxy’s a friend now.  If he had anything to do with this, it shouldn’t be so bad.

 

With a positive attitude, she heads to Lena’s apartment.




She actually goes into Lena’s building and up to her room the normal way.  A bag of food in one hand and a board game in the other, her strength and inability to get winded gives her no reason to complain about holding so much.  And the elevator ride up to the top floor is strangely soothing.

 

She kicks her foot against Lena’s door a couple times.

 

When the door opens, it nearly takes her breath away how beautiful she is.

 

Of course she knows Lena is beautiful.  Everyone knows that.  It’s not rare that Kara has marveled at her friend’s looks, or thought about how gorgeous the woman in front of her is.  What was it the Music Meister said?  Oh!  She has eyes .

 

And what her eyes are seeing is Lena with her hair down, wavy and tinted lighter brown in the light of the hall.  She’s wearing a long sleeve purple shirt that’s a little too long on the arms.  The fabric covers most of her hands.  It’s adorable.  So are her sweatpants.  Kara loves to see her dressed so casually.

 

She’s shaken from her thoughts when Lena speaks.

 

“Did you lose your key?”

 

She has a key?

 

Forming words is just not her speciality today, but Lena doesn’t mind.  She touches Kara’s arm, guides her in, and takes the board game into her own hands as she speaks.

 

“Don’t worry about it.  There are plenty of spares.  This won’t be the last time you lose your key.”  She smiles with that playful look in her eyes that means she’s going to poke fun at her.  “Or the first.”

 

Kara takes off her glasses and laughs, putting the bag of food on the counter.  If Lena notices how strained it is, she doesn’t comment.

 

“Well, you know me,” Kara chuckles a little too loudly, “always forgetting things.”

 

She notices the box of pizza and container of potstickers on the counter.  She’d forgotten Lena mentioned them, making her words true.

 

“OOOOH!!” Kara rubs her hands together and makes a beeline for the food.  “The best meal in the galaxy!  If only we had-”

 

Lena holds up a finger.  “Wait for it.”

 

She goes to the fridge and shows Kara - 

 

“CHOCOLATE PECAN PIE?!” Kara braces herself on the counter, jumping up and down like a child.  It only makes her happier when she hears Lena’s delighted laughter.  

 

Kara’s a little taller now, she realizes, and Lena grabs her and pulls her to the floor.  She’d been flying.

 

“Lena, you’re the best person in the universe.”

 

Kara wastes no time in opening the box.  Manners disregarded, she gets a fork and digs in.

 

“Second best.” Lena says, settling against the narrower side of the counter.

 

Kara spares a look at her, forkful of pie in her mouth.  Lena’s looking at her with such adoration that Kara has to look away, cheeks filled with smiles and pie and tinted an embarrassing shade of red.

 

She knows this is something she wants.  For Lena to look at her the way she used to.

 

And even if she knows this isn’t the real Lena, she can’t find it in herself to care.  She’s a version of Lena, and Kara will feel the same about any version of Lena Luthor.

 

“Even though I eat dessert before dinner?” Kara asks.

 

“Oh,” Lena says, the playfulness back in full force, “I’m sorry, darling, did I say it was you?”

 

“Lena!” she practically whines.  Two times in one day.  Kara bets that if someone touched her face right now, the heat would melt them.  Any version of Lena likes to embarrass her.  And any version of Kara lets her.

 

Lena is invulnerable to heat, so it seems, because she places the back of her hand against Kara’s face.  Lena gasps.  “So your heat vision isn’t the only hot thing about you.  My mistake.”

 

Kara buries her face in her hands.  She hears Lena walk to the opposite side of the counter and dares peek at her through her fingers.  She’s leaning against the counter, arms crossed on the granite, a content smile on her face that slowly turns into something akin to worry.

 

“So, are you feeling better?” she asks.

 

Kara removes her hands from her face fully, plays with the sleeve of her shirt.  She is feeling better.  But she can’t stay in this world forever.  And she has to figure out what she wants.  And what if nothing will feel like this again?  What if in the real world, her and Lena will never get to where they are here?  Will there always be something strained and painful hiding beneath the surface of their relationship?

 

Paragon of hope, she remembers.

 

“Yeah,” Kara says, head dipped low, “yeah I am.”

 

She looks like she’s analyzing her, scientist that she is. “I’m not one hundred percent convinced.” She says.  “Which is why we need Rapunzel and Flynn Ryder on that TV as soon as possible.  Get the food.”  She laughs when Kara visibly gets happier at the reminder of snacks.

 

Kara isn’t bothered at all when they’ve settled on the couch under a blanket and Lena sits right against her.  She isn’t bothered at all when Lena lays her head on her shoulder.  Kara even puts her arm around Lena, holding her close as Flynn Ryder climbs up the tower.  It doesn’t come as a surprise that Kara wants Lena so close after being so far away for so long.  They’ve never huddled together like this before, but Kara wishes to take this practice into the real world.

 

Lena starts playing with Kara’s hand absentmindedly.  The feeling of it should make Kara sleepy, being touched like this, but Kara’s senses go on high alert, even higher than usual for her.  She feels Lena’s hands on her own down to every minute detail.

 

Her heart begins to flutter.

 

Strange.

 

Lena intertwines their fingers.  Brings Kara’s hand to her lips.  Nuzzles deeper into Kara’s neck.

 

Flutter.
