Autumn. The beginning of the end. If she were cynical, she’d read somewhat into that, why their people insisted on having weddings as the night-less days turned into eternal nights, but she knew it had more to do with the harvest than anything else.

Their people across the Holds and Clans had worked tirelessly over the spring and summer to bring in the harvests and prepare their people for winter. Alex had joked that in marrying before winter, the newlyweds would either spend the winter learning the disgusting habits their spouse had and learning to deal with them, or…. Followed by a waggle of her eyebrows.

Kara didn’t want to think about it, or she really wanted to think about it, considering her wedding was hours away and her wife was… was… perhaps the most amazing woman the world had ever seen.

It had been a long year, following an even longer decade, and the people had laboured long and hard to store their supplies for the winter. Wagons from Thorul had been spread across the country, barrels and boxes baring the Luthor motif of a bear being delivered to people who scorned the sight of it yet couldn’t turn from it.

The Trickster god Loki’s work if Kara had ever seen it. The Luthor Clan, of Hold Thorul, were the most powerful family in the country. They had wealth, property, respect, and slaves, and had held Kingship over them all for longer than she’d been alive. In fact, she’d grown up with that motif hanging over head, even with the lands between them.

With the death of the King of Jarls, Lionel Luthor, Jor El, Jarl of Krypton had been appointed the new King and Kara had been relieved. With an El as the King of Jarls, she didn’t have to worry, even her parents had been quietly relieved.

Lionel hadn’t been a cruel man, confident and powerful, but not cruel. Still, he had demanded a tribute that many of the other Jarls had scorned, though reluctantly agreed when he and his allies had threatened war. It had been a subtle threat, but a threat all the same. As the wisest and the strongest, appointed by his fellow Jarls, it was his duty to oversee the other Jarls with their disagreements and judicial issues, and it was only fair that his Hold receive fair compensation for his time.

With supporters from other clans and other Holds, Luthor held court, and charged his fees. Some Jarl’s had rejoiced at his death; a sickness had taken root in his chest and slain him after a long winter.

Her own uncle, Jarl Jor El of Argo Hold, had narrowly beaten out Lionel’s young son, Lex for Kingship to become the King of Jarls.

And then the King’s party had been ambushed miles from Thorul, and Argo’s settlement had burnt to the ground, with everything living within its borders slain.

She’d been alone then, bar her cousin, and Kal hadn’t wanted her. Instead, she’d found home in Midvale, with the Danvers. A decade of training later and she’d been one of the only Shieldmaidens in the Hold, with her sister Alex.

Then, of course, Lex Luthor had raised tribute until the other Holds were crippled and the Jarls were agitated and murmuring about treason. When his raids became too much, when Kara was a victim of the senseless violence and thought dead, Alex had enough. It had been her sister’s passion and rage that had rallied the Jarls and Holds to war, and come Spring, armies had gathered like storms on the horizon, marching on Thorul under the banner ‘El Alliance.’

King Lex had to die, him and all his kin which was only his younger sister, Lena. Only by the time their armies had marched to Thorul, Lex was already dead, Kara had been returned to them, and his sister had taken control of the Clan and Hold.

The war had ended, rather anticlimactically, and the Jarls, still angry and wanting retribution, had demanded some recompense from Thorul for what Lionel and then Lex had taken from them. A union, a vow before Odin and Freyja ensuring that Thorul, and the Luthor Clan, never saw fit to raise their hand against the other Holds again.

Years of tribute coupled with an already impressive annual harvest and access to a bountiful iron mine meant that even if Lena Luthor, Jarl of Thorul, was willing to maintain her peace, to repay the unjust tributes sent to her, she was still a threat. Combined with the fact that she had proclaimed herself Queen of Jarls, like her brother and father before her, had made the other Jarls nervous and angry.

Together the Jarls and Holds had barely been able to scrape the men and resources required for a war with Thorul, and she could field the losses easily, while they had all faced starvation come winter if the war had gone poorly for them, or if it lasted more than a few weeks. Lena had the resources and wealth to continue her father and brother's reign even though she had agreed to the peace, so she had to be kept in check.

Lena’s terms had been quite simple. She was now the leader of Thorul, and the Luthor Clan and she would accept the duty of Queen of Jarls over the others and would ensure the crimes her brother had committed would be made public and compensated for. She wasn’t interested in carrying on her brother’s work, but she would defend herself if she needed to.

To summarise: Lena was in control of the biggest army in the Holds, one capable of taking on the army of all the Holds combined, and she was willing to pay compensation and reveal the true nature of her brothers’ crimes to all, but she would do so as the Queen of Jarls, the highest power in the land.

The other Jarls hadn’t had much choice, her army was equal to their own, and even if they did defeat her in combat, all Thorul had to do was shelter behind their walls and wait. The men taken from the other Holds had been all that could be spared with the harvest coming in, and they’d starve before the Thorulian’s did.

Still, the Jarls were angry and frustrated. There was no guarantee that Jarl Lena would honour her words, there needed to be something more binding. Someone needed to be close at hand to keep an eye on her and Thorul.

As Lena refused to abdicate from leadership of her clan, to allow someone else control of Thorul, either within her own Hold or from another Hold, then she had to be monitored another way. Marriage had been decided.

Lena Luthor, Jarl of Thorul, was to wed someone the ‘El Alliance’ deemed suitable. They hadn’t wished to be cruel, choosing someone so old that they’d have nothing in common, nor had they wanted a strong leader who could take Thorul’s resources and name themselves as Jarl of Jarls, call himself King. No, they had needed someone relatively young, unmarried, who would be kind to Lena and her people, and who could be trusted not to turn on the other Jarls, and someone Lena would agree to marry.

Kara, when the names thrown forward were all belonging to men old enough to be Lena’s father, or her age but with no qualities worthy of the woman, had offered herself instead.

She had no bride price or dowry, and she couldn’t give Lena an heir, but she could vow to be faithful, to care for Lena’s clan like her own, to treat her fairly and with kindness, and the other Jarl’s knew she wouldn’t turn out like Lex.

Lena, Jarl of Thorul, had agreed, on one condition; she would be crowned officially, on the same day that she and Kara were to be wed. The El Alliance, faced with a war, had reluctantly agreed.

Armies had dispersed back to their Holds in relief and their people had returned to the land, seeing to their livestock and the harvest, waiting for the end of Autumn when the Queen would be crowned and the daughter El, Shieldmaiden Wolf, Jarl of Argo would reign next to her.

Alex tried to talk her out of it, of course, convinced the conniving Luthor was up to something with one breath and talking about how her baby sister was to be married on the other, and with that came teasing. Oh, the teasing.

Kara had hoped that, on the day of her wedding, her sister might let up on the teasing, but the smirk on Alex’s face as she ducked into the tent that Kara had called her own for a few days was telling.

“Enjoying your last day of freedom?”

Alex had been making many comments along those lines. She wasn’t so open about her disapproval of the match, not like others, but she made many snide remarks about the wedding and Kara’s betrothed. And then she’d tease Kara about her upcoming wedding. It was as though she were two people. Alex; the protective sister, and Alex; the sister who was about to become an in-law.

That Lena had been the one to save Kara’s life and nurse her back to health had gone a long way to getting her sister on Lena’s team back when Kara had stumbled back to Midvale and to war preparations. She had been wounded in battle, suspected dead, and had then been dragged away by a healer from Thorul. She hadn’t been well enough to leave before winter had set in and travel between the Hold’s was impossible, so she had to stay where she was.

It had allowed her to heal, but also gave her time to really get to know the woman who had saved her. Alex was impressed and grateful for Lena, until she learnt that Airmed, the healer who was sending supplies to various Hold’s attacked by Lex’s men, was Lex’s little sister Lena. Kara had faced the same conundrum while stuck in Thorul, being faced with a supposed monster from children’s tales, a Luthor in the flesh, and having her be the same beautiful and wonderful woman who had saved her and was nursing her back to health.

In the end Kara had thrown aside all preconceived ideas of who Lena Luthor was to get to know the woman, and she hadn’t been disappointed. It had been that belief that had driven her to offer her own hand to the young woman.

It had been a whirlwind since then. The details had been discussed between Jarl Danvers and Jarl Lena, and then the El Alliance had withdrawn to their Hold’s to prepare for the winter. Wagons carrying hundreds of supplies had departed Thorul for the other Holds, returning supplies that had been unfairly taken as tribute. And then, once the harvest was in and the Holds were almost ready for the winter, the Jarls and their families, and anyone else of importance, had departed for Thorul.

Jarl Lena was crowned this morning, and the afternoon would host a wedding, binding the clan of Luthor, and El and Danvers together and ensuring peace.

And now Alex was in her temporary home while she prepared for her wedding, likely she was here to offer her assistance.

Alex picked up the leather jacket that the leatherworkers from Midvale had painfully hand crafted. It was a delicate piece that would be laced at her back like a corset, but was thicker, closer to a leather jerkin than a shirt, and had elaborate designs sewn into it.

Kara held her arms out, aware of the maroon colours that would soon be her own.

Tradition dictated that the bride, if she were to be marrying into another family, would take on the colours of her new family, the same would be said for anyone marrying into a clan or family above their own.

As a member of El Clan, though lacking the prosperity they’d had over a decade ago, Kara couldn’t marry much higher, especially as she was an El herself. If she married, she’d be marrying into a lower household, unless she married into another Jarl’s clan, which had been decided for her when she was a girl. Her spouse-to-be wasn’t determined before Argo had burnt, but she knew that it would be her duty to wed, and carry children of, a member of a Jarl's family.

Until Ragnarök had descended on her life. Then marriage had been far from her mind.

Thorul had always been a prominent Hold, and the Luthor Clan was one of the wealthiest beyond the power of their Hold. Kara was marrying above her station. She was a Shieldmaiden, her adopted family had some wealth but they weren’t on the same tier as the Luthor’s, and she had no wealth or power herself, though she had some success in battle when necessary. The only thing Thorul and Jarl Lena Luthor gained from this union was the trust that Kara had gained.

Kara was known to have a stout heart and fierce moral compass; she would not allow her wife to continue the practices her brother and father had. Kara would turn on her wife if necessary, break her oaths to the gods if it meant protecting people- she would keep Luthor in line, while having enough power to protect herself from anything Lena might have thrown at her.

Her wedding wasn’t one that would conform to traditions, in fact, Kara wasn’t even sure what to expect. It wasn’t every day two Jarls married, let alone to women married. To say that her and Lena’s union was something completely new, was an understatement.  

Theirs wouldn’t be the first marriage to end a feud, but it was the first involving such high stakes…. The first marriage to end a war. As such, many of the expected ceremonies had been… cast aside. Lena Luthor had her own ideas for her marriage, conveyed by a very fierce woman who had almost vanished when surrounded by the Housecarls escorting her.

Jarl Jeremiah Danvers of the Danvers Clan of Midvale hadn’t been able to protest, though Eliza, his wife, had stormed into the Thorul longhouse and demanded to speak with the Queen on her arrival, and hadn’t left for several hours. When Eliza finally left, it was with the satisfied smile of a woman who had got what she had wanted and knew something that others didn’t.

For the wedding ceremony, they’d begin at Odin’s Peak, a flat point on a cliff on the other end of the bay that Thorul’s main settlement rested. It was a considerable walk across the cove, and through the sprawling buildings that made up the settlement and eventually you’d pass the wooden palisades that guarded the settlement.

They hadn’t been able to host all the guests within the settlement, even with the massive longhouse that almost dominated the buildings, so temporary accommodation had been set up on the outside of walls.

Most of the guests would be housed there, for two reasons. The first being, that Thorul didn’t have the accommodation for hundreds of people, and the second that there weren’t many people who were willing to enter the settlement and sleep under the same roof as the bears. Kara thought it was nonsense, and Alex had, quiet loudly, proclaimed she wanted to see their legendary hospitality up close. She was probably going to sneak or bribe her way into the private storage of the Jarl and try to drown herself in Luthor ale.

“How are you feeling?” Alex asked as Kara pulled her hair out of the way so that she could lace the jacket at her back.

It fit tightly, custom tailored to her form over the summer months while she’d been out working in the Holds. She waited patiently for Alex to finish with the cords before rolling her shoulders a few times and swinging her arms, stretching the maroon shirt out beneath the jerkin.

When she was settled enough, the fabric not gathering under the leather, she nodded to Alex.

“I’m… not really sure,” she said after a while as Alex moved around her.

The second piece went around her torso, beneath her breasts. It wasn’t a design she would have chosen and was certainly more delicate and pretty than what she was used to, but it was designed for ceremony, not protection.

Tailors typically didn’t design wedding armour for women, and she was certain her nuptials had caused a stir, but she was mostly out of earshot, and if she or Alex was in earshot, they were also within physical reach… so they’d be apologising soon enough. Alex might voice her own doubts to Kara, and to her father, but she’d defend her sister's honour like the fall of leaves in Autumn.

“I never thought I’d get married,” Kara offered as she helped hold the corset in place while Alex tied it in place. It fit her perfectly, just as the jerkin. It was made of firmer leather than the jerkin, almost like a belt across her torso with runes carved into it. Her movement wasn’t even limited by its presence and she stretched out a few times to test it. If only her battle armour was as light, pretty, and as useless.

“Hail,” a voice from outside caught their attention and Alex strode over to the tent to open it.

Kara, and the rest of the guests who had opted to not stay within the Thorul Longhouse, had camped outside the settlement in tents and hastily erected shelters. Kara’s had a pile of furs for her bed and a small fire pit for warmth. There was a small table and two chairs, as well as a larger table and a few repurposed barrels for storage or work benches. The tent was lent to her personally by Lena, and was bigger than most, and her betrothed had also provided her with anything she might need while she lived outside the Longhouse.

Most of their communication had been done by Lena’s thrall, Jess, a mighty fierce woman who was usually accompanied by a big red-headed man whose mother had hailed from across the sea. He was a slave as well, one of Lena’s guards. He didn’t have the blood to be a Housecarl, but he was a fierce fighter and very loyal. He had been the one to carry Kara’s belongings to the tent, by himself, carting the barrels over his shoulder with an ease that made Kara wish to offer sacrifices to Thor.

A familiar face appeared in the gap in the tent and Jess poked her head in and ducked her eyes submissively.

“Queen Lena requested I give this to you,” she said and lifted the swath of fabric she had draped over her arms in Kara’s direction. She was a very efficient woman, always speaking highly of Lena, and she had been initially distrustful of Kara but appeared to be warming to her presence. Kara hoped so. Life as Lena’s wife would be hard enough without Lena’s confidant hating her.

“We can afford to outfit our own,” Alex frowned, with perhaps a bit more attitude than necessary, arms folding defensively.

While Midvale had wealth, it didn’t have the wealth of Thorul, no one did, and Lionel and then Lex had liked to flaunt it. It was a sore spot for many of the clans. Alex especially hadn’t liked it, hadn’t liked how the Luthor’s flaunted they had money and power instead of taking care of their people.

“She would be pleased if you would honour her by wearing it,” Jess continued, almost bowed over the gift as she offered it in Kara’s direction and ignoring Alex, who bristled at the disrespect.

“We don’t need Luthor’s char- “

“Alex,” Kara interrupted gently. “Please.”

Alex huffed but unfolded her arms, snatching the fabric gift from Lena’s slave and gesturing for her to leave.

Jess ducked her head in Kara’s direction, a bow far deeper than the one she gave to Alex and vanished.

“You don’t have to wear it,” Alex said and threw it on an upturned barrel while Kara took a seat. “Mum spent weeks on your wedding cloak.”

Kara lowered her eyes but didn’t comment.

Alex returned to prepping her for the ceremony.

Kara had already bathed and dried her hair next to the fire, and Eliza had brushed it out until it fell around her shoulders in golden waves that surely equalled Thor’s. Some of her fondest memories of her mother had been Alura brushing out her hair until it shone like spun gold, and weaving flowers into it.

Alex’s fingers were gentle but firm as they combed through her hair and braided it back from her face.

It had been a tradition since they were girls, the two of them had combed and braided each other’s hair, and Kara relaxed into the familiar motion.

“I’m gonna miss you,” Alex said after a long while as she deftly wove strands of Kara’s hair around.

“You too,” Kara replied instantly, and she’d miss her sister as though she had lost parts of herself. She and Alex had been inseparable for years and the longest they’d been apart had been the winter when Alex had thought Kara had been killed in battle.

“I’ll write,” Kara promised quietly, and Alex huffed but there was fondness to the way she tugged on Kara’s hair.

“No, you won’t.”

And so maybe writing wasn’t Kara’s supreme skill. She knew her runes and could read and write as was expected of an El and Danvers, but… her communication wasn’t always the best. Alex usually took care of that side of things. Kara would just… forget until someone came and banged her on her head with parchment or doused her in ink.

“I’ll come visit,” she promised instead, twisting around to look at Alex.

“If your wife lets you,” Alex said, and there was bitterness to her tone which had Kara turning in the chair completely.

“Alex.”

“I’m just saying,” Alex muttered, a mutinous tone to her voice as she stepped away from Kara and lowered the braid tie.

“How do you know she’ll even let you out? You won’t have any authority here!”

It was a familiar worry of her friends and family. Though she was a Shieldmaiden and would be able to defeat her wife if it came to a fight, in this regard she was technically the wife. She was marrying the highest person in the land, the Queen, and so would fall under her wife’s authority. What the Queen wanted, she would get, and Kara would have no power here in Thorul. She could bring slaves with her, but Lena had told her, through Jess, that all of Kara’s needs would be seen to by Thorul. She would be their Lady and would be taken care of.

Of course, Alex had read that as a threat, as a way of controlling her sister, and had begged Jeremiah to reconsider the marriage. Kara would be alone in the bear’s den, and Alex was scared for her.

“I’ll be fine, Alex,” Kara tried to reassure her sister, who had started to pace restlessly.

“Look at what happened with cousin Thyra! She got married and we never saw her again! And Thorul isn’t like Midvale, you’ll be hundreds of miles away!” Alex’s voice was lifting with her distress, anger and fear and helplessness lacing her tone.

“What if she, I don’t know, locks you up or something?!”

“Alex, will you just stop!”

Her sister froze at her outburst and stared at her a long moment before anger flared in her eyes.

“Why is she choosing you,” Alex snarled with the tone of someone who had, had this conversation many times over, and still tried even knowing the outcome would be the same.

“Of all the people, why’d she pick you? Lena Luthor can have anyone she wants. The Jarl’s themselves were interested in a union. Why would she take you? Hasn’t her family taken enough?”

And Kara wasn’t privy to the exact details of her upcoming betrothal, not really. All she knew was that the Jarls and leaders of the El Alliance had met with Thorul’s delegates to come to a peace between them and Thorul. Marriage had been the only way to ensure it, for both parties, but Queen Lena had been reluctant, until she wasn’t, and Kara was betrothed. Kara knew her own reasons for agreeing to the match but didn’t know what Lena’s were.

“She didn’t,” Kara shouted, anger bursting from her and she shook her head helplessly. “I offered.”

Alex could have imitated a carving. She froze so quickly it was as though she’d been lost to the frosts of Jotunheim.

“I offered, Alex,” Kara repeated helplessly, begging with her sister to understand.

She looked away, looked at the ceremonial equipment that lay on the bench in her makeshift home and shrugged, lost.

“They were just… throwing suitors at her and talking about her like she was this, this terrible person when she isn’t and I couldn’t let that happen and then they were talking about how they’d treat her because she was the last one left and I didn’t want anyone to hurt her and I knew that I wouldn’t and I didn’t want anyone to treat her and her people cruelly because of what Lex has done when all she has ever done is try to heal and help and she has been so kind and sweet and gentle and nice and-“

“Kara,” Alex lifted a hand to halt her rambling and she blushed a little, raising her shoulder sheepishly.

“I didn’t want anyone else to marry her so I asked Jeremiah to put my name forward,” Kara confessed quietly, as though speaking above a whisper would bring someone down upon them.

“I know the Jarls won’t think I could betray my own alliance, and I have Kal and you and mum and dad and I wouldn’t turn on my own people. And… I hope I can be trusted enough to not hurt any of Lena’s.”

Her sister was quiet a long time, staring at her with an unreadable expression before it turned shrewd.

“And it also helps that she’s Airmed?”

Kara couldn’t fight her flush and averted her eyes.

Kara had plenty to say about her supposed death, and the woman who saved her and nursed her back to health. She had been as pale as the frosts in winter, with hair as dark as raven feathers, with lips as red as blood.

Airmed had been kind and sweet, a healer and giver if there had ever been one. Kara had spent all winter in the woman’s care, quietly hidden in her house while she recovered, even accompanying her through the Hold to provide aid and supplies. Thorul had greater pathways and roads than any of the other Holds, using their wealth and resources to ease their means of travel and trade, so winter travel was easier.

It had taken Kara a while to put the pieces together, that the familiar symbol that originated across the sea to the east with the clans there, belonged to the healer she’d been living with. Lena told her it was a Celtic Knot. It had appeared on various boxes of supplies sent out around the Holds over the years when Lex had started to raid the Holds in earnest. They’d only had a name and symbol, but this person had helped dozens of families across the Holds, even when their own clans and Jarls couldn’t or wouldn’t help. Airmed, and the woman healing her who was called Lena were the same.

Of course, then the woman she knew as Lena and as Airmed welcomed them to Thorul as its Jarl, as a Luthor. She had spiralled a little, but she was over it now. She’d come to terms with it and now welcomed it.

Her betrothed was Lena Luthor, Jarl of Thorul, and she was an amazing woman. Kara would be proud to call her ‘wife’ when they were married in less than…. A few hours.

The sun was a few hours off setting, and by the time it passed the horizon, she’d be a married woman; a Jarl, a Queen. It was a petrifying thought, but she was also… helplessly excited. She felt giddy at the thought.

Alex gave her a knowing smile, the crease of her brow smoothening into something close to resignation, if not acceptance.

“Alright then,” Alex moved over to the bench holding her wrist armour, unable to fight the inevitable any longer.

“Let’s get you ready.”

Kara had already bathed after the coronation this morning, and merely needed to dress, speak with her elders, and then make her way to Odin’s Peak.

Each of the couples in Thorul got married there, or at least the villagers that were close enough to travel to the traditional location did, and it was where their Jarl was appointed. It had also been the location of Lena’s coronation this morning. It was sacred to Thorul.

Jarl Lena had been crowned as Queen of the Jarls (officially, rather than proclaiming it and strongarming the other Jarls into agreeing like her forebearers) and she’d given Argo Hold and its lands back to Kara, as well as appointing Kara as the Jarl of Argo. It wasn’t her birthright, but she had claim to it, and no one was about to argue with the Queen, and her betrothed knew it.

The El Alliance and Queen Lena had decided to hold the coronation and the wedding on the same day, to make life easier for all involved. The Jarls and other important clan members had already gathered for the official naming of Jarl Lena as the Jarl of Jarls, so they had decided to hold the wedding afterwards. There were already supplies and people gathered, adding a union only increased the sense of peace and prosperity for all.

After the coronation, all parties had retired for the day, and Kara had prepared for her upcoming nuptials. She already had a sword, one that belonged to her father and that Lena had given to her from the Thorul armoury- it had been taken as a trophy when Lex’s raiders had razed Argo. Traditionally she’d raid the crypts of her ancestors for a blade but… she hadn’t been home in years and she hadn’t had the time.

Her father’s sword, owned once by her great-uncle, would suffice.

Kara held out her left wrist and let Alex slide the greave onto her form arm. It was hardened leather on a soft leather seal, with patterns on the outside which matched the waist belt.

She could have done this part herself, using her other hand and her teeth if need be, but she was happy to let Alex prep her.

After the first one was secure, she pulled out the shirt, pulling out the fabric so it wasn’t too tight or uncomfortable, and then left Alex to dress the other arm.

There was a knocking on the wooden pole at the door and Eliza ducked into the tent with a warm smile. Their mother was wearing the emerald and green of the Danvers Clan of Midvale, with an emerald cloak over her shoulders.

She had aged over the winter months, but her eyes were still gentle and kind.

“Out you get.” Her voice was slightly teasing and scolding, like how it had been when she had caught Kara and Alex playing at ‘raids’ in their fathers’ quarters, having taken some small swords and shields and were pretending to be soldiers. Kara didn’t know what he did, though by the looks of him he was a smith; he was a very big man with biceps that looked like they could wrestle a bear.  Kara didn’t know much about him, but she knew that Lena had healed his son of some illness when no one else could, and he owed her everything.

Lena’s final witness had caused some controversy, her thrall Jess. The woman had been a slave since she was a girl, Lena’s confidant in all things. She was the closest thing Lena had to a sister, and her presence there was indicative of Lena’s authority. No one would dare say anything to the queen, and Jess’ word would suffice because Lena commanded it.

Kara’s witnesses were her cousin, Kal, who represented her family and the El Clan, her adoptive father Jarl Jeremiah Danvers, of Midvale, and her sister Alex, who would stand with her.

The trio would stand on each side of Lena and Kara, flanking them until where the two brides would join, like the tip of a triangle.

Thorul’s Gyðja, their religious leader, a pale woman with long dark hair called Andrea, would oversee the ceremony and communion with the gods.

She was in conference with Lena, the queen with her crown on her head as was fitting for something so official, and Kara came to a halt.

Her betrothed’s profile was one Kara had imprinted so deeply into her soul that she was certain that even if her sight was taken, she’d be able to recognise Lena by touch alone.

The Queen was wearing a black spotted cloak, the coats of many large black cats from the hot places to the south combined to make it. It fell to her ankles and was cinched across her shoulder with gold. Kara suspected the clasp would be a bear, most Luthor belongings had a bear on it in some form.

She was wearing a navy-blue dress, with black leather down each of her flanks and it was edged with runes in gold. Her raven hair fell in curls down her back and her skin had taken on an amber shine, she looked like she was glowing like coals in a fire.

She looked magnificent and Alex had to grip her arm to drag her to the altar. Odin’s Peak had its own stone altar, and Lena and Andrea were standing behind it, while Lena’s witnesses stood off to her side.

Kara’s witnesses were talking to one another, which was always a surprise to Kara. Jeremiah knew what Kara thought of her cousin, the love, and the hate she held for him in equal measure. The Jarl respected Kal and didn’t. Jeremiah didn’t like that Kal hadn’t taken Kara, as was his duty as her elder, and as the last of her clan. Kal couldn’t genuinely care for a grieving teenager, but he was a man by their standards, and should have taken his kin in. Instead, he had abandoned Kara to go off raiding, returning with riches and respect, enough to get the attention of the daughter of Jarl Metropoli.

Kara was always confused with how she felt about her cousin. They hadn’t been close when they were younger, but they were cousins, they were blood. He was all the family she had left, and he abandoned her. And then she had Alex, and Alex had helped fill the hole in her where her people had left. Alex was everything, Alex had stood by her as they both trained to become shieldmaidens and had been with her on every battle after. They’d only been apart when Kara had been injured, and there was no one else Kara wanted at her side when she advanced on the next path of her life.

She was nervous, eager, and kept darting glances at Lena as she stood next to her.

Lena had bathed with flower water, that much was obvious from the sweet floral scent around her.

Her betrothed kept her gaze forward, chin high and demeanour calm. She was always calm, always in control, and she wasn’t showing any signs of the fire running through Kara’s bloodstream.

A gold necklace, clunky against her delicate collarbones, was an ostentatious display of wealth, just as the crown on her head, and both golden surfaces seemed to catch the fading light of the sun.

When everyone was settled, Andrea began the ceremony.

She started by welcoming their guests, naming the two brides and their family lines, and even touching upon the reason for the union, the peace between the Hold’s.

Then she summoned the gods. Kara’s heart was pounding in her chest, her blood racing through her veins and she had to swallow to get moisture into her mouth. She had never taken part in something so important, and she was nervous and excited.

Andrea’s assistant came forward through the crowd leading a goat for the god Thor.

It had a fine set of horns, though Kara’s nose curled at its scent, and she wondered why a sacrifice to the god would be made when neither she nor Lena could impregnate the other. Andrea instead asked for fertility for their relationship, for the Hold, and for the peace in the other Holds. She called upon Thor to bear witness to the union, to bless their pledge and give weight to the vows that would follow.

There was an altar of stone in front of the two and Kara watched as the goat was led before it and bound. Andrea had a ceremonial dagger and a set of bowls for the blood and organs, ready to be gifted to Thor.

Speaking to the gods required practice and training, Kara was certain that she’d never have the vocabulary or manner to call upon the gods. The few times she had tried, ended with her feeling awkward and unsure. Communion with the gods was best left to those who had experience in it.

Andrea spoke words Kara wasn’t familiar with, and lifted the blade, only for Lena to step forward.

Kara’s head snapped over to her and her eyes went wide. Was she leaving? Did she not want to get married now? Unease and fear twirled in her body, along with resounding disappointment. She wanted to marry Lena, and for more than the peace their union fostered.

Lena and Andrea conversed quickly and the Gyðja nodded, though it was with obvious reluctance.

Jarl Lena stepped back into position, looking as unruffled as she had when she started, as though nothing had happened.

Andrea continued the ceremony and when she lifted the knife, her angle changed. Puzzled, for the leverage to kill the animal quickly and efficiently, was wrong, Kara frowned at her.

Instead of slaying the beast, Andrea brought the blade down across the goat’s skin, and it bellowed in pain and jerked against the rope, but Andrea had no use for it.

Instead, she brought the blade with the blood on it over to the bowl and shook the blade into the bowl. Her assistant released the animal and dragged it away, and then another animal was brought forward.

This time it was a sow, and several Housecarls had to drag it up to the altar and tie it down. It was large for its species, and Andrea continued the ritual as she had with the goat, adding the blood to the same bowl. She called upon Freyja for her blessing upon the marriage, for love and affection between the two women, as well as fertility, again for their marriage, Hold, and for peace (even if children wasn’t the goal). She was also the goddess of sex, and this caused a few good-natured chuckles from the crowd when her blessing was invoked- which was the standard of a wedding.

The final animal, an offering to the god Freyr, was a magnificent horse the colour of night. It was a beautiful beast, intelligence flaring in its dark eyes. It shied away when Andrea made the cut to its coat, right across its breast and nearly dragged the four Housecarls away with it, until Lena stepped forward to calm it.

It took a long while for the beast to still, but under her gentle hand and kindness, it calmed and allowed them to lead it away.

Three sacrifices was a lot, though the Luthor’s could shoulder the expense, but it was also important that this wedding be a success, thousands of lives depending on the union, peace itself did. They’d appeal to as many gods as it took for the marriage to be a success.

“Before the witnesses and gods, Thorul does swear that the offspring of these animals, one every year on this day, be dedicated to their god, the blood of sacrifice in this union sacred until their death,” Andrea intoned, voice carrying and sounding very impressive to Kara’s ear.

Instead of sacrificing the animals, Lena had elected to spare their lives and instead keep them as sacred animals, animals dedicated to the gods they would have been sacrificed to. Giving an offspring each year was a generous gift, one fitting for such an occasion.

Andrea took a small bundle of fir twigs to gather up the blood, and then flicked it over the two brides, before turning it on the other witnesses. There wasn’t enough blood to go around, not like there would be if the entire beast had been bled, but the intent was there. The blessings of the gods had passed through the blood of their sacred animals and onto the couple.

With the blessings on the couple, it was now the time of the sword exchange. She and Lena hadn’t decided on who would take traditional roles and she waited, side-eying her bride, to see if Lena would move first…. And when she didn’t, she swallowed. Right. That made sense in some ways.

Kara was physically more capable, and if it came to a fight, she’d be the one defending them.

Her hand shook as she reached for her blade and gently pulled it out. She held it with the pommel upright and the tip of the blade facing the ground.

Wetting her lips, she glanced up at her wife, whose eyes were haloed with fire in the setting sun, her gaze warm and gentle and paralysing. Before she was caught in Lena’s gaze, and held there forever, Kara thrust her hand out and offered the blade to her bride.

Lena accepted it with ease, wrapping her long, dexterous fingers around the hilt and drawing it to herself. She let it rest at her side, holding it almost awkwardly in her nondominant hand.

The ease in which she handed off her own blade to Kara made that even more clear, she was clearly right-handed.

Lena’s ancestor’s blade was very pretty, more ceremonial than useful to Kara’s well-trained eye. There was gold at the cross-guard and pommel, rubies and diamonds clutched in the cross-guard with a large diamond set in the pommel. The hilt was wrapped in red leather as well, keeping to the Luthor colours of maroon and gold.

It was probably worth a small fortune, and mostly useless, but it didn’t need to be. Kara would be doing the protecting for them both.

She vowed that silently as Lena switched the sword to her other hand.

Movement at her side drew Kara’s attention to where Alex was handing her a ring and she swallowed. This was really happening! She let out a shaky exhale and took the ring, carefully balancing it on the golden pommel before guiding it carefully through the air as an offering to her wife.

It was a gold ring, with a blue stone embedded in the centre. Kara had wanted to give Lena something that was part of her, to remind her wife of Kara. Gold, for Kara’s hair, and sapphire for her eyes.

Lena took it with nimble fingers, fingers that Kara had watched almost obsessively in the past and slid the ring onto her ring finger.

Then she offered Kara her ring on the pommel and Kara carefully pulled the sword back to her and looked at the ring.

It was a simple gold band with a little Celtic Knot engraved where a stone would go. It was practical, both Kara and Lena, and it was perfect.

Kara slid it onto her ring finger, slipping her Jarl signet ring to her other hand.

Lena reached out with her ring hand and wrapped her fingers around the pommel, her long, slender hands causing tingles where her skin touched Kara’s.

For her vows, Kara hadn’t given them too much thought, and by that, she meant she had stayed awake for what felt like a thousand sleepless nights, trying to find the right words for Lena.

There were things that she wanted to say to her wife, but those words were for her wife alone, and not for the witnesses, guests, or even the Gods. Just Lena.

“Before the gods, the witnesses, our friends and family, I give you my body, that we two might be one.  From this day, you are the blood of my blood, that our lives be bound. I give you my spirit, til our life be done,” Kara swallowed, caught by the emerald gaze of her wife.

“I give you my heart, til the end of days” she whispered, words for Lena alone, not for the guests, but she didn’t care who heard her words, not really, she wouldn’t take them back. She was not ashamed of the way she felt about Lena, and she didn’t care who knew it.

Lena’s eyes had always held her attention since she’d first awoken from her fever all those months ago. In some light they were emerald, as green as the fields in spring, other times they were as blue as the ocean, and other times, they were gold, the hills around Argo in autumn. Now, they were a mix of green and blue, hallowed with the gold of the setting sun.

“While you wish it, I shall be yours, all that is mine is yours to have. I am yours to command, a free woman, but I will serve you in all ways you require. I will give to you, the first cut of meat, the first sip of wine. In your eyes I shall smile each morning, your arms I will reach for in the night. I’ll shield your back as you will shield mine, and I’ll keep you from all harm if you would stand beside me.”

Kara was a woman of action, not of words. She had little talent for flyting, nor was she a wordsmith, but she thought she got her message across. She was Lena’s, always, and she would keep her safe and protected and cared for, to the best of Kara’s ability.

She hadn’t given much thought to the vows other couples had spoken, and she couldn’t deny she had a greater respect for wordsmiths and for people who devoted their lives to communing with the gods, words were hard, harder than she had thought, and she was almost embarrassed by her own.

Lena inclined her head slightly, just once, in a slow nod of approval and Kara’s heart danced in her chest and she felt warm and golden and giddy, almost like the first time Alex had gotten her drunk. She had felt weightless then, as she did now, looking into Lena’s eyes.

“Jarl El, Wolf-maiden of Argo, daughter of Midvale,” Lena began, calling Kara’s names before the gods. Kara was the Jarl of Argo since the Hold had been returned to her, though she wasn’t sure what state her old home was in, and she was the adopted daughter of Jeremiah and Eliza Danvers of Midvale, so her history was called there. The Wolf-maiden was new, but was clearly a mix of Kara’s Shieldmaiden title, the Wolf. She didn’t mind the name and mouthed it to herself with a little nod. It was less of a mouthful than Jarl Kara Danvers Zor El of Argo and Midvale, Shieldmaiden Wolf.

“I have given you the greatest gift that can be given,” she spoke clearly and calmly, voice carrying over the faint hum of the waves at their back. “I have returned your Hold and your home to you.”

Kara’s grip on the sword tightened for just a moment, and Lena’s pinkie shifted ever so slightly to brush against her skin, and she relaxed.

“I vow to help rebuild Argo to its former glory and beyond, and indeed, as my betrothal gift, my morgedn-gifu- though it is unorthodox to give it to you now-, supplies have already departed for the Hold. Rebuilding the longhouse began shortly after the truce was negotiated, and labour and supplies will remain until the work is complete.”

Kara couldn’t help but gape at her, and her expression was mirrored by those around them. She was being beyond generous, though a Luthor could afford it. And to have started the build while the rest of the people were scrambling around their Hold’s and farms to prepare for winter just went to show how wealthy and powerful Thorul was. That Lena could spare the men and supplies to begin rebuilding the settlement was telling, but also really went to show how kind her wife- were they married yet? Lena hadn’t said her vows just yet, so maybe they weren’t? - was.

She gave her Argo Hold back, though it didn’t need to be returned, and she appointed Kara as Jarl, when women Jarls were rare and Kal had a higher claim. To outsiders it might look like Lena was making Kara more powerful so that she was more powerful, but Kara thought of Lena’s gentle heart and knew that it was to make her happy. Lena had given her a priceless gift to make her happy. She had the thought, then, that if her wife-to-be treated her with the same compassion and kindness as she had, then her marriage would likely be a happy one, regardless of what others feared.

Kara had been more than willing to wed Lena, for multiple reasons, but she couldn’t help but feel that perhaps the gods were giving her something to fill the hole where her family and clan had been. Lena couldn’t make it all go away, she couldn’t take it back, and Kara wouldn’t want her to- she wouldn’t be who she was without it, and she wasn’t so foolish as to question celestial plans- but she could heal her wounds, ease her pain. Lena was a healer; it was what she did. And maybe, just maybe, she could heal more than Kara, maybe Lena could heal all the Hold’s.

There was a mark on Lena’s neck that moved as her throat bobbed, and it suddenly held Kara’s attention until Lena spoke again.

“With the gods as my witnesses, as my judge and executioner should I fail to uphold my word, I vow to honour you, Jarl Kara Zor El of Argo, above all else. Your people are my people.”

Kara gulped at the promise but her gaze on Lena’s didn’t waver. It was a big responsibility, to assume guardianship over an entire Hold, and to willingly take on the duties and burdens of another was unheard of…. But so was a two-woman wedding, and two female Jarls. Jarls never wed Jarls, they wed siblings or children, but never the other leader. Two were too many wolves in the dog pack, as Alura had once told her. But Lena was adopting Argo as her own.

Next to her, Alex bristled but Kara ignored the movement as she understood Lena more fully to wager as to why she was taking Argo under her protection.

Kara was, for all appearances, marrying above her station, she was the wife in this regard. Until she or Lena died, or if she was granted a divorce- which would end their treaty and war would break out again, so til death would they part! - Thorul was now her home and clan. But in giving Argo back to her, Kara had some power, and in Lena vowing to protect Argo as her own, it meant that she was almost fulfilling the vows of a wife…. To take another’s clan as your own was to either forfeit your ancestors for your new family… or…. It meant to conquer and claim.

Alex was probably upset because she thought it meant Lena was asserting her dominance over the marriage by giving Kara Argo formally, but unofficially maintaining control of the land. Kara didn’t think so. Lena was vowing to help Kara with Argo, however she needed it. It would be different if they were allies, or if Thorul had maintained control over the land that the Hold resided in, but she had given that to Kara. Lena was giving Kara a great deal of power, rising her to an almost equal status. It was bold. It was daring with a little bit of mischief. It was just like her wife.

Kara couldn’t help the twitch of her lips into a smile, which Lena caught by the way her own lips curled in satisfaction.

“I swear to remain faithful through all paths in life, from this til the next. I chose you to have to hold, to be bound forever. You are my wife, my equal, my partner in all things.”

Kara’s smile pulled wider at her lips and she ducked her head with a little nod. That was perfectly acceptable to her. She was Lena’s and Lena was hers, from now and forever.

Lena’s vows weren’t as ornate as Kara’s, they were simple but strong, and soon Andrea was addressing the guests.

“Witnesses! Guests! Honoured gods! I present Queen Lena Luthor, Jarl of Jarl’s, of Thorul, and Jarl Kara Zor-El of Argo, the Wolf Shieldmaiden, wedded!”

She and Lena lifted their hands and presented the sword to the gods, tip raised to the heavens. She had almost forgotten she was holding it with Lena, and she looked down at the blade while the gathered clan’s made their presence felt. It was a pretty thing, but she doubted she’d use it in a fight. She favoured axes for their brute strength, and this sword really was too pretty.

There was applause and hollering, good natured jeering from the crowd. Kara wasn’t sure how pleased they truly were at the union, but she didn’t care. She was happy. War had been averted and her wife, her wife! She could say that now! Lena was her wife! - was kind and sweet and generous.

She had high hopes for the future and glanced at her wife.

Lena was staring straight ahead, her crown gleaming in the sunlight like it was freshly molten, and with a calm expression, but with satisfaction in her gaze. Kara darted her eyes away. Her wife was truly beautiful, and she was painful to look at, she shone so brightly.

Kara turned her attention from her wife to the more pressing future.

The bride race was the next step in the ceremony, a race down to the feasting hall where they’d have their first meal as wives and then the festivities could truly begin.

Kara, as wife of the Jarl, as wife to the Queen, would run in Lena’s place as Lena had no family to run for her- and Kara had never, ever seen Lena move more swiftly than her ground-eating glide. Maybe Kal or Alex would run on Kara’s behalf, she wasn’t sure and would let them sort it between them. Maybe they both would run, the winner winning more than the race and some sort of point in the battle between the two. Kara would let them sort it themselves. Kal was her blood, yes, but that was all he was. Attachment was there for the bond that they shared, but her family and home was with Alex and Midvale… and now with Lena and Thorul.

Kara would win the race, she would, losing was not an option, and the Danvers Clan and Kal would serve her and Lena for the rest of the night, though Alex would probably be the better natured about it. Kal still held Thorul and the Luthor’s responsible for what had happened to their people and would fulfil his obligations with resentment.

The very pretty and almost useless sword that Kara now carried as representative of her marriage would be thrust into the ceiling or pillars of the feasting hall, as hard as Kara could manage. Though not a traditional marriage, Kara considered herself to be married and she absolutely would fulfil her obligations to the best of her abilities. She would honour her ancestors and her clan, both of them, the best she was able.

Typically, the bride would feed the groom first, but as there were two brides Kara wasn’t sure on the ceremony of it, and then decided that she would take care of Lena. It would send a message, she affirmed with a sharp nod as the crowd began to disperse with cheer towards the feasting hall. She, the more physical and masculine of the two, was content to be the Jarl’s wife, and more than happy to provide for her in every way.

Yes, that would be acceptable. Plus…. Her eyes darted to her wife’s mouth, lingering on the red of her lips before skirting away again as her body flushed.

The bride-ale was offered in horns and mugs, first the wife giving to the husband, and then she would drink from the horn. Blessings of the gods would be asked for, and then the feasting would begin.

The first bite would go to the husband… and they were offered to him by his wife… by hand so…so that meant that Kara’s fingers might touch Lena’s lips. The thought almost made her dizzy and she was brought from her mental spiral by a clasp to her back.

“Congratulations,” Jeremiah said, a great smile on his face and he strode off towards a few of his Housecarls, no doubt they were ready for the food and drink that Thorul was offering. The scents that had lingered on the wind all day had made Kara’s mouth water, even though she had been too nervous to eat, and now she was suddenly ravenous.

As she had suspected, Lena’s thrall offered to run for her, but Kara declined before Lena could comment. She wasn’t sure how successful Jess would have been.

There was some surprise in Lena’s gaze as she met Kara’s eyes, but she inclined her head in a painfully regal fashion before walking off down the gentle slope.

Kara watched her go, heart twirling and heat moving from her chest to her belly at the movement of Lena’s hips beneath her dress. It was hard to define her shape under the cloak, but Kara could just make out the gentle lines of her body and could easily fill in the blanks from memory.

Alex nudged her. “Luthor not running herself?”

Kal El snorted, for once agreeing with Alex. “Too good for us?”

“I’m running,” Kara declared sharply, swinging around to eye her sister and cousin. It occurred to her that she now outranked them both.

Kal, because she was now the Jarl of Argo, which would be a festering wound for him, and Alex because she was the wife of the Jarl of Jarl’s. It was a humbling thought. To keep her happy, and keep her wife happy, they would defer to her, even if their pride would take the beating.

If she were anyone else, they wouldn’t be able to risk offending her, offending her wife and Thorul, and face possible repercussions. What a thought. She was glad that Alex, at least, wouldn’t treat her any differently now that she was a Jarl’s wife, the Jarl’s wife. Her sister had promised to push her over if her head grew too large.

Ahead of her she could see James and Winn, with Eliza and Jeremiah, and at her side was Alex and Kal.

It was humbling to know she had her family on her side as she ventured down this path and she was suddenly excited for what would come, and very, very hungry.

“Race you!” She bellowed and broke into a dead sprint. She had a hall to get to.

Alex yelped and Kal let out a complaint, but their words lingered behind her as her body bolted down the slope.

“Move!” She ordered as she ran, and people scrambled from her stampede like ants, though a few shouted encouragement.

Kara didn’t hear them; she had a race to win. The tug of her cape was heavy on the wind, and she should have taken it off, but she didn’t have the time for that right now, and so instead leant forward to compensate for its drag. The pretty, ceremonial leather armour she was wearing moved well with her, it was lighter than her mail and easier to move in, and she was thankful as she’d take any advantage she could.

“Cheater!”

She heard behind her from Alex, who was hot on her tail, though she suspected Kal would catch her first. Her cousin had longer legs, and Alex was built for dancing with a blade, not running.

She burst past her wife, who was walking with Samantha on her arm, and she felt a flare of jealousy that she quickly squashed. Sam was a wonderful friend to Lena, and Kara had been the one to marry her, so she had nothing to be jealous of. Instead, she should put that energy to running.

Kal did catch her first, but Kara was determined and pushed her legs faster, running down the hill and bolting across the beach and up the dunes. The sand offered her too little resistance, though the path along the edge of the grass would have more obstacles, so it was a tough choice.

She settled on the grass. She could jump over obstacles and dodge people, but she couldn’t keep up with her long-legged cousin on the sand.

People scattered out of her way as she ran, and she had a moment to thank whoever had planned the guest tents because they were all in an orderly pattern with clear lines in between each clan’s area. It was probably Lena who had organised it, she was very efficient and ordered like that.

With enough of a warning people were able to duck into tents or dart down other paths to escape her, and she was victorious as she slid to a stop and placed her hand against the tent-hide.

Then she doubled over, gasping. She wasn’t built for speed. She was built for stamina, for swinging around her great axe, not for running.

Her chest was burning, and her thighs were shaking, and maybe she’d add jogging back and forth along the sand to her exercise routine. Lena had made her walk it when she was recovering from her injury, but Kara hadn’t been on sand for months, so maybe she should rectify that. In full armour, she decided as she panted harshly. She’d build stamina for it soon enough.

Kal nearly flopped on the ground near her, and people were shooting them amused looks as they entered the hall. Kara would wait for Lena though, and she really needed to get her breath back.

The tent was massive, the biggest Kara had ever seen, and even as she drew desperate breaths into her lungs, she couldn’t help but admire its size. It had five main pillars, and Kara did not envy the men who had to lift them into place, each beam was almost as round as she was, and the smaller beams were the size of her thigh, stretching into the sky and holding the hide tight.

Inside there were four great tables with the table for her and Lena at the end, and massive amounts of food nearly buckled each table. Benches ran the length of the tables and against the tent wall, and barrels and barrels of ale and food was placed intermittently. Animals were turning over fire-pits dug into the ground, and had been since dawn, and hundreds of ale-horns and mugs and plates and cutlery had been prepared for the meal. It would be a feast worthy of the gods, and a stark reminder of Thorul’s superiority over the other Hold’s.

Eventually Lena caught up to them and Kara watched her approach, a grumbling Alex next to her who was resigned to serving Lena for the night, however Alex didn’t have to worry. Anything Lena needed or wanted; Kara would take care of. Kara just hadn’t wanted Lena to have to serve her family, Kal especially. Lena was alone, so to represent her family, she would have to serve the Danvers and the El’s, and there was only one of her, and she was the bride, and she was the Queen, so there was no way Kara would allow her to be lowered to a servant. No, if her family wanted anything, they could help themselves. Kara and Lena would see to each other.

There wasn’t a low bar over the door, but Kara held out her arm all the same, and Lena slid hers into the crook of Kara’s. It wasn’t the closest they had been since the betrothal had been announced, or even the closest they’d been in general, but they were married now, and Kara could look and touch without fear of anyone noticing or commenting.

Lena smelt good, her movement layering the air with the floral scents she bathed in. She said she bathed frequently and covered her skin with oils because she worked with herbs so often that she feared she smelt like them and wanted to smell nicer than the earthy scents of the medicine she crafted. She had also mentioned that being clean helped her with her healing, and as she was the best healer Kara had ever seen or heard of, she was inclined to believe her.

Her wife was warm next to her, Lena’s heat passing through her clothes and into Kara’s skin, almost burning at the touch. She was tingling pleasantly where their skin was joined, Lena’s hand resting casually on her forearm, the brush of their shoulders as they walked, her hair reaching out to toy with Kara’s body.

The next part of the ceremony was to plunge the sword into the house, it was to represent good fortune and bless their marriage, and Kara’s muscles tightened.

The pillar holding the tent, which would suffice as Thorul’s longhouse would not accommodate so many people, was as round as her waist. It looked to be solid, and Kara eyed it as she withdrew the sword and Lena gently tugged away. Kara missed her presence instantly and couldn’t help but feel annoyed that this pillar was getting in the way of their closeness.

She drove the sword into it, driving all her anger into the blow and was satisfied with how far she had sheathed it. The blade would stay there until morning, when she and Lena exchanged their morgedn-gifu, or Kara would. Lena had already given Kara hers.

She had her own gift prepared, a small white kitten after she had learnt of the loss of Lena’s ratcatcher a few months ago. The cat had kept Kara company as she healed, its skinny body rattling as it purred in her ear, comforting the invalid Kara when Lena had been out. Cat had kept her company.

Kara had been very surprised that Lena hadn’t named her pet, only to realise that Cat had been the name a young Lena had given to the pet, and she hadn’t had the heart to call it anything else.

Lena had access to riches and power that Kara couldn’t hope to achieve, and unless she crafted something with her own hands, which was a possibility, but she had been just so busy, she had come up empty on what to give Lena. Then one of the cats was known to be pregnant and Kara had come upon an idea.

Lena’s Cat had survived the winter but had died shortly after Kara had returned to Midvale, and Lena didn’t yet have another, so Kara thought that a kitten could be a kind morning gift for her wife.

With satisfaction Kara eyed the depth of the blade and offered her arm to Lena again.

People were already seating themselves and pouring wine, but they wouldn’t dare partake until she and Lena had, and as her belly growled, she hastened her steps.

Lena let out a faint breath and Kara darted a glance at her, only for her to see Lena’s brow lifted fondly, a curve to her lips, and she went red again before turning away.

Lena always made her feel funny. Made her feel awkward and unsure, almost like she was a gangly new-born foal, eager but wobbly. She wasn’t sure if she liked the feeling, if she liked the way her stomach would swoop whenever she was gifted with a smile.

Their table had copious amounts of food and drink on it, from what looked like pig and fowl, to chicken and fish. Kara had hunted a deer especially for the bridal table, and suspected its meat was present too.

Barrels of bridal ale were behind the table and chairs, and Kara let Lena go around first, which had nothing to do with the way she wanted to watch as her wife removed her cloak and laid it over the back of her chair.

It was hot in the tent with the flames and all the bodies and Kara swallowed and glanced away, right into her mothers knowing smile, and she flushed even further and looked anywhere but at the people next to her.

Thousands of people were crammed into the hall, squished together on benches, and elbowing each other for room. It wasn’t ideal, but having two feasting halls negated the ceremony, and once they’d gotten food in their bellies and ale in their hands, the guests wouldn’t protest the cramped quarters.

Among the plates and horns and cutlery, there was a carved wooden bowl with a handle. It would fit easily in her palm, and Kara reached for it before Lena could. They both paused with the hands on it, and Lena’s skin was so soft and smooth beneath her own, that she almost jerked her hand away.

She had washed her hands repeatedly early in the day, to rid them of any dirt and grime that might be present, even using a fine blade to clean under her nails. She had been painfully aware of Lena’s hands in comparison to her own for months. Lena’s were long and slender and gentle and pale, with a few calluses from wear, but they were nothing like Kara’s. Kara’s were big and strong and usually dirty from labour or death (animal or human targets) and they were worn and hard. She didn’t want to hurt Lena with them or have her shy away from how ugly they were.

Instead, the two of them eyed each other, Kara’s hand over Lena’s on the carved cup while they silently fought over who would be serving who.

Kara tried to slide her fingers under Lena’s, staring her wife in the eyes.

“Let me?” She breathed when Lena’s hand tightened.

Her wife’s eyes were searching, but after a moment she released her grip, trailing her fingers across Kara’s scarred skin and it tingled in her wake. Kara gulped and snatched the bowl and turned, presenting her back to her guests least they see the flush on her cheeks.

It gave her a moment to gather herself, and she glanced over the carved bowl. It was a rich dark wood, smooth and polished, and the handle was a… wolf…. Of course, it was. Lena had probably arranged this to give to Kara, to serve Kara with, and she couldn’t help her sigh.

Lena was so earnest and kind in her drive to make Kara happy, but she didn’t realise that she already had enough to be happy over.

She quickly poured the bridal ale, aware of Alex and Kal giving her disproving looks for again taking the place of the wife, of the ‘purchased’, but Jeremiah seemed neutral, and Eliza was just smiling. Knowingly. Again.

Odin had gifted her mother his Eye because she was certainly channelling the god of late. Kara really wanted to know why Eliza was behaving the way that she was, but some inner voice also to her that she didn’t want to know.

One of Thorul’s jewels was their beehives. An entire family was dedicated to the art of beekeeping, honey collecting, and then the various enterprises that came of honey. They had it available in large quantities because they cultivated the bees themselves. It had been thankless work at first, and many of the ancestors had been stung, but now it meant that the Hold had a regular production of honey, which had allowed for the massive amounts of ale provided for the feast.

The bridal ale was golden, lighter in colour than honey, and almost completely clear. It was like liquid sunshine in the mug and had probably been made at the height of spring, allowing it to settle these long months before the wedding.

It smelt like honey, rich and sweet, and Kara turned to face her wife.

Lena had been watching her, chin slightly lifted but gaze a little lowered, enabling her to look up at Kara through her lashes. She looked very pretty, and Kara nearly fumbled with the cup, but her ancestors gave her strength, and she didn’t drop it, or worse, spill it over her wife.

Mindful of her strength, suddenly feeling as though she were of Jotunheim, Kara offered the ale to her wife.

“To Odin,” Lena said as she took a sip, lips curling briefly at the taste, and then she offered it to Kara.

It was sweet, and cool to drink, and slid across her tongue with delight. It was very yummy, and she licked her lips as she pulled back. “To Freyja,” she toasted, completing the ritual.

Kara swallowed as she held Lena’s gaze, wanting the drink back so she could soothe her parched throat, but Lena instead reached for a loaf of bread.

It was freshly baked and crackled as Lena split it, and Kara’s heart pounded.

The morsel was fluffy and pale between Lena’s fingers as she held it up to Kara’s lips and she opened her mouth to receive the gift. Her skin tingled where she touched Lena’s fingers and they lingered on her lips a moment, before pulling back and offering Kara the bread.

Under any other circumstance the bread would be a delight, she’d dip it in soup or lather it with butter or jam, but it turned lumpy and thick in her mouth, tasteless as all her body focused on Lena’s fingers against the sensitive skin of her lips.

She’d never thought her lips could be sensitive, that they could tingle at just a touch, a brush of Lena’s skin on her own, but they did. They were singing and she swallowed several times to get it down before taking the bread absently, her eyes on Lena.

The bread was still warm, and she didn’t care to look at it as she broke off some and offered it to Lena, her heart pounding in her chest.

She was hot all over, and she could have sworn the entire hall could hear the beating of her heart.

Lena’s breath was the first thing her fingers noticed, and they thrilled at the heat of it, and then her lips slowly parted to take the morsel. Kara’s heart stopped.

Lena’s lips were soft, slightly wet from the ale she’d just drunk, and they were warm, but not as warm as her mouth.

Kara bit down a whimper as Lena’s mouth accepted the bread piece and there was a flick of wet heat at the tip of her finger. Her tongue. Oh, Odin! Kara ripped in a ragged inhale as Lena’s mouth covered her fingers and pulled the bread away. Kara’s heart started again, her entire body erupting in shivers and there was a jolt of something in her stomach.

Lena chewed slowly, eyes on Kara, who felt like she was a mouse staring down a wolf, but she couldn’t move as her body tingled and the blood rushing through her veins reached the chambers of her heart and set about ringing them like a bell, happy and joyous.

A slight rise to Lena’s lips was all it took for Kara to lean heavily against the table, afraid her legs would give out. Lena’s eyes had darkened, and Kara’s lips parted, and her body felt like it was buzzing. She felt like she sometimes felt around Lena, all nervous energy but magnified. It was exciting. It was terrifying.

Eliza cleared her throat, ripping Kara back to the present and she tore her gaze from Lena, flushing head to toe and trying to swallow down the tightness in her throat. Lena’s cheeks were slightly red, but she gained her composure quickly, a veil covering her eyes so quickly Kara thought she’d imagined it.

Lena pulled her gaze from Kara and looked out over the guests, giving no indication that she had felt any of the weird energy that had sparked between her and Kara.

“Thank you coming. Jarl El and I wish you merriment and encourage you to partake in Thorul’s hospitality,” she inclined her head once in a semblance of a bow, the Jarls seated nearest to them bowing their heads, and then the feasting began like an arrow fired from a bow.

Hands dove on a fowl leg, others grabbed leg of lamb or goat, and some went for fish. Instantly the tent was full of noise and good cheer, and someone had a lyre in the background and was playing a merry tune. Various flutes and drums and off-key singing added to the general chaos, but it was a good kind of pandemonium, it was pure joy and merriment. The harvest was in, the war was averted, and there was food and mead plenty!

Kara nearly fell into her chair, heart still pounding unevenly. She was married. It was official. She was a wife. It was…. She didn’t have the words to explain how she felt and glanced to see if Lena perhaps had the same expression on her face, but Lena was engaged with Sam on her left and Kara turned back to her food with a barely audible sigh.

She went through the motions of piling her plate with the bounty from sea and earth, but she wasn’t hungry and picked absently at the food.

She was content to just watch the people around her be happy, unsure of the melancholy taking root in her chest, it was completely opposite to how she had felt earlier.

She knew eyes were on her though, friendly or foe, so she tried to eat, even though she couldn’t taste it. Even the bridal ale, so sweet on her tongue, felt too thick to drink too much of.

Another sigh was cut off with a strangled sound when she felt weight rest on her thigh and she nearly lurched to her feet and was grateful the table was a little above her thighs, otherwise she’d have knocked them.

She darted a glance at Lena as she settled, and after a moment Lena’s hand rested on her knee again, hesitant, and unsure and Kara slid her hand under the table.

Could she take Lena’s hand? Or did she want to remove it?

Hesitantly she reached out to touch Lena’s hand, only for it to dart away, but Kara had expected this, and suddenly she wanted to hold Lena’s hand.

She grabbed it and linked their fingers, for once unmindful of her strength and the ugliness to her hands.

Lena paused in her retreat, fingers stilling, and then relaxed, a smile curling her lips.

Lena’s profile loosened, though she was still in conversation with Sam, and had her ale in her other hand.

Lena squeezed her hand reassuringly and Kara beamed, suddenly starving again, and she stabbed at a piece of meat with her knife and brought it to her lips.

She and Lena were okay, there was no need for her misery. They were married now, before the gods and everyone here, and nothing could take that from them. She didn’t know why she had been so upset, maybe because she hadn’t really had much time with Lena alone and they hadn’t had the chance to talk.

She knew Lena wanted this, she had agreed after all, but they hadn’t really talked on much more than that. It was, in some regards, just like the marriages of everyone else. It had been decided by powers outside of Kara’s control and the first amount of time she’d spend with her wife was tonight, it would be then that they could talk privately without eyes watching them. She knew Lena hadn’t changed, and her gifts, delivered by Jess, indicated Kara was on her mind, but she really wanted to talk to Lena properly. There would be time for that later, though, she knew as she was pulled into a conversation with Jeremiah on her ungainly rush across the beach during her bride’s race.

Time flowed, just like the ale and mead, and people were singing and laughing and enjoying Thorul’s hospitality. It was as good as legend said. Meat of all colours was spread across the tables, with ales and mead and wine and vegetables. No expense had been spared, and Kara wondered how much food would be left, but had a feeling Lena would be giving it to the orphan’s house in Thorul- she always made sure they were fed and clothed, and even arranged trades for them if she could. As a leader she would be beloved by all her people, especially after her father and brother.

It was Alex, whose distrust of everything Luthor didn’t extend to their ale, who demanded Kara dance with her wife, knowing Kara’s loathing for attention and her inability to dance. It had been much amusement for the elder Danvers when they were children. Kara’s balance lowered the more she drank, and it was always a laugh for the longhouse when Alex got her drunk enough to dance for them. Usually, she would fall over or otherwise disgrace herself, to the amusement of her clan.

Unfortunately, Alex’s shout got Lena’s attention, and from the way her head snapped around, eyes wide and lips parted hopefully, Kara was helpless to deny her, and knew, without Lena even asking for it, that she’d willingly walk into Ragnarök for her.

With a sharp glare to Alex, but a fond smile for her wife, Kara stood and was rewarded with Lena’s grin.

Lena knew she couldn’t dance and had, in fact, taught her a few dances while she had been recovering, locked in Thorul for the winter, but she was clearly pleased that Kara was willing to do this for her.

Her friends from Midvale laughed and jeered as Kara took a fortifying gulp of mead and moved out with Lena to the front of their table. It would be a tight squeeze, but it would be worth it.

Conversations near them were halting and Kara could see the smug grins on all her friends and families faces, knowing she was about to fail drastically, but with Lena’s hand in her own, she was suddenly confident.

“Ready?” Lena asked her, and it had been so long since Lena had spoken to her directly that Kara let out a happy sigh. Her wife still had her accent, still curled her words in that foreign way that Kara had appreciated but now realised she loved.

Suddenly embodied, perhaps by the squeeze of her hand that her wife gave her, Kara nodded.

She could do this, she would do this, and she’d do it for Lena.

A familiar tune was being played on a flute and Kara nearly grinned. Lena had taught her this song herself, those long weeks while Kara had recovered, and then she had taught Kara the dance.

Kara had been reluctant at first, citing that she didn’t dance, but she had been bored and locked inside so had let Lena teach her.

“I’ll swim and sail on savage seas, with ne’er a fear of drowning, and gladly ride the waves of life, if you will marry me.” Kara wasn’t sure where the song had come from, only it was a well-known one between lovers. Some said that a raider had gifted it to his beloved before he journeyed away, but no one was certain. Still, it was a well-known song and was always sung at weddings.

“No scorching sun nor freezing cold will stop me on my journey,” and she’d drunk enough that she didn’t mind singing. She wasn’t singing for anyone though, she was singing just for Lena, whose eyes were soft and warm, as she pulled away, moving down the length of the table and away from Kara.

There was a story to this song, a reluctant woman who wanted to stay but walked, until love pulled her back to the raider.

“If you will promise me your heart and love me for eternity.”

They were a few meters apart in front of the table and Alex was grinning at her, ale almost spilling from her horn as she waved her hand around in encouragement. The rest of the guests were grinning and hooting encouragement as more instruments joined the tune.

“My dearest one, my darling dear, your mighty words astound me,” Lena was smiling with her eyes as she stepped forward and Kara was pulled towards her helplessly, her feet carrying her to where her heart wanted to go.

Lena held her hand out in offering, stepping closer to Kara who grinned and pressed their wrists together.

“But I’ve no need for mighty deeds when I feel your arms around me.” They walked one way, gave a little hop, and then walked the other, changing their wrists as they moved.

Kara took Lena’s hands with a grin, and now her wife was starting to smile, lips pulled back and eyes dancing and Kara was breathless.

“But I would bring your rings of gold, I’d even sing you poetry,” she vowed with a smile and their feet tapped together with their crossed hands.

“And I would keep you from all harm, if you would stay beside me,” Kara sang and dropped to one knee, letting Lena dance over and around her before guiding her back to her feet.

“I have no use for rings of gold, I care not for your poetry. I only want your hand to hold,” and now Lena was on one knee, and Kara faltered slightly, but followed the instruction and danced over Lena’s leg, before helping her stand.

“I only want you near me,” they sang in unison, though there was more to the lyrics than just words, Kara was speaking from the heart. She wanted no one else.

Food was flying off the tables and alcohol was spilled over horns and mugs as the Vikings grabbed whatever was in their hands to bang against the table, creating a heartbeat for the song that was spinning her and Lena together.

“To love and kiss and sweetly hold, for the dancing and the dreaming.”

Drunken and off-key singing was added to her and Lena’s, but it didn’t distract from them, if anything it wove them together as they spun and danced around each other, always touching, sometimes drifting apart before the music pulled them back together.

“Through all life’s sorrows and delights, I’ll keep your laugh inside me,” and Lena let out a laugh then, smile wide and free, hair spinning around them, crown glinting. And there was nowhere else Kara wanted to be, no one she wanted to be dancing with.

“I’ll swim and sail on savage seas with ne’er a fear of drowning, I’d gladly ride the waves of life if you will marry me!”

And Lena spun back into her arms, their bodies pressed together, and Lena’s head tilted up to face her. Her wife was rosy cheeked and breathless, and ever beautiful for it, and Kara swallowed as she gazed down at her, certain all the emotions she was feeling were shining through her eyes.

“You are the only one I’ll ever love,” Kara breathed, slightly winded from their dancing and singing. She wasn’t sure she was willing to admit it, but with Lena in her arms, her heart racing from dancing and from having her wife so close, she felt like she could tell someone, tell the only person who mattered.

She couldn’t deny that she was afraid of Lena’s reaction, whether she would be rejected or accepted, but in the end it didn’t matter. Lena needed to know how she felt, even if she didn’t feel the same but…. Something told her that Lena wouldn’t reject her. Maybe it was wishful thinking, but Kara thought that it might just be hope, the most dangerous gift of all.

Lena glanced between her eyes, searching, before she nodded, and an even more powerful smile shone from her eyes.

She lifted their hands and placed a kiss to Kara’s knuckles, so light that Kara gasped, and her heart twirled, dancing in her chest with pure joy.

“If it's not you , it's not anyone, Jarl El.”

And Kara couldn’t fight the emotion that built in her chest and burst from her body with a smile.

She didn’t know what the future held for them, only the Gods did, but she was confident that her life would be happy, healthy, and that she would love and be loved by the amazing woman in her arms.  And Kara Zor El, Danvers, Shieldmaiden Wolf of Argo and Midvale, wife to the Jarl of Jarls, couldn’t wait for her life to start.
