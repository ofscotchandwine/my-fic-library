Bane of my existence

 

 

The portrait stared back at her, taunting her, mocking her, like a bad joke of the universe.

The wedding dress fit her exquisitely, no doubt made to her measurements, undoubtedly by some big name designer. The smile on her face was so real, it made her want to vomit.

What filled her with rage was who was holding her, as if she was the sun shining down on her. Kara Fucking Danvers, in a white suit with blue lapels, the exact same shade of blue as her supersuit. It was insulting, how prominent the picture was in her living room.

If Lena could even consider that her living room.

It was a penthouse, very similar to her own taste, at least in architecture. Its furnishings is what gave her pause. It wasn't the minimalist chic she favored, nor the expensive antiques the Luthors had on the old mansion. It was more… vintage, not necessarily old, but with a little wear and tear… scrapped and well loved.

And it screamed Kara from so far away that it almost gave her a headache.

The rings on her left hand were so heavy with significance, she had to immediately take them away. As soon as she saw them.

And then she saw the inscription inside the silver wedding band: Stronger Together.

What a joke.

What's worse… Lena wished it was real. It was what hurt the most about Kara's betrayal, nothing was real. Not the friendship, not the shared looks, or the subtle touches. It was all a ruse to keep her in check, and in surveillance.

It filled her with rage.

Rage she couldn't quite manage when boots softly landed on their balcony.

"What did you do?!" She asked, turning around.

"We need to talk," Kara replied, so ceremoniously serious that she seemed like she wanted to crawl out of her own skin.

"No shit, Supergirl," Lena sarcastically bit back. "What did you do this time?"

The hero cocked her head, in confusion, the ridiculous crinkle appearing on her face.

"I know you did something… Since we are apparently married, and I would never, willingly marry someone who has only lied to me," Lena spat out.

Kara physically took a step back, as if Lena had struck her.

"You know?"

"You thought I would forget how you lied to me for years?" She spat out.

"I didn—" Supergirl stopped her own lie and then sighed. "There was no ulterior motive to our friendship, Lena. And I didn't get any morbid satisfaction for keeping my identity secret, despite whatever your brother told you."

Lena crossed her arms, lifting an eyebrow, "that's not what I asked."

"I didn't do anything," Kara replied. "Crisis happened, I spent an incalculable amount of time at the vanishing point, your brother was there, after playing with The Book of Destiny and killing several universes' Superman. Maybe you should ask him."

Lena observed how the blonde rubbed her own forehead with her hand, a thin bracelet calling her attention… She was wearing one identical to it too. One she didn't remember ever having before waking up in this universe... or whatever it was.

"Look, I panicked when I woke up," Supergirl said. "I didn't expect to wake up married after seeing another world destroyed because I couldn't save it. I panicked and I ran. I came back because I wanted to tell you the truth about what happened. I figured I owed it to you."

"So you could manipulate me again into being on your side," Lena nodded.

The look the superhero gave her, was too… indecipherable to Lena. But then again, she'd never really known her, as much as she believe she did.

"I told you that not telling my identity, might have been the biggest mistake of my life," she murmured. "I wasn't lying. I wasn't going to make the same mistake again."

Slowly, Supergirl walked closer to her. And she did something, that made her suit fold into itself until it disappeared, leaving her with cozy clothes, that were so Kara and her glasses in her hands. She ended up closer to Lena, closer than they have been since their confrontation at the fortress.

A shudder ran across Lena's spine. She almost took a step back, but she refused to cower in front of a liar.

"But, you also needed to know the whole truth. We are married in this universe, that changes things," Kara whispered.

"Not for long, I assure you," Lena defiantly said, raising her chin to look directly at blue eyes. "I don't want to be married to a liar."

The blonde nodded, seemingly sad, "I understand. If that's what you want, we can have a human divorce."

Lena frowned, the way Kara referred to a divorce getting her attention, "What do you mean human?"

Kara didn't react to the question, instead she looked at her with an expression Lena recognized as regret.

"I do wonder how we— they fell in love in this world," she said instead.

Lena had to restrain herself from reacting.

"How did she tell… you? How did you react? Was there any consequence? How was the wedding?" Kara continue to ask, clearly not expecting an answer.

Lena crossed her arms, refusing to even acknowledge the questions, instead she repeated her own.

"What do you mean human, Kara?"

The blonde sighed, rubbing her forehead, her glasses still in her hand, "I know you won't believe me anymore," she started, very low. "And In all honesty, I just figured it myself… Yesterday, I suppose."

Lena knew this Kara, or at least she thought she did. The nervous rambling, getting to a point of almost not saying anything at all. She lifted her eyebrow, daring.

"The reason as to why I waited so long to tell you the truth, why I was holding you, to this very different standard to anyone else in my life," Kara continued. "I didn't lie at the Pulitzer party," she clarifies. "It just took me a while to recognize the truth behind why I wanted to protect you so fiercely, why I didn't want to lose you, more than anyone else in my life if you knew the truth."

Lena didn't say anything, she simply waited, looking at Kara's nervously playing with the glasses in her hands.

"It doesn't fix anything, of course. It's not an excuse, but it finally made it make sense, to me," the blonde said, without looking at Lena. "Why telling you felt different to anyone else who I did tell, though I must say I haven't told as many people as you think I did."

Before she could continue in her babbling Lena sarcastically interrupted her. "And what's this magical revelation?"

The answer didn't come for a few seconds, Lena was rapidly losing her patience.

"Today we fought the Anti-Monitor again," Kara started after a moment, the rambling was clearly about to start again. "I thought I was going to die… Again. When I use my heat-vision for long periods is easier, if I access my feelings… the loss, the pain, the rage." She shrugged, "it's usually being sent away by my parents, Krypton's destruction, how Clark left me as a kid in the care of strangers. But today I thought of something else… Someone else. Lena losing you felt exactly like losing Krypton all over again. I wasn't willing to test fate by telling you, when one of the first things your mother said to me was that you were going to hate me when you figured it out." Kara lifted her head, looking directly at her eyes. She had big crocodile tears in hers. "But lying to you— for so long— it only assured that that would be the only outcome possible, because you are right, you told me how you felt about lies and deception."

Kara took one of Lena's hands, and it was so deliberately slow and careful that Lena could have stopped it at any moment. Maybe she should have.

"I apologize, Lena," the blonde whispered, clearly trying not to be too emotional. "I know you don't think I'm sincere, and I doesn't change anything, but I'm sorry."

Lena hated it.

She hated how sincere Kara sounded, looked. How clearly she was trying not to openly cry, not letting the tears fall from her eyes.

Lena hated that she believed it.

"I love you, Lena," she whispered. "I'm sorry, I should've told you the truth about everything… My alienness, my identity, my feelings, but I never used you, I promise yo—"

Lena grabbed the super's pastel colored sweater, and kissed her so she would just shut up.

Hands flew to her waist, and then one of them cradled her cheek, too soft, too delicately, and Lena hated it.

She didn't want delicate, she wanted it to hurt, to bruise.

Kara seemingly didn't feel the same. Not quite.

"Lena…" she rasped against her lips, pleadingly.

"I don't want to hear more apologies right now," she said. "Or excuses, or revelations. I don't want you to say anything else, it fixes nothing, but I want you to prove it to me."

There was a pause, as if time had suspended itself just waiting for Kara to crash their lips together again. This time with the appropriate aggressiveness and hunger that Lena was craving.

The crushing feeling of the blonde's arms around her waist almost made all the heartbreak worth it. And she let herself enjoy the passion with which her wife was kissing her, manhandling her with. Hands roamed freely and desperate through her body, and for the first time, at least that day, she felt real… desired. Cherished, almost.

She hated it.

She loved it.

She never wanted to stop.

And yet, her brain couldn't stop thinking about the question that the blonde never answered, "what do you mean human divorce?"
