She glanced around the grimy room in which she was being held. It was dim, the only light coming from the rectangular window high on the paint-peeling wall behind her and the single dangling, flickering lightbulb above her head. There was a hose with a spray nozzle on one wall and on another, an assortment of knives and other sharp, torture implements. The metal chair she was tied to was directly above a drain. The dark grey walls and floors had some sort of stains on them—rust, maybe; blood, likely. The smell was something between sour milk and rancid garbage, with just a hint of something metallic.

It was right out of a cheesy horror movie. She supposed it might put some on edge, maybe even frighten them, but Lena Luthor? Not a chance.

Growing up in the Luthor mansion, she could dissociate with the best of them.

“Tell us what we want to know!”

SMACK!

Another slap across her face. Lena had all but gone numb on her face at that point. They’d been at this for several hours, at least, getting absolutely nowhere. At least one of Lena’s fingers was broken, another was definitely dislocated (judging by the angle at which it currently rested), she was pretty sure she would come out of this with at least one black eye, as well, and she had a small cut underneath her jaw from the knife that’d been pressed there early on in the process.

Amateurs, Lena had thought.

“You’re persistent, I’ll give you that,” she laughed mirthlessly.

“Oh, you think this is funny?” one of her kidnappers asked before he socked her straight in the stomach.

“Oof!” Any oxygen she had in her was forced out and she was sent into a coughing and gagging fit trying to catch her breath. The chair she was tied to nearly toppled back from the power of the hit.

Lena had been prepared for this. Long ago, when she’d first joined the Luthor family, she was almost immediately enrolled in special self-defense and combat classes, weapons training and psychological training. They assumed she or Lex might be kidnapped by Lionel’s business competitors or someone trying to make a few million from a ransom scheme. She doubted anyone ever would have guessed she’d be taken for her supposed knowledge of an alien’s—or, specifically, a Kryptonian’s—identity.

When she finally regained control of her breathing she replied with a sneer, “It’s a little funny, yeah.”

One of the three men in the room slammed his hands down on the arms of the chair in which she was restrained, squeezed his ham hock like hands around her wrists—Lena may have twinged slightly at the pain; she was only human, after all—and got right in her face.

“Tell us!” he bellowed through gritted teeth, the veins in his neck bulged with tension.

Lena rolled her eyes. “If I tell you, then you’ll just kill me right after. So, I see this going one of two ways: One, I keep you on the hook long enough that you all just get sick of it and kill me out of frustration. Or two, and this is the more likely scenario, I keep you on the hook long enough that Supergirl finds us, you all go to prison, and I get to go back home.”

Of that, she was certain.

She saw the flash of fear in his eyes, but it was quickly replaced by anger. He growled and pushed back, turned towards the steel door. He motioned for his associates to exit first before he whirled around.

CRACK!

“Fuck!” Lena cried out; her eyes watered as blood spilled from her now broken nose.

“Not so funny anymore, is it, bitch?”

He lumbered out, shaking out his fist before he slammed the door behind him.

I just have to bide my time. Kara will find me.

Kara had been back from the Phantom Zone for about a month. She was just barely back in the groove of things; still experienced some PTSD type episodes at night. Lena had been woken during the late hours to a light tapping at her window and found Kara floating outside—a timid smile and a questioning eyebrow, as if to say “may I come in?”—at least half the nights since her return. She was helpless to do anything but grant Kara’s silent wish.

After everything they had been through, after all the time they had lost both to stubbornness, pettiness, misunderstanding and literally lightyears of physical distance, there was no answer but yes to be given. No place other than Lena’s bed and arms and heartbeat for Kara to seek solace and safety and peaceful quiet. Their lives, their traumas, so different but so similar. Two sides of the same coin. Their destinies undeniably and infinitely intertwined with one another.

Kara would curl into her side, wrap a strong arm around Lena’s midsection and rest her head under Lena’s chin. She would listen to her even, deep breaths; listen to the solid and steady rhythm of her heart and she wouldn’t move a muscle.

At first, Lena fell asleep as she tenderly rubbed Kara’s back. Then, the more nights Kara came to see her, she wrapped her arms more securely around strong shoulders; she ran her long, slender fingers carefully through Kara’s hair. The night she placed a gentle kiss at the crown of Kara’s head, is the night she fell asleep feeling the wet of Kara’s tears burn through her pajama top; the sound and sensation of Kara’s wretched sobs still reverberating through her body, her penthouse, and her soul.

A week or so after that, she saw Kara off again from her balcony just after dawn. Kara suggested a movie night, just the two of them, and again Lena was powerless to deny her.

“There are some things I want to talk about with you,” Kara had said shyly.

Lena merely smiled and nodded and waved as she felt the gentle whoosh from Kara’s departure.

She was on her way to the Tower later that morning when her car was slammed into from the rear and she was forcibly removed from the driver’s seat; a bag thrown over her head with some chloroform over her nose and mouth and a couple of punches to the stomach for good measure.

The kidnappers had the sense to remove her watch, probably their way to prevent her from keeping time. Unwittingly preventing her from being able to send a signal to Kara.

The only thing they did right, she’d thought.

She had dozed off a couple of times. Hadn’t been fed once. Had been given very little water. She could still feel her feet, despite the how tightly they were bound to the chair legs, though the lack of circulation had become more apparent when she tried to wiggle her toes.

Honestly, she was just happy the bleeding from her nose had stopped. She wasn’t keen on how swollen her eyes had become, though. She had made a mental note to look into plastic surgeons to properly fix her nose when she returned to National City.

Her head lolled forward, shoulders hunched in the same direction, until loud sounds and music blared into the room. Her body snapped up, jarred, and her muscles stiffened instantly from the dehydration that had started to set in.

Sleep deprivation. Great.

She tried her best to mediate and close off. It was only a marginally successful effort.

At some point later—hours, days, minutes?...—they removed her from the chair and instead placed her on her knees, her hands still bound at the wrist and hoisted over her head; immovable thanks to the rope being threaded over a utility pipe above and then fastened to an anchor point on the floor.

Punches and knees to her sides, her kidneys, her stomach rained fast. She gave them nothing but the bile in her stomach and the moans and groans of pain.

They left her to dangle for a long while, nothing but the flickering light and the strain of her shoulder and back muscles to keep her company.

When they returned, they cut her down and threw her battered body onto a table. Her shirt was unceremoniously ripped open, buttons flying in every which direction. Her eyes remained closed, even with the sound of metal scraping nearby. She refused to give them the satisfaction of fearfully whipping her head toward any menacing sound.

When the knife sliced the skin just above her left eyebrow open, she gritted her teeth; hissed through the sting.

When the first metal skewer was inserted, ever so slowly, into her right side at an upward angle, she had no choice to but scream out. Tears once again trickled from her eyes.

“It doesn’t have to be like this,” one of her torturers whispered; his breath reeked of some awful combination of liquor and whatever disgusting food on which they’d gorged themselves. “Just tell us her name and this can all end. I’ll even make it quick. I promise.”

Lena looked at him—his grizzled and gaunt features and purple face betrayed his alcohol-soaked liver. His shaky hands possibly betraying something else. By the look of it, he wasn’t long for the world.

“Fuck you,” she whispered as harshly as she could muster before another skewer was shoved into her body—scraped her ribcage, dangerously close to her lung—and another excruciating cry ripped through her.

Kara, where are you?

Later, when she’d been placed back in the chair, her third captor asked, “Is it really worth your life to protect an alien?”

Lena, with her head tilted over the back of the chair, felt a deep, twisted laugh bubble through her chest and up her throat. She lifted her head, felt the blood drip down from the cut above her eyebrow that had reopened, and looked him up and down; a demented smile on her lips. His haggard appearance almost rivaled hers, she wagered. She could see it in his tired brown eyes.

“Without a doubt,” she replied between breaths. Her body was exhausted after all she’d been put through. “She’s more human than you’ll ever be.”

The man rolled his eyes and shook his head and signaled for the other two. They approached her from the sides and gripped her biceps tightly while the brown-eyed man went behind her and placed a wet towel over her face, then proceeded to pour water over it in short intervals as they all screamed at her to tell them Supergirl’s name.

At first, she tried to reason with herself that the water was refreshing. She was dehydrated, and they were giving her excess of it now. But as it rapidly filled her nose and throat, caused her to gag and sputter and choke, she was slapped back to reality.

They poured, she choked, they set her down. Wash, rinse, repeat. They did it at least five times; she’d lost count after three. Her chest ached from the force of the coughs to try to expel the water; the exertion of straining against not just the ropes, but also their hands.

When they finally gave up and left her alone, Lena swore she saw Nia—in full Dreamer regalia—in one of the corners. Her face twisted in horror at Lena’s state. But when she tried to call out for her, Nia was gone.

Lena hunched forward and allowed a few silent tears to drop from her weary eyes before she tried to get any bit of sleep before the next round began.

The three men were huddled together in the corner nearest to the door. They whispered angrily, but quietly enough that Lena could only make out every third or so word they uttered.

“This is useless. She either doesn’t know or she’s dumb enough to die for the alien,” one said.

“He’s right. We’ve held her for too long. Any longer and we risk getting made. We gotta cut and run,” another agreed.

The third, the one with the fists like ham hocks, looked over his shoulder in disgust.

“Fine,” he barked out. “But we’re gonna have one last bit of fun with it before we go.”

A demented laugh gurgled from his mouth as he exited the room briefly, then returned with a generator and some jumper cables.

Lena’s jaw clenched. She had accepted the possibility that Kara and the rest of the Superfriends might not find her in time. She was prepared for this to be her final bow. But like hell she would go down without at least some verbal jabs.

She cocked her head to the side, pointedly stared at the generator and then back to the group.

“I have to say, I really don’t understand your plan,” she retorted, deadpan in tone. “Is it a get caught plan? Because if that’s what you’re going for, you’re doing great.”

She threw in a sarcastic thumbs up, for good measure. Well, as best of a thumbs up one can give when they’re bound by the wrist to a metal chair.

Mr. Ham Hock’s nostrils flared. He shoved the generator into the arms of Brown Eyes as he charged forward.

“You think this is funny, you little bitch?!”

SMACK!

He punched her so hard in the mouth she tasted the blood before she felt it. She hurriedly made sure he hadn’t knocked out any of her teeth before she continued.

“You’re so fucked!” Lena cackled maniacally. “When she sees what you’ve done?” Lena spat out the blood that had pooled in her mouth. “She won’t kill you…but you’ll wish she had.”

“That’s it!” Ham Hock bellowed. “I’ve had enough of your shit. Give me that generator!”

Brown Eyes glanced between his two associates, and then to Lena, trying to determine the truthfulness of her statement. Ultimately, he decided it wasn’t worth it any longer. He shoved the generator into the Purple-faced People Beater’s arms and ran out of the room, leaving the door ajar. Lena heard his footsteps grow softer and softer.

“That fuckin’ pussy,” Ham Hocks grunted. “Bring that shit over here.”

Purple Face did as he was told and set the generator at Lena’s feet. His head whipped around not a minute later, toward the door.

“D-did you hear something?” he stuttered, worry splashed across his emaciated face.

Ham Hocks attached the jumper cables to the back legs of the chair, not paying attention to his remaining partner.

“What?”

A sickening shriek echoed through the building at that moment.

“That was Gary. Something got Gary!”

“Shut the fuck up, Carl, and turn on the gen-”

Ham Hocks didn’t get the chance to finish his sentence as a flash of red and blue coursed through the dim haze.

She heard more than she saw Carl slam into the wall behind her and his pained moan that followed.

Ham Hocks didn’t get as lucky. He was knelt next to Lena one second, then the next she heard various punches and groans and squishes and one very precise snap and scream.

That was either an arm or a leg.

Kara loomed terrifyingly over his writhing body.

When Lena heard the telltale sound of her heat vision warming up, she knew she had to stop it before Kara did something she would regret—something that would haunt her for the rest of her life. Kara was good. Lena would never let her become a killer, even if those assholes deserved it.

“Kara,” she whispered just loud enough for only Kara to hear.

Kara’s body stilled immediately; her shoulders wilted. She turned slowly on her heel, eyes still glowing red, until…

“Lena,” she exhaled, the serene blue returning.

“I knew you’d come,” Lena murmured as she battled against her body and mind’s desire to turn off and finally recuperate.

Kara nearly tripped over herself as she settled next to Lena’s side. She worked quickly to remove the bindings from Lena’s wrists and ankles. And then she worked up the courage to take in the damage that had been done.

Her fingers ghosted over bruises and cuts and puncture wounds. She didn’t dare touch Lena’s body. The tears pooled in her eyes and spilled over when she got to Lena’s bloodied and battered face.

“I’m so sorry I failed you,” Kara’s voice cracked and her bottom lip trembled.

“You didn’t, darling,” she assured. Lena’s eyes fluttered closed as her body’s resolve finally gave out. She fell forward into Kara’s waiting arms.

Kara had to stop herself from squeezing Lena tightly in relief that they’d finally located and rescued her. By the sound of Lena’s slowed heartbeat, and the obvious state of injury, she knew she needed to get Lena back to National City and to a hospital as quickly as possible.

She took a steadying breath before activating her comms device.

“Sentinel, I’ve got Lena. She needs medical assistance, so I’m getting her back to National City right now. There are two of her captors in the room, unconscious and…hurt. The third is near the building’s entrance.”

“Copy that, Supergirl. We’ll see you back home,” Alex replied.

Kara carefully lifted Lena in her arms and wrapped her cape around her to keep her as warm (and as covered) as possible on the flight home.

The kidnappers had gone to Nevada—close enough for a quick escape, desolate enough to not attract much attention—and refurbished an abandoned grocery store with an old lead-lined meat locker. The lead was the only reason Kara couldn’t find Lena’s heartbeat on her own.

Thank Rao for Nia, she thought as she sped out of the building and shot up into the air.

Two weeks, a rhinoplasty, several pins and dressing changes, a splint and a cast, and countless rounds of fluids, antibiotics and food later, Lena was finally released from the hospital. Kara never left her side once, even at Lena’s (and Alex and J’onn’s) insistence that she at least go home and shower.

When it was clear she wouldn’t, Alex finally brought her several changes of clothes and her toiletry kit.

Kara slowly pushed Lena’s wheelchair toward the hospital’s main entrance, while she listened intently to the doctor’s aftercare instructions.

“Is the wheelchair really necessary?” Lena wondered aloud, as if she didn’t know it was hospital policy. A small smirk formed on her lips as she waited for Kara’s-

“Yes! I would have wheeled your bed down here, if they had let me,” Kara huffed.

“I’m sorry, Dr. Leonard. She’s very protective,” Lena reached back as best she could and jokingly patted Kara’s arm.

“Ignore her, she’s clearly still high on her pain meds,” Kara quipped back.

Dr. Leonard, a dead ringer for Viola Davis (in Lena’s estimation), laughed good-naturedly. “You seem to be in good hands, Ms. Luthor. Please don’t hesitate to call if either of you has any questions.”

They parted ways with waves as the automatic doors opened and Lena was greeted by the bright, bright sun. She closed her eyes and basked in its warmth, a content smile on her face, while Kara put the brakes on the chair and loaded their things into the waiting town car.

In total, she'd been missing for four days. If anyone had asked Lena, she would have said it felt closer to four years.

She felt Kara’s hand slide over her own and when she opened her eyes, found Kara kneeling next to her, much like she had the day she was rescued. Lena fondly trailed the backs of her fingers along Kara’s cheek, eliciting a small blush.

“You ready to go home?” Kara inquired softly.

“More than,” Lena replied happily.

They finally had their movie night. After Kara made sure she got back to her apartment safely, she took off for a quick stop back at her apartment for an overnight bag.

She touched back down on Lena’s balcony in less than five minutes.

About halfway through Don’t Tell Mom the Babysitter’s Dead, Lena snuck a glance at Kara, who was halfway under the covers, nestled snugly in Lena’s bed.

She took in the firm jawline in contrast with her soft cheeks. She trailed her eyes along the ridge of Kara’s nose, lingered on her plump lips as they happily recited the lines, then moved down to her long, slender neck. She felt her heart skip a beat and her breath hitch.

Kara noticed. She paused the movie and turned her attention fully on Lena.

“What’s up?” she asked with a light smile.

Lena’s brow crinkled as she weighed her potential question.

“What is it you wanted to talk to me about?” she tried, biting her lower lip.

Kara’s eyes tracked it expertly for a moment, her tongue darting out to wet her own. Then she shook her head, confused. “What are you-” she paused. “Oh.” She lowered her gaze and swallowed thickly. Until she didn’t.

“Kara…”

“Lena, what are you-” was all Kara got out before Lena’s lips were mercifully on hers.

They both had a lot of healing ahead of them, both physically and mentally. But they would do it together, as fate intended.
