    "Oh, oh, sure," Kara said. There was a lull; a moment, and then the sound of Kara's feet padded to leave. "I'll, uh, I'll make you a bowl just in case-"

    "Kara?" Lena interrupted, turning to watch her leave.

    "Yea?" she asked, spinning around with a hopeful, glass-half-full voice.

    "We should discuss what happens. When we get back."

    "When we…" Kara parroted slowly, her hope fading in the face of Lena's solemness.

    "I'm sure Alex will have questions," Lena continued, voice even. "And the DEO. You've been missing for months, and a Luthor is responsible. I'll comply with everything-"

    Kara stepped forward. "You don't have anything to worry about-"

    "You don't know that-"

    "Yes I do. No one is going to hurt you, Lena. It was Lex. Lex did all of this. We both know it-"

    "Lex has had months to build a case-"

    "I don't care. I don't care how long it takes me: I will convince Alex, J'onn, anyone - everyone," Kara pressed. "The-the only thing you need to worry about is what to do with all your free time without me to deal with. Ok?"

    Lena nodded because her throat wouldn't let words out.

    Kara smiled reassuringly and left.

    And there was just one problem.

    A knock. A final knock.

    "Hey," Kara called softly.

    "Can't sleep?" Lena said from her oversized, rumpled mattress. A less-crusty, mild-smelling jumpsuit was laid across her lap. Atop that was a scroll while a soft glow of a lamp came from overhead.

    "I feel like a kid on Christmas eve."

    Lena hummed.

    "I, uh… I also thought you might be cold," Kara said with a gesture at the cape clutched in her hand. A gust of well-timed wind crashed against the shelter. Lena fought back a shiver and glanced between the cape and Kara.

    "What about you?"

    Kara shrugged as she laid the fabric on the edge of the bed. "I'll be fine. I've still got a bit of the sun lamp juice."

    Lena couldn't fight the frown that scrunched her brow.

    "Goodnight, Lena."

    "Kara," Lena called after a moment. A head poked back through the threshold, and Lena swallowed her second-guessing. "There's plenty of space."

    Lena had spent months stranded on an alien planet building a ship that would take her 27.632 lightyears back to Earth. Her primary nutrition came from eating amorphous goop while her hygiene was sponsored by Power Goop-protected waters. Her everyday life included blocking out the howls of Goobs and resolving her anger around a deceptive brother who left her to die.

    Sharing a bed with Kara Danvers, her former best-friend-turned-lone-companion, was hardly the strangest thing when faced with all of that.

    And yet the nervous way Kara climbed onto the mattress mirrored the tremor in Lena's own pulse. Awkwardly, Kara tugged the cape over them, careful to leave a valley between them. She rolled onto her side, back to Lena, and for that Lena was both relieved and disappointed.

    "Is this, uhm, this ok?"

    Lena nodded because words were dangerous.

    "Good, that's good uhm, g-goodnight," Kara called over her shoulder.

    It shouldn't have been strange. They'd done it before, once upon a time; in a smaller bed no less. Yet Lena had hardly moved: sitting upright, scroll still across her lap save for the moment it was lifted to situate the cape, and now she was no closer to sleep than she had been that morning.

    She sat statue-like for an unknown length of time, the dim light glowing across glyphs she no longer processed. Instead, her mind raced with everything else. The years, months, and days; the truths, lies, and in-betweens. The schisms, bandages, and scars.

    The familiar cry of Goobs became a source of solace to Lena's mind. She focused on the sounds; the drone-like qualities. Had she not been so focused on finding a way home, she might have appreciated the chance to study the world: its vegetation, fauna, technologies, and regions.

    Listening now, she heard notes of sorrow. Had they always been there? Clouded by fears of the worst, had she overlooked the nuance of their harmonies?

    "Wha-sat?" Kara asked, stirring at the latest call.

    "Just Goobs," Lena said simply.

    "Mmm, sound close," Kara mumbled groggily, turning toward Lena.

    "That's probably just the rest of the sun lamp juice talking."

    Kara chuckled but didn't open her eyes. Instead she curled into Lena. Instead an outstretched hand wrapped its way around her leg. Instead she sighed comfortably into the lull of sleep. Lena swallowed and hoped Kara couldn't hear the erratic thrumming in her chest.

    She didn't, but not because Lena was particularly clever and capable of muting her chest. It was because a cry rippled across the desert and drowned out anything within three hundred feet.

    Kara's eyes shot open and Lena's fingers crunched the brittle scroll. A hand retreated.

    "That sounds too close," Kara said, sitting up and squinting through the tiny slit of window. "Rao, it's right there," Kara whispered. She clambered to the edge of the bed before Lena could process what 'it' was.

    Lena took one glance and saw the oversized silhouette less than a football pitch away.

    "Kara," Lena said, jumping off the bed and reaching for Kara's forearm, "it's fine. They've never gotten past the door before. It's just curio-"

    A thunder of footsteps barreled across the desert ground. The ground under Lena's feet shook. The walls shook.

    "It's getting closer," Kara replied. "We need to go-"

    An explosion of sound ripped and rattled through the space. Lena felt Kara's arm pull her into her chest as dust and debris blew through the corridor into the bedroom.

    "Is that-?"

    A howl erupted and Lena understood immediately: the Goob was inside.

    Kara was half a step ahead of her, already aiming for the doorway.

    "Wait," Lena hissed, unwilling to detach from Kara. "It might leave. It might not notice-"

    Another crash billowed and sent Lena yanking Kara with her back into the room.

    "It's hurt. It's calling for more; for help."

    "How do you know?" Lena asked, fear betraying the way her voice cracked. She didn't have time to reprimand it away, so instead she clung tighter to Kara's wrist.

    "Because I've seen it before. It's a brawl gone wrong."

    "Then we sneak out. We leave and-"

    "And lose weeks worth of fuel and risk the ship and all of our calculations?" Kara replied far calmer than Lena felt. "No way. I'll distract it-"

    "Because that worked so well last time-"

    "Last time there were like ten of them. One injured Goob isn't going to be a problem-"

    "Like hell you're going out there."

    Lena glanced between the doorway that led to the giant-sized danger and the very human Kryptonian she held closely to her side.

    "-and you get the fuel to the ship," Kara finished, looking far too resolute for having suggested the insane. "It's at the base of the manhole. You get it onto the ship, and I'll meet you there."

    "We can do it together," Lena said. "We'll just both go-"

    Another guttural howl rattled through the shelter. Lena shivered. Kara's shoulders squared. Her stupid cape sat abandoned on the bed.

    "It'll hear us. If it sees us in the tunnel we're toast: it's faster and we'll be cornered-"

    "How exactly do you plan to distract it-"

    Kara cut Lena's question off with a shrug. "You didn't disassemble the taser wand, right?"

    Kara's confidence did little to sway Lena's nonexistent faith in the plan. In the nonexistent plan. In the crapshoot chance that this would work. A hole burst overhead and sent debris flying into the room.

    "We don't have time to debate, Lena," Kara added. Perfectly timed howls in the distance sided with Kara. It made Lena's heart skip. "Do you trust me?"

    "Not with a houseplant," Lena retorted dryly. Her fingers clasped more firmly at Kara's forearm.

    "Fine, but I'm still going."

    Lena clenched her jaw and followed Kara's eyes looking down to where Lena's grip linked them, and Kara offered a silent lift of her brow.

    "The wand is dead. There's a spare battery at the charging station," Lena instructed, staying close to Kara as they navigated the hall toward the threshold. Crumbled walls made it nearly impossible, but eventuality persisted: the lumbering Goob came into view. It was bigger than Lena remembered. Six clawed appendages, armored skin, and a towering frame that, in the oversized space, seemed more at home than her and Kara ever had, labored across the room. Two arms on its left side hung uselessly. Thick toxic yellow dripped from heavy gashes, leaving thick, viscous droplets in its wake.

    "See? Easy," Kara whispered with a quick nod and a move to leave. Lena's hand reached to stop her one final time.

    "Don't do anything stupid," she added, earning a wry smile before Kara slipped into the shadow and out of Lena's sight.

    Now instead of sheet metal enveloping overhead, the dusty hues of an incoming sunrise shone through a Goob-sized hole. Wreckage made the interior almost unidentifiable, and the dread of feeling like a puny ant didn't leave Lena with a lot of confidence.

    She lingered only until Kara gave her a quick nod and hurled a leftover gadget across the room. The distraction worked, and Lena's legs took over. She ran. She ran harder than she'd ever run in her life through the main space, around pools of Goob blood, and past the piles of forgotten gadgets with eyes trained on the tunnel's entrance. She didn't look back, and she didn't slow. Lungs burning and heart racing, she sped through the tunnel where the stockpiled crystalized Power Goop sat waiting to be brought to the surface.

    It took an eternity and a stitch in her chest to get there. When she did, she grasped the enormity of the task: there were twenty satchels.

    Twenty. All brimming with delicate, fragile, meter-long crystals intended to power them back to Earth.

    She swallowed against her dry mouth and scooped up two, immediately draping them over her shoulders. She glanced up the length of the ladder and, after a quick calculation, another two were added. It was an impossible weight, but twice as many trips was more.

    Step by step.

    Crystals clinked.

    Rung by rung.

    She climbed. Straps dug into her shoulders, her lungs screamed for rest, her hands slipped with sweat; distractions.

    When she found the surface, she gasped; a break. Coole air filled her lungs. A blast of sand followed. A cough came next. Laden with the impossible, she unlatched the ship's cargo hold.

    A shaky lob sent the first into place. Frangible crystals clinked and clanged against the metal edges. Tired arms sent the second spilling to the ground. A cry of defeat was drowned by a gang of Goobs on the horizon. Time felt endless and like nothing at all.

    She blindly grabbed and slid the loose crystals into the holder before lugging the third then the fourth into the same space.

    The Goobs cried louder.

    She stumbled backward; shuffled toward the manhole; descended the ladder to the remaining sixteen bags.

    Another four; another trip; another tremble of struggling muscles. A renewed howl sent her foot slipping and eyes glancing back down the tunnel: darkness.

    She surfaced with a huff and dragged the satchels, one at a time, to the cargo hold.

    Another howl.

    It was taking too long.

    Kara was nowhere, and it was taking too long. Lena squinted toward the horizon where the shelter poked out of the landscape; where a hole had been ripped out of the roof.

    And then another howl.

    Adrenaline sent the next eight bags to the surface. It heaved them into the cargo hold.

    The sun's shadow peeked over the horizon; a reminder; a deadline.

    Another descent. Fatigue dropped her the final three rungs to the dirt below.

    A crawl took her to the final bags. She dragged them through exhaustion and began the last ascent.

    Step by step.

    Crystals clinked.

    Rung by rung.

    A tear overshadowed.

    "No- no!"

    Time slowed.

    A weight lifted; another plummeted.

    Lena stared down at the bag; at the crystals broken, shattered, and scattered. Her voice echoed through the tunnel; listened to it rip through the silence.

    The silence.

    She paused; froze; held her racing breath.

    Her ears strained but silence permeated. The only soundtrack came from the racing pulse pounding in her ear and the howling wind overhead; a dying symptom of night.

    Nothing.

    Kara.

    Without a second thought, she raced back, satchels forgotten, bare feet slamming against the well-worn dirt path toward the heart of danger, her own lodged firmly in her throat.

    Fear replaced adrenaline; a new motivator.

    The dark tunnel blurred past, but her eyes stayed set on the dim light at the end. It grew closer and stretched further with each step. Again it took a lifetime and no time before she stumbled to a halt just at the edge; just out of view; panting.

    Lena peered into the room - into the silence: carnage, debris, holes the size of semi-trucks, structure hanging precariously, shelves demolished, and no Goob. Then there; there tucked between it all crouched the familiar clad superhero against the far wall.

    Lena let out a held breath and surged ahead, foot clumsily tripping over- she glanced down: the wand. Uncharged and unused. Unbelievable. She picked it up and waved it at Kara's huddled form. "You didn't even use it?" Lena shouted. "The one thing you-"

    "Can we do this later?" Kara whispered. It barely carried across the room. From across the room Lena could see Kara's chest panting. What she couldn't see was the look of outright horror, or the streak of blood running down Kara's hairline, or the way her right arm hung uselessly at her side.

    "Why-"

    A steel beam crashed through the wall and landed where Kara had been moments earlier.

    "Because of that," Kara growled, leap-frogging over bay after bay of demolished shelving units at a very human speed. She ducked just in time to avoid another unidentifiable killing object from decapitating her. "Get to the ship."

    The tone was non-negotiable, and for a moment Lena nearly complied.

    Nearly.

    But then she heard the Goob's howl radiate with rage. Then she heard the desperation in Kara's voice: "Lena, go. Please."

    Then she watched in horror as the Goob cornered the powerless Kryptonian; the powerless Kryptonian who rolled painfully away from the deadly thwack of a makeshift club held in one of the Goob's arms while two others swung with second and third tries; the powerless Kryptonian who stumbled to her feet, unable to sidestep the follow-up blow.

    And, self-preservation be damned, Lena sprinted toward the wreckage, toward the demolished shelves, and toward the unidentifiable charging bay buried under an avalanche of debris.

    A chorus of howls and grunts and falling metal filled the space while Lena, blinded in pursuit, clawed and clawed until her fingers wrapped around the smooth metal cylinder. She wrenched it free and clicked it into place, waiting just long enough to see the wand's tip flicker to life before rushing toward the mammoth of an alien.

    "Between the plates," came a yell; advice that directed the Goob's arm to sweep across another wall of metal, sending it cascading toward the voice. It simultaneously revealed a soft, mucous-covered patch of flesh where the Goob's overlapping armor folded up. "See it?" came the voice, winded and broken.

    A flash of blue darted to the left, distracting the Goob and allowing Lena to close the gap. Every rational instinct screamed to run, but some things weren't rational. She honed in on the distracted Goob, waited for it to raise its arm for another swing, then-

    "Now!" Kara yelled, and Lena, eyes locked on the tender surface, lunged forward and stabbed upward, lodging the electrified tip into the opening.

    Balance broken, Lena's fingers slipped from the handle, and she stumbled to the Goob's right. A swift woosh signified an arm swiping in her direction. She ducked a second arm and rolled against a refrigerator-looking object to avoid the crash of something overhead.

    Then the clashing continued.

    Then dread filled her chest.

    Another mound of metal went spilling through the space.

    She'd missed.

    Her eyes scanned the blind rage, the flailing arms, the tiny glint of an exposed wand.

    Then they fell to the growing pile of metal; to Kara.

    Zero thought went into the way she clambered back toward the staggering beast; the way she blindly scaled a nearby pile of gizmos; the way she leapt and wrapped her fingers tightly to the side of the Goob. The Goob spun, attention pulled from the mound to the very powerless Lena dangling desperately from the shingle of armor. The Goob's arms lifted in unison, revealing the wand. With a final surge of strength, Lena hoisted herself toward the wand and pressed up, forcing the wand further into the tender flesh.

    An arm made contact with her ribs and she dropped with a crash just as a howl of pain erupted from the Goob. Shrieks grew desperate; frenzied. Legs stumbled backwards; sideways. Body slumped into walls, across the space, against the facade. Arms blindly swiped at its armor, a final, desperate search for the device shooting bolts of electricity through its body.

    A final cry; a last wail; a single, ground-quaking tremor, and it was over.

    Lena wasted no time: her eyes tracked to the corner.

    "Kara?" Lena whispered then shouted then cried. "Kara?"

    Dust settled, morning light cast long shadows across the wreckage, and Lena could find no hint of the familiar red and blue suit.

    "Kara!" she shouted over and over, stumbling across and through mounds of sharp, rusting, broken metal. A cut to her leg, then her foot, then her right hand didn't stop her. Layers were ripped away while worse and worse thoughts piled up. She whispered and begged and swore until finally - finally - she saw the first glimpse of blue, then the flicker of a gold S then a sea of deep crimson red.

    So much red.

    A cough. Shatterable and weak.

    Kara coughed. Not Supergirl. Kara.

    "Kara?" Lena gasped, heart, lungs, and being stuck in the knot of her throat. She ripped away the final pieces with strength she didn't possess and crawled to the collapsed form. "Kara? Kara, you're…"

    So much red.

    "Lena-"

    "It's gonna be ok," Lena promised with unfounded authority. "You're gonna be ok."

    Kara's chest rose and fell with remarkable shallowness. The raspiness of each inhale gripped at Lena's own.

    Her shaking hands didn't know where to go; where wouldn't hurt; where would help.

    So much red.

    Her eyes flicked up; surveyed the battlezone; honed in on the med-supplies.

    "Don't," came the same weak voice; a trembling hand fell as a companion to Lena's thigh. "Don't go."

    "I'm not. I won't. I just - I just need to get…"

    So much red.

    Lena reached and squeezed the hand with wavering reassurance and rushed over to their feeble supplies thinking only of the sun lamp they'd disassembled the day prior. Where was it?

    Hands shaking, she ripped through the meager piles of makeshift gauze and aloe-like salve. She searched knowing there was nothing else.

    A sense of dread sprouted in her mind; grew in the pit of her stomach; took root in her heart.

    Not like this. Not this way. Not now. Not when they'd been so close.

    "Fuck!" she screamed, her blood-stained hands sweeping the useless supplies to the floor. A sob broke.

    "L-Lena?"

    Panic. Fear. Self-loathing. Lena looked around: nothing but useless scrolls, broken alien technology, and the sad, pathetic makings of a home.

    Her mind raced. It raced and stumbled and searched. It pled for something; anything.

    Her eyes scanned across; under; over then, finally - finally they looked up, up, up. It was there she found her miracle poking through: the rising sun. She prayed to a deity she didn't believe in for to rise fast enough.

    "We need to move you," she explained once back at Kara's side.

    And Kara, brave, suffering, heroic Kara simply nodded with trust Lena didn't deserve.

    They stumbled to a patch of light, growing brighter through the punctured roof overhead. It was a dusty pink morning. It might have even been beautiful. Kara made it a dozen steps before knees replaced feet, and all Lena could do was help break the fall.

    "Wait here," Lena whispered, earning a broken chuckle from Kara's blistered and bleeding lips. It made Lena's eyes sting. "I'm going to try-"

    A hand she didn't know was clenched with Kara's squeezed weakly.

    "Stay."

    It was barely a sound at all.

    "The sun lamp. I can… I'll find it, and I can-"

    Kara smiled through a wince and shook her head. "You won't beat the sun."

    "But let me try," Lena begged. "I need to try-"

    "Stay."

    "Kara, I won't-"

    "You need it to get home."

    "I am home, you idiot," Lena sobbed, tears rolling freely down cheek and nose, chest aching with everything unsaid. "You're my home."

    A hand settled against Lena's cheek. It was shaky. It was fragile. It was cold.

    "Then stay."

    "This history doesn't need to repeat itself," Kara interrupted softly.

    "But it might."

    "And that's reason enough not to try?" Kara asked.

    Lena sat with the question; with the weight of every past experience that stacked up in defense of a 'yes'. But then she looked up at Kara; at the vulnerability staring back at her.

    "So you want this too?" Lena asked, and Kara broke into a wet laugh.

    "I didn't think I was being subtle," Kara replied. "But yes, I want this."

    "It won't be easy."

    The space between them was lost and two arms wrapped themselves around Lena.

    "You're worth it," Kara whispered, a smile playing against Lena's lips.

    "You're an idiot."

    Breakfast was forgotten. Sort of. Kara seemed sated. Lena however -

    "Ooph."

    Lena pressed down onto Kara's wrists; a front really, given Kara's strength. "My turn."

    "You're-? Oh, oh," Kara flinched away, and Lena froze.

    "Sorry, I didn't-"

    "No, no," Kara rushed. "It isn't, uhm… I've just never, you know."

    Lena scowled. "Ever?"

    "I have, just… I'm better at it alone? I don't have the best track record; losing self-control and super powers don't really mix well," Kara chuckled. "And I don't want to hurt you."

    Lena looked down at Kara and rested a hand to her cheek. "Ok."

    "Yea?"

    Lena refrained from pouting. "Selfishly, no, but I get it."

    Kara nodded. "Thanks."

    And for all intents and purposes, Kara seemed to forget about it.

    Lena did not.

    "Kara?" Lena whispered two days later.

    She watched the oblivious Kryptonian from across the room. "Kaaara?" she whispered again: zero reaction. Zip. Nada. Zilch.

    Lena smirked and reached for the nearest tool. "Hey, Kara?" she called louder this time.

    "What's up?"

    "Catch!" Lena called, and an allien wrench flew past Kara's head.

    "What the hell?" Kara called with a belated cry.

    But Lena only laughed as she closed the distance, climbed up the couch, and slipped into Kara's lap, her legs straddling either side of her. Kara's hands shifted automatically to her waist even through her confused expression.

    "You're powerless," Lena smirked, lowering her lips to Kara's neck.

    "I…" Kara said slowly before the softening of realization spread across her face. "I'm powerless."

    _ _ _

    "Last chance," Kara said, chest heaving. Her skin glistened with sweat, hair plastered to her forehead. She had never looked so human. "We could stay. We'd need to, you know," she panted, "redecorate, obviously. The color palette is so bland, and I think my handy-woman outfit needs an update but-"

    Lena smiled, head resting on Kara's stomach. It hadn't felt natural in so long. She stared out at the red sunset threatening them with a final round of danger, a last stream of grey goop meals, and the waning moments until their return to Earth. Then she turned toward Kara.

    "I think we can solve this little red sun problem when we get back."

    Kara laughed, wrapping her arms tight around Lena and rolling her over to sprinkle her with kisses.

    The rest came without fanfare as endings often do.

    Night fell, and they made quick work of trimming the Power Crystals free. For added security, they plucked a few extra from the secret passage. In no time at all, the maps were updated, the cargo was loaded, and a week's supply of grey goop had been stowed onto the ship.

    Lena spent the final day reviewing calculations not out of dread but out of scientific caution. Kara spent the final day concocting a feast of their favorite goop recipes. They spent the night in tangled limbs taking advantage of a final powerless evening.

    They rose before the sun, and Kara laid her handy-woman outfit to rest with a tiny pout and a few words of thanks. Lena rolled her eyes but offered a moment of silence when Kara asked for it.

    Then they crossed the space for a final time: the furniture was worse for wear, the patched holes were hardly up to code, and the floor was fractured with scars that would rather be forgotten. Lena wouldn't miss it.

    "I don't think we're getting our security deposit back," Kara quipped, and Lena smiled. "And we still have time to carve our initials into the table."

    "I think we've done enough damage."

    "Which means defacing one tiny slab is nothing."

    "Next time, vandal," Lena chuckled.

    "Next time?" Kara grinned. "You thinking of an anniversary trip?" And before Lena could roll her eyes, an arm slipped around her shoulder and a kiss was pressed to her temple.

    No, Lena wouldn't miss it. Everything she needed was coming with her.

    They climbed through the new front door - Kara first then a helping hand pulled Lena out into the rosy light and morning breeze. Lena looked skyward, capturing the fading stars one final time.

    The ship door yawned open and turned Lena's attention back to the metal form and the woman stepping into it.

    "Coming, slowpoke?" Kara called over her shoulder. Lena shook her head and rolled her eyes; now out of affection.

    The latch sealed shut, the panel of lights lit up, and Kara triple-checked the refreshment nook was ready for a mid-morning hydration break. Charts were updated, routes were synced, and intricate seat buckles clicked into place. A bag of goop sat waiting in Kara's lap.

    Lena flipped the row switches and paused on the final one. Her fingers settled over it before her eyes glanced at the initial readings.

    It was working.

    Of course it was.

    She took a final, calming breath.

    "Ready?" Lena asked.

    She turned to Kara and saw her hand extended. Lena reached for it and felt its warmth entwine with hers. It passed along a small squeeze. A promise.

    "Ready."
