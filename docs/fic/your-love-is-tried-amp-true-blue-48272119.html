your love is tried & true blue

Lena loves her little boxes.

They are neat and they are orderly, and they keep all of her feelings just below the surface. Right where they should be.

There is a box of pain, a smaller box for joy. There are several about L-Corp, a scattered amount of childhood memories. Then there are her boxes packed full of her emotions for everyone who has made the slightest imprint on her, filled with horrible twisting feelings that rage in her stomach before she slams the lids and tucks them up on the shelves she cannot reach.

Lena loves her little boxes until Kara Danvers, when one box in particular stands out from the others. When one box becomes full of unbridled happiness and such delicate care that Lena can hardly stand it. When a certain box glimmers with sunlight and Lena is forced to glance back at said box which is much, much bigger than any of the ones around it. When Lena takes the substantial box down from the shelf of her mind carefully, pries it open cautiously, and can hardly face all the love that’s inside.

Kara Danvers comes with breakfasts for dinner and game night on Saturdays. She comes with warm hugs and rose lattes and white mochas and always an extra one for Lena. She comes with her presence and her ferocity, her inability to let Lena into harm's way and her firm belief in all that is good.

It’s frustrating, really, how much she cares for Kara. She’d been able to fight her emotions, had gotten so good at it after every vulnerability became another sword that could be driven through her chest. But Kara Danvers wore her heart on her sleeve, and who was Lena to protest?

The thing about them, about Lena and Kara, was that it had been years in the making. During that first interview when Lena had known Kara was going to be important. At their first lunch date, a tentative slip of something settling in her mind. But time had hurt and time had healed, and even through screaming fights with icy wind settling around Lena’s ankles in the glimmering Fortress, Kara had shone. Even through nearly a year of anger Lena simply could not stay away.

The thing about them, Kara and Lena, is that Lena finds herself feeling a certain way more and more lately. Lillian would call it frivolous. Lex would call it a weakness. Kara would call it love.

Kara herself escorts Lena to her first appointment with Dr. Cohen. Kara brings her a pastry and whispers that she’s so, so proud of Lena and gives her a hug before Lena dazedly makes her way into the therapist’s office, shuts the door, looks at the walls.

While studying the restless prairie in black and white framed behind Dr. Cohen’s curly dark hair intently Lena tells her everything, tells her about her boxes and about the friends she tries desperately not to push away and about her family, the Lex and Lillian of it all, and then about her family, the Brainy and Kelly and Jack and Ruby of it all.

And finally, looking away from the faded grass and its deep blue frame, Lena tells her about Kara.

--

Kara is waiting just outside when Lena is done, toeing the ground and fiddling with the buttons on her phone. She looks up instantly and smiles softly, tilting her head towards the door, and Lena’s breath leaves her in a rush.

“So?” Kara asks softly when they’re outdoors, and Lena fights the urge to just pull her into a big hug right there on the street. Instead she meets Kara’s gaze and smiles, says, “It was good, I think,” and lets Kara beam at her, bite her lip, and whisper her praise one more time.

--

They meet up for dinner on Fridays, they always have. This week, though, neither of them feel like going out. Lena is exhausted and Kara has an article to write and she does, through mountains of Thai takeout.

“What’s a synonym for… or like, a word for, I guess… what's a word for world changing? Or, like,” Kara gestures wildly, fork still in hand and brow furrowed, “like, oh my god, everything’s different now because of this. You know?”

“Revolutionary?” Lena replies, eyebrows raised fondly. Kara’s eyes light up at that and she nods wildly, stuffing noodles into her mouth before leaning back over her laptop and typing at top speed.

“What’s this article about again?” Lena asks, leaning back into the couch. She knows, basically, but there’s something magnetic about Kara when she’s talking about something she cares about. Her articles are all prized to her, from the major Pulitzer-winning exposes to the little puff pieces about holiday celebrations in National City. It’s very Kara, Lena thinks, to care so equally about everything she works on, to put effort in at all times, to blush the same way whenever Lena tells her that her newest piece is good, no matter the contents.

“Oh,” says Kara, pleased, “Well, it’s about the accessibility of art in National City. Like, there’s this new program, right? It works with high schools all over the city and gets all the kids to museums and theater performances and even concerts and stuff, so I’m talking about that, plus just about ethics and art and prices and stuff. You know,” she finishes, slightly pink, and glances at Lena before looking back at her computer, smiling.

Lena just reaches out to touch Kara’s arm, giving it a little squeeze before pulling up the name of the program on her phone, scouring the website for a place to make an anonymous donation.

--

Alex and Kelly get married in the early afternoon, with flowers and a cloudless sky and the perfect amount of breeze in the early fall air. It’s a perfect wedding, really.

And Kara looks so happy, so proud. She’s beaming all through the vows, at the edge of her seat, and when J’onn finally pronounces them married she’s never seen Kara bounce up so quickly.

So when she sees Kara alone at a table, looking lost and a little scared, what else can she do but comfort her?

That’s just what they do. What are friends for, Lena thinks somewhat bitterly as she pulls Kara into her arms, holding her tight.

While she’d been stuck in the Phantom Zone, Lena had spent most nights at Kara’s place.

The first day Alex and Brainy and Nia had been there too, curling up on the couch and the air mattress to go against Lena’s protests and let her take Kara’s bed. But as time had gone on, they’d filtered home. Every so often she’d get Alex and Kelly over, or Nia when she was feeling especially guilty, once even J’onn.

She’d just found it so hard to sleep at home, knowing that Kara wasn’t in her apartment and in her bed, curled in three blankets and draped across the mattress. And Kara’s bed was warm, and soft, and every time she woke up in it she could almost hear Kara beside her, grumbling as her alarm blared.

One night, though, she’d been joined by Alex alone. She’d walked in with her head low and jaw set, throwing herself down on the couch next to Lena.

“Kelly’s with Esme,” she’d said unprompted, “but I just… I really miss her, tonight.”

And Lena knows.

They’d talked for hours. At the beginning of their friendship Kara had always tried to get Lena and Alex to bond, swearing that they’d love each other if they knew each other. How ironic, Lena had thought, that it should happen while Kara was…

“Lena?” Alex had said, when the clock was inching towards midnight and the bottle she was nursing was running on empty, “Can I ask you something?”

Lena had hummed her assent.

“And can you promise not to… get mad, or freaked, or anything?”

“I guess so,” Lena had said, puzzled, fiddling with the edge of Kara’s couch.

“What… what are you, to my sister?”

It had come suddenly, then. She felt scrutinized, felt examined, felt that familiar eye of an older sibling, that look that meant they knew. Alex didn’t look angry, thankfully, but her brow was furrowed and Lena felt her chest tighten. She knew her lip was trembling, willed it to stop.

“I—,” she’d started, and it sounded so pitiful that she cut herself off, and that was when she’d broken, lapsing into sobs, feeling Alex sigh and reach for her, feeling herself pulled into a hug that was so very Danvers, so very comforting that all at once she understood how Kara could depend so squarely on this woman.

“It’s okay, Lena,” Alex had said when she’d calmed down a bit, sobs morphing into silent tears, “it’s okay to love her.”

And now here she stood, holding Kara in quite a similar way, wondering why she was cursed to have so much love from Kara, so much love it felt unbearable, but for it to be just short of what she really needed.

They break apart eventually but Kara stays close, smiles at Lena like she really means it, and they wave at Alex and Kelly until they’re a spot in the distance, until Kara hugs her and murmurs, “Let’s go home.”

--

Four weeks into therapy, when the leaves are starting to change and the air has a bit of the bite that makes Kara inhale deeply whenever they step outside, Lena receives a visitor.

She first notices it Thursday morning when she reaches blindly for her counter to grab a piece of fruit before walking out the door to work and her hand falls through to thin air.

She glances away from her phone screen, scattered with bar graphs, scans the counter and the table and even the fridge, just in case.

There’s nothing to be found.

But she’s running late already, so she grabs a protein bar from above the fridge and makes her way out the door.

She would have thought nothing of it had more strange things not begun to occur. Twice during the workday she could swear she’d seen shimmering light out of the corner of her eye, only to turn and see nothing out of the ordinary, predictably. When she’d arrived home from work she’d realized, while on the phone with Kara, that her couch’s throw pillows were scattered onto one side of the sectional, not spread out the way she always laid them. And as she finally crawled into bed, listening still to Kara talk happily about the dogs that she’d seen outside of CatCo that afternoon, she noticed her alarm clock, unplugged and strewn on the ground with a familiar shimmer faintly around it.

And sure, Lena knows her best friend is Supergirl. She knows that Kara and her team could probably have this figured out in an instant, pin down the odd light Lena keeps seeing and the strange happenings in her peripheral vision. But she’s tired, and… well. And, Kara doesn’t have many opportunities to just be Kara, has confessed that their nightly calls make her days feel normal, and Lena really doesn’t want to be the one to pull Kara out of bed and back to the Tower at this time of night.

Besides, what’s the worst that could happen?

--

It turns out, come morning, that the “worst” is better constituted as just plain strange. Bizarre.

Lena wakes on Monday morning not to the sound of her alarm clock, but to the wheezing of a… something at the foot of her bed. Checking the time, she notes it’s a good three hours before she needs to be up, and the sky is still gray.

Subtly moving her fingers toward the button on the watch Kara had gifted her, Lena moves very slowly into a sitting position, squints down the end of her bed.

There is a man sitting there. A man with strange tufts in his hair, emanating the same shimmering light Lena had thought she’d hallucinated. And he’s sitting there, fiddling with her comforter and making an odd sound halfway between a sob and a laugh.

Lena pushes the button.

At that very moment the man spins around, and Lena jumps out of bed toward her bedside table with a taser inside, and the man groans and says, “No, no, not now,” and Lena lunges behind a column a few feet away.

“Lenaaa,” she hears, and it’s almost astonishing how childish this… intruder… sounds. “Come on, I’m here to help. I’ve been called upon. By the universe. For you, and for Kara Zor—,”

At that moment there is a crash against her window and Lena leaps out from behind her column, taser at the ready, to see Kara. She whirls around, spots Lena, and seems to relax tenfold.

That is, until her eyes fall upon the strange man who is now in the corner, crouched down and face screwed up in anticipation.

“Mxy,” Kara growls, and she moves toward him before he snaps his fingers and disappears, only to reappear a millisecond later levitating in a cross legged position above Lena’s memory foam mattress. Kara’s expression is deadly.

“Kara!” The man— Mxy?— exclaims, as though greeting an old friend. “I’m so glad you’re here. No, don’t do that,” he says, lifting a finger, and Lena turns to see Kara looking resigned. She furrows her brow.

“Mxy, I thought you were reformed!” Kara says, “I mean, when you… well, when you helped me last year—,”

“Oh, I am, I am! I’m here of my own free will. Here to… sort a few things out, shall we say?” At this, he turns to Lena, moving into a strange sort of bow. Lena stares at him, then at Kara, who is gazing back at her looking a strange sort of wrecked.

“Mxy,” Kara says dangerously, “Leave Lena out of this.”

“Well, that would be problematic,” Mxy says, and he raises an oddly arched brow, “seeing as she is the precise reason we’re here. Now, let’s see…”

And then he snaps his fingers.

Lena feels like the floor gives out from underneath her, feels like she did the one time Kara had talked her into a rollercoaster, when the butterflies from their clasped hands had mixed with the stopping of her heart on the tallest drop and rendered Lena dysfunctional.

It’s like she’s falling through a void, and somehow it’s both a split second and eternity that she spends in freefall, heart pounding loudly in the echoing silence.

But then she’s back, both she and Mxy are, except they’re not in her bedroom anymore. No, they’re sitting on a sandy beach, and Lena is suddenly clasping glasses with pink paper beach umbrellas and limes inside.

“Costa Rica,” Mxy announces, leaning back on a hammock and blowing Lena a kiss. “I just couldn’t stand that room for one more second, you know? So… blah. You should consider hiring an interior designer,” he says.

It takes a moment for the comment to register in her mind, still grasping for reality. She isn’t sure how to respond, but is saved the trouble when Mxy leans forward.

“So, Lena Luthor. You are in love with Kara Zor-El, and you don’t want to tell her because she just got out of the Phantom Zone, and everything’s so delicate, and blah blah blah,” he says, gesticulating wildly as he speaks, “Am I right or am I right?”

Lena elects to gape at him eloquently in lieu of an answer.

“That’s alright, hon. I already know.”

“Who are you?” Lena finally blurts, ducking away from Mxy’s attempt to press her nose, brow furrowed.

“Mister Mxyzptlk, of course. Did Kara not mention?” he does an exaggerated pout, sidles up next to Lena. “Last year, when she was just begging me to change the past to make it right with you?”

“She… what did you say?”

“But then, if you don’t know me,” Mxy says, seemingly delighting in avoiding Lena’s question, “I’ve been just dying to meet you ever since I talked with Kara. And of course, now that I’m a free man again, I decided what better use of a lovely morning than to show you a little something, fix things up. What do you think, hmm?”

Lena does not like to be taken by surprise. She does not revel in being baffled, or blindsided, or shocked. The world has an order to it, she firmly believes, and things can be predicted. That’s just the way it goes.

It is safe to say, though, that she has never felt more baffled, or blindsided, or taken by surprise. Mxy is floating oddly in midair, legs crossed, throwing and catching a glowing blue orb in his palms.

She toes the sand, draws a little spiral pattern. If she is a true scientist, it would be shameful to let something as anomalous as this slip by her. If she knows one thing… You’ll regret it if you don’t, Lex had said to her in front of so many experiments and prototypes and maybes and that last time, with Non Nocere in her inventory and pain coursing through her blood.

She could run. She could press and press the signal watch, yell for Kara, see how long it’d take her to appear. She could fight Mxy, or beg him to take her home, or subdue him with threats of Alex Danvers and her array of agents.

And yet…

The little man suddenly looks up from the glowing blue ball, eyes meeting Lena’s exactly. It’s almost a dare.

“What is it you want me to see?”

--

She’s freefalling again, whipping through time and space and whatever else, and then suddenly she is sitting in an unfamiliar living room.

The couch she’s been dropped onto is a deep green, still glimmering with excess blue energy that flickers, spins, and vanishes into the air.

It’s a cozy room, furniture reminiscent of something but location itself a beat or two off from what she knows. There is an open tiled fireplace, mantle set with framed photos, and bookshelves tucked into the corners of the room. The rug beneath her feet is familiar, gray shag carpet and rough around the edges and she knows it, knows it well.

She stands, makes her way around a wide easel and a rickety little lamp to the tall arched windows, with a breathtaking view of the hills, much nearer than usual, and the sun rising softly above them.

Lena wouldn’t be able to say exactly what it was that made her turn slowly to the mantle. A tingling on her back, maybe, something in the air. But she does, and she has to fight the urge to pinch herself at the strange array of items atop the stone.

Her eye goes first to a photograph, framed in cherry wood with little etchings along the edges— a beautiful frame, a gift, most likely. It is a photograph of her and Kara.

Kara’s apartment, the one she knows, is adorned with pictures, many of the two of them. That is not out of the ordinary.

But Lena can’t remember posing for the picture. She can’t remember ever being curled up beside the trunk of a tree like that, with Kara, in blue jeans she isn’t sure she recognizes. She would remember looking that joyful, that carefree.

A parallel universe, perhaps. Not so different from her own, Mxy must have made sure of that… she still knows Kara, after all, and still owns her favorite white blouse and Kara still frames photographs of their life together, here.

Beside the picture is a stack of polaroids, scattered haphazardly as if Kara had meant to put them somewhere but had forgotten, thrown them down at the last minute. But when Lena lifts the stack, she is taken by surprise yet again.

Game Night! is scrawled in permanent marker on the base of the image, a shot of Kelly and Alex and Nia curled up on the couch. But there is a date beside it, assured and unwavering in that permanent ink, and Lena really shouldn't be so shocked as she notes, with some dizzying disbelief, that it hasn’t happened yet.

Flipping through the rest of the stack her hypothesis is proven right. Image after image of herself, in clothes she doesn’t recognize, taken at places she’s never been, all marked with dates she’s never lived.

Lena Luthor is a scientist. She is a woman of logic, of reason. Still… to be confronted with yourself in another reality, in another future?

“Mxy!” She finds herself demanding, and isn’t quite sure if she’s trying in vain. “Can you… what the hell…”

There is a pop, and Mxy is sitting on Kara’s couch. Lena fights the urge to kiss him, extending a shaking hand.

“Where am I?”

“Well, I thought it was clear!” he exclaims, snapping his fingers so that a bowl of purple gummy worms appears in his lap.

“Well, it isn’t,” Lena huffs. “I gather it’s an alternate reality, no more than five years into the future.”

“You would be correct. One and a half, to be exact,” he says cheerily, holding out the bowl in offering. Lena grabs a handful.

“Okay, fine. And this, this isn’t my future?”

“No, it is not. But you’d do well to pay attention, there are parts of this you might want to bring back with you,” he says, attempting to wink and ending up instead with a very odd looking blink.

“Mxy, why?”

“I have merely placed you within a world that will help to dispel, in the simplest of ways, your frankly bizarre idea that Kara is reminded of the Phantom Zone when you are nearby.”

“I don’t— I never said that, Mxy, that’s not—,”

“Don’t bother arguing, I never have the time,” he says, sighing theatrically. “Speaking of time… that should be her now.”

Who, Lena is about to demand, but there is another pop and he is gone once again, only a bit of dust beneath the coffee table in his place.

Her question is answered moments later, when a key turns in a lock and the sound of a very familiar voice floats up to her from a downstairs she hadn’t been aware of.

“No, I told her not to follow the story, but I think she’s going to go ahead regardless, so we’ll have to check on it on Monday—,” footsteps are coming up the stairs, now, an emerging creature waves excitedly at Lena even with a phone wedged on her shoulder, blowing her a kiss before stooping to remove her shoes.

Kara looks different, here, somehow. It isn’t that she’s older, it’s only a year and some, and Lena isn’t sure it’s the alternate reality part either. Kara isn’t taller, isn’t dressed too differently, doesn’t have a new haircut.

No, it’s more intuitive. There’s an ease about her, a confidence with which she sets down her leather briefcase that feels a beat removed from her Kara. The way she speaks, the assuredness in her step, the way she’d looked at Lena…

She looks comfortable, safe in her own skin. Something Lena has never seen before in Kara Danvers, only in Supergirl. Something that suits her well, makes Lena look twice. As if, she thinks to herself, she isn’t always doing double takes when it comes to Kara. Still.

“Okay, I have to go,” Kara is saying as she strides into the kitchen, rooting through the cabinets. “Yeah. I’ll talk to you later, Cat.”

As Kara hangs up the phone she turns back towards Lena, a bag of chips now securely in her arms, and Lena feels a sudden lurch of anxiety as she anticipates Kara speaking to her, Kara expecting some other Lena, maybe one who is well versed in the world, maybe one who is confident in her happiness, maybe one who knows what she wants, for once in her life. Maybe Kara will tell her to leave, maybe she will be startled and call for Alex and her friends will come and take her away from this one familiar place.

“Hi, honey,” is what Kara actually says, reaching into the bag of chips and making her way over to the couch, pressing her lips to Lena’s hair as she passes, “how was your day?”

It’s then that Lena stumbles. It’s then that she feels a strange burning, a white hot sensation on her left hand and lifts it to her face. It’s then that she notices the delicate ring atop her finger, hazy gem on a twisted band. An engagement ring, undoubtedly.

When she gets ahold of him, she may just kill Mxyzptlk.

--

Kara, on this strange Earth, is not too far off from the one Lena knows. She’s chatty and excitable, rambling on about work, where she seems to have found a perfect rhythm working alongside Cat again, and Esme, who Lena learns will be coming over on Saturday for an aunties’ day.

All in all, it feels familiar, except for the glaring reminder of the difference glittering on her finger.

Upon further inspection, the mysterious apartment shows sure signs of such a development. It is clearly theirs, a combination of Kara’s easel and puffy quilt on the bed and Lena’s expensive coffee maker, her own familiar books stacked on the shelves. The sun begins to fade on the horizon, gifting them a view of the browns and golds of the hilly landscape not far off. It is theirs and there is one bed, there are two robes in the bathroom, there is a mutual story told in the pictures that line the yellow-brown walls, one of which seems to detail the precise event that birthed the twisting ring band.

They’ve talked for hours when Kara finally lifts her phone to order Chinese for dinner, giving Lena a small smile as the phone rings, dictating the excessively long order into the receiver.

It’s strange, Lena thinks, to see her like this, just slightly removed from her own life. To see Kara, in her own element, joking with the restaurant over the receiver, sticking her tongue out at Lena, cocking her head questioningly at Lena’s undoubtedly strange stare. Lena looks away.

“Oh, did we have Sprite in the fridge, for game night?” Kara asks after she hangs up, “Kelly says they’re trying to kick the soda habit while they still can but I have to indulge the kid when she’s over here, you know?”

“Um,” Lena says ineloquently, “game night?”

“Yeah, of course! It’s Friday, remember?” Kara says, a little quirk to her eyebrow, affection in her gaze as she leans over to touch Lena’s arm as she stands.

“Right,” Lena says, “right, game night. Obviously.”

“You work too hard,” Kara sing songs from the kitchen. “Shoot, I think we’re out. Okay, I’m gonna swing by the deli, I’ll only be a minute.”

And then Kara is coming up to her, and leaning forward to press a soft, chaste kiss to her lips. “Be right back,” she says, and then she’s taken off through the opened window.

It’s just a peck, just a hint of a kiss, really, but Lena feels it in her very bones. The smell of Kara’s hair, flitting forward into a curtain, the familiarity with which she leans in. That’s what’s so jarring about this, she decides as she takes a shaky step towards the enclosed staircase. The familiarity.

She and Kara are living in a townhouse, of sorts. The walls are dark brick, set with big windows and wood on the floor and ceiling. The staircase leads down to a mudroom, a long hallway with a bathroom, an office space, another bedroom with more golden light reaching in, stretching in the scent of pine and rhubarb, glancing across the floorboards.

It’s a home, that’s the only way she can describe it. Green and brown and lovely, memorable and familiar.

Lena’s not sure if she’s ever had a home before, a home the way this one was, the suggestion of such comfortability that burst forth from every couch cushion. Certainly never like this.

If she gets to be here, if she gets to live out whatever strange fantasy Mxy has dropped her in, she decides then and there that she will do it right.

--

Game night is, as always, a loud affair. There is a significantly lower degree of cursing, due in part to Esme, who sits curled into Kelly’s side for most of the night.

A year and a half may not be a very long time in the grand scheme of things but Lena feels her breath leave her when she sets eyes on Esme for the first time. The girl has shot up several inches, dressed in jeans with flowers embroidered on the seam and with her hair down around her shoulders. She looks older in the odd way that children will sometimes, looks happy.

Then there’s Kelly’s rounded stomach, the extra care Alex seems to be taking with her wife throughout the night. The promise of something new, and Esme looks excited to her core whenever she touches a hand to her mother’s belly, and Lena almost tears up at the looks on all three faces.

Brainy and Nia arrive together, giggle privately to each other on the loveseat as J’onn sets up a board game, exchanging teasing jibes with Esme as M’gann gazes fondly at them from the armchair. It feels right, natural.

Kara is the star of the night, Lena thinks, although that might just be how it seems to her. She is radiant, though, always brighter in company of their friends but today she glows ever more luminescent. Pressed up against Lena, joking with Esme and as viciously competitive with Alex as Lena can remember, it feels like all of the best occasions of their friendship pooled together.

Since Kara, her Kara, got back from the Phantom Zone, large events have been somewhat muted. Lena isn’t sure if it's Kara’s doing or if it’s something Alex anticipated would need to happen, in the way older sisters are wont to do. And Lena really can’t complain, can’t begin to understand, but wishes she could. Wishes, when she sees Kara a little more jumpy in the field, hears her pacing, watch her fiddle with her fingers in a way she never had before, that there was something she could do. That she could hold Kara until the pain went away, she could take it all, that she could fix it.

And so Lena isn't sure if Kara is alright, isn’t sure if she ever will be totally alright again. But here, with this almost Kara who she’s already quite fond of, there is peace. Maybe she could learn something from this Lena, she thinks almost bitterly.

“Lena and I went to that new board game cafe, actually,” Kara is saying, and she is looking over at her with such clear love in her eyes that it’s almost unbearable. It’s like being at home, being beside Kara, but magnified, multiplied by a thousand. Kara has a hand on Lena’s arm, and she’s telling some story that Lena’s sure she’s supposed to remember so she’s nodding along, and it feels almost like everything she’s wanted for so long.

Kara had always been flirty when they’d started their friendship, back before Lena knew she was Supergirl and before their fight and Lex and everything that came later. Kara Danvers meant fond teasing and leaning across the lunch table and prolonged eye contact, she was fierce hugs on the couch in Lena’s office and date crashing and a hundred other things. Lena had thought, initially, that Kara had some strange little crush on her, had leaned into it, before realizing that Kara was just like that. And then the Phantom Zone had happened, and Kara had gotten somehow more clingy, more affectionate. And now this.

“One more round,” Kelly is declaring, “and then we have to go. Esme needs a good night’s sleep if she’s going to get here bright and early for aunties’ day.”

“Yeah, remind me why you felt the need to force me out of bed to bring my daughter here before nine on a Saturday, Kara?” Alex says, raising a pointed eyebrow at her sister.

“Because, it’s my half birthday, remember?” Esme says, rolling her eyes. “We have to make a half of a cake. And we need it to be ready for our special half lunch!”

“Yeah, Alex,” Kara says, smirking at her sister. “Obviously.”

“Fine,” her sister huffs, “but Kelly’s right. One round, then we’re going.”

“And it’s Esme’s turn to choose,” Lena says, her voice sounding foreign, out of place to her own ears, but Esme beams over at her and Kara gives her a tiny smile.

“Better make it a good one,” Kara says, scrunching her nose at her niece, who nods resolutely and goes to pick up a slip of paper from the bowl serving as the charades pool.

As Esme mimes her way through an array of strange actions, Brainy cheering loudly in support, Kara leans over closer to Lena.

“Hey,” she says, mouth nearly on the skin of Lena’s cheek and she shivers at it, “you okay? You’ve been quiet.”

It isn’t her Kara, this isn’t her world, and yet there’s a familiar rush of affection when Kara says something just privately for her, the same urge to just kiss her (and she can, here, but it feels strange to kiss Kara when Kara is back home). It isn't her home but there’s something about this place, something that’s drawn her in and made her welcome.

“I’m all good,” she says, and Kara nods, pleased. “Just tired, I guess.”

“Mmm, okay,” Kara hums. “We’ll go right to bed. Have to be up early for that half a cake, you know.”

Despite herself, Lena giggles into Kara’s hair, making her smile wider. Across the room, Alex groans.

“You know, it really is disgusting. I love my wife more than anything, but you don’t see us… necking in public.”

“What’s necking?” Esme says serenely, setting her final slip of paper down to Brainy’s applause.

“Nothing, sweetie,” Kelly says, “But I am pretty tired. Alex, Esme, are we ready?”

--

The bedroom of this house is cozy, dimly lit before Kara flicks off the lights, bed covered in a soft green duvet, complete with Kara’s quilt from the old apartment. Kara is in bed first, watching from the pillows as Lena roots through the drawers of the cabinet, somewhat taken aback by some of the clothing selections her parallel self owned.

“Leeeena,” Kara groans eventually, “I’m so tired and I won’t be able to fall asleep if you don’t come to bed.”

The ridiculous thing is that Kara is, in some ways, the same in every universe. And since Lena really is the same, she’s unable to argue.

The bed is, in that moment, the most comfortable one she’s ever felt. It might, of course, have something to do with Kara tucking her dark hair behind her ear, pressing a soft kiss to the top of her head as Lena wiggles down into the blankets.

“Goodnight, darling,” she hears herself saying before she remembers that it means something different, here, though honestly it’s always meant only one thing. Kara hums happily, snuggles in close, and rests her chin on Lena’s shoulder.

She falls asleep to the sound of Kara’s breathing.

--

She wakes late to the sound of loud laughter from the kitchen. It’s disorienting to stand up into a room that isn’t hers, to check her email filled with reports by people she doesn’t know. In the bathroom she tries to give herself a pep talk, tries to remind herself that she can call Mxy at any point (though, she thinks bitterly as she spits toothpaste into the sink, the likelihood that he’ll appear and take her home is much too low for her liking).

Kara and Esme are in the kitchen, which looks like it was hit by a bomb of flour. Even the fridge is coated in a thin layer of powder, but the two of them look so excited to see her that Lena can’t even remember to chastise.

“Lena! Guess who’s seven and a half today?” Kara says, coming over to stand beside her, an arm on her waist.

“Not a chance,” Lena says, ruffling Esme’s hair. “Hey, Es. Happy half birthday.”

“Thanks!” the girl says, chewing on her lip as she looks intently down at a cookbook. “Are you gonna help us bake? Cause honestly, Aunt Kara is terrible. She didn’t even realize that baking powder is really important for cakes, and I’m not eating something flat.”

“Hey!” Kara says, outraged. “Is this what being seven and a half does to a girl? I want the nice Esme back!”

“Too late,” Lena says, and Kara looks at her, betrayed. “Darling, where’s my mug?”

Kara’s brow furrows and Lena knows instantly that she’s made a mistake. “What do you mean?” she says, reaching up to open a cabinet door. “It’s always in the same place. Remember, you gave me the whole lecture about not reorganizing the kitchen every month, because your mug never moves?”

 

“Of course,” Lena says, wondering how she can play this off. “I just, uh… I need my coffee, I guess, I’m so out of it.”

Kara gives her a funny look, but nods and turns back to the recipe. “Okay, it says to slice the strawberries in half, I’ll do that, your moms would kill me if I sent you home missing a finger.”

“Fine,” Esme sighs, sliding a cutting board coated in strawberries in Kara’s direction. “You worry too much, Aunt Kara.”

“According to your moms, it’s not nearly enough,” Kara responds, a mock serious look on her face so ridiculous that Lena can’t help but snort.

It diverts Kara's attention to her, which she’s grateful for despite herself because she looks so genuinely happy. It’s a look she hasn’t seen on Kara in a long time, not when they were fighting, not quite the same as it always had been since the Phantom Zone. It’s nice, Lena thinks as she manipulates the expensive coffee machine, to see her like this.

Before she knows it Kara is in front of her, apron coated in flour dust, smile wide, and leaning forward to press a kiss to Lena’s cheek. “Hi,” she says, eyes warm and happy. Lena can’t do much but smile in return.

It’s strange, she thinks to herself, because this is not her Kara, nor her Esme. They have never baked half a birthday cake, Kara has never hidden the second slice of a PB&J behind her back and told her niece that sandwich bread can be made in fractions. She’s never baked like this with Kara— and suddenly that’s all she can think about, how can she have never made a cake with Kara, how can they have never ruined a recipe and decided to get ice cream instead?

There’s a fierce something in her chest, something mournful and sharp. She’s felt many things since Mxy had left her here— confusion, affection, love. But this… she misses them, her Kara, her Esme, her home.

Even in boarding school, Lena had kept the other girls’ at an arm’s length. In college, she’d panicked when any crush turned out to be requited, whenever people would invite her over, whenever she got too close. Kara Danvers, with her unwavering faith, was the first person in so, so many years who Lena had allowed to breach the tightly guarded brick wall, to open the boxes stored neatly and sealed within.

And even then…

--

It’s that night that Lena is first woken up.

Esme had gone home several hours before, full of cake and clutching several new presents— Kara insists to Alex that half birthdays are, in fact, very important events that do require gifts, thank you very much, and Esme backs her up with very compelling puppy dog eyes.

All day, Lena had felt the admission of her imposter status on the tip of her tongue. Who was she, she thought to herself as Esme and Kara sat enraptured with Finding Dory playing on their TV screen, who was she to witness this? Who was she to steal the life of this other Lena, to be there for Kara and be somehow not enough?

And so she’d gone to sleep with a knot in her stomach, with Kara’s kiss still heavy on her temple, chewing on her lip and twisting her fingers together.

It’s three in the morning when she hears the wheezing breath beside her. It takes a few minutes for Lena to wake up— a dream, she thinks at first, compelled to lay her head back down and tune it out, deal with it in the morning. But there’s something— call it her Luthor sense, or call it love— there is something wrong with Kara, she knows this in her bones.

She hates how lovely it is to sleep beside Kara, how convenient it is that, in the night when she is thinking of her, when she is worried about her, when she wants nothing more than to catch a whiff of her citrus shampoo, Kara is mere inches from her. And now, as she sits up and reaches for the lamp, it is truly more convenient than ever.

Kara’s eyes are screwed shut, her mouth is twisted, her brow and pretty cheeks are wrinkled. She’s twisting, as if frozen to the spot, and she’s shaking and pale. Something is very, very wrong, Lena knows.

But should she touch her?

Maybe she shouldn’t be there at all, Lena thinks. Maybe Kara will be upset, will wonder why she tried to help at all— maybe she will only make it worse. It’s a Phantom Zone nightmare, Lena would bet money on it— but then, they haven’t ever fully talked about Kara and the Phantom Zone, have they? Maybe Kara doesn’t want her to know, will be disappointed if Lena forces her way in…

All of this goes out the window when Kara makes a sound; a little, whimpering, sad sound that shakes as it leaves her lips. It chills Lena to her very core— Kara should not sound like that. In any world where Lena is, in any universe where she is near Kara, she will never let her sound like that. Never let something that horrible touch her.

But you did, a horrible voice whispers to Lena as she delicately touches Kara’s shoulders, tries to call her name. You did let something horrible touch her. It’s your fault she went there at all, it’s your fault she didn’t come home sooner. You’re the reason she’s like this.

“Kara,” she says then, trying to quash the voice in her head. “Kara, sweetheart. Wake up.”

Kara’s eyes open with a start, and they are wild— like a frightened horse, or like she’s looking into the eye of a storm. Clouded and afraid, unseeing for a split second. Then they soften, and she takes a shaky breath.

“Lena?” she says. Her voice is cracked, dry— she swallows harshly.

“I’m right here,” Lena says, praying her voice sounds soothing— “Kara, you had a nightmare. It wasn’t real, you’re home, you’re with me. You’re safe.”

“It wasn’t real,” Kara repeats, breathing slowly and methodically in and out. “I’m home. I’m home, and you’re—,” she reaches out, touches her hand to Lena’s forearm, “—you’re really with me. It wasn’t real.”

“That’s right,” Lena says quietly, “Kara? Would you like me to get you a glass of water?”

“No, don’t—,” Kara says quickly, her grip on Lena’s arm tightening, “please don’t go yet? I, um… I need you to stay, for a minute.”

“Of course, yes, of course,” Lena rushes out. “I’m sorry, um, it’s my first time doing this, I— of course I’ll stay.”

Kara breathes out all at once, slumps back into the pillows with Lena’s arm still clutched in her hands. She breathes slowly, eyes closed, and Lena feels calm begin to wash over her again— Kara is alright.

A few minutes pass that way, in silence, Kara’s breathing a rhythm in Lena’s heart. Suddenly, Kara’s eyes flash back open.

“What do you mean?” she says, brow furrowed.

“Hmm?”

“What do you mean, this is your first time? I’ve had probably a hundred nightmares since the Phantom Zone, Lena. You help me, like, monthly. What do you mean?”

Shit.

The thing is, Lena thinks to herself as Kara turns her head, eyebrows working and a question in her face, the thing is that Lena could cover for herself. She could lie, chalk it up to a late night misunderstanding. But she doesn’t want to lie to Kara. Why would she?

Kara is the person she can trust, in every universe.

“Um,” she says, and slides down the bed so she’s parallel to Kara as she speaks. Kara takes this as a cue, snuggles in closer, her eyes wide and watching.

“Um,” she starts again, “so. You might know an imp, by the name of Mr. Mxyzptlk?”

--

They stay up the rest of the night talking. Kara is suprised, but not stunned by any accounts— she is, at the end of the day, Supergirl. She has seen stranger.

Most of the time is spent filling Kara in on her own Kara, her own universe. She seems to have endless questions— are the potstickers better here, or at home? Is Kara any different? Does Lena think she could get the other Kara here, so they could switch places and see if Cat would notice?

“I’m sorry I didn’t tell you,” Lena says, yawning as a ray of sunlight slips in from behind the blinds. Kara hums, rests her head on Lena’s shoulder. “I’m sorry I didn’t… I don’t know, leave the second it got weird. I guess… I guess I was curious. And you’re older, a bit, you’re more… I don’t know. Unrestrained? I guess I just— my Kara, she’s still coping. She just got out of the… of the Phantom Zone,” Lena says softly, “she’s struggling. And you just seem so adapted. So adjusted.”

Kara laughs quietly, just a breath of air. “Hmm, I don’t know about that. Maybe. Maybe more than I was a year and a half ago, I’ll give you that. But adjusted? Not quite.”

“I mean, I know you just—,” Lena gestures to the bed, to where Kara had been frozen mere hours before, feeling very stupid. “But, I don’t know. How do you find the space, in it all, to live your life?”

Kara considers this for a moment, working her jaw. She opens her mouth, closes it, opens it again.

“I have you,” she says.

Lena stays quiet, and the silence in the room feels suddenly all consuming.

“I’m not… of course I’m not past it. I still have nightmares every so often, I still think about it all the time. But I’m here now, and Lena— it’s because I have Lena in my life, that I’m able to have it be what it is. I can have Supergirl, and work with Cat, and spend time with my friends, and have my niece and my sister— I can have weekends and nights with my fiancee, I can be in love, I’m allowed to be. It’s because she— you— never deserted me, it’s because I always had someone by my side. It’s because I had my person right there with me the whole time.”

Kara looks at her, all of a sudden. “Isn’t that what you do for your Kara, too?”

Lena is struck by it. “I— I don’t know,” she says, stumbling over her words.

Kara just smiles. “Isn’t it?”

--

When they finally deem it appropriate to get out of bed, they devise a plan as Kara flips pieces of French toast.

“I don’t know if we have to tell Alex, and the rest of them,” Kara says, popping a strawberry into her mouth. “I mean, you’re not a danger to our world, and nothing has changed as far as I can tell. You know Alex, she’d overreact. We’ll just… lay low, you keep being yourself, and then we can figure out how to send you home. And then my Lena, maybe she’ll just, I don’t know… rematerialize?”

“Mxy won’t have disappeared her permanently,” Lena says, a drop of contempt in her voice, “he’s a maniac, but he knows better than to cross my Kara. Probably you, too. Probably me, now.”

“True,” Kara says, worrying her lip. Lena is, all of a sudden, hyper aware of her own complicity in this, in the invasion of another’s life, in the vanishing of a Lena, an aunt, a friend, a fiancee. Someone who Kara needed.

As lovely as this is— and it is lovely, isn’t it? To wake up in Kara’s arms most mornings, to mix hot chocolates for the two of them and cuddle up on the couch just like it was at home, but somehow sitting even closer? To have been kissed by her? Although that will stop now, she thinks with a note of sadness as she reaches up to grab two plates and silverware. It had been strange, to be kissed by another Kara. To note the sameness of her face, her smile, her eyes, and yet the slightest of differences in the tilt of her chin, in the tone of her voice, in her walk. Strange, but not hard to pretend, with the tiniest bit of effort, that she was home. That it was her, really her.

“Hey,” Kara is saying, and Lena starts, jerks her head around to glance at her, “hey, it isn’t your fault, okay?”

It’s unnerving, sometimes, how well Kara knows her. Even in another universe, Kara knows her. Anywhere, everywhere.

“I am sorry, though,” Lena says, eyes down. “I never wanted you to… to lose your Lena, even for just a bit. I just… I’m sorry.”

“It’s alright,” Kara says earnestly. “Really, it is. I’m worried about her, and I miss her, of course. But I know she’ll be alright, and she’ll be coming back soon, and I know your Kara misses you just as much. That’s why we’ve got to figure out why you’re here, okay? And then we can get you home to her.”

Lena smiles softly, nods her head once. It’s mild, but Kara will understand what she means. Kara always does.

“But first,” Kara says, and she looks genuinely unhappy to be saying it, “um, it’s been a few days, and Jess has been covering for you… you take more time off now than you used to, anyway, so it’s not a huge problem, but… well, she’s getting curious. And the Foundation always runs more smoothly with you there.”

“The Foundation?”

Kara’s eyes widen. “Oh,” she says, “well, this you have to see.”

--

The Lena Luthor Foundation is a tall, glassy building downtown. Lena looks up at its daunting exterior, feeling a bit like she’s headed into combat. It reminds her of the day she first walked into LuthorCorp as CEO, the way she’d coached herself not to slouch or look back, the way she’d felt all the glances and whispers piercing her like hot darts as she stalked down the halls to her new office.

Kara reaches for her hand, gives it a quick squeeze before walking ahead. “So, I told Cat I’d be in about an hour late,” she says, “so I can give you a quick rundown, and leave you with Jess, and then I’ll see you home the moment I can, okay? Oh, and I’ll be here for lunch, Jess sets it up so we can eat together at least three days a week.”

Lena nods silently, gazing up as they enter the Foundation through tall dark doors. The atrium is massive, echoing and eternal, and she can see up into windows all around her as her heels clack on the tiled floor. Kara seems completely at ease— she waves to passing employees, stops to chat with a security guard as they reach a row of metal detectors.

Lena, however, is not. She can’t remember the last time she walked into a building with such ease— not even LCorp. She can’t remember a time where there seemed to be no chance that the other shoe would drop, that everyone she thought she could trust would turn against her in a heartbeat. At LCorp, she had tried to be as accommodating as she could be, as good of a boss— she made time to be in the labs, time to host internship programs that she directly oversaw. She tried to know her employees, really know them, tried to forge real relationships, unlike the way Lex had run the company, the disgust for anyone and everything beneath him emanating with every business move.

And yet… and yet, Lena could never be sure. If the world decided, one day, that she was no longer to be trusted, who would stand by her side? Jess, surely. Sam, of course. Kara, she has grown to learn. But who else?

But even with all that she poured into her work, all the programs and projects… even with it, she couldn’t buy people’s loyalty. She wouldn’t want to.

This, here… the very air felt different. A Foundation, Kara had said. Not a company, not a corporation. Designed to help people.

“So, what do we do here? Exactly?” Lena asks tentatively once they’ve finally stepped into the elevator, Kara pressing the button for the top floor.

Kara smiles over at her. “You don’t have one in your universe, yeah?”

“I’ve been thinking about pivoting LCorp for some time now, honestly,” Lena says, not even sure of what she’s saying as the words pour out. “People don’t trust it, people aren’t ready for it completely, you know? Not that it’s my fault, but… I think there’s something to be had in a change of direction. Humanitarian work, programs like that— I don’t know. I suppose it’s something I’ve been thinking about.”

Kara’s face is soft, open. “That’s what my Lena thought, too.”

“And so this, then,” Lena gestures around her, “this is what came of it?”

“I’d imagine your needs are somewhat different on your own earth,” Kara says, “but at its core, this is a community organization. You do outreach programs, education, funding. It’s truly whatever the city needs, through a technology and science lens. So there are summer camps and afterschool programs to teach kids about coding, chemistry, robotics. And then there are also food banks and shelters for more immediate support. Education, and using the money from the technology you’re still developing for a good cause. And,” she says, smirking over at Lena as the doors ding open, “you work very closely with Supergirl.”

Lena almost doesn't recognize the Jess seated behind her desk— she seems almost calm, a word Lena would never in a million years have attributed to the woman in her own universe.

“Hi, Jess!” Kara calls, and Jess perks up from her computer, sends them a cheery wave and smile.

“Lena, I have you scheduled for lab work this afternoon,” Jess says, swiping across her computer screen, “and we have a class of tenth graders coming in to learn about biology in our products, I wasn’t sure if you still wanted to make an appearance but they’ll be in Classroom B at eleven thirty. And Kara, if you’re picking up sushi for lunch make sure to remind me to send you my order, okay?”

It seems almost too good to be true. She’s always hated business, really— shareholders and board meetings and a carefully crafted defensive exterior that would have to remain impenetrable lest anyone try to undermine her. But this, this is different. This is lab work, and teaching, and doing good. Real good, the kind of good she’d professed to Kara that she wanted to bring to National City, so many years ago when she’d arrived here with stars in her eyes.

Is this even possible, she thinks to herself as Kara and Jess pull up the menu for a sushi spot near the office on the wide screen computer, is this something she could do, at home? Maybe not exactly. Not in the same way, and anyway, it would take some real time and compromise. But… could she?

This Kara had seemed so very proud of her Lena’s work. Here, there were kids filing in for field trips, herself working alongside the best minds in tech to develop— something she’s been angling for for years, something she’s never really had time to devote herself to. She thinks of Kara, her Kara, thinks of the foundation she had been writing her article on so long ago now. Thinks of the excitement in her voice, the hope she always had. And Supergirl could help her, with a feat like this… she could find a place for Supergirl and Guardian and Dreamer, for outreach, for community.

She resolves herself to learn as much as she possibly can about this Lena’s work— she can craft a plan, she thinks with a spark of excitement, inspiration she hasn’t felt in a long time. What better time to create her very own turning point?

--

There have been times, in this short stint, where Lena has forgotten. She’ll catch a glimpse of Kara, out of the corner of her eye, typing away on her laptop with one hand and solemnly eating chocolate chips with the other and think, for a moment, that they’re back at her Kara’s place, that they’ll go into the Tower the next day and Kara will accidentally let slip that Lena stayed the night and Alex will get that pinchy expression, and Nia will scoff as if in disbelief, and she will order Kara pizza for lunch and get a hug that lasts just a touch longer than it is supposed to.

But then she remembers, all in a rush. They’ve taken to debriefing every night— mostly, it’s brainstorming about how to get Lena home. About what she’s learned.

“So Mxy told you he knew you were in love with your Kara, and then he said he wanted to show you something,” Kara says, speaking through a mouthful of pretzels, “and he dropped you here.”

“Right,” Lena says, kicking her legs up onto the couch and dropping her head back onto the cushion with a sigh. “I mean, I thought I got it— I need to be there for my Kara to help her through this, I shouldn’t think that she doesn’t want to have the Phantom Zone conversation with me. It’s like you said, you can work through it because she’s supporting you. So that’s what I need to do.”

“Well, that can’t be all of it,” Kara says, tapping her fingers along the coffee table, “or you’d be home already. There has to be something else. Are you sure this is the angle?”

“This is it,” Lena says, “I know it. I—,”

Because what else could it be? She has been so paralyzed by the fear that Kara will see her and think only of the Phantom Zone. It was, after all, her fault. But if Kara needs her to be by her side, she will be— she would do anything for Kara. Any imp in the universe would know that.

“— I just know,” she finishes awkwardly, and Kara nods once. She knows. Lena is sure of it.

She can’t help the nagging feeling that something is missing. Kara’s right— if this is it, if this is the angle, why is she still here? Jack’s voice, in her ear: sleep on it, Lena, it’ll show a different face in the morning.

On some level, maybe, Lena is afraid to go home. Maybe she thinks of Kara, of her Kara, alone in bed at night and waking up with those wild and stormy eyes, and maybe she can’t bring herself to see it. This is safety, here… this is the aftermath. The wake, the calm stillness of the sky after the lightning.

Maybe it’s that. She can’t be sure. For now, she takes another handful of pretzels, and a Kara who isn’t quite her own and isn’t quite foreign, either, launches into a theory about quests and alternate universes and the likelihood of time loops.

For now, she is wherever she is, and she is not where she isn’t, and time is frozen and also hurtling by. The Lena she has always been is waiting in the wings, perhaps, waiting for the right moment, the millisecond in which to pounce.

--

Once or twice, Kara almost kisses her, in moments between remembering. Lena’s body aches with it, compels her to ignore who she is beside and pretend that it is the same girl who she hopes is waiting for her. She loves her with all her might, now and before and onwards, and really, what more is there to it than that?

--

She gets brunch with Alex, who talks for a half hour about wedding venues for her and Kara. Esme writes a book for school about love, and she and Kara are featured on the cover, beside Esme’s moms, a large heart over their heads like a halo. Her own love seems to be thrown at her from all directions, and yet it is and isn’t enough. She longs, suddenly and helplessly and for the first time that she can put a name to since coming here, for her own Kara to hold her. And still days pass.

--

When Lena wakes in the night to hear a now-familiar gasping and rustling from beside her, she feels something different in the air.

She turns on the lamp and flips over in a heartbeat, touches her hand very softly to Kara’s cheek. There’s pain, tension in her clenched jaw.

“Kara, honey,” Lena whispers, rubbing her arm, “you're okay. It’s a nightmare, I’m right here. You’re okay.”

It takes Kara a minute to pull herself out of it, this time, and Lena guides her along, tells her where she is and what is going to happen. Kara unlocks herself, it seems, legs kicking before stilling and arms relaxing under Lena’s touch. Finally her jaw unclenches, and she takes a deep, shaking breath, and the worst of it has passed.

Lena doesn’t try to get up this time, just curls into Kara wordlessly. Tries to be the comfort Kara needs, tries to be the other Lena.

It’s slow, the recovery. Kara’s breathing evens out, her eyes blink closed again. “Thank you,” she murmurs finally. “I’m glad you’re here. Thank you, Lena.”

She’s glad she was here, too, strangely. And then the thought hits her like a knife to the heart— Kara, her Kara, waking up alone and afraid, coaxing herself back to sleep in the room that is so big and dark and that Kara has confessed can be claustrophobic when her mind turns against her. In the bed she’ss told Lena feels more safe when she’s beside Kara. And Lena has been so far away.

She wants to be with her, she realizes. Wants to hold Kara, to breathe with her, to rub her back until she is calm again and ready to talk about it.

Suddenly, Lena understands.

--

Mxy waits, conveniently, for Kara to leave for work in the morning before he appears. She is chipper, talking excitedly about getting to coach a cub reporter and show her how to conduct a real interview. It’s endearing, it’s light, it reminds Lena so much of home that she feels it like a buoy in her stomach.

And then he is perched a few inches above the sofa, his face almost mocking. Lena wants to wring his stupid, arrogant neck.

“Soooo,” he says, shimmying a bit in midair. “Was I right, or was I right?”

“There’s no need to gloat,” Lena says through gritted teeth. Mxy lets out a little squeal. She glares at him.

“I won’t even ask for a thank you— that’s how reformed I am. I’ll even leave this to you, so long as you talk to her.”

“I have to talk to her,” Lena sighs.

“That you do,” he chirps, then gestures towards the framed photographs of her and Kara. “First, though… maybe you should break the news to your little lover?”

It’s a strange thought, that she’ll leave this Kara, this slice of the life that they’ve built, this little corner of a universe— not hers, exactly, but not too far from it. And this Kara, this slightly older, slightly more distanced version— well, they’d shared something, now. Despite it all.

She’s sure Kara is more than ready to have her Lena back properly, to get her life back on track. She’s been accommodating, kind, truly caring without judgment— what kind of a person, Lena thinks to herself, is so unfailingly wonderful even in the face of their own worst fears?

Kara, that’s who. In each and every universe.

And Kara will understand that she can’t explain in person. Kara and this other Lena, this mirrored image, will live a long and happy life together, she can sense it in the very air of things. Kara will be happy with a note, she’s sure, and that is what she leaves her. It’s simple; she isn’t even entirely sure how these things work, in terms of universe hopping— can she leave things behind? Will anyone remember she was there?

Still, she scrawls in small print on the corner of Kara’s shopping list, beneath the block lettered demand for stroopwafels, a quick note of thanks. It can’t hurt, she figures, for Kara to know that.

Maybe, she thinks as she takes Mxy’s hand solemnly, trying to ignore his excited chattering and pompous smirk, that’s what it’s all been about. Maybe if Kara does know how she feels, if she is honest and real, just maybe the world won’t crumble to pieces beneath her feet.

She isn’t sure of it yet. But, as the cozy living room disintegrates around them, complete with the view of the sky and the images littering the walls, maybe it could be true.

Lena had dreamed of hermiting upon her return home. Maybe running a long bath, looking up ticket rates for impromptu vacations— ways in which she could escape, just for a bit longer. Make it last.

Kara, it seems, has other ideas. Within three minutes of Lena’s fall from seemingly nowhere and nothing onto her own couch, in what now feels like an unfamiliar apartment, Kara appears breathless on her balcony. Her face sags in relief the moment her eyes meet Lena’s.

“Lena, thank Rao you’re okay,” she says all in a rush, bursting through the sliding doors to collapse herself into Lena’s arms. “Mxy, he— I’ll kill him, I told him never to— and your signal watch was down, and Nia’s dreams weren’t— Rao, I was so worried,” she says finally, leaning back to hold Lena at arm’s length. Her eyes are teary.

“I’m okay,” Lena answers, and it comes out as if she’s in a dream. “But… Kara, we have to talk.”

Kara’s eyes are still wide and darting across Lena’s face with worry as Lena guides her to sit beside her, to lean back into one of the throw pillows that Kara had convinced her to buy in the early days of their friendship. Kara leans forward, waiting.

“What happened to you, Lena?”

And how can she ever explain it, really? How can she define in clear words what it was to bake half a birthday cake for Esme, for another Kara to pull her into bed at night, to wake up to screaming and have the memories hit her hard every time?

And yet, she thinks, watching as Kara reaches for her hand with true care in her face, who but Kara would care enough to listen, to understand?

“Well, Mxy told me he wanted to show me something. He knew how I felt,” she says, and takes a breath— there’s no use in not being honest, she supposes. “He knew I was afraid that you didn’t want me near you, anymore. That after the… the Phantom Zone, I was only reminding you of the bad things.”

Kara looks incredulous, mouth open, about to defend Lena’s honor no doubt. She starts to speak.

“Kara, I—,” Lena interrupts before she can, “I’m sorry, I just… I need to get through this, okay?”

And what’s so lovely about Kara, what has endeared Lena to her over all these years, is the fact that she sits back. Closes her mouth. Gives Lena an encouraging little smile. Nods for her to continue.

“So,” Lena breathes, “he dropped me on another Earth. And I was in a different apartment, it wasn’t quite mine or yours. And you and I were— we were… we were, um, engaged?”

She sneaks a glance at Kara, tries to gauge her reaction. It’s hard to name. Her eyes are wide, she looks a little caught off guard. A little starstruck. And there’s something else there, something deeper…

“Um, anyway,” she continues, tries to shake it off, “it was different, really different, but everyone was still there. Esme would come by to spend the day with us, and Alex and I had brunch, and… and I had rebranded L-Corp, into a charity and community foundation. Supergirl and I were public allies, and you would come to my office for lunch, and… it was perfect, Kara. We were healing.”

“It sounds amazing,” Kara says, voice a little raspy. And sure, maybe Lena panics for a moment at the fact that she’s implied a universe where she and Kara are engaged is a perfect one. But she really can’t bring herself to lie anymore.

She’s spent years, it’s felt like lifetimes, in the middle. She and Kara, never quite defined, always dancing around the elephant in the room. Always together, always something almost bubbling to the surface and falling just slightly short.

Maybe Kara doesn’t love her back. Maybe she will want space. But she would never cut Lena out completely— she knows this now. Perhaps she always has.

And maybe… maybe that other life could be real. Maybe something not as picture perfect, but just as wonderful, could exist. Maybe she and Kara, her real Kara, could be something.

So she steels herself.

“And… the other Kara, she was still having nightmares, sometimes. About the Phantom Zone, about her past. But, Kara… I was there to help her. And I was worried that it would be horrible for her, that it would make it worse… but she wanted me to be there. She said it helped.”

Kara looks pensive, almost wistful. “That… that sounds nice. Really nice.”

“And… well, I realized something. I realized that I want to help you, and I want to be near you, and be with you, and I always have. And that maybe you want that too. And that, maybe there’s no point in delaying it, and pushing ourselves apart, and not being honest. Maybe… Kara, I—,”

And Kara is there. “Lena,” she says, “you can tell me. You know you can tell me anything.”

“Seeing us… engaged,” Lena breathes, “I— well, I love you, Kara. I want to be with you. I don’t want either of us to heal alone anymore. I don’t want things to be delicate anymore. I was avoiding talking about it for so long, and then the Phantom Zone happened and I was so scared to lose you, and… it just seemed easier this way. To not really talk about it. Especially if, you know, I would bring back memories.”

“Lena,” Kara says, earnest eyes and moving somehow closer, “you could never be a bad memory for me. Never.”

“I think I know that now,” she finds herself saying. “I think, seeing everything in front of me, seeing a you who wasn’t, well, you, and the way she cared about the me who wasn’t me…we could have that, too. If… I mean, if you wanted that.”

“If I… Lena,” Kara is near a whisper, shaking her head slightly, “I want that. I have always wanted that. You… you are everything to me, how could you ever doubt that?”

The light of the early twilight is peering in through Lena’s floor to ceiling windows and she almost wants to shut the blinds, to lock all the doors, to make it so that she and Kara are the only things in a thousand foot radius. To be able to gaze into Kara’s eyes and know that she is the only one. To move closer, closer still until she is enveloped completely.

When they finally do kiss, it feels like no end is in sight. It feels like days with Kara always have, like their joking has always been— with no hurry, with no goal, just with the intent of being there, then. Has Lena ever existed in a moment in her entire life? She isn’t sure, but as Kara’s tongue slips between her lips the thought is even further from her mind. All she can think about is Kara’s hand finding its place on her thigh, Kara’s tiny noises from deep in her throat, the smell of her and the taste of her and the way it feels like they could consume each other, like nothing else would ever matter.

Somehow it’s even better, because it’s Kara, when they break apart, when Kara gives her a tiny shy smile, when she asks tentatively if Lena wants to talk, and Kara can make nachos for the both of them. It’s perfect, it’s everything Lena has wanted, it feels strangely multidimensional— after all, there are hundreds of Karas out there, hundreds of Lenas. She finds it hard to believe, now, that they can exist separately from one another.

Lena Luthor may not have believed in fate, in destiny. But in that split second, where Kara was rooting through Lena’s fridge in search of cheese, she was almost swayed.

Lena Luthor and Kara Danvers are married on a Tuesday, nearly two years later. Esme is the flower girl, twirling down the aisle with a beam on her face to rival even Kara’s. They have no bridesmaids, because their friends can’t decide to which bride they will belong— instead, J’onn officiates, Alex and Kelly weep, Brainy takes seven baking classes to provide the wedding with twelve dozen cupcakes more than Lena had asked him for. They request no presents, and will only accept donations, most of which go to the charity outreach program of the newly established Lena Luthor Foundation. Cat Grant is present, denying that she shed a single tear. Lena knows, and feels she can speak for her wife as well, that it could not have been more perfect.

Inviting them would’ve been too strange, they decide, but they do send a wedding announcement (complete with photograph) along to Mxy, once they track him down. They ask him if it’s possible to drop it on the other Earth, if maybe the other Kara and Lena could see it. He disappears in a shower of sparks.
