I thought you'd find me eventually

            “I thought you’d find me eventually. I mean … I wasn’t sure how long it would take - maybe three months, but probably one knowing you - but I knew you’d come eventually to find me. So predictable, even now. I guess that’s why we’ve always had so many problems in our relationship, right? I’m sorry. Really, I am. I was kind of a dick a lot of the time, and sometimes you were too, but I used your predictability against you and then got mad that you got upset. Dick. Move.”

 

            Lena let out a quiet scoff, blinking rapidly as she ducked her head, unable to meet Kara’s eyes as her own smarted with stinging tears. She wrapped her arms around herself and closed her eyes, listening to Kara speak without interrupting her. It had been so long that Lena didn’t even think she would have been able to reply under the shocking appearance of her missing friend.

 

            “I’m trying, though, to be a better person. I hope that you of all people can see that, because I’ve never met anyone who’s ever tried harder than you, Lena. It’s possibly one of my favourite things about you. I could name them all, of course, but … well, there are other more important things to get out of the way, now that I have the chance. I’d be lying if I said that I hadn’t been waiting for an opportunity to do this for a long, long time, and the circumstances could’ve been better but … I should take my chance while I still can, right? Who knows what’ll happen in the future, so I should at least be honest with you - for once in my life.”

 

            Clicking her tongue with a sinking feeling in her chest, Lena looked up with heartbreak written on her face, gripping at the silk over her heart as she watched Kara face crumple. Her lip wobbled and she looked down, something that made Lena’s facade falter even further, watching the one person who’d always been so stoic and strong waver. Of course, she’d seen Kara come undone in a similar way twice before, thought that each time they would have finally been honest with each other, but neither of them had ever managed to get past that roadblock of friendship. 

 

            They could say they loved each other in a dozen ways, that they were scared to lose each other, scared to be honest, and yet even when saying all those things, they dressed it up under the guise of friendship every single time , and let each other get away with it. She wondered if that was the route it would take this time too, scared that it would and terrified that it wouldn’t.

 

            Kara sighed heavily, a grim look of resolution as her shoulders slumped. “You probably know where this is going - I think we’ve both known for a long time but … well, it’s us - and I hope you- you can let me finish saying it before … I don’t know what you’ll do. I think you know how I feel, and maybe it’s wishful thinking, but I think you might feel the same way. Just a little. So hopefully that’s enough to make you hear me out, because … I love you, Lena. I’ve said it a hundred times but I think this is the first time I’ve said it with any real intention to make you fully and truly understand that I love you. I love you in every sense of the word, in every way possible, and it kills me to have to tell you like this because … I should’ve told you every single one of those hundred times before, but I was too cowardly, so … there it is.”

 

            There was a tremor in Kara’s voice, and despite her suit and that heroic symbol on her chest, she fiddled with her fingers and looked down. It took everything in Lena to not go to her, to stay rooted to the spot, silently shaking as she cried, her cheeks wet with tears.

 

            “If you feel the same way about me … I’d like it if you could say it back, but I- I understand if maybe I misread everything. I won’t know either way.”

 

            Lena flinched at Kara’s hollow laugh and watched as the hologram flickered slightly, the life force wavering. She looked at the blue figure before her and wished so desperately that she was real, that it was Kara, not just some recorded imitation. That was why she’d gone to the Fortress of Solitude though, scoured the place for anything to help bring the woman she loved back. She hadn’t anticipated finding a secret message hidden away just for her. Hadn’t anticipated standing in the freezing cavern as she listened to Kara speak for half an hour, rambling on nervously before she’d finally gotten to the point, to the last, heartbreaking gift she’d left for Lena.

 

            “Or who knows, maybe I will. Maybe I’ll be back before I know it, or maybe it’ll take some time and you’ll move on and be old if I ever get out of the Phantom Zone. If I know you though, you’re working as hard as you can to bring me back, cursing me every second of the way for how stupid I am. I guess that’s how you would’ve found this - I hid it pretty well so only you would look here. Only you for when you got desperate enough to find another way to try and bring me back. I just- I want you to know that it’s okay if you can’t. I made my peace with leaving, and I didn’t plan on coming back, and I hope you can find peace in my absence if- if we don’t see each other again.”

 

            A sob caught in Lena’s throat before she let out a shuddering breath, and Kara inhaled deeply as she stared back at her.

 

            “If that’s the case, I want you to do something for me. It’s kind of why I left this for you in the first place, because I know everyone well enough to know they already have someone. I know they’ll miss me but they have someone to rely on, someone to comfort them. Everyone but you. And I know that we were that person for each other, who could take the loneliness away, who could understand each other and the pressure of what a family name can do to a person. And so, I want to be the person to ask you to live. It’s a big world, with a lot of good people in it, and you deserve that love, that kindness and happiness. If there’s one last wish I have, it’s that you go out there and enjoy life - for me. No regrets, no guilt, just love for what we shared. I wish it could have been more, and I’m sorry for ruining so much of our time together - I wish we’d had more time to be happy, even as friends, after making up - but I just- I wanted to- to say goodbye to you properly. And that I loved you as much as anyone could love another person.”

 

            She trailed off and Lena could see the tears in the hologram’s eyes from when Kara had been recording it, could see the effort it took for her to plaster on that sunny smile. Could see just how hard she was trying to keep her composure to get through it all, for Lena’s sake. But even then, her smile faltered as her bottom lip wobbled and she let out a breathless laugh that was more like a sob, before reaching up to kiss her fingertips and flutter them in a quick wave. The last image of her that Lena had before the hologram winked out was such gut-wrenching sadness in Kara’s eyes that she felt like she was going to be sick.

 

            Sinking down to the hard floor, the cold seeping into her until she wasn’t sure if she was shaking from the cold or the uncontrollable sobs that the hand clapped over her mouth couldn’t stifle. Lena couldn’t even see, as blinded by tears as she was, her breathing shallow and jagged as she struggled to try and even it out. Her chest constricted so tightly that she wasn’t altogether convinced she wasn’t about to go into cardiac arrest, the painful squeezing so unbearable that it was all she could do to weather it.

 

            By the time she stopped crying, her eyes were puffy and sore, the whites threaded with red and her cheeks and nose blotchy. Breaths hitching, she pushed herself up off the floor, fingernails lilac from the cold, and wiped her face on the arm of her wool coat.

 

            Then, despite the pain, the overwhelming sadness and the devastation that threatens to wreck her once more, she reached out for the shard with the hologram in it and made it play all over again.

She rarely left the Fortress of Solitude unless necessary, her quick trips through the portal to shower and cram something into her mouth and pass out in a warm bed for an hour or two few and far between. Lena mostly sustained herself on coffee and bundled layers to keep her warm and awake, avoiding phone calls from concerned friends and business calls from her mother and board members. They could oust her if they wanted to - Lena didn’t care.

 

            Her mind was solely focused on one thing now, and that was bringing Kara back. She worked tirelessly in the frozen cavern, fingers numb and trembling, muscle stiff and joints aching as a deep, seeping cold made her teeth ache. Her skin dried out and her lips chapped, cheeks constantly reddened and that bone-deep sensation that she’d never be warm again, but Lena didn’t let up. They’d tried, of course; that first week, they’d all tried, putting their heads together for some way to bring her back, but it had failed and they’d lost hope. But not Lena, never Lena.

 

            No matter Kara’s last wish, she would never have been able to settle for anything less, never would have been able to be happy in a world without her. Not when she was still alive somewhere unreachable. But that was what Lena did - she went to those lengths no one else could, did the impossible and went to the furthest reaches of science and space and something almost magic to save her friends. And so she worked diligently with that hologram playing on repeat to keep her company.

 

            “Lena, it’s me. I mean … I’m recording myself right now, before I go, so that I- I can get some things off my chest. I think that it’s going to be hard for me to say goodbye to you, so I thought maybe I’d say it here to practice. I have a lot to say to you.”

 

            “Yeah, well, I have a lot to say too,” Lena grumbled, teeth grit as she turned a wrench, screwing a bolt tight on the machine.

 

            “I think most of all I have to say sorry. For so many things, but maybe I’ll start with leaving you in this way. Honestly … I think maybe it would be easier for everyone if I died because we know that- that the chances of me surviving this are low. But it’s not something that you’ll know for sure because … well, if I survive it, I’m not sure I’ll even be able to come back anyway. I know you hate unsolved problems, so I’m sorry to be the cause of another one. I think death would have been kinder in so many ways.”

 

            “You’re always so … annoying,” Lena said through clenched teeth, tugging at another nut as Kara droned on and on, “with that damn superhero shit, you know? You don’t have to sacrifice yourself all the time - it’s getting annoying, having to save you every time. And it’s not good for my ego either.”

 

            The hologram let out a faint laugh as Lena spoke over it again, muttering her curses, before continuing. “Although, knowing you, you won’t listen to what anyone says and you’ll try and save me anyway. I know how stubborn you are.”

 

            “Not as stubborn as you.”

 

            “Who knows, maybe you’ll do it again. I wouldn’t be surprised. You’ve shown your brilliance over and over again, saved us all more times than we’ve thanked you for. It’s a bit late now, and I’m not sure I’ll remember later, but … thank you.”

 

            Lena snorted, tossing the wrench aside and wiping damp hair off her chilled brow. “Thank me when you’re back.”

 

            “I didn’t leave this for you as motivation to bring me back though. It’s just- it’s a last goodbye. The last talk that I don’t think I’m brave enough to say to you. I hid it pretty well - at least I think so - so with any luck, maybe you’ll never find it. Maybe you’ll bring me back without seeing me embarrass myself as a hologram, and I’ll be able to destroy it and we can pretend everything is the same as it’s always been. We’re in a good place right now; it almost feels selfish to ruin it after I’m gone. But … who am I kidding? It’s you. And, well, if you’ve made it this far, you’ve clearly found me.”

 

            Kara lightly laughed and Lena shook her head, a muscle twitching in her jaw as she took a sip of water from a bottle and then poured steaming coffee from a thermos. She was covered in grease and flecks of metal and her thick overalls over her bundled layers were a mess of burns and stains and spills from days of work. Dark circles ringed her eyes and Lena was short-tempered and antsy.

 

            “Who am I kidding? I thought you’d find me eventually. I mean … I wasn’t sure how long it would take - maybe three months-“

 

            “A week,” Lena snorted, shaking her head as she drained her coffee and then stooped to pick up her wrench as Kara forged on. 

 

            “But probably one knowing you-“

 

            “Underestimating me again,” Lena sighed, clicking her tongue with faint amusement as she squatted in the blue ice.

 

            She worked as Kara talked, occasionally offering up comments over her endless stream of words. It helped, in some ways. Those forty-six minutes and thirteen seconds were the most cherished part of being at the Fortress, looping over and over as Lena came up with new answers, new curses, new pleas.

 

            Some days she just didn’t have it in her to think of another way to create a portal to the Phantom Zone. Each failure made her want to give up, but she’d slump in the floor, at the foot of the hologram as wet patches soaked into her clothes and left her shivering, and she’d cry and plead and beg and pray, over and over as Kara talked and Lena sat with her failure.

 

            She’d go home and sleep it off and allow a scalding bath to invite some prickling heat to her extremities before she’d leave again. Sometimes she was gone for a week, snatches of naps stolen on the spread-out oilcloth as she shivered in her foolish exhaustion, leaving herself with a hacking cough and head cold from long exposure to the frigid dampness of the place, and she’d dry swallow pills when she dragged herself through the portal, unable to conceive the idea that she should give up already.

 

            Every day, she was there without fail, toiling away as she welded and sawed and programmed and screwed and drilled. Hours slipped into days slipped into weeks, but Lena didn’t pay attention to anything that existed outside of bringing Kara home. 

 

            “It’s possibly one of my favourite things about you. I could name them all, of course, but …-“

 

            “You should have bloody listed them, seeing as I’ve had to listen to this thing nonstop since I found it,” Lena sighed, leaning against a benchtop as she sipped soup from a flask and gave the hologram a wry smile. 

 

            She was in one of her better moods, exasperated but quietly hopeful and determined, still able to find a spark of humour amidst it all. 

 

            “I guess I’ll just make you do it when you’re back,” she murmured, pouring out more soup and draining it in a gulp before screwing the lid back on. Then she got back to work.

 

            “You probably know where this is going - I think we’ve both known for a long time-“

 

            “Of course we have,” Lena muttered, making a quiet scathing sound. “I think the whole world knows it.”

She was into the fourth with that sickening feeling of guilt and hopeless failure making her stomach twist nauseatingly, because how could she not figure it out? She was the one person who always saved Kara and she didn’t one to start failing now, not when she was the only option. But four months in, all she had to show for it was a constructed and deconstructed and reconstructed portal that she tried to configure with the Phantom Zone without success. 

 

            “If you feel the same way about me … I’d like it if you could say it back-“

 

            With a low snarl, Lena kicked the portal and then slammed her hand into the metal, numbing pain making her regret it almost instantly. Still, she had enough anger in her to snarkily reply to the hologram, shooting it a dark look.

 

            “Yeah, well, I’ll say it when you’re back. I’m not a coward.”

 

            It was a lie, because there had been so many chances to say it before she’d chickened out yet again. But after seeing the message, knowing for sure how Kara felt, even after everything, she knew that this time she’d be able to say it. Lena couldn’t remember ever saying those words in any way that really meant something and she resolved herself to never say it unless it was to the right person. It would be nerve-wracking, and maybe she’d let Kara broach the topic first, but she’d be honest this time. It was one of the bargains she tried making with some higher force of being, pleading for Kara to come back and promising she’d be honest this time.

 

            “I just- I want you to know that it’s okay if you can’t. I made my peace with leaving, and I didn’t plan on coming back, and I hope you can find peace in my absence if- if we don’t see each other again.”

 

            “No, it damn well isn’t okay. You might’ve made your peace, but I certainly haven’t made mine, Kara Danvers. There won’t be any peace without you.”

            “Maybe you’ll bring me back without seeing me embarrass myself as a hologram, and I’ll be able to destroy it and we can pretend everything is the same as it’s always been.”

 

            “Don’t think you’re getting off that easily.”

 

            “I wasn’t sure how long it would take - maybe three months, but probably one knowing you - but I knew you’d come eventually to find me. So predictable, even now.”

 

            Lena snorted and lowered the welding mask, a white-hot flame igniting on the welder as she turned it on and crouched before a metal panel.

 

            “Apparently not predictable enough. One week! You thought you could outsmart me for longer than that? Come on, Kara, you’re better than that.”

 

            “I wanted to- to say goodbye to you properly. And that I loved you as much as anyone else could love another person.”

 

            “It’s not a proper goodbye unless it’s face to face,” Lena muttered darkly to herself, typing away at a keyboard as she took in the long strings of code, correcting small errors and adding in new blocks of text. “You can say the rest to me then too.”

 

            The six month anniversary was almost too much to bear. If her phone had been charged, she would have seen the missed calls off Alex - not just from that day, but spanning weeks and months, all unanswered, as well as hundreds of texts off everyone else. But Lena wasn’t in the habit of keeping her phone charged, her faith in anyone else bringing Kara back so low that it seemed an annoyance to have to decline disturbing calls. 

 

            Instead, Lena spent the day drinking. She had a full bottle of whisky stashed alongside the few necessities she’d started bringing by that point - toothpaste capsules and caffeine pills and protein bars to keep her energy up, a sleeping bag and a camping cot meaning fewer trips home - and opened it then. Sitting on her cot, the hologram playing over and over again as she nursed her drink through the lonely hours, she grew drunker and drunker and more and more miserable.

 

            Night settled, unnoticed inside the fortress, and Lena was angry and devastated, distraught as she paced and swigged the last remnants of whisky, hurling accusations and blame at the looped hologram in between hitching sobs. Her eyes were red again, her skin pale - save her red cheeks and nose - her chapped lips trembling as she sniffed, half from a lingering cold and the other half from tears.

 

            Raking a hand through her greasy hair, a muscle twitched in Lena's jaw as she tipped her head back, clenching her teeth.

 

            “Why did you do this to me?” she shouted at the blue figure, her voice breaking as her eyes filled with fresh tears. Coming apart at the seams, Lena thumbed her eyelids and held her breath, trying to keep her composure, even through the haze of liquor. “How could you do this? You know what leaving would have done. How? How?”

            “Fuck, fuck, fuck!”

 

            Lena slammed her hand against the machine in her frustration, her toe throbbing in its boot and her heart breaking just a little bit more. She didn’t care if she broke the machine anyway - it was useless junk that couldn’t bring anything back without killing them.

 

            Scrubbing a hand over her face, she listened to the machine whine, upset by her smashing the buttons and denting the metal, Lena drew in a deep breath before letting out a choked sob.

 

            “If you feel the same way about me … I’d like it if you could say it back, but I- I understand if maybe I misread everything. I won’t know either way.”

 

            “You stupid, stupid fool,” Lena miserably shouted at the hologram, her face slack with devastation as she stood before it, hot tears running down her cheeks as she screwed her hands into fists. “Of course I do! Of course I-“

 

            Another sob choked her off and Lena staggered forward, her knees unable to support her weight anymore as she sank down to the frozen ground before the hologram’s base. Folding her arms, she buried her face in them and sobbed and sobbed, her shoulder heaving with the effort.

 

            “I love you,” she finally admitted, saying the words she’d promised she’d only ever say to Kara’s face. “I love you, I love you, I love you.”

 

            “Not the welcome back I expected, but I can’t say I haven’t been dreaming of hearing you say that,” came a hoarse voice from behind her.

 

            Startling, Lena whirled around, her boots scrabbling on the frozen floor as she pushed her back up against the hologram’s plinth. Blinking owlishly, her lips parted and she let out a forceful exhale, brushing hair out of her face as tears fell and cleared her vision.

 

            Standing before her, impossibly yet there , was Kara, looking very much the same, save for a pallid look of someone seeing a ghost and a haunted look in her eyes. Lena’s mouth opened and closed as she took in the solid form of the Kryptonian and the swirling portal behind her, unable to make a sound as her slow mind tried to process it.

 

            “Kara?”

 

            “Lena.”

 

            “But you- I- how did-“

 

            A smile, all too familiar, if a little hesitant, curled Kara’s lips, but didn’t quite reach her eyes, as she patted the metal frame of the portal.

 

            “Your portal.”

 

            “But I- it doesn’t work. I sent mice through and they … they died”.

 

            Letting out a rasping laugh that made Lena’s stomach lurch with how familiar it was, that warmth and amusement like a song to her ears, Kara’s face crumpled with bewilderment.

 

            “Well, of course a mouse wouldn’t survive it; they can’t breathe in space, can they?”

 

            Lena’s eyes widened a fraction and a choked, pitiful sound fell from her lips. “Oh. So … it’s you?”

 

            Kara gave her a wary look then, her facade breaking slightly as her wan smile wavered and a dark expression crossed her face.

 

            “Is- is it you?” she quietly asked Lena.

 

            “What-“

 

            “There were … things. I watched you die. All of you. And I- I don’t know if this is another dream.”

 

            Staggering to her feet, Lena stood for a moment, staring at her with mournful, disbelieving eyes. And then she lurched across the space, almost tripping over her feet in her haste, and found herself scooped into Kara’s arms halfway across the space. They both sank to their knees, clinging to each other as they shook with silent sobs. Lena buried her face into the side of Kara’s neck, breathing in the smell of her as she clutched at the red cape, the fabric so real that she couldn’t deny Kara’s solid presence. She could feel that burning heat of her working her way into Lena’s cold chest.

 

            “I love you, I love you,” Lena quietly sobbed into her neck, her eyes squeezed shut as she clung to Kara, reeking of whisky and day’s old clothes and grease. But she was there and so she said it like she promised she would.

 

            And when Kara whispered it back and pulled herself away, Lena leant into the kiss she knew was coming and knew in that moment that nothing had ever been more real to her. Kara’s lips, trembling from some hidden fear and her quiet sobs, against her own chapped and cold ones was more than she’d ever dreamed of, and Lena let out a choked laugh as Kara pulled back.

 

            Reaching up to touch one of Kara’s cheeks with tentative fingertips, Lena swallowed thickly and felt the knot in her chest unwind slightly as the guarded look in Kara’s eyes abated to tender relief.

 

            “I thought you’d find me eventually.”
