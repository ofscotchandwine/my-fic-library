{Prologue}

Villains get bored.

It was a fact that not too many people considered. Which in Mxy's opinion, was just downright stereotypical. Villains are just as rightful to a nice entertaining time as anybody, if not more. Villains had so much stress on their shoulders; planning the end of the world, scheming to defeat the superhero of the day, creating new and innovative ways to torture people.

The list went on.

It all took a toll on a villain. 

Which was why Mxy enjoyed playing around so much. Messing around with people and pranking them was one of his favorite past-times. And it looked like it was time for another one of his famous pranks. But not on a human. Humans were too gullible. They were no fun anymore. 

No. Mxy wanted someone much more interesting. Maybe one of the Bat Family. Or better yet, the Batman himself. 

No, no.  He thought to himself. Batman was never fun, even with practical jokes.

Maybe pick a new metahuman. Someone with flame powers? That can definitely be fun to play with. 

He sat thinking with his feet perched up on the arm of his throne in his lonely 5th dimension.

No, no, no. It wasn't fun when you didn't really know the person. 

He took a sip of his cocktail, appreciating the distinctive taste of it. It was a special cocktail only ever served in the 5th dimension. He only ever got the good stuff.

Maybe one of the Kryptonian cousins.

Mxy's eyes widened. A grin plastered on his face. 

Maybe I should visit an old dear friend. It's been a while since I've seen my darling Supergirl. 

"Miss, are you alright?" 

"Maybe we should call an ambulance."

"Miss, can you hear me?" 

Kara blinked her eyes open  and groaned. There was a blinding light shining down  over her eyes and she squeezed them  back shut when a searing headache followed. 

"She's waking up."

She put her hand up to block out the light, blinking several times until her vision was no longer blurry. 

The first thing she saw was a diamond ring on her finger, it sprinkled colors of the rainbow as it reflected the sunlight. 

She frowned at it. 

She then noticed several heads hovering over her. They circled her, all with worried expressions on their faces, and Kara wanted to ask them what it was they were staring at. 

"Are you alright, Miss? Can you stand up?" the old man asked gently. 

Stand up? Why would I... 

And then realization hit her. That ache in her back, the pain at the back of her head. She was lying down on the floor. On the road. And small pebble rocks were digging in her scalp. 

What happened? 

Kara swallowed down and tried to speak, but it proved to be harder than she had anticipated. She squeezed her eyes shut, trying to dull down the throbbing headache. It was futile. Her pulse hammered against the sides of her head and amplified the sounds around her tenfold. 

She ignored it for the time being, deciding to focus on getting up. Kara braced her hands against the ground and straightened up in a sitting position. Her headache turned into a dizzy spell, which then turned into nausea. 

Something was wrong. She could feel it. But she couldn't remember anything. 

"Here, let me." A woman offered as she took Kara's hands and helped her up from the ground.

"Th-thank you." Kara whispered hoarsely. She frowned at the fact that she didn't recognize her own voice. 

Everything felt off. Wrong, even. 

But she ignored it. 

"It's no problem. I think you should get yourself checked. I can drive you to a hospital if you'd like."

Kara shook her head. "No, no, it's alright I'm-" 

She wasn't fine. 

"I'm fine." she finished weakly. 

Kara looked around her, the crowd slowly dispersing aside from the woman who was helping her stay balanced. Kara didn't recognize the roads or the shops around her. She didn't recognize the skyline. 

"Wh-where am I?" 

"Gotham City," the woman replied. 

That's odd. 

Kara didn't remember living here. In fact, she didn't remember much of anything. 

"Are you not from around here?" the woman pulled Kara out of her thoughts with her question. 

Are you not from around here? 

The question itched at Kara's brain. It ticked her off. Rubbed her the wrong way. Kara knew she wasn't from here. But... 

She couldn't remember where she was from. 

"N-No. I'm not. I'm from..."

Kara searched for the place in her mind. She knew she was from somewhere. Everyone was from somewhere. 

"From-"

From?

Kara was getting annoyed. She swallowed down thickly again, closed her eyes to think. 

Why can't I remember where I'm from?"

"From-" 

"It's alright, dear. I'm sure it'll come to you. You're just slightly rattled is all."

That made sense. Kara nodded. 

"Will you be alright on your own? Would you like me to call someone for you?" 

Call someone? That seems like a good idea. Call someone. 

But Kara suddenly realized she didn't know who to call. No name came to mind. Surely, there must be one person she could call. 

She couldn't remember any. 

"No, it's fine. I'll call them myself," Kara replied, slowly getting her bearings in place. "Thank you."

"No problem, dear. You take care of yourself now," the woman said before walking away. 

Kara was left standing on the sidewalk, alone. Surprisingly, she felt relieved. Now she could question everything around her without looking like an unstable person in front of that nice lady. 

Where was she from? Why was she in Gotham City? Was she married? If not, why was she wearing a diamond ring? If yes, who was she married to? 

And most importantly... 

Why couldn't she remember her own name? 

Kara walked the streets she didn't know. It was a foggy and humid evening. Kara had a feeling Gotham city was often this way. Billboards and shops scattered everywhere that she didn't recognize. Skyscrapers that hid their heads among the clouds. 

None of it looked familiar. 

A feeling bubbled inside of her. It told her that she belonged to a much sunnier place. A livelier one. This city didn’t feel like home. The air wasn’t familiar, it scratched at her skin.

Kara turned to a small alleyway to gather herself. There was no one in sight and it looked like the back entrance of a restaurant on one side and an apartment building on the other side. A big green dumpster sat a few meters back and Kara distinctly noticed she could hear the buzzing of a fly from over there.

That wasn’t right. How could she hear a fly from this far away? She shook her head, convinced herself that it was the headache and the dizzy spell still playing an orchestra inside her mind.

She leaned against the wall, took a deep breath and went through what she could remember.

I woke up on the road. Was I in an accident? 

She squeezed her eyes, trying to remember anything resembling pain from before she passed out.

Nothing.

My name. What’s my name? My mom’s name? My dad? A brother? A sister? Do I have a family?

It felt like an army of ants marching over her skin. She scratched nervously at it, paranoid beyond sanity.

She held her hand up and looked at the ring on her finger.

I’m married? Who am I married to? What’s their name?

She tried picturing what her spouse looked like, but she groaned loudly when she couldn’t even remember if it was a man or a woman.

Kara gave up. She decided to work with what she had. She looked at herself. She was wearing black jeans and a light blue button down. 

Nothing that gave away any clue as to who she might be. 

Kara checked her pockets next. She found a pair of keys, which looked to belong to an apartment or a house. No way of knowing where that might be, though. One key was much smaller than the others and looked to be for a locker of some sort. But the other two were for doors.

And when Kara closely compared them, she realized that they weren't copies of one another. They were two different keys.

Do I have two houses? An office? Do I even have a job? 

She fished her hand in her other pocket and found a wallet. No pictures. No ID. No credit cards that had her name on them. Just some money and a punch-card for a coffee shop called Noonan's. No address on that either.

Suddenly, The sound of a man arguing loudly with a woman echoed in her ear. Kara looked around her, but the alleyway was empty. 

Weird. 

She looked the other way. Nothing. 

"I want my damn money now, bitch!" 

Kara flinched when his voice was ringing in her ears. Her eyes widened, looking above her. Right. Left. Nothing. 

"I gave you what I owed you! I told you I'm done!" 

"You're only done when I say you're done," he grunted. 

Kara was confused. Disoriented. Where was the sound coming from? She began walking towards the direction of the voices. Slowly at first. 

"Ow! You're hurting me! Let go of me!" 

Kara began running. She looked around her, trying to find the woman who needed help. How was her voice so loud yet she was nowhere in sight? It made no sense. 

"No, no, no, please! I'm sorry! I'll get you your money! Just please don't hurt me!" 

Kara was losing her mind. The woman's voice was so loud now. Deafening in her ears and ringing in her mind. She focused on the sound, eyebrows creasing in concentration, and ran as fast as she could. 

Only she was no longer running. 

She was flying. Bolting through the sky and blasting towards the sound of the woman in need. She clenched her teeth together as she dashed down and finally landed like a meteorite in the ground, gravel flying around her and dust hovering in the air. 

Kara lifted her head up and was met with a frightened man looking towards her, his fist in the air aimed at the woman he clutched with his other hand. 

"S-Supergirl," he stuttered, fist slowly going down. 

Kara felt her blood boil, "Let her go," she said slowly. Dangerously slow. 

He immediately flinched away from the woman like she had a contagious disease and scurried off, running on his hands and feet before taking off. 

Kara followed him with her eyes, realizing that she could see him even from miles away. Hear the labored breaths he took. The frightened pulse of his heartbeat. 

She then blinked away from the scene and eyed herself confusedly. 

Huh. I could fly. That's handy, I guess. 

"Thank you, Supergirl," the woman said, finally getting up. "You saved my life."

Supergirl. There was that name again. Why was this woman calling me Supergirl?

"What brings you to Gotham?" she asked with a small smile. 

If only I knew, Kara thought sarcastically. 

"National City not keeping you busy enough?" the woman asked. 

Oh! That's something. I must be from this National City place.

But then something else hit Kara. This woman thought she was this Supergirl person. But Kara wasn't exactly... A girl. And not a super one too. 

Right? 

It all made Kara's head hurt. The identities, the missing pieces, the unfamiliar surroundings.

Kara figured she'd answer with a, "Guess not," before flying off. 

She found it odd that she remembered how to fly but not her own name. Her senses were slowly coming back to her. The powered up ones that is. 

She could hear people in the city. Crimes being committed and kids crying when told it was bedtime. She heard the hoot of an owl at the edge of the city and the beeping of a cash register of a convenient store. 

Deciding to try her luck on something, Kara focused her hearing. Letting it travel across the city and beyond it. Listening to bits and pieces of conversation. 

"Maybe I should just quit my job."

"I heard that her husband is cheating on her!" 

"Your credit card's declined, sir."

"Mommy, can I take another cookie?" 

"Boy, it sure is hot today in National City!" 

There! 

Kara stopped. Focused on that one voice. 

"Yeah. I don't think we've had a heatwave like this in a couple of years now."

She concentrated on where the voice was coming from. It was coming from the East. 

She flew towards the voice. Finally going home. 

Yes. This. This is much more familiar. 

Kara smiled at the sight in front of her. Even though she didn't much remember the names of the streets or where her apartment was, she felt the familiarity flow through her. Like the smell of fresh coffee in the morning. 

Interesting. I remember what coffee smells like. Do I even like coffee? 

Something told her no. 

Her stomach rumbled, announcing its presence and Kara thought she could figure out much more with food in her. 

Only problem was, she didn't know what she liked. 

"Guess I'll just try a little bit of everything, then."

Questions still rattled her brain. Why couldn't she remember anything? Why was she able to fly? Who was she married to? 

Who am I married to?

That last one made her heart ache painfully in her chest. Like she was missing someone she didn't know. Like that person could be missing her, looking for her at that very moment and Kara was just... 

She sighed. 

Somehow, the fact that she was able to fly and had superpowers told her to stay away from the police. 

Unless everyone in National City could fly? 

Looking up at the sky and finding only birds told her that the answer was no. 

Mxy smirked as he watched the helpless woman try to make sense of everything around her without having any memories. He decided to amuse himself even more by making her bump into someone she knew.

“Let’s see here, my little Kryptonian. Let’s see what would happen if we do,” he swiped his hands in the air, twinkled his fingers a little and swooshed the blue mist, shooting it in Kara’s direction, “this!”

She walked into the first restaurant that she could find. It seemed a bit fancy but she was having a rough day as is. She figured she could treat herself. 

She was seated at a table and was given a menu. Looking through her options, she had no idea if she liked any of them

She found it odd. She could distinctly remember the taste of chicken, but couldn't remember whether or not she liked chicken. She knew how sweet strawberries were but not the unique taste that was them. Not the difference between the sweetness of strawberries and the sweetness of chocolate. 

Her head hurt every time she thought too hard about it. She put down the menu and huffed out. She didn't want to think too hard about this. Didn't want to stress too much on it. Because if she did, she knew she might freak out. Might panic. 

It was very clear. She had some sort of memory loss. Amnesia maybe. Disorientation. Some sort of consequence of an accident probably. 

But there was no car. I'm not physically hurt. I'm fine. 

Except she wasn't. 

She looked around at her surroundings. There was a couple sitting a couple tables away not speaking to one another. They were on their phones texting other people. An old man sat alone eating a steak with some wine. Kara turned left and suddenly met striking eyes that held her own. 

It was a woman. Looking straight at her. With a raised eyebrow, she leaned back in her chair like she owned the place. She looked at Kara with a confused smile, but one that was calculating as well. Like she didn't know what Kara was doing but was willing to sit back and watch. The woman looked expensive. She screamed sophistication. Her hair was done in a neat ponytail, a pair of black Ray-Ban's perched on her head. She wore a tight sheath dress. It was dark red like her lips and Kara decided she really liked that color. Or maybe she just liked it on her. 

Kara went back to the woman's eyes. That raised eyebrow was still very much waiting for her and Kara looked around her. The woman was definitely looking at her. 

Is something wrong with my face?

Kara ran a hand through her hair, thinking it was tousled or something. She brushed her cheeks and around her mouth. 

Rao, if I look like a mess before I eat, wonder what would happen after. 

The woman gave her a questioning gesture then pointed at the seat in front of her. 

Kara looked around her once more. There was no one besides her in that direction. She looked back at the woman and found her gesturing to come over. 

Kara placed a finger on her chest and mouthed the word, "Me?" 

Kara was met with a roll of her eyes and with that action she suddenly realized how green they were. Forest green. But also a bit lighter than that. 

They bore into her. Frown deepening the longer Kara just sat there. And so, Kara relented. She got up and walked towards the woman's table. 

"There you are. What on Earth were you doing sitting on another table?" the woman asked. 

And that voice. That voice struck something in Kara. 

Maybe I know this woman. 

"There... I am?" 

“Have a seat, darling. I only have you for the lunch hour and that's barely enough,” the woman said as she opened a folded napkin and placed it in her lap.

Kara opened and closed her mouth, looking around her to see if there were any hidden cameras or people watching. Everyone went about their business.

No way I’m associated with this woman!

For the last time, she looked around before feeling like she should simply go with the flow. She dragged her seat back, sat down, and tucked herself closer to the table.

From that moment, she decided she would nod and smile to whatever the women said and hopefully get away with her paying for lunch.

She sure looks like she could afford it.

"What took you so long?" the woman asked. 

Kara stuttered, "I ummm forgot?" 

The woman scoffed, "Hmm. Guess I'd just have to leave you something to remember me by."

"W-Wha...?"

Kara didn't have a chance to complete, because next thing she knew, a soft foot was running over the back of her calf. The action caused her to jerk her knee up, rattling the spoons and plates, and spilling a bit of wine. Kara was halfway out of her seat, scared shirtless of this woman. 

She was merely met with a smirk, "Playing hard to get?" 

"Wh-wha- No!" Kara hissed, slowly sitting back down, "I don't know who you are."

"Oh, so it's foreplay that you want, baby," the woman husked, foot returning to Kara's calf and going up, up, up... 

"W-woah!" Kara's knee jerked up again, more wine spilling. But this time, she couldn't jump out of her chair. Because just as this woman's foot made contact in between Kara's thighs, a waitress came by to take her order. 

Kara clutched at the stranger's foot from under the table nervously and coughed a little to cover her grunt. 

"I'll have another glass of the Chateau Cheval Blanc, please," the woman said, mumbling her next words, "Since most of it was very much spilled."

"Right away, Ms. Luthor." The waitress turned to Kara. "And for you, ma'am?" 

"Water," Kara blurted, a little too fast, a little too high pitched. "Just water, please," she added, clutching at the foot that was trying to do sinful things under the table. 

"Of course." The waitress nodded and walked away. 

"Parched, baby?" the woman asked. 

"I have no idea who you are!" Kara whispered. "This is not a game! I woke up in-" 

"Here's your wine, Ms. Luthor." The waitress came with two glasses. "And your water." she smiled. 

"Thank you." Kara said kindly, taking a sip of the water to clear her mind. With the waitress gone, Kara wanted to continue where she left off. To tell this woman that-

"You're wearing your ring," the woman said, with a clear frown on her face. Her foot slowly retreating from its place to Kara's relief. 

And Kara didn't miss that scowl. Like she didn't like the ring being there. 

"I thought we agreed you'd take it off when we were together."

And suddenly it hit Kara like a ton of bricks. One look at the woman's hand, and it confirmed that Kara was definitely not married to this woman. 

But from what was happening under the table... 

Oh shirt! I have a mistress?! 

Her face expression showed absolute shock as she gave the woman a once over. It made sense. The secret looks. The whispering. The sinful playfulness. 

Kara looked around her before leaning in and whispering, "Are we sleeping together?" 

The woman gave her a questioning raised eyebrow, "Well, there's not much sleeping involved per se." she smirked. 

Kara gulped, "Are you my..."

The woman clearly enjoyed whatever game this was, "Fuck buddy?" 

Kara jerked her knee again under the table, causing more wine to spill. 

"Oh, for the love of!" the woman exclaimed, clearly annoyed, "Do you know how expensive this-" 

"Nope!" Kara shouted, causing several heads to turn her way. She got out of her chair, and walked backwards, pointing at the woman and keeping her in her sight. "No, no, no. I'm not this... This person!" 

"Where are you going?!" the woman called. 

But Kara wanted out. She dashed through the door out into the hot sunlight. She loved the sun, it gave her comfort. She ran towards an alleyway behind the restaurant and jumped into the sky, flying as high as she could.

Okay. So, basically I found out quite a bunch of things about myself. I'm married, I have a mistress who I'm cheating with, I can fly, people call me Supergirl, I like the sun, and I'm sleeping with a crazy lady. 

Cool, cool, cool, cool, cool, cool, cool. 

Kara was still very much hungry. It felt like she hadn't eaten in days. She flew to the other side of National City for food in order to avoid that lady at all costs. 

Whenever she landed somewhere, people looked at her like she had sprouted another head. 

Was flying not a common thing around here? 

She was too hungry to dwell on it. She saw a hotdog stand on the street and almost flew towards it. 

"Hiya there, what can I getcha M- Supergirl!" the man behind the stand choked when he raised his head up. 

Kara could never get used to that. She didn't understand the reactions she was getting. Did she normally not interact with people at all? Or maybe she really wasn't this Supergirl person and whoever this person was not-

Her stomach grumbled loudly. She really really was too hungry to think about this. 

She chuckled nervously, "Yup, it's me. Can I please get a hotdog?" she asked, before changing her mind. "Actually make that two," she said, pulling out some dollars from her wallet. 

The man stared at her with an opened mouth, still frozen by the fact that Supergirl was ordering a hotdog from him, "Y-yeah. O-of course. Coming right up."

Kara watched as he picked up a hotdog with the pair of tonsils. She licked her bottom lip in anticipation, stomach rumbling again. 

As soon as she was handed the holy hotdog, she downed her first one in one bite. Not even caring to put any ketchup or mustard. She wasn't even sure if she liked them. The second hotdog was eaten in precisely two bites. 

She put two fingers up with chipmunk cheeks, "Two more please."

The man nodded hesitantly. She ate the next two pretty much the same way and held up another two fingers. 

17 hotdogs later, Kara was nearly full. Not quite, but nearly. When she asked for two more, the man showed her the empty pot. She pouted but paid him and left. 

It was a nice day out. Most people around her looked a bit disgruntled by the heat, but Kara welcomed it. The sun felt good on her skin. Like it rejuvenated her. Gave her power. She had literally nowhere to go, or none that she remembered, so she sat on a park bench and thought about what she could piece together so far. 

So, first order of business, I probably need a phone. To help me get around the city. Maybe Google myself too. 

She looked around. Where would someone happen to buy a phone from? Maybe if she-

"Oh my God, it's Supergirl!" a teenage girl screeched, a little too loudly for Kara's sensitive ears, "Oh my God, oh my God!" Once again, there was some type of high pitched screech, "You're just randomly sitting down on a bench in the park with normal people clothes?! That's mind blowing!" 

That caught Kara off balance. 

Normal people clothes? What type of clothes would I normally be wearing?! 

She looked down at herself, she was decently covered, nothing odd about that. 

Maybe I should be wearing less clothes since it's hot? 

"Can I get a selfie? Please, please!" the girl who Kara had almost forgotten about pleaded. 

What the heck is a selfie?! 

Kara stood there expressionless. Or rather her expression had a hint of confusion on it so it wasn’t completely expressionless.

"My Instagram followers would freak!" somehow the girl kept talking, confusing Kara more and more with every word. 

"So, can I get a selfie with you?" the girl asked again. 

Kara silently nodded. A very unsure nod. 

"Yes!" the girl then sat on the bench next to Kara, put her phone at arm's length and smiled. And that's when Kara finally understood. 

Oh! We're taking a picture! 

Kara smiled for the picture, suddenly realizing that this was the very first time she had seen how she looked. 

She had blonde hair, flowing down her shoulders in waves. Blue eyes stared back at her and Kara didn't recognize the person behind them. It was like staring at someone in a magazine. No idea who they were but appreciating the beauty nonetheless. 

The girl took the picture and talked Kara up for a few more minutes. Kara sat there frozen, confused out of her mind as she nodded to things she didn't know whether or not to nod to. The girl laughed when Kara nodded at something in particular and Kara thought maybe she shouldn't have nodded there? Or maybe it was good that she nodded? 

God, it was all so confusing. 

Eventually, the girl had to go, she waved to Kara and walked away and Kara let out a sigh of relief. Kara continued to walk around the neighbourhood, wondering where she could get her hands on a phone. Several people were looking at her like she was wearing a clown nose.

She giggled, imagining herself with a clown nose.

When the stares and the looks became too much, Kara decided to listen in and figure out what it was they were weirded out by.

A woman nudged her friend from across the street, “ Hey, hey. Look over there. That’s Supergirl, right? She’s just walking around.”

“What is she wearing?!”

Kara found it odd, looking down at her choice of clothes. Looked normal enough.

She continued to listen in on conversations around her.

“Is that Supergirl?”

“Supergirl is crossing the street!”

“What is she wearing?!”

“Mom, look! Supergirl!”

The more Kara listened, the more she realized people were concerned mainly about two things: what she was wearing and why she was walking.

She went to rectify that immediately. She walked into the first store she could find. 

A few minutes later, she came back out looking like a new person. The store didn't sell pants, but she did find a few items that appealed to her. 

Supergirl walked out and into the streets of National City wearing heart shaped sunglasses, a tank top that said 'I'm a snack. Come eat me.' with a huge bag of popcorn clutched in one hand and a blueberry slushie in the other. 

That's more like it, she thought to herself, making her way to a nice looking bench under the sunlight. 

“Now, where to find a cellphone?” She wondered to herself, looking around the park while shoveling popcorn into her mouth.
