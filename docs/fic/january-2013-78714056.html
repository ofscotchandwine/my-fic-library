    Chapter 1

    Agatha roused on a frigid Saturday morning to familiar infant wails, her wife already gone at work for the day. Rio had already done Nicky’s early morning routine before heading out, so Agatha slowly moved through her own morning. She collected her four month old from her crib down the hall, feeding and changing her, rocking a happy baby against her chest. Since going back to work, Agatha had missed the lazy days with her daughter, ones where she’d stay in her pjs til’ noon and spend the day doing tummy time or making sure Nicky didn’t shove random objects into her mouth.

    Once she’d gotten Nicky down for her midday nap, Agatha hauled ass to the bedroom, collecting all the neglected laundry and tossing in a load before going to shower. She silently congratulated herself for timing everything out perfectly, with an infant, that shit was better than drugs. Once dressed, she returned to the nursery, her hair still damp against her sweater, her leggings and thick socks keeping her somewhat warm in their century old house that was prone to drafts. Nicky had apparently not appreciated Agatha’s confidence, because she had graced her mama with a heinous diaper blowout.

    Motherhood is so magical.

    So, after bathing Nicky, changing her and feeding her, she got her dressed again. Agatha slipped on the first tiny sock, patterned all over with small white bunnies, before kissing her daughter’s chubby foot and slipping on the second sock. When she was smiling and gurgling again, Agatha scooped her up, heading downstairs and putting her in her baby bouncer while Agatha went about folding through the literal mountain of laundry. Rio did what she could while she was home, Agatha knew that, but it didn’t suck any less that she was left doing this alone.

    She was three quarters of the way through her chore when a sob rang out, Nicky’s fisted hands flailing about and knocking into the sensory toys dangling from the bouncer. Agatha tucked her semi-wet hair behind her ears, furrowing her brows as she knelt down on the floor to unclip and cradle her baby. She wasn’t hungry, she just ate. She didn’t need changing, Agatha held up Nicky’s tiny form, sniffing. Yeah, she definitely didn’t need a diaper change. She wasn’t tired, her next nap wasn’t for two more hours. Okay, okay–Agatha just needed to think.

    Could it be colic? Usually infants would’ve already had it before four months, it should be cleared up by this stage. She was bathed, fed, changed, and rested. What the fuck was Agatha missing? She gently shushed Nicky, sitting on the couch and softly singing while her daughter kept shrieking. Maybe she just needed a little love, didn't we all? So, she went upstairs to the nursery to rock her, it was fool proof. Nicky was a very touchy kid, as in, she loved physical touch, not that she was erratic. Four month olds can’t be erratic. Agatha rocked, and rocked, and rocked.

    Nothing stopped the cries.

    Odd.

    So, she worked off her sweater, placing Nicky’s head against her chest, maybe skin to skin? Another thirty minutes and Nicky was still not feeling too hot. Hot! Agatha hoisted herself up, rushing to the bathroom for the pediatric thermometer. Being married to a pediatric surgeon meant that they were well stocked on any pediatric health supplies. Only, Nicky’s temperature was fine. Healthy, even. Okay, plan B, or plan C, if she was being picky. Agatha got dressed, bundling herself and Nicky up for the freezing temps outside, and loaded her into the car. A nice long drive could soothe her, there was nothing wrong with being particular, she was Agatha’s daughter after all.

    An hour of nonsensical driving later, and Agatha pulled over, slumping forward and resting her head on the steering wheel. Nicky’s cries were louder now, if anything. She rubbed her temples, sighing to herself. What if there was something wrong and Agatha didn’t notice? Would she really miss something like that? Better not to take any chances. She shifted the gear back into drive, pulling off and driving to the only person she’d trust to look at her daughter.

    Her wife.

    She parked at Mass Gen, striding down the halls with Nicky in her carseat still screaming like a banshee. Agatha hooked the carseat around her elbow as she stormed into the PEDs wing, she was on a god damn mission. The nurses all curiously tilted her heads as she approached their station. Oh, this was not the normal crew, must be the weekend gang. Fuck. “Hi,” Agatha bluntly said, her head spinning from her daughter’s distressed sobs. “I need you to get Dr.Vidal for me.”

    “Um,” a younger nurse, no more than twenty-five, cautiously spoke. “Is your child a patient?”

    “No,” she barked. “I don’t care where she is, page Dr.Vidal right now.” “Dr.Vidal is in surgery.” The nurse pointed to the surgery board on the opposite wall. “If it’s urgent, I can direct you to the ER, ma’am.”

    Agatha let out a deep breath so as not to tear this woman’s head off, though Nicky’s wailing did start to make her think it was a good idea. “I am not going to the ER, you will page Dr.Vidal right now before I shove my foot up your ass!”

    “Ma’am,” she grimaced, her tone subtly judgemental. “If you continue, I will have to call security-”

    “Does that mean you’ll also call the fucking doctor?!”

    “I can't page a doctor because a stranger asks me to…”

    Agatha held up her left hand with her engagement ring and wedding band on display. “You can call down to that OR and tell Dr.Vidal that she’s two seconds away from Mrs.Vidal leaving her ass if she doesn’t close up and get here NOW!”

    “Oh.” The nurse shrunk down a bit. “You’re her wife?”

    “My god, what do they even teach you in school?! Tell her I’m holding this place hostage til’ I see her.” Agatha strutted away, finding an empty room free of a patient and slamming the door shut. Okay, maybe slamming things would not ease her daughter, that was a bad idea. She set Nicky’s carseat on the bed, trying her best to smile through the pit in her stomach. After five minutes, she texted Jen to see if she was in today. Luckily, she was able to meet Agatha even if Rio wasn’t. Jen knocked on the door, slowly peeking her head in. “Agatha?” “Hi,” she sniffled, mirroring her daughter’s own anguish as her eyes flooded. Jen walked in, her pink gyno scrubs basically reflecting off the fluorescents. “What’s so wrong that you commandeered a room?”

    Agatha motioned to Nicky. “She hasn’t stopped crying for three hours straight. I’ve fed her, changed her, burped her, took her temperature. I tried skin to skin, I tried singing and rocking, I tried driving. Nothing has worked.”

    “Okay,” Jen chirped, plucking Nicky from her carseat and smiling. “Are you giving your mama hell?” The only response was more agonized tears. “And that’s why you’re my favorite goddaughter.”

    “Please, just…” Agatha trailed off. “I don’t know what I did.”

    “Let’s check you out,” Jen told her baby. “I bet it’s nothing serious.” “What if I hurt her or something? What if I smacked her head?”

    “Well, did you?” Jen asked.

    “Not that I can recall,” she dryly replied.

    Jen set Nicky down on the bed, unwinding her stethoscope from her neck. “Then you probably didn’t.”

    Agatha silently watched from where she was perched on the bed as Jen conducted an exam, nervously chomping her lower lip as her mind whirled. What if she had hurt Nicky? What if she’d done something wrong? What if she was negligent? Fuck, what if she was no better than her own mother? Nicky never screamed like this with Rio or Lilia, what if she fucked up her daughter and Nicky would have to live with those consequences for the rest of her life? The door burst open, Rio hastily ripping off her Snoopy scrub cap. “What’s wrong?!” She panted, having clearly sprinted here.

    “I think I hurt Nicky,” Agatha wept, wiping the snot from her nose. “She won’t stop crying.” Rio cocked her head, crossing the room and kissing Agatha temple before lurking over Jen’s shoulder. “What happened?”

    “I don’t know!” Agatha’s face fell into her hands, feeling overwhelmed with guilt and shame. What kind of mother was she that she couldn’t protect her own child?

    “Okay, she’s most definitely not hurt in any capacity,” Jen muttered. “Agatha, take me through the morning, maybe it’s environmental.”

    She repeated everything, down to every singular detail she could remember. “I was almost done with the laundry when she started crying.” Agatha finished, looking between the two for answers.

    Rio blinked, silently moving Jen aside. “Hi, mi vida,” She smiled down at their baby, plucking off one sock and examining her foot. Rio then plucked off the other, clapping her hands together. “Mystery solved!”

    Agatha leaned over in shock, her eyes widening at Nicky’s poor pinky toe strangled by what looked like a thread. “What is that?”

    “Jen, tweezers.” Rio placed a kiss to Nicky’s tear stained cheek. “I know, princesa. It’s almost over.” Rio was handed the tweezers, methodically unwrapping the string. “Hair tourniquet. You got her dressed while your hair was still wet, the hair wrapped around her toe and dried. Her toe was strangled as a result, happens all the time. And since the skin isn’t broken, and the skin hasn’t swelled over the hair…” Rio pulled the strand free. “She’s fine.” A weight lifted from Agatha’s chest. “She’s okay?”

    Nicky immediately quieted as Rio picked her up, gently bouncing her. “She’s fine. And so, so brave, mi amor.”

    “Thank fucking god,” she murmured, taking Nicky when Rio handed her over. She peppered kisses atop her baby’s scalp. “I’m so sorry, hon.”

    “It was an honest mistake.” Her wife shrugged. “Plus, at least she’s not a boy.” “What does that have to do with anything?” Agatha asked, cradling the back of Nicky’s head. Rio pursed her lips, holding back a grin. “Let's just say, a hair tourniquet can wrap around any…appendage.”

    Nicky seemed to be mostly calmed, her breathing evening out as her little hand blindly groped at Agatha’s locks. She squeezed her daughter close. “I- I thought I did something to her.”

    “Why would you think that?” Her wife questioned, placing a hand on her knee and rubbing small circles with her thumb.

    “I don’t know,” she blubbered. “I was just so fucking scared, like, what if I’m doomed to repeat what happened to me?”

    Rio didn’t know the gory details, and Agatha loathed the idea of telling her. It was unspoken —her childhood. Her wife didn’t ask, god bless Rio’s patience that Agatha never felt worthy of, and Agatha would go as long as possible without telling her.

    Jen checked the time, giving Nicky a little wave. “I gotta go, but you were so brave, little girl.” She looked between the two of them. “Let me know if you guys need me and Al to babysit, you need a break.” And with that she was out the door and rushing off. “Baby,” Rio softly said, taking a seat next to her. “You’re the best mom Nicky could ask for, she loves you. I love you. You’d never hurt her on purpose.”

    “How do you know that?” Her voice broke as the tears gushed. “What if-” “Because I know you.” Rio placed her free hand over top of Agatha’s where it laid on Nicky’s back. “It was an accident, and it’s so common amongst new moms. You couldn’t have known. You didn’t hurt her.”

    “I didn’t hurt her,” Agatha echoed, more for her own peace of mind than anything else. Rio nodded before standing up. “Let’s go home.”

    “Don’t you have work?”

    “Alice can handle an appendectomy,” her wife cooed. “It’s not the end of the world if I have to cut my shift by a few hours to be with my wife and sick child.”

    “But, Nicky’s not sick.”

    Rio lifted her index finger to her lips in a shushing gesture. “Don’t tell anyone else that.” Agatha chuckled, getting Nicky situated back in her carseat. “Okay.”

    Rio held her hand as they walked out of the room. “You take Nicky home, I’ll change and meet you there.” She quickly kissed her, ducking down to kiss Nicky too. “Thank you, baby.”

    Rio began walking away, calling back, “And don’t you dare touch that laundry! I'll finish it when I get home!”

    Agatha smiled, the stress melting away as she made her way back to the parking lot and loaded her daughter into the car. She took a beat once she climbed into the driver's seat. No divorce this time, she’d married too damn well.

    Less than forty-five minutes later, Rio came home with flowers for Agatha, and a small stuffed dinosaur for Nicky and her bravery. She picked up their daughter, mocking a ‘rawr’ of a dinosaur to make Nicky giggle.

    Their four month old was smiling in Rio’s arms as Agatha said, “Did mami bring you home a T-rex?”

    Her wife looked up from where Nicky’s hands were fumbling with the stuffy. “It’s obviously a Brachiosaurus…”

    Agatha cocked her head, giving her a warning look after the day she’d had. “Is it?” Rio tucked her tongue against her cheek. “…Not important.”
