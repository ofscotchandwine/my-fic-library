london, august, 2001

 

It’s late when Kara gets back, so late that her eyelids feel heavy enough to sink right down to the bottom of the ocean. Her jacket—nearly entirely soaked by the rain that’s been steadily coming down since early that afternoon—gets stripped off the moment she walks through the door. It’s the only thing she has of Alex’s, and her sister will probably kill her if she ever finds out that Kara got the leather wet like that, but she just tosses it onto the coat rack and moves slowly towards her bed. The jacket is tomorrow Kara’s problem.

 

Everything is tomorrow Kara’s problem except for what’s waiting for her in the bedroom— who’s waiting for her in the bedroom.

 

The lights are off, but the curtains are still open, so it’s the faint yellow light of the streetlamp outside that cuts across the duvet. It only catches Lena’s shoulder and collarbone, the edge of her jaw, so that’s all Kara can make out, save for the shape of her body and the spill of ink black hair across white pillows.

 

Home.

 

Kara walks in quietly, just in case, but Lena makes a little grumbly noise and moves one hand in a sleepy wave. Kara takes the invitation to sit on the edge of their bed and receive her welcome home kiss.

 

It’s a good kiss. Lazy, familiar, exactly what she’s been looking forward to all day.

 

“How’d it go?” Lena asks once there’s a sliver of space between their mouths again.

 

Kara hums a barely audible little sound and lets gravity take care of that tiny, pesky, little inch again, until Lena’s sleep-warm lips cushion her fall. “Lousy,” she whispers into her mouth. “No one wants to be told their 40 million Euro Manet’s a fake.” With the tiniest tilt of her head, she focuses her attention on the perfection that is Lena’s cupid’s bow. 

 

Lena groans in response; Kara takes a deep breath to keep herself even just somewhat in control and gets hit in the face with it all—Lena’s perfume, and shampoo, and the fancy lotion she puts on before bed. Why did she want to be in control again?

 

“You smell nice,” Kara mumbles. She never wants to move.

 

Lena nudges at her shoulder, the one that’s attached to the elbow propping herself up on the bed and the hand running through soft, dark hair. “Change,” she says. “You’re getting the bed wet.”

 

“But baby, you’ve never complained about that before.”

 

Lena smiles, bites at Kara’s lower lip. “Go,” she commands, her voice soft.

 

Kara obeys. She makes her way to the closet, unbuttoning her shirt on the way. “How was your day?” 

 

“Great,” Lena says, sleep still clinging to her voice. The sheets rustle around, and when Kara takes a peek back she sees that Lena has turned onto her stomach. The long line of her spine now rests in the angle of light cast by the streetlamp outside. Kara wants to get in bed with her and never leave. “There was a breakthrough on the British Museum case.”

 

Kara shakes her head and tries to focus. The shirt gets tossed in the hamper, and she leans down to wrestle her boots off next. “Really?”

 

“Mmhm. They found a boot print. A really good one. The left heel was worn way down.”

 

Kara stares down at the boots in her hands. “Huh,” she says. “That’s—a good lead.” She tosses the shoes to the back of the closet and heads for the bathroom, her pants hanging open.

 

“I know,” Lena says, leering at the space between Kara’s waistband and the bottom of her bra as she passes by. 

 

Kara hits the bathroom lightswitch, squinting at the sudden, jarring brightness. She wishes she could stay in the cool dark of the bedroom with Lena, where her eyes had acclimated to see everything perfectly.

 

“And they found a hair,” Lena calls out.

 

Kara sits down on the edge of the tub, her heart in her throat. “A hair? Wow.”

 

“Only part of one, but it’s in the lab. So hopefully, by noon tomorrow, we’ll know his DNA, what kind of conditioner he uses, if he streaks his hair, if he has dandruff, everything. Isn’t that amazing?”

 

Kara stares at her shampoo and conditioner bottles, nestled right next to Lena’s fancy ones. “That is amazing,” she says. She presses the meat of her palms to her eyes and takes a deep, measured breath in.

 

“Maybe they would’ve found the evidence if I hadn’t been there to point them in the right direction, but who knows. It seems like Scotland Yard can handle the rest of this one without me, anyway. What I wouldn’t give to know how he got past my motion sensors.”

 

Kara takes her hands away from her face and blinks hard. She reaches out and turns on the shower tap. “You’re just being modest,” she calls out, raising her voice to be heard over the water. “They’ll be begging to consult with you again.”

 

“Are you coming to bed, darling?”

 

“I’m gonna take a shower first,” Kara says, eyes locked on the window. It’s a big one—Lena loves to leave it open on clear nights and look at the stars while she’s in the bath. It’s certainly wide enough for Kara to wiggle through. “Don’t let me keep you up.”

 

With a steady hand, she reaches out and nudges the bathroom door closed. 

 

Kara’s spent her whole life running. This shouldn’t feel any different than all the times before.

 

But it does.

Lena Luthor arrives at Scotland Yard’s latest crime scene with a double espresso and a raging headache.

Lena's heart begins to pound.

 

She gets to her feet again and begins to rattle off what she knows. “The security’s a closed loop, and none of the alarms were triggered. They didn’t short the circuits, or cut power, because both of those options come with alerts to law enforcement—I should know, I designed the system myself.”

“Mr. Livingston says he opens it every night for the fresh air.”

There isn’t much of a view, though at the moment it’s all marred by the flashing lights of the law enforcement personnel clogging up the street. Shit.

“Jewel thief, mostly active in the 1970’s. Pioneered the Snart Special.”

 

“Snart Special,” the detective echoes, copying it down. “Never heard of it.”

Lena just looks from the ruined wood-grain paint job to the muddy boot print by the window. 

 

Hi there, darling, she thinks. It’s been a while.

“It is,” Lena confirms.

 

“Mm. Good.” Kara presses a smacking peck to her mouth and pulls back a little, laughing when Lena makes an exaggerated noise of complaint. “I want to say something, one sec.”

 

“What?” Lena whines.

 

“I love you.”

 

Lena scoffs. “That’s all?” She tries to lean back in, but Kara keeps her distance.

 

“I love you,” Kara continues, a dangerous little grin tugging at her mouth, “like a thief loves a gallery.” 

 

Lena shakes her head, but there’s a smile pulling at her lips. “Oh, you’re romancing me?”

 

Kara’s hint of a smile fades, but the warmth keeps beaming out of her eyes. “‘Like misfortune loves orphans,’” she continues, her voice barely above a whisper, “‘and fire loves innocence, and justice loves to sit and watch as everything goes wrong.’” 

 

“Lemony Snicket,” Lena says. 

 

“Required reading for kids like us, huh?”

 

“Guess so.” Lena drags her thumb along the swell of Kara’s jaw. “I don’t have any literature to quote at you.”

 

“No?”

 

“No. I just love you.”

 

“Mm. Good enough for me.”
