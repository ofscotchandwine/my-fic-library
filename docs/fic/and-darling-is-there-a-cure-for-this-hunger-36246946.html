and darling (is there a cure for this hunger?)

“Are we still on for tonight?” 

“Are you kidding?” Kara asked as she jogged across the crosswalk. A car honked at her and she heard a shout, so she turned and waved at them but continued walking down the sidewalk. “You’re buying me potstickers, right?” 

Lena’s warm laugh filled her ear. “That’s right.” 

“Supergirl!” 

Kara turned and smiled as two young girls ran up to her. “Hi, there,” she said as she kneeled down. “Wow, those capes are amazing!” 

“We’re just like you!” 

Kara smiled through photographs and a few signatures that turned into a few more before she finally took off with a wave before she accidentally shut down a city block. Again. 

“Someone’s popular today,” Lena commented. 

Kara sighed. “I know. Don’t get me wrong - I love that I can inspire that kind of happiness or excitement, but -” 

“The public eye isn’t a forgiving place to be,” Lena said. “I would know. It’s exhausting, having people look at you all of the time.” 

“Yeah,” Kara sighed, touching down on a less crowded street and continuing to walk towards Catco. 

“When are you coming over?” 

“Cat wants me in on this next meeting, but then I’m yours. Promise.” 

“Alright, hero,” Lena said. “I’m holding you to that.” 

Kara hung up with a smile and waved towards another shout across the street, holding in a faint grimace at the sight of more heads turning her way. It was something she hadn’t gotten used to, yet. 

She had just sped up slightly and was thankful for a large bus coming down the street that cut off their line of sight when - 

Kara froze in place. She turned on her heel to follow the person who had just turned away and started walking down a side street. That couldn’t be her. It wasn’t possible. 

Kara followed down the street, then down another, keeping pace with the woman. That was the right shade of hair. Her body type was the same, long and slim. 

She had just turned the next corner, checking to make sure that no bystanders were nearby so that she could approach and see if it was really her, when Kara suddenly couldn’t move, fifth dimensional energy surrounding her. 

“Supergirl.” 

Kara looked up at Nyxly. 

“Did you miss me?” The woman smiled without humor, that spark that had always lit her passion, her rage, gone from her eyes. Kara realized with sinking horror that she was looking at a shell of a person. A fifth dimensional imp who apparently had her magic back, who looked like she had nothing to lose. 

Oh. Kara felt some of the fight drain out of her locked muscles. 

Kara shouldn’t have sent her to the phantom zone. She had known that, before, but had managed to brush it aside in the victory and the freedom of getting rid of Lex Luthor once and for all but - 

She shouldn’t have done that. 

“I’ve had a lot of time to think about this,” Nyxly said. “I’m sure you’re wondering how I got out, but -” she waved a hand. “Why does it matter? You have bigger things to worry about.” 

Kara realized she could still move her mouth. “Please, this is between me and you.” 

“I know that,” Nyxly said. She started pacing slowly back and forth, a tiger in a cage. There was no one around, Nyxly had walked her straight into a back alley with no access to the busier streets. “I’ve had a lifetime of torment to think about what I wanted to do with you,” she said thoughtfully. “What do you take from the woman who has it all? How do you decide?” She stopped right in front of Kara, who was still frozen in place by energy she couldn’t fight. “I don’t care about hurting anyone else. I just want to hurt you.” 

“I’m sorry,” Kara choked out. “Nyxly, I’m -” 

“I don’t want to hear that.” Kara felt her mouth freeze shut. “I think I’ve decided,” Nyxly said. “I hope that you agree that it is the best and most efficient way to ruin you, Supergirl. You have everything you’ve ever wanted. Everyone knows that Kara Danvers is Supergirl and they accept you for it. You have family that love you, support you, surround you -” Nyxly’s face did something before she composed herself. “You have everything ,” she hissed. “That’s what I’m going to take.” 

Kara’s eyes widened, assuming the worst. She tried to speak but nothing would come out. 

“Don’t look at me like that,” Nyxly said. “I said the only person I’m going to hurt is you. Everyone knows you, and you are happy.” She took a step closer. “So I’m going to make everyone forget.” 

Kara felt herself start to panic, just a little. 

“I’m going to make everyone on earth forget that you’re Supergirl,” Nyxly said. “And that you are Kara Danvers - I’m going to make them forget that Kara Danvers even exists. You will become no one. You will be a ghost. You will have to watch them live without you, as if you were never there.” 

Kara was panicking, now. She tried her hardest to fight her, but she’d never been able to fight fifth dimensional energy. She was racking her brain on how to call Alex, how to ask Lena for help - 

“And they’re going to forget,” Nyxly whispered, right in Kara’s face. Kara felt the breath leave her lungs at the look in her eyes, inches from her own. Nyxly had nothing - Kara was the reason that she looked like that - 

“Right. Now.” 

Nyxly’s eyes flashed, her hands moved in a pattern, energy flowing from them out into the world, and Kara fell into darkness. 

 

Kara woke up alone. 

She sat up and looked around, but Nyxly was gone, along with all traces of her. With fear running through her veins like blood, Kara took off into the sky and sped until she was landing in front of the new DEO. 

She walked in like she usually did, as if she was somewhere she belonged. The fear started to abate slightly as no one stopped her and she continued on down the halls towards where she knew Alex’s office was. 

“Hey!” 

Kara felt total relief at that voice, and she turned around, ready to regale Alex with the craziest story, when - 

“No one’s allowed in here without clearance,” Alex said, stalking up to her and eyeing her up and down. Kara felt her mouth snap shut. “Do you have it?” 

Kara’s veins turned to ice. She stammered in her sister’s face. Alex looked at her like she was a stranger, or more accurately, an inconvenience in her day. 

“Someone get her out of here,” Alex called over to some agents nearby. She shook her head as she turned around and walked away. 

Kara followed blindly as someone grabbed her arm and walked her back outside. She looked up at him once they reached the bright sunlight. 

“Do you know who I am?” She asked him. 

He frowned at her, his brown eyes confused and slightly annoyed. “Should I?” 

Kara turned around and walked down the sidewalk, pulling out her phone and dialing the number she had called last. It rang twice and then went straight to voicemail. Lena never did that, she always answered Kara, or texted her to say she was busy. Kara called again and focused her hearing across town, where she knew Lena was still at her office for her foundation. 

A phone was ringing. A frustrated sigh and the clacking of heels. “Jess, did we put the spyware on my new personal phone? You know, the one that filters out spam, reporters, unknown numbers?” 

Shuffling. “I thought so.” 

A hum. “Strange. We might have to strengthen it.” Another sigh. “And here I thought I had finally gotten passed the scrutiny.” 

“Maybe it’s a Nigerian prince who just needs a little bit of money for a plane ticket.” 

A laugh. “Sure, Jess.” 

“I’ll get right on that, Miss Luthor.” 

“No rush,” a slight sigh. “You should get out of here, it’s almost five.” 

“I’ll say the same for you.” 

“I’m not the one with the fiance. Aren’t you two having dinner?” 

“Not until seven.” 

“Jess.” 

“Fine. But promise me you won’t stay too late. Do you have any dinner plans?” 

A pause. “I don’t think so. I’ll probably just order in something here. Didn’t a new juice bar open up across the street?” 

Kara felt the phone slip through her fingers and heard it hit the sidewalk as if from a great distance. She felt her knees give out as she reached down to grab it and barely caught herself on one hand before her face hit the ground. 

In a daze, she walked to her apartment. 

“Oh, I’m sorry,” Mrs. Clancy, the landlord said as Kara opened the door at the same time as her. “Can I help you?” 

Kara stared at her. “Um, I was just -” She started to point towards the stairs, but the words got stuck in her throat. Mrs. Clancy, who Kara had just had an hour long conversation with last week about the unnecessary ban on pets within the building, was looking at Kara as if she’d never spoken a word to her. 

Kara swallowed hard and squeezed her eyes shut for just a moment. “Um, I was wondering about renting apartment 3B?” 

“Oh!” Mrs. Clancy said. “Well, come on in.” 

Kara followed her into her office and sat down on the chair across from her desk. “I can’t tell you how happy I am to hear that, no one has wanted to rent that apartment for years!” She shook her head as if she couldn’t believe it. “Now, do you have proof of funds and can you provide two months rent along with a security deposit?” 

Kara opened her mouth to say yes, and then realized that she probably didn’t have a bank account anymore. “Um,” she swallowed and looked away, feeling like her breath was coming a little too short, as if the pressure behind her eyes was going to burst, as if - “Do you think I could get back to you with that?” She managed to ask, standing up. “But I want to live there - please don’t let anyone else rent it.” 

“Okay, dear,” Mrs. Clancy stood up as well with a frown. “Are you alright?” 

“I, um -” Kara shook her head. “I need to go -” She rushed out of the room and back out onto the street, where she turned into the alley that looked up at her window and shot up to it, crashing into her apartment in a heap and crumbling to the floor. 

 

Kara was able to realize a few things after that. 

The first was that everyone had forgotten who she was. Nyxly was thorough - there was no record of Kara Danvers or Supergirl anywhere. 

The second was that she still had all of the photos from before. She wasn’t sure how that was possible - surely Alex or Lena would realize something was wrong if they saw a picture of a stranger on their wall - but Kara was glad all the same. They felt like lifeboats, those photographs, and Kara took every single one of them down off of the walls or bookshelves or dressers and surrounded herself with them on the floor. 

The third was that it was entirely her fault. She’d given Nyxly misery and had received it in return. She deserved it. 

 

For days, she just watched.

Alex drove Esme to school every morning. Kelly would pick her up and Esme would go on and on about her day, what she had learned and the kids she had talked to. 

Nia had recently gotten a promotion at Catco, and Kara listened to her talk to Brainy about how hard Cat was being on her but how she thought It was because Cat believed she could be a really good reporter one day. Kara knew that was true, but couldn't tell her. 

J’onn was flourishing at the DEO with Alex again. Brainy was working with them as well. Along with - 

Kara didn’t go near Lena. Not again. She didn’t know if she could take it. 

 

Kara was laying on the floor of her living room, as she’d been doing for days, when she heard Mrs. Clancy again. 

“Someone came up about 3B,” she was saying. “But they just disappeared on me. Maybe I should open up the showings again. It’s such a nice place…” 

Kara sat up and spun around, watching through the wall as the older woman paused on the landing, before turning around, talking to the person on the other end of the phone about what they wanted for dinner. 

Oh, she needed a job. She needed money. She didn’t have a job because Kara Danvers didn’t exist. Kara Danvers had never been an assistant, and then a reporter, and then editor-in-chief - 

She felt herself crumble under the weight of that. No, some things were still too painful to think about. 

After a few moments, Kara stood up, threw on some new clothes, and went back outside. 

She wandered around, looking for ‘help wanted’ signs. She realized that she would probably have more luck online, but the sun felt good on her skin. And nothing felt good anymore, so she was going to take it where she could. 

A loud noise caught her attention and she turned around another block to see a construction site up ahead. They were repairing the bridge that had been ruined in a fight recently, another thing Kara had broken when she was trying to make the world right. 

She walked up to the chain link fence and waved at a large man that was standing nearby. “Hey,” she called over, her voice thick with disuse. She cleared her throat. “Are you guys looking for any extra help?” 

She ended up in the foreman’s office, sitting on a squeaky metal chair in front of a woman at least two feet taller than her with a permanent scowl on her face. 

“Ever worked in construction before?” 

Kara shook her head. 

The woman raised her brow. “Ever done anything like it?” 

Kara shook her head again. 

The woman sighed and leaned back. She ran a hand over her face. “Look, I wouldn’t even consider it, but we just lost a couple of people and I really just need the hands.” 

“I’m pretty strong,” Kara said. “I can be a lot of help if you just tell me what to do.” 

The woman eyed her for a moment too long. Kara started to shift uncomfortably. 

With a sigh, she leaned back. “How long have you been here?” 

“I’ve lived in National City -” 

“No,” the woman said. “Earth.” 

Kara stared at her, wide eyed. “Ma’am, I don’t -” 

“It’s Beth,” Beth said. “I hire lots of unregistered ones, just so you know. They all look the same. Like that .” She pointed at Kara. “Don’t ask me how it happened, but it seems like every alien in National City took one look at my big cranes and decided that they were close enough to a spaceship for them.” She rolled her eyes, but when she turned them back on Kara, they were more open, kind. “I can help you get registered, if that’s something you want.” 

Kara took a breath. “Okay,” she found herself saying. “What do I need to do?” 

Beth leaned down and grabbed a folder. “Let’s start with a name.” 

“Kara.” 

“Kara?” 

“Kara Zor-el,” Kara said. 

The woman made a face. “That’s easy enough.” She laughed sharply. “You wouldn’t believe the kinds of names I’ve had to try and spell in English.” 

Kara smiled slightly for the first time in days. 

 

It was a little better, after that. Kara met Todd and Cai and Eli, all of them aliens, all of them refugees on earth just trying to find their way, and Kara found herself smiling in the breakroom with them. She smiled again when they dared her to lift a beam over her head, and when she did, they cackled in delight and called Beth over to see. Beth had her lifting a lot more things after that. 

Kara didn’t go see her family. She tried not to listen as much, having lost hope that it would wear off after a day or too, even if the hope was flimsy to begin with. She had seen Nyxly’s face. Her revenge would be final, and true. And it was. 

 

One day, Kara was in Noonans, a spot she only allowed herself to go sparingly, when she was craving her old life so much that the best she could give herself was something familiar to look at, smell, taste. 

She had just grabbed the cinnamon roll from the barista and smiled slightly with her thanks, had just turned around to retreat back to her apartment again, when she came face to face with one person she had avoided more than all others. 

“Oh,” Lena gasped slightly as the coffee splashed over her hands. “Sorry, I wasn’t watching where I was going.” 

Kara gaped at her, basking in the sudden sight of her again. Her hair was pulled up, the top of it braided to the side as she’d taken to doing in the past year or so. She was wearing green, the kind that made her eyes shine. 

She was looking at Kara in confusion, as Kara just stood there and stared. 

“I’m so sorry!” Kara finally got out, shaking her head and reaching back for some napkins. “Is your hand okay? Was the coffee too hot? Did it burn you?” 

Lena laughed slightly. “No burns.” She smiled slightly. “Don’t worry about it.” 

“No, I’ll get you another one,” Kara said. “That was my fault, I should have been watching where I was going.” 

“Well,” Lena tilted her head. “I wasn’t going to say it, but…” 

Kara felt herself growing lighter, like a balloon filling up with air. “Let me buy you another one,” she said, and then amended, “If you feel like it.” 

Lena stared at her for a moment. “Okay.” 

“Okay?” Kara asked, not quite believing that it was happening. 

“Well, when a beautiful woman demands to buy me a coffee, who am I to refuse?” Lena asked playfully. Kara sucked in a breath. “And my friends have been telling me that I need to say ‘yes’ to more things and get out there,” Lena admitted. “So I guess this is me doing that.” 

“Those sound like really good friends,” Kara said softly. 

“The best.” Lena smiled down at her half-full cup before looking back up, biting her lip slightly. Kara couldn’t take her eyes off of that glimpse of teeth. “Look, I have a meeting right now, but why don’t we set up another date?” 

Kara nodded quickly. “Yes, okay, that sounds great.” She found herself smiling, brilliantly, brighter than she had in what felt like years. “I would love that.” 

Lena blushed as she reached down and pulled out her phone. “Here, give me your number.” 

Kara did and Lena put it in her phone, where it used to be saved under her favorites, where it used to be the most frequent number she ever texted or called. 

“I can’t wait to hear from you,” Lena said, looking back up at her. And -

Oh . Kara had always wondered what it would feel like to have Lena look at her like that. Like - with interest. With intent. Like she - like she was something Lena wanted like that . 

Kara swallowed hard and nodded again. “Me too.” Yeah. She liked that look. 

 

They met up and it felt like a date. It was just coffee, but Kara had the kind of sweaty hands she got before prom and Lena looked at her like that again and again, and if that was the new normal, Kara could get used to it. She could, she kept telling herself.

“Where are you from?” Lena asked.

Kara almost said it, but caught herself. Lena would ask Alex if she knew Kara and then Kara would have to come up with a whole slew of lies, and Kara was done lying. 

“You probably wouldn’t have heard of it,” Kara said. 

“I’ve been to a lot of places,” Lena defended. 

“On Earth?” Kara asked. “Anywhere else?” 

Lena’s eyes widened slightly, but her expression stayed open. “Well played. You have me there. No, I have not been to any other planets.” 

“So, you probably wouldn’t know it,” Kara winked and took a bite from the scone they were sharing. 

Lena tilted her head. “When did you come to earth?” 

“I was thirteen,” Kara said. “I was pretty young.” 

“But old enough,” Lena said, eyeing her. Kara looked away. 

A finger tapped the back of her hand. “Thank you for telling me that.” Lena was looking at her like she couldn’t believe she was real. 

Kara’s lips tugged up into a smile. She knew the feeling; it made her brave. “Your turn. Tell me something you probably shouldn't share on a first date.” 

Lena didn’t correct her, and instead talked about the time in college she got so drunk that she emailed all of her professors everything they’d ever said wrong in their lectures. Kara had heard the story before, but she laughed just as much as she had the first time.

 

It was different, getting to know Lena at that point in her life. She was more open. She had a support system. She wasn’t resistant at all to more coffee dates, lunches, dinners. 

“Hey, you.” Kara walked into Lena’s office, carrying bags of food. “Lunch?” She held them up with a hopeful look. 

“How did you know I was just about to call you?” Lena asked, standing up and walking over to her. The new office was different from the old one at L-Corp - where Kara had brought her hundreds of lunches and where they had fought and hugged and learned to love each other - but this one had a couch that was black instead of white. They sat down together on it and Kara pulled out some of the containers. 

“This is my favorite,” Lena commented as she grabbed the salad. “How’d you know?” 

Kara felt the ache in her chest. “Just a lucky guess,” she said, smiling through it. It eased as Lena leaned over and pressed a light kiss to her cheek, fingers lingering on her skin. 

“No glasses today?” Lena asked. 

“Oh.” Kara reached up as if to touch them, fingers passing through open air. “I don’t really need them, honestly,” she admitted. “They’re more of a comfort than anything, and…” They didn’t give her much comfort, anymore. 

She left them on her dresser that morning, right above where her suit lay folded underneath four pairs of old pajamas. 

“I like you without them,” Lena said, reaching up to brush her thumb lightly over her cheek. 

Kara worried that sometimes she stared at Lena for too long, too intensely for such a new relationship in Lena’s eyes. But in that moment, she allowed herself to look back at Lena as a little bit of the adoration she felt for her bled through her eyes. 

Lena cleared her throat and looked away. “Are you really hungry?” She asked with a light laugh. “Kara, this is so much food.” 

Kara smiled, still staring at her. “If you learn anything about me today,” she said as she reached over for one of the cartons. “You should know that I can eat my weight in these things.” She picked up a potsticker and popped it in her mouth. “Literally.” She gestured to all of the containers. 

Lena laughed and Kara felt the world turn a little more right.

 

The first time Lena took her to her apartment, she was nervous. 

“Why are you looking at me like that?” Kara asked as they rode in the elevator. 

“I’m waiting for you to realize that you’re walking into the den of a Luthor and run away.” 

Kara threw back her head and laughed. “But you’re the good one,” she said as she reached around and pulled Lena closer by the waist. 

Lena huffed lightly. 

“Really?” Kara asked. “What is it?”

“I don’t bring people home,” Lena admitted quietly. “I don’t date, at least I haven’t in -” She frowned and shook her head. “It’s been a long time,” she eventually said, looking back at Kara. “For this and everything that comes with it.” 

Kara reached around with her other hand to encircle her waist with both hands. She tilted her head thoughtfully and stared at Lena. “It’s new for me too,” she finally decided on saying. 

“That is very hard to believe,” Lena said, lightly pushing her. 

Kara shrugged. “I haven’t wanted to date anyone that wasn’t you in a long time,” she said. 

Lena narrowed her eyes slightly as she looked up at her. “Are you just saying that to make me feel better?” 

Kara groaned and leaned her forehead down on Lena’s shoulder. “Lena.” 

Lena hummed and scratched lightly at the back of her head. “Promise you’re not lying?” 

Kara raised her head and met her eyes. “I promise.” 

A song came on Lena’s overhead speakers later, after they’d eaten dinner and were sitting on the balcony overlooking the city. Kara had started listening to her impulses, had given up second guessing herself during this second chance at life and love. 

Kara stood up and pulled Lena with her. 

“What are you doing?” Lena asked dubiously. 

“Dancing,” Kara murmured in her ear as she started swaying them back and forth. “I can’t believe I’ve never danced with you before.” 

Lena laughed lightly and shook her head, but reached up to put her arms around Kara’s shoulders. “I’m not a great dancer.” 

“I don’t believe it.” Kara leaned her cheek against the side of Lena’s head and closed her eyes, letting the familiar heartbeat and breath of the woman in her arms lull her into something like peace. The ache of it eased, slightly. 

“You scare me, you know,” Lena whispered, turning her cheek so that it was against Kara’s shoulder, speaking to the skin of her neck. 

“Why?” Kara asked, turning slightly to press her lips against Lena’s hair. 

“I just met you,” Lena’s hands tightened on Kara’s shirt. “But already, you’ve carved out a place in -” She didn’t say heart, or life, but Kara heard it. Her heart beat double time because of it. 

“I’m not going anywhere,” Kara said quietly. 

“Promise?” Lena asked. 

“I promise,” Kara said, squeezing her eyes shut against the city lights. And so quietly she was sure Lena couldn’t hear, she whispered, “Where else would I go?” 

 

Kara was at a jobsite, casually listening to her sister talk with the others at the Tower - they still did some work out of there since it was so private - when she heard her name. 

“Remember how I said I was seeing someone?” Lena asked. 

“Yes,” Alex said. “What happened. Did he fuck up?” 

Nia chimed in, “Did you fuck him?” 

A thump as if something was thrown. “No,” Lena laughed slightly. “I was just wondering if I could bring her to the next game night.” 

A squeal and what sounded like Alex’s brain exploding. “Did you just say -” 

“Yes!” Kara could imagine Nia jumping up and down. “Yes! It’s that serious? Oh my god, show me a picture! Show me, show me, show me!” 

“Hold up, Luthor. Since when are you gay?” 

Lena laughed. “Alex, my sexuality has been well exploited by the tabloids for a decade. My relationship with Andrea was very publicized.” 

“Yeah, but I thought, with Jack -” 

“Don’t participate in bi-erasure, Alex,” Nia said. “We accept all colors of the spectrum in this tower.” 

“Right. Sorry.” Alex said. 

“Oh my god,” Nia exclaimed. “Damn, Luthor. Get it.” 

“What?” Alex asked. “Oh. That’s her?” 

Nia whistled. “Please bring her. God, do you think she could bench press me?” 

Kara tuned out with a slight laugh. 

Eli knocked their shoulder against hers. “What’s so funny?” 

Kara smiled and knocked their shoulder with hers. “Nothing,” she said. She looked up and squinted at the bright sun. “It’s a sunny day. What’s not to laugh about?” 

They gave her a look before shaking their head and walking off. Kara stood still and let her skin soak up the sun for a little longer. 

 

“I’m here!” Kara called out, having sped from a few blocks over and turned the corner at a human pace so that she could run up to where Lena was waiting. “Sorry I’m late, Todd wanted some help with this new house he just bought but there was a leak and so then I had to shower but now I’m here.” She took a deep breath. 

“It’s alright,” Lena said, reaching out and smoothing out her shirt with a light laugh. “Did you run all the way here?” 

Kara shrugged and leaned down to kiss her cheek. 

“How is your hair this perfect if you just took a shower?” Lena asked as reached up to brush some of it back. “It’s not fair.” 

“Alien genetics,” Kara winked and turned to open the door for her. Lena’s laugh followed them both into the room and Kara looked up and suddenly found herself looking at all of the people she loved. 

“Everyone,” Lena said. “This is Kara. Kara this is -” A series of introductions commenced, with Kara shaking hands and smiling brightly at the people she’d known and loved for years. People who had known and loved her in return. Alex was the hardest one. 

Kara held up a bottle of scotch, already pushing tears back. “I heard you like the good stuff.” 

Alex took the bottle with a narrowed look. She glanced at the bottle. “It’s my favorite.” She turned to scrutinize Kara. “Trying to buy your way into my good graces?” 

Kara grinned broadly. “Is it working?” 

Alex begrudgingly smiled and softened as Kelly came up to her side. “We’ll see.” 

At one point, when the games had commenced and she and Lena had continued their reign as champions of game night, Kara looked around the room and felt her eyes burning again at the sight of them all, around her again. It was almost like before. Almost. 

Kara stood up to get another drink as an excuse to wipe her face. 

A hand curled around her elbow. “Are you alright?” 

Kara nodded as she glanced over. “Perfect.” 

Lena stared at her for a moment, before she turned to look over the group. “I get emotional too, sometimes. I can’t believe that all of these people care about me.” Lena visibly swallowed, her eyes cutting away and down. 

Kara took a step closer, into her space. “You deserve to be surrounded by these people, Lena,” Kara said. “You deserve every good thing.” 

Lena’s breath caught. “You can’t say things like that when we’re in a room full of people, Kara,” she warned, her eyes dark. 

“Hey, you two!” Alex called over. “No funny business by the chips.” 

Kara smiled and took Lena’s hand. She laced their fingers together and tugged her back over to the couch. 

It was different, being around them as an outsider. She wasn’t a part of the group, not really, but was there as an extension of Lena. Kara sat back and watched that first night, overwhelmed at the sounds of her family around her again. 

 

Lena revealed her vigilante status in a much less dramatic way than Kara did. 

Their dinner was interrupted by a call, where Kara heard Alex ask for Lena’s help with some rogue alien on the southside of the city. 

“Is everything okay?” Kara asked, following Lena as she stood up from the table. 

Lena turned to face her. She hesitated a second, before: “No. Do you want to come with me?” 

Lena took her to the DEO, where she ignored Alex’s furious look with a wave and showed Kara where the magic happened. Nia and J’onn were out on the field and Alex was trying to decide whether to send Lena out or join them herself. 

Kara had watched them complete numerous missions since they’d forgotten her. Now at the DEO, she sat back and watched her sister handle the threat efficiently, effortlessly, without her. 

“If only Superman were here,” a nearby agent muttered. “He’d have him in a cell in a heartbeat. I could make it home in time to watch -” 

His voice faded away as Kara realized something that she hadn’t before. They still knew who Superman was. And he wasn’t around because he was on Argo, with Lois and the kids. Kara blinked and sat up straighter. Nyxly said that everyone on earth would forget her. But Kal wasn’t on earth. 

 

It was easy enough to figure out a way to call him now that she had access to the DEO. 

“Kara!” He said as he walked through the portal she’d created, his form sharp in Kryptonian clothing that made Kara look back up to his face sharply. “What’s going on?” 

It had been a long time since someone had looked her in the eyes and she’d known - she knew that they knew her, for all that she was. 

“Hey, Kal,” she walked over and hugged him tightly. He froze for just a moment before hugging her back.

After she’d explained, he started pacing angrily back and forth. 

“So how are you fixing it?” He asked. 

Kara raised her brow and leaned back in the chair she was sitting in. “I’m not.” 

He turned and put his hands on his hips, signature pose, and frowned at her. “Why not?” 

Kara felt the weight of it again. She tried to shrug it off. “This is my life now, Kal. Nyxly’s magic was final and anything I do is just going to make it worse, or -” She thought about the way Alex might look at her if she knew the truth, but couldn’t remember. She thought about how Lena would blame herself. “It would make things worse,” she decided. “I’m fine. I just thought you should know.” 

He stared at her as if he didn’t recognize her. She’d gotten very good at receiving that sort of look, lately. 

“Well, I don’t accept this,” he finally said. “And I’m going to fix it.” He turned to walk back. 

“Wait!” She called out as she rushed forward and grabbed his arm. “This is my mess and I said that I want to leave it, Kal.”

He turned, blue eyes burning down on her, a mirror of her own. “Then I’ll make it mine,” he said, before ripping his arm out of her grasp and flying through the portal. 

 

Kara was happy to let her sister take over as National City’s protector until the afternoon she almost died. 

Kara was working downtown, helping put together a new housing project that had just been accepted by the city council, when she heard a faint rumble. She turned her head and focused her hearing, but relaxed after hearing J’onn materialize and the voices of Brainy and Nia calling out to each other as they fought the new threat. 

Kara went back to work and tried to drown it out, but eventually the fight came to her. 

As they rounded a corner, she saw that Alex was with them too. The people at the jobsite started running to their cars or just away from the action, but Kara hung back, her eyes on her friends. 

“Nia, go left!” Alex called out. “J’onn try and distract him!” With a sharp turn, she drove her bike through the creature's legs and then skid to a stop, coming to a roll and pointing her weapon up at them. 

It seemed to be going well, until it suddenly wasn’t. 

The creature decided that it would be in it’s best interest to start pushing buildings on it’s adversaries. A good plan, honestly. A plan that might have worked, if Kara hadn’t been there. 

They were all distracted and with a low whistle, Kara could see clearly the piece of stone that would fall on Alex, who had just been knocked prone and was looking up, realizing with horror that she was about to be crushed - 

“Kara?” 

Kara looked down at Alex, the large piece of stone harmlessly caught on her back. “Hi,” she said with a smile. “Beautiful day, isn’t it?” 

Alex blinked up at her, before a large crash made her turn and start fighting again. It ended soon after, and Kara followed the others to the DEO after Alex gave her a look that told her they needed to talk. 

“Kara?” Kara heard her name again once they arrived. Lena was there, walking quickly towards her. “What are you doing here?” 

“I - um,” Kara hesitated for a moment. “I kind of caught a piece of building before it fell on Alex and now I think she wants to talk.” 

Lena opened her mouth, before closing it. “Oh.” 

“I’m really strong,” Kara explained. 

“That’s fine,” Lena soothed a hand down her arm. “You don’t have to answer anything you don’t want to, darling. I know Alex can be intimidating, but -” 

“No, it’s okay,” Kara said. “I don’t mind.” 

It all came out once they were all gathered together. 

“So, you’re an alien?” Alex started. 

“Alex,” Lena warned. 

“Yes,” Kara answered, smiling slightly. 

“And you have powers?” 

Kara just shrugged. 

“What sort of powers appeared once you came to earth?” Brainy asked, leaning forward curiously. “Apart from super strength.” 

She took a deep breath. “I’m actually from Krypton,” she admitted. 

They all stared at her. “Like Superman?” Nia asked. 

Kara nodded. There was another beat of silence as they realized what that meant. 

“He’s off world right now, but we can get in contact with him,” J’onn said. “Kara, if you want to know someone else from your planet -” 

“Thank you, J’onn,” Kara said. “But we’ve met. He’s actually my cousin. It’s - very complicated.” She frowned down at her hands. “We had a disagreement recently. We just don’t see eye to eye on some things.” 

“Well,” Alex started, “If you ever want to join us in a more permanent sense, you’re welcome to.” She met Kara’s eyes. “You can join the team, if you want.” 

Kara looked around at them all. Lena, who she was a little nervous to look at, smiled encouragingly at her, and reached over to squeeze her leg under the table. She let her hand rest there. 

“Thank you,” Kara started. “But I’m okay, right now.” She looked around at them again. “I, um, I tried the whole superhero thing, once, and...” She cleared her throat and looked down. She couldn’t look at them. “It didn’t end well,” she finally said. “I don’t think I could do it again.” 

Lena squeezed her thigh again. 

“We respect that,” J’onn said. She looked up and met his understanding gaze and nodded in thanks. 

“But you have all the same powers that Superman does?” Nia asked, a little helplessly. 

Kara laughed. “If you ever need me, I’ll be there.” She met Alex’s eyes. “But you don’t need me. You take care of National City just fine on your own. I think you’ve got it covered.” 

 

Things changed even more, after that. Kelly and Lena had become very close. By extension of their significant others, Kara and Alex had to spend a lot of time together. It was nothing like it was before, but it was enough. It’s close enough, Kara reminded herself every time. It’s close enough. 

But nothing really worked to cover up the open wound where her sister used to live.

 

One night, Lena was at Kara’s apartment searching through her closet for a sweater, when Kara heard a thump. 

“Ow.” 

Kara smiled. “You okay?” She called out. 

“Yes,” Lena grumbled. “What is this box doing back here anyway?” 

Kara froze, before she sped into the room and grabbed the wrist that was reaching for the box. Lena’s brow raised as her hair settled down around her face, having blown back at Kara’s rapid approach. “Yes?” 

“You can’t -” Kara shook her head. “Don’t look at those.” 

“Okay,” Lena said slowly, sitting back on her heels, never taking her eyes off of Kara’s face. 

“I just -” Lena deserved an explanation. “I want to show you these one day,” Kara admitted. “Just - not today.” 

“Okay,” Lena said again. 

“It’s all of the people that I lost,” Kara admitted in a whisper, the words dragged out of her. 

A hand reached up and grazed her jaw, her cheek. She let Lena tilt her head back up. “Okay,” Lena said again, placing the softest of kisses against her forehead. “It’s okay.” 

 

Time passed. Years. Enough time passed so that Esme started calling her Aunt Kara again. And Kara started carrying a bracelet around in her pocket. 

One evening they were in the apartment that they shared, and Lena was talking about an experiment that had gone wrong, beautiful in the soft lighting of their living room, and Kara was struck, again, at how lucky she was to have this after everything.

Kara had loved her before and she loved her after. Those lost years were held in a small corner of her heart that only pulsed every once and a while. 

She reached out and tucked a strand of hair behind Lena’s ear. 

“What?” Lena asked, smiling. 

“I love you,” Kara said. 

Lena’s smile softened. “I love you too.” 

Kara brushed the back of her knuckles down her cheek. “I’m going to love you forever.” 

Lena laughed slightly. “Someone’s feeling sentimental. Darling, what’s wrong?” 

“Absolutely nothing,” Kara said as she pulled the box out of her pocket and dropped down on one knee. “I’m just wondering if you’d like to marry me.” She smiled a watery smile. “If you feel like it.” 

 

She was happy, as close as she’d ever get to the fullness she felt right before Nyxly ruined everything, when Kal came back. 

“Kara, meet me at the fortress.” 

Kara heard the voice from hundreds of miles away, but she turned to follow it, sidestepping into an alleyway and then shooting off into the sky. 

He wasn’t alone. 

“Hi, Kara,” Mxy looked despondent, standing next to the towering figure of Kal. 

“Mxy,” Kara said, walking over and embracing him. “I thought you were gone.” 

“Not for good,” Mxy said, glancing back at Kal. He turned back to Kara, eyes shining. “I’m going to fix what she did, Kara.” 

Kara suddenly couldn’t breathe. “What?” 

Kal moved beside her. “I told you that I’d fix this,” he said quietly. “I couldn’t fix what happened before. I left you Lex. I left you - I just left you, with the Danvers -” He sucked in a sharp breath. 

“Kal -” She shook her head. 

“I know.” He held up a hand before placing it on her shoulder. “But I had to fix this. This was too much, Kara. No one should have to live through this.” 

She stared at him, tears leaking out of her eyes. “He can do it?” She whispered. 

“I can,” Mxy said, stepping forward. “I can make everyone remember you.” 

Kara reached out and steadied herself on Kal’s arm. “Wait -” She wiped her face and looked at Mxy. “Not everyone.” 

“What do you mean?” Kal asked. 

Kara thought about the suit she hadn’t worn in years. She thought about the aliens that knew her as a coworker, as a friend, a fellow refugee. She thought about walking down the street holding Lena’s hand. 

“Just my family,” she said. “I don’t want the world to remember me - I don’t want them to remember Supergirl.” 

“Kara -” 

“I haven’t been her in years, Kal,” Kara looked up at him. “I didn’t realize how tired I was until I stopped and I can’t -” She stopped and pressed her lips together hard, for a moment. 

“You don’t want the world to remember Supergirl?” 

“Look where it got me,” Kara said. “This happened because of something I did that I shouldn’t have done. I went too far, and - I might do it again.” She took a deep breath and looked at Mxy. “So just my family. Here,” She reached in her pocket for the small journal she kept there and wrote down the names. She gave it to him. “Just those people.” 

He gingerly took it. “Are you sure?” 

She nodded. “Yes.” 

“Alright,” Mxy said. He looked at the paper again, before closing his eyes and humming a strange note. 

He opened his eyes. 

“Did it work?” Kal asked. 

Kara waited a moment, to see if anyone would feel differently, but nothing seemed to be -  

And then Kara’s phone started ringing. She looked down at it to see Alex’s name. It eventually went to voicemail, and then started up again. It buzzed with text after text after text. 

“I have to go,” she said, the realization of what was happening making her throat close up. She looked up at Kal and tugged him in for a hug. “I can’t tell you what this means to me,” she whispered against his shoulder. 

His arms were tight around her. “Go,” he said. 

 

She landed in the tower. They were all there. 

Alex was the first to turn, her face shiny with tears. The breath rushed out of Kara as Alex flung herself at her. “Kara,” her sister sobbed into her shoulder. “I’m so sorry. I’m so -” 

“Alex?” Kara asked. 

Alex’s arms tightened. They felt tighter than Kal’s. “Yeah, Kara. It’s me. I know you.” She sucked in a shaky breath. “You’re my sister, Kara. I remember.” 

Kara’s face crumpled in the shelter of her sister’s arms. 

Alex finally let go so that Nia could hug Kara, then J’onn, then Brainy. Kelly was just letting her go from a warm hug when the elevator dinged. 

Kara met Lena’s eyes from across the room and she knew. Lena hadn’t looked at her like that for so long - 

“Kara -” Lena choked out when Kara reached her, grasping her jacket with tight fists and shaking, her face pressed into the now-wet skin of her neck. “Kara -” 

“It’s not your fault,” Kara whispered into her ear, because she knew that’s where Lena’s mind would go. “It’s not your fault. It’s not your fault.” 

Lena shook her head and sobbed in her arms. Kara wrapped her arms around her tighter. 

Finally she leaned back to look up at Kara again, hands came up to graze her jaw. The bracelet on her wrist glinted in the light. 

Kara’s eyes followed Lena’s as she glanced at it. 

Kara swallowed. “Do you still want to marry me?” She asked quietly. 

Lena’s lips parted, before a small laugh came out of them. A hint of a smile. And then - 

“Yes,” she said, voice thick as she leaned up to press a kiss to Kara’s cheek before tucking herself back into her arms. “Of course I do.” 

The others came over to pile on top of their hug, arms overlapping in love, and Kara felt whole again. 

 

Later, the truth of it came out. 

“Nyxly did this?” Alex asked in disbelief. 

Kara nodded. “She wanted to ruin me, after what I did. And so she did.” 

“You didn’t do anything wrong, Kara. We were saving the world -” 

“I did something wrong,” Kara said. “I still have nightmares from the phantom zone. I’ll have nightmares the rest of my life. And I still sent her back there.” Kara shook her head. “I deserved what she did. No, Alex. I deserved it. I did.”

Alex was furious. Lena was holding onto Kara’s hand tight. “She was going to destroy everything!” 

“She didn’t deserve the phantom zone,” Kara said. 

Alex shook her head. “Well, if I ever see her…” 

“She’s not coming back,” Kara said. “She got what she wanted.” 

“What do you want, Kara?” J’onn asked. “Because if I’m not mistaken, we are some of the only people who know who you are. Why didn’t Mxy fix the whole world’s memory?” 

“I asked him not to,” Kara admitted. This was the part she was most nervous about. “I - I know that, before, I had decided to introduce the world to myself, and it was really good. It was.” She looked to Lena to confirm. “I was happy and I was working on dealing with it.” She took a deep breath. “But after all of this…” Kara sighed. “I wasn’t lying, back when I said that I didn’t think I could do the whole superhero thing anymore.” She shook her head. “I don’t know if I can be Supergirl again.” 

Alex opened her mouth as if to argue, but Lena beat her to it. Her hand moved to Kara’s back and she moved it soothingly up and down, a grounding weight. 

“Kara doesn’t have to touch a cape for the rest of her life if she doesn’t want to,” Lena said, daring anyone to refute her with a hard gaze. 

Alex’s mouth closed. Nia smiled softly at her. Brainy reached over and squeezed her shoulder. J’onn nodded with respect. 

And Lena - Lena was looking at her like that again. 

After more questions were answered, Alex finally let Kara go after one last, long hug. “I’m sorry,” Alex whispered. “I’m never letting this happen again.” 

Kara leaned back and nodded. “I know.” 

Alex let Kelly drag her away and Kara felt another presence come up to her side, warm and familiar. 

“Ready to go?” Kara asked, looking down at her. 

Lena’s eyes were shining up at her. She smiled softly. “Take me home, hero.” 

And she did.
