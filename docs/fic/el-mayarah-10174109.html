Betrayal. It was a word and concept Lena Luthor was intimately familiar. Before Lex’s crusade against superman and his imprisonment, she had been used to so called ‘friends’ as a child, growing up knowing that they were leeches and only wanted her for her Luthor name. The children of her parent’s associates didn’t count, they were raised to be wary of anyone outside the family just as she was, and any fun was competitive and edged with malice. She thought she had found friends in college, but had quickly learned their value once news broke of Lex’s arrest, his trial, and imprisonment. Lena was used to being alone, but that didn’t make it any easier, so she had moved from the shadow her last name cast over her in Metropolis for a fresh start, and maybe, the chance for friends.

Funny how the one person in National City that she had grown to care for, and even call her friend, her only friend, had been the one to stab her in the back, but the best assassins always were. Sighing she twirled the amber liquid in her decanter and tilted her head back and downed the lot, breathing harshly as its fire burned its way down her throat. She wasn’t a drinker, usually, but tonight she’d make an exception. After all, it wasn’t every day you set your own mother up to be arrested. Sure, she didn’t set Lillian on her path, but she had helped and then brought about her demise.

She snorted to herself as she padded towards her room, snagging the bottle by the neck and letting it dangle between slender fingers. What a Luthor she turned out to be. One friendly with an alien, and one who betrayed their matriarch to protect a bunch of aliens she didn’t even know or care for. Lex would probably kill her for real this time. The thought caused a bitter ache in her chest as she thought of her brother and she filled her glass as she walked down the dark hallway and downed it in quick succession.

At least this burn was for a reason.

It was well past midnight and she’d been slowly working her way through a bottle of Whisky. She had soon run out of ice-cubes but hadn’t been bothered enough to get up and go to the fridge. The liquid straight would do her fine. She wanted the oblivion of unconsciousness, not a pleasant buzz. And she was almost there, she absently thought as she staggered against her door-frame and stumbled over her feet and towards her big, inviting bed.

After turning on her mother and watching the police cuff her, they had taken her in for questioning. Not the police, no, it had been the agency that worked with Supergirl, the DEO, their uniforms said, she had tried to figure out their full name while they dragged her into a black unmarked vehicle and drove her to a secure location. Agent Danvers had been with her, ( Supergirl had met them at the DEO) and had basically demanded she hand over the virus, the actual isotope, and she had refused. It was L-Corp property and would remain L-Corp property, and if they tried to take it like her mother had when she sent that human cyborg, then she’d bring her full might against them.

Perhaps she would have been more willing to hand it over, but the medical advancements that could be achieved with the virus were no mere developments and she would not hand that over, not until she was certain that they had examined every avenue that lay before them.

Supergirl had to step in between her and Agent Danvers as they stared each other down. Apparently Kara’s sister was second in charge while her alien boss was getting medical attention. She wasn’t sure what for, he seemed to come out best in the fight between his twin, but whatever, it wasn’t her problem. Supergirl had talked to Alex and said that this wasn’t fair, and that Lena had been the only reason that J’onn and Mon El, and countless other aliens were still alive, and that they caught the leader of CADMUS. Lena had just wanted to go home, and drown her sorrows in an expensive bottle of whisky, maybe two.

Alex had placed her hands on her hips, staring Supergirl down, and the hero hadn’t faltered, though Lena had to admit it was quite a glare. “Fine,” she said eventually and the Super smiled tiredly at her and then looked at Lena.

“Thank you,” she said and it was the first time someone had actually thanked her.  Alex huffed, threw her hands in the air and stalked away.

He hadn’t known what to say to that, his bigotry called out to his face. Kara was willing to forgive him, if Lena did, and she knew Winn was frustrated by the double-standards.

Apart from that, life was going splendidly…if only she knew what her girlfriend was up to.

‘There’s no rush. I’ll be here when you’re ready. Text me when you’re on your way.’

With Lena’s assurances Kara went back to work, promising to finish her article before speeding out to see Lena.

It was difficult and she only got a paragraph in before she gave up with a huff. She’d finish her article later, when she could concentrate on it and not on whatever it was that Lena had to show her.

She tossed her coffee-cup in the bin and started to clean up, putting her pens away and straightening her paper. She paused when she opened her drawer and caught sight of a velvet box, before quickly putting it in her messenger-bag.

Closing her laptop she thumbed out a message to Lena before pocketing her phone and taking her belongings home. Quickly checking to see Snapper was no where to be found, she ducked out of the cubicles and darted down to the elevator.

The human world moved so slowly, especially when she was in a hurry, and she might have caused hairline fractures in the floor of the elevator as she tapped out her impatience.

With barely passable human speed she ducked through the other employees and was soon out in the street, darting into a frequently used alleyway and shooting up into the sky in her suit.

Taking the time to drop off her belonging she was soon shooting across the sky to where Lena was waiting.

She’d memorised the route she would be taking, and soon she was outside of the city and flying across pasture and fields of crops, tracing a road until she could track Lena’s heartbeat.

Within minutes she came upon the co-ordinates and had to come to a halt. There was not a farm there, or there wasn’t any longer. Instead, there was a massive, and she meant massive, building in the centre of what was once a big field.

It was a recent build too, some of the equipment was near-by, and the dirt it was built on had only a few clumps of grasses growing in it. Plus, she could smell the new paint and the fresh young wood that went into its construction.

It was four stories, with reflective windows and she itched to use her vision on it, to see what was inside, but restrained herself.

If Lena had brought her here to show her something then she should wait to see what it was, it was only fair.

Her girlfriend was already outside holding a folder, eyes on the skies waiting for her and she felt a flood of warmth flare in her chest.

“Lena!” She grinned as she landed next to her and straightened from her landed crouch.

“Hello, Darling,” Lena smiled and tilted her head up for a kiss. They had been careful in public, as Kara Danvers and Lena Luthor, and had only interacted intimately as Supergirl and Lena when they were in the company of friends or were alone, so if Lena was asking for a kiss, then she believed they were safe. Still, she took a quick check around them and as she did Lena lifted a brow as if to say, ‘I’m waiting.’

Grinning Kara complied, lingering for a moment, before pulling away and glancing over Lena’s shoulder.

“So, what is it you have to show me?”

Lena looked uncharacteristically nervous, it was why she had lingered at her lips, hoping to comfort her.

Lena wet them under her gaze, slowly, as though she hadn’t quite thought about it.

“I… this building is 390,000 sq ft set on seven acres,” Lena said, clasping her hands before her and straightening, and Kara frowned.

Lena was talking to her like she was a business partner or rival, or employee, not her lover and she was a little confused.

“Hey? What is it?” She reached out and intertwined one of their hands and ran her thumb along Lena’s skin.

“I’ve been working on this for over eighteen months, nearly two years,” Lena brushed off her concern, though her grip tightened which gave Kara some comfort.

She tried to think back and couldn’t pin point anything Lena had mentioned to her about a project that was taking so long, especially one she didn’t think she’d ever heard of.

“I- when I accepted all of Krypton’s knowledge I knew that I-that we- couldn’t let it fall into time and space,” Lena stated nervously and she wasn’t able to meet Lena’s eyes. Kara was struggling to catch them, more concerned with what Lena was saying, and what she wasn’t saying.

“I didn’t want, when I die, all of that knowledge to die with me.”

Lena had been using the knowledge she had been given to her advantage. Patent after patent had been approved and L-Corp was like a Phoenix, rising from the ruins of Lex’s madness and creating a beacon of light for earth. Together, Lena and L-Corp, had taken some of Krypton’s advanced technology and adapted it to earth, to enrich the lives of almost everyone in it. She’d started to clean the atmosphere, and within two years L-Corp and its subsidiaries planned to be free from carbon emissions, with their buildings powered by clean, renewable energy.

“Lena, what?” What were you talking about? What had you done? Why was she here? What was in this building?

“So I took steps,” she confessed quietly and slowly held out the folder to Kara.

Bemused she took it and flicked it open. Moments later she was gasping. It was a land title, deed to the property in her name.

“Lena, what’s going on?”

“I have the entirety of Krypton’s information and knowledge in my head, Kara,” Lena needlessly explained. “I wanted you to have a little bit of home. There’s nothing in here that can be used,” she promised, before ducking her head shyly and adding, “ at least nothing I don’t have a patent for.”

Kara slowly lifted her eyes from Lena and turned the full power of her vision on the building behind her.

It immediately became clear what Lena had done and Kara nearly fell to her knees.

“I had to design most of this myself,” Lena explained, and Kara could vaguely register her words as though they were from a great distance, or muffled underwater.

“I then sent the designs to various groups and professionals around the country to build what I could not. The team, lead by Jess, has been putting it together for weeks.”

Kara let her feet draw her forward, absently handing the folder back to Lena as she entered the door, as though she were entering any random double-doored room at Catco and not taking a walk through her entire culture and home.

Lena caught the door behind her as Kara brought her hands to her mouth and let out a broken cry.

The space in front of her was mostly taken up by a giant wall, a mural that instantly made her feel like she was home.

It was as though she was standing above Argo City, staring out at if from a cliff point and seeing it proudly nestled amongst the rocky landscape, tinted in red. In the sky she could see the outlines of Krypton’s many moons, and if she focused on the city, she could see the great spires grasping at the sky.

Above the mural, where the landscape merged into crimson sky, there was a small etching of her ‘S’ glyph and the words, ‘For my love, we will remember for you,’ in Kryptonese.

She could feel tears already streaking down her cheeks and made no move to hide them as Lena came up behind her.

“Each floor is split into sections. I couldn’t fit it all,” Lena sounded sheepish, but also annoyed at her own failings. “I- I know it isn’t going to be the same, but I thought that maybe you could bring Alex and Eliza here… if you wan-oomph,” she grunted as Kara spun around and pulled her into an embrace, sobbing opening into her collar.

“Lena,” she croaked out and couldn’t get any further words out past the ball of emotion in her chest.

“Shhhsh,” Lena soothed and rubbed her back, pressing her close. “I have already started planning on the next one, but I wanted to give you this.”

Kara just shook, the chasm in her chest sitting open wide and tossing out her pain and loss as the mural of home looked on.

Eventually her tears subsided and Lena slowly pulled away. “Do you want me to call Alex?”

Kara glanced around, using her vision to scan through the concrete pillars to other sections of the building and she slowly nodded.

“Okay,” Lena agreed easily. “Do you want me to come with you?”

Kara nodded, interlocking their fingers as she wiped away her tears and Lena nudged the folder she’d dropped. “Ill get that later,” she said and smiled at Kara, ready for her to lead her.

Swallowing Kara glanced around and slowly led Lena to the left of the mural. It carried on for quiet a while, depicting the rough and rocky surface of Krypton, as well as its beauty beneath a red sun.

With one hand holding Lena’s, she trailed the other absently over the paint while Lena called Alex.

The bottom floor was dedicated, Kara soon learnt, to Krypton’s history and geography, even the parts they weren’t so proud of. The floor had been sectioned off further to discuss parts of their history, as well as different cities, important stars, as well as their moons.

Kara read every word in the building, she gazed upon every perfectly rendered picture, every intricately crafted city diagram, even tugging Lena around a perfect replica of Argo City, pointing out where her house had been, where she’d gone to school, and where her parents had worked.

She had to mockingly scoff at the laws that had caused Krypton to explode, and had traced Astra’s name as her story was immortalised, how she had tried to save their people and had been betrayed. One of the things Kara instantly noticed was that, for the conflicts, there were two paths of choice, neither wrong or right, which told their stories, leaving the viewer to make up their mind as to who had been just.

In the clothing area Kara had a brief pang of longing for what Lena would look like in traditional robes, the sharp lines and high collar, the long robes, and maybe even her house crest. She shook the thought off, she’d likely never get that, so it would only cause her pain to think on it.

She had to ask how Lena had managed everything, and her answer had been surprising. Somehow Lena had gotten the designs to costume designers, and she quietly told Kara she had commissioned the Weta Workshop for much of the props and costumes. Kara didn’t want to think of the expense, had only pulled Lena into a hug as they stared at the traditional clothing of the Guild’s. She could picture Lena in the Science Guild’s garb and knew that she would have fit in well with them, constantly striving for more, for better. Lena was driven like that, aspiring.

“Father used to make this for my birthday,” she had said when they came across the food section, detailing how Kryptnian’s grew, prepared or made their food. “It tasted like….hm, sort of like wet and cold cinnamon and raspberry, maybe? I don’t- I’ve never had it on earth.”

Lena just squeezed her hand and let her pull her around the displays

“Oh wow,” she gasped when they entered a musical section and she saw a traditional instrument. It was a string one, long and slender, sort of like a harp but it was played on the lap. “Does it work?” She reached out to pick it up, stroking her fingers down the strings before tears formed at her eyes at the sweet, musical sound.

With shaking hands she replaced it and they carried on.

“I tried to teach Alex how to play this,” Kara recalled fondly when they came across a few games. “I had to make the pieces from stones and shells…. She was terrible at it.” It was a game that Lena hadn’t played with Kara yet, so she was eager to give it a go.

“I’d like to play at some point?” Lena had offered and Kara had grinned, nodding and then going on to the next relic from her past.

In time they were joined by Alex, who Kara heard enter the building and gasp as she realised what it was, and she called out until she found them.

The three of them walked around, Kara clinging to both her sister and her lover and she had almost run out of tears at this point. It was an odd feeling, walking around her past and pointing things out, as though it was someone else’s history, someone else’s home. She was feeling empty and exhausted by the end of it, and Alex had left them to wander the building herself, and Lena directed her to another room. It was one they had avoided so far, but Lena pulled her away to it.

“This is probably my favourite room,” she explained as she opened the door. Inside was dark, lightless, until Lena pressed a panel on the wall by the door and the room lit up.

“It took me a while to program all of the stars, and get them right, but I know you said-when you first came here-that you would look up at the stars and try to find home, so….”

Kara couldn’t speak. Astra had been the one to teach her about the stars, to tell her the stories of their constellations and the glints of light that were other planets and suns. It had been one of her most precious memories with her aunt. Stargazing had been their thing, and when her aunt had time they would go to different observatories on Krypton to gaze out at the different stars.

“This is-,” Kara rasped out, eyes turned skyward to stars she had memorised as a child.

“I wanted to see the view from your bedroom window… did I get it ri-oomph.” For the second time Kara slammed into Lena and buried her face into the shelter of Lena’s neck. She had no more tears to cry, body exhausted and chest empty but glowing with warmth. Lena let out a little chuckle and kissed her hair.

“I love you, darling.” A moment later she reached down to interlock their fingers and led her into the middle of the room. In the middle was a pile of blankets and cushions, clearly making the room comfortable so that they could lie back and star at the stars, as though the were thousands of lightyears away, unhindered by the ceiling and roof, as though they were on Krypton.

After a time, with Kara cuddled into her side and pointing out the constellations and their stories, even though she knew that Lena knew them all, and likely knew them better than her own memory did, Lena lifted her hand.

A bright blue light appeared around her hand and she slowly tilted it to the side, and the stars changed!

The hologram was 360 degrees, capable of zooming in or out, like a telescope, and also able to change the view.

Together the two of them lay under the stars for what felt like forever, but wasn’t long enough, though was only an hour or so.

A knocking at the door, and then unwelcome artificial light announced Alex’s presence. It was clear she had been crying too, and she had her phone in her hand.

“I’m going to head out, will you be okay?”

Kara reluctantly left Lena’s arms and darted over to her sister, taking comfort in the familiar smell of her laundry powder and hair wash.

“Shhsh,” Alex soothed, and her embrace was as tight as she could make it, as though she could force her love for Kara through her arms and into her heart. It warmed her.

“I love you,” Alex whispered, and Kara squeezed her tighter.

“Love you, too,” she choked out and Alex slowly pulled back, glancing over her shoulder to Lena, who was doing something on her phone.

“Did you ask her?” Her voice was low, so low that only Kara would be able to hear it.

Kara shook her head and Alex smiled at her, squeezing her arms before she released her.

Kara had been carrying around a ring for a few months now, having gone shopping with Alex for it and had been trying to find the right moment to ask.

She had briefly flirted with the idea of asking Lena now, but ultimately decided against it. This was Lena’s gift to her and she didn’t want to turn it into something else. The moment wasn’t right. She was too emotional and exhausted to ask Lena to marry her right now, but she would soon.

“Ill see you later,” Alex said and lifted her voice. “Bye, Lena.”

Lena lifted her hand in a wave and the blue light materialised around her hand and the stars moved again.

A frown crossed Lena’s face and she flicked her fingers back, putting the stars back as they were.

Kara followed Alex’s exit as she returned to Lena and nuzzled up against her. Lena was warm and soft and she felt cocooned in her presence.

Letting out a little sigh, and fighting back the sleep creeping up on her, she laid her head on Lena’s chest, tucking under her chin.

“I can’t believe you did all this for me,” she whispered, content with the soft thrumming of Lena’s heart.

“Mh,” Lena let out a little hum and kissed her hair. “I love you.”

Lena didn’t need to say anything else, Kara already knew that they would do anything for each other.

“Can I share this with anyone else?” Kara asked after a long while.

Having her culture back, or parts of it, meant more than words could say, though she had a feeling that sharing it with others had been Lena’s initial goal.

The worlds written in Kryptonian above the mural came to mind, ‘for my love, we will remember for you.’

“This is yours, Kara,” Lena said softly. “You can share it with whoever you want, for as long as you want.”

The magnitude of Lena’s gift was encompassing and Kara could only let out a choked, ‘Lena,’ because she didn’t know what to say.

Lena understood though, tucking her in close and bringing her arms around her, and Kara was home, surrounded by Lena’s warmth and under the light from Krypton’s moons and stars.  Kara Zor-El lost her entire planet, her home, and it took fourteen years and thousands of light-years until she found it again, until an incredible human woman gave it back to her.

As she drifted off to sleep, the familiar constellations blending with the comforting heartbeat of the woman she loved, her two worlds blended into one and Kara Zor-El Danvers was home.
