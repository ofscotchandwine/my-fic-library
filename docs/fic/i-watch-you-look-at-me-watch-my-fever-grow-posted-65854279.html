    Agatha doesn’t get pregnant the first time they try. Or the second. It takes her a few days to get past it both times, needs to stay home and away from work for a bit.

    But the next time, Agatha pacing around the bathroom two weeks after their third round, while Rio bites the skin on her thumb raw from nerves, the third time is different.

    “Third time’s a charm,” Rio had said before Agatha took the pregnancy test, “That’s what my aunt used to say.” It’s one of the only things she remembers her aunt saying—hopes that in itself is some kind of lucky charm.

    The timer on Agatha’s phone beeps and Rio scrambles to switch it off as her wife practically throws herself through the bathroom door. She stares down at the test for so long Rio thinks they might have been frozen in time.

    Until Agatha looks at her. She doesn’t say anything but Rio can see it in her eyes that it’s different this time.

    “Agatha—” She starts, doesn’t want to get her hopes up.

    Agatha’s mouth opens and closes a few times, and she looks back down to check, covers her mouth with her hand, and then nods.

    “Yeah?”

    “Yeah.”

    “It’s positive?”

    Agatha rushes to fling her arms around Rio, kisses her neck over and over, “It’s positive, Rio.”

    She doesn’t know how long they stand there for, only that they’re both crying, heavy tears rolling down their cheeks. They’re definitely not bad ones.

    It hits Rio in that moment that a few years ago she was someone with no family—a restaurant left in her name the only thing she had left, and now, not only does she have a wife, a child on the way, but a whole group of people that have been with them both through all of that, and all that is to come. A family.
