Summary

loving is easy

When it happens for the very first time, Lena is shocked more than anything else.

Thing is, Noonan’s, despite being one of the only two cafés on the street, really isn’t The Place™ to be at lunch hour…or any hour, for that matter.

For one, it’s right in front of a Starbucks, and most of their targeted customer base doesn’t spare a second glance at their beaten up old storefront.

Secondly, it’s pretty universally known—especially if their one star Yelp reviews are anything to go by—how absolutely shitty their service is. Which Lena can attest to, being one of the shitty servers and all.

It’s not an exaggeration either is the sad part: their coffee grinder hasn’t worked properly in months, their machine is wonky at best; too old and too complex to really be reliable and the only chef at the afternoon shift quit when things started spiralling uncontrollably.

And even though Jack and her were a hell of a duo, two geniuses sadly do not equal one competent baker.

Not to get it wrong, Noonan’s isn’t ripping anyone off for these delicacies or anything. All the items on their menu are cheap as fuck, discounted and then some, have to be when they don’t get any business—ideal for most salaried employees.

But an average customer would still like what goes in their stomach to be FDA approved, right? Noonan’s…very much does not guarantee that.

It’s not even a café on most days, if there’s any cross-checking involved with a dictionary open. It’s barely a café, a sad attempt at a knockoff if you will, like it was trying to be one but then the Starbucks opened and it just stopped giving a fuck.

Their management clearly stopped caring long ago, knew it was game over already and that has been that ever since for Noonan’s fate. No shot for revival, just an excruciating wait till doomsday.

All of which boils down to one thing: Noonan’s is barely wobbling on its last leg, teetering over the edge of a complete shutdown—the absolute definition of an unreliable job, with that sort of knowledge looming over as a threat(/blessing?).

Which should’ve been a problem, in the grander picture, to any sane person. But also, to Lena, it really isn’t a problem whatsoever, on practically any level.

Infact, from her perspective, it’s the exact opposite of a problem, a total non-issue, and it sort of even makes Noonan’s the perfect place for her to work at because a) it’s always empty, b) their requirements for hiring are…extremely low so it’s virtually impossible to mess it up and c) she gets to earn some extra cash with zero work, which is always a plus ever since she's been cut off.

Not that she needs a lot of motivation anyway because pretty much any activity that spites Lillian is already pre-approved in Lena’s to-do list, and that’s reason enough, but working at Noonan’s has more benefits than just that.

Mainly, the entire reason she works here, despite all the other advantages, is because she gets to spend more time with her best friend Jack (a Master’s degree is no joke, okay? They’re not really big on time these days) who had been working here since before Lena’s and Noonan’s financial downfall, and disregarding any further considerations, Lena couldn’t find a reason why not when Jack asked her to tag along.

So, yeah. Noonan’s isn’t Lena’s least favourite place by a long shot but that doesn’t mean literally any other human being with half a brain would choose to enter its premises.

Seriously, it’s a shitty place beyond Lena’s personal agenda, beyond what her own pro vs con list can explain away. A shitty, shitty place that Lena thinks is a miracle has managed to sustain itself for so long. So shitty she wouldn’t even wish coming here to her worst enemies. Maybe except Lillian. But—that bad.

Which essentially means Noonan’s is perpetually empty, and Lena pretty much gets to do what she wants in the place.

Like today, her shift is halfway over, and if this day is like any of her other days here, they’re not going to add any more to the grand total of three customers they’ve had this year (all of whom were handled mainly by Jack, all of whom demanded an immediate refund).

That assumption is where she goes wrong.

She’s not even moderately wrong, is the thing. She’s properly wrong with immediate consequences because only a minute before Lena’s finally about to plug in her airpods and unwind in the employees’ room, a girl walks in.

And the moment she sets foot into the shop, Lena swears she feels the air vacuum out her lungs.

Lena’s first reaction, obviously, is shock—of course it is. Noonan’s never gets customers. Ever.

But the more she stares, she realises the underlying emotion is something else entirely. Her eyes are glued onto this woman, and even if she tries, she can’t move them away, can’t help but stare.

Probably because she’s ridiculously attractive, but who needs a reason?

Lena still can’t form a sentence, can’t force her brain to produce a thought longer than wow, she’s cute. And even though the customer isn’t wearing anything particularly fancy, just the overall way she presents herself makes Lena feel underdressed.

“Oh god, there is, isn’t it?” the girl scrambles, stumbling over herself, in a way Lena never would have believed she’d find adorable on any person, no matter the circumstance. “The Starbucks was, um. Crowded? So I came here, but there was lots of—like, too many—pigeons in front of the—”

Lena’s eyes widen.

Oh fuck.

“I don’t even need any insider info for that,” Jack laughs. “It’s so fucking obvious. To anyone with two eyes and half a brain.”

“That’s not true. She’s probably not even interested in someone like me—”

“Have you ever looked at yourself in the mirror?”

Lena rolls her eyes. “This is hopeless, Jack.”

“Lena, she buys the god awful coffee you make every day,” Jack says. “She even buys my little baking experiments! And those are definitely not something worth paying for—”

“She’s probably too sweet to turn us down,” Lena scoffs. “And—and if Kara wants to spend her money on bad coffee and inedible pastries, those are just her personal financial and dietary decisions!”

“Do you hear yourself?”

“Give it up, Jack.”

Jack rolls his eyes. “If you didn’t wait to get asked out and did the asking out yourself, maybe you’d stop complaining about not having a girlfriend all the time.”

Lena sighs, rests her head on the counter, practices all the restraint she can to prevent herself from banging her forehead against the surface till her brain explodes.

***

“Hi, Lena.”

Lena looks up, a little startled, notes from her last class scattered across the counter. The sight of Kara, especially after the whirlwind of emotions Lena just went through the past few days, makes something in her snap, makes something not in her snap too, and without realising, the tip of the pencil she had been gripping breaks.

“Hi,” she says back, voice meeker than she means it to sound. She shakes her head, coughs, and repeats again, calmer, louder, “Hi.”

“You okay?” Kara asks, gentle, eyebrows crinkling together. “You look a little spaced out.”

“Yeah, yeah, just a ton of school work,” Lena dismisses, waves around at the scattered pages. “What’s up with you? What do you want to order?”

“Oh. Just the regular,” Kara says, as if on autopilot. “Whatever works. I’m fine with anything.”

Lena raises an eyebrow, and now that she’s not fully distracted by her own efforts to impress Kara, it hits her how increasingly unattached Kara has become to the real reason she comes to Noonan’s—the coffee.

Lena is sure she’s fed Kara a variety of coffee-resembling drinks over time, with zero consistency, and even though she makes the drink as methodologically as possible in intent, it definitely does not come through in execution because their very broken-beyond-repair coffee machine never cooperates.

But she goes to make it either way. Except once the shot of espresso is poured out, she can’t ignore the smell that hits her nose, more pungent than it ever has been now that she’s actually paying attention to anything apart from Kara, and she can’t help but cringe.

She still goes ahead and steams some milk, dumps it in the cup and holds it out to Kara—but before Kara can take it, Jack’s words from earlier start ringing in her head. Something stirs in Lena, and there’s a curiosity she can’t push down, the urge to taste whatever the fuck is in the cup is insurmountable. She raises the cup to her lips, ignores Kara’s protests of wait! and takes a sip.

Lena can’t even swallow, can’t even hold her breath long enough till it goes down her esophagus. She grabs an empty cup as fast as she can manage and spits out the weird burnt bitter concoction, tastebuds protesting, eyes wide—

“You had this—” Lena points to the cup, partly angry, partly mortified. “You had it every day! Kara—what the fuck is wrong with you? How are you alive?”

Kara flushes, avoids meeting Lena’s eyes. “Wasn’t that bad,” she says.

Lena raises an eyebrow—downright glares, challenges her to disagree. “The aftertaste is killing me—”

“Um. Streaky and Krypto didn’t mind it much,” Kara mumbles. “They’re my pets,” she clarifies a moment later. “Streaky is my cat, Krypto’s my dog.”

“Kara,” Lena says, doesn’t know if she should laugh or cry, because huh?, and she just has to ask—“Why did you buy this crap?”

Kara looks away, cheeks reddening by the second. She mumbles out her answer so quickly that Lena has to strain her ears to hear the, “It was a reason to talk to you,” that comes out.

“What?”

“I—um. I wanted to get your number,” Kara admits, shy, hands reaching up to readjust her glasses. “The first time I came in—I just. I wanted to get your number, ask you out on a date.”

Oh god, Lena thinks, feels faint, light.

“Yeah—the Starbucks wasn’t crowded that day,” Kara continues, wringing her hands together. “I just always noticed you from across the street and thought you were really pretty, so one day, I mustered up the courage to ask you out.”

“Why didn’t you?” Lena whispers.

“I don’t—I don’t know!” Kara groans. “You were just so much prettier up close and I panicked. And—I did come back! I came back the second time and you did all you could to try and shoo me away—”

Lena’s eyebrows knit together. “That did not happen—”

“I called you pretty and you told me the food is inedible!”

Lena pauses. So that did happen. “Oh.”

Kara scratches the back of her neck. “Yeah and then—I just kept chickening out because I’m a wreck around pretty girls and you were not making it easy—with your entire—”

“My entire?”

“Clothing choices,” Kara says.

Lena sucks in a sharp breath without even really intending to. “Right. My bad,” Lena says. “I thought that would escalate it.”

“You wanted me to ask you out?”

“Obviously!” Lena says, exasperated. “I just didn’t think you’d be interested and then I saw Winn—”

“Winn?”

Lena hides her face in her hand. “I don’t know, okay? He just seemed like your boyf—”

“Winn came for moral support,” Kara interrupts, and the puzzle pieces start slotting into Lena’s head. They’re both idiots. “But I figured you were in a bad mood and—that didn’t work out.” Kara says, awkward, cheeks turning pinker by the second.

Lena decides that now is as good a time as any to finally start being bolder, doesn’t even want to imagine what else could go wrong, doesn’t want to risk it. “You could still ask me out.”

“I could.”

“You could,” Lena repeats. “It’s not too late or anything. We’re both single.”

Kara sucks in a sharp breath; nods. “Do you want to go for a cup of coffee—good coffee? As a date. Romantic in nature, just to be clear.”

“God, yes,” Lena says, almost too quick, smile wide wide wide. “I would like nothing more than to go on a romantic-in-nature coffee date with you.”

Lena lets out a long, heavy breath—finally.

Unsurprisingly, Noonan’s shuts down shortly after.

It’s a normal afternoon, and all the warning she gets is a got a buyer, no need to come from next monday in the work group chat.

She’s pretty sure that such short notice must be some sort of violation against a code of conduct, and if she does choose to pursue it with the Department of Labor, she’s sure she’d have a solid reason to file a claim...but it’s just not a bummer. Not to her.

Noonan’s 100% redeemed itself for all its sins in her books.

It gave her Kara. That’s all that matters, right?
