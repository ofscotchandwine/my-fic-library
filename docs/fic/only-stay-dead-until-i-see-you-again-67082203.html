Summary

12 months after Agatha Harkness died in an ordinary garden on an ordinary day, she claws her way out of an unmarked grave with a heart that beats, lungs that breath, and skin that is neither mottled nor rotting.

Though she is not entirely unchanged, waking with a hunger that is impossible to satisfy, a severe inability to stop any impulse her resurrected mind conceives of, and a new, potent flavour of magic thrumming through her veins, one very different to any she has previously wielded.

And the universe, sensing the effect Agatha’s newly ignited existence is having on it's sacred balance, dispatches the only individual capable of remedying it: Death.     Chapter 1: After the In-Between

    AGATHA In the end, dying had been as simple and as easy as being born, merely a predetermined process that was fated to take place whether she wanted it to or not.

    And much like being born, she couldn’t remember dying either. Both moments were just that, they were moments. Soon forgotten, like the residue a dream leaves in your mind before daylight and all its distractions lap them up and hide them away.

    Nor, could she recall what had taken place in the in-between, not to start with at least. Though, if she really tried to stretch her mind back to that moment in the garden when it had all gone dark, she could recall rushing heat, weightlessness, and then the smell of moss, peaty and damp.

    According to Billy, she had died that day. In his optimistic opinion she had done so to save him, but she knew better.

    It had been an impulse, that was all.

    An impulse to save another boy, one long dead.

    An impulse to be a single shade kinder than she was known to be.

    An impulse to kiss her wife, to claim her mouth, and possess it in a way she hadn’t allowed herself to for so, so long.

    An impulse to test a long-awaited theory.

    And as usual, her being a creature of whim and compulsion, all of those impulses had merged together into a single determining thought, one that had quickly transformed into a single irrational act.

    And then she had been dead, just like her boy.

    So, all in all, the whole process of dying, the before, the during, and the after, all blended together like rain on an oil painting, like big fat drops of water that cause the colours to smear and mesh together in a manner uncontrived from an artist’s hand, creating instead, an image designed at fate’s discretion.

    Although, sparks of the after did bleed through occasionally. Like flashes of a cottage covered in ivy and glimpses of a blackened heart surrounded by thorns.

    On the whole though, the only thing she can recall with any level of clarity is the silence. She had no way of knowing either whilst dead, how people had reacted to it. Whether covens around the country had celebrated the long-awaited demise of infamous witch-killer Agatha Harkness.

    Whether Billy had grieved for the witch who had saved him.

    Whether Rio now possessed an Agatha-shaped gouge in whatever her soul was crafted from, one that sat just as ragged and bloody as the far smaller one scored into it three hundred years before.

    She did know some things, however. She knew, for instance, that just like with any other death, at any other place, at any other time the world would continue to turn. That life, as it tends to do, moved on. She knew that the grass that grew above her grave transitioned through the seasons with ease, turning from a bright, verdant green with frost-nipped tips to the short, yellow stubble of a New Jersey drought.

    She was sure too, that that was something she could feel at the time, even without a pulse; the changing temperature of the soil; the earth rotating with her encased inside of it; every blade of grass that tickled her nose; each root that curled around her ribs.

    Or perhaps that was only towards the end of her sabbatical beneath the soil, as the unfamiliar magic that now existed within her started to build and pulse, breathing life into what it shouldn’t.

    Either way, the story that we all thought ended with ivory bones collapsed in a pile of purple satin; salty tears from Death’s own eyes falling to the earth, tears that fell with neither a face nor a shroud to prevent their silent descent towards the soil, soil that yearned so desperately to ease it’s mistress’s pain; and a copse of blossoms the colour of amethyst did, in fact, not conclude on that fateful evening.

    Instead the tale simply paused, rallying itself for the next chapter which began one year later to the exact date. On an evening when the moon was full in the sky, as close and bright as a lantern hanging from a tree, and a fine mist had swept over the quiet gardens of Westview, coating the neat, mown lawns in dew, making them catch the light and sparkle under the full moon’s yellow glow.

    There is symmetry to be seen too, in the inception and closing of these parallel chapters. because this section, much like the first, begins with crawling.

    Though this time, she wasn’t crawling her way through a scarlet hex, or a hallucination of her own creation.

    No, this chapter begins with Agatha Harkness crawling through dirt.

    Up through layers of thick New Jersey clay that required clawed fingers; through sections of rubble and stone, passing all manner of remnants: bones, bottles, bricks. Choking her way through crumbly soil, damp and soft, and finally turf, which, with a muffled gasp she pierced and then made bigger, scooping great chunks of mud out the way to reopen her unmarked grave, as though the earth was birthing her anew.

    Which is ironic indeed, because she could certainly remember this.

    The moment her arms breached the surface, she hauled herself upwards like a woman drowning, flailing desperately. Her eyes remained clenched shut to protect them from falling soil, her mouth still filled with the dirt she could feel in her throat and lungs. Halfway out, with only her legs still encased in the earth below, she pressed her stomach flat to the ground, almost folded in two as her lungs finally begin to flex in and out, like a steam engine receiving a fresh shovel of coal, huffing at first and then falling, with a haggard wheeze, into a steadier rhythm.

    Her vocal chords, rusty with disuse, stretched their aching wings as a strained cry forced it’s way up her throat, through clenched teeth, and between her lips when at long last, with a final squelching tug, her legs popped free and she lay panting on the damp grass, chest heaving, eyes closed tight.

    Retching, she choked on the clods of earth blocking her throat, spitting them out to rest beside her grave, as her hands, now shaking with the first bolt of adrenaline-fuelled blood they had received in twelve months, scrabbled at her face to pull the soil from her nose, from her ears, from her hair, and her eyes.

    She tried to open them, but the moonlight, though merely a dark reflection of the sun’s true power, felt like it possessed a fire all its own, especially whilst being compared to the pitch dark hole she had crawled from. Slowly though, bit by bit, flinching all the while, she was eventually able to squint and look around, discovering that the garden she had died in was still exactly the same.

    Exhaling a deep ragged breath, she raised both hands, still shaking, and drifted them down her face, her throat, her stomach, ensuring her skin was still there, that she wasn’t, god forbid, a rotting corpse.

    “Thank fuck for that.” Were the first words she murmured when her palms met smooth, soft skin. Her voice husky from it’s year of silence and the dirt lodged against her vocal chords. Bracing herself, she shifted onto her hands and knees, managing to crawl forward the few paces to reach the porch steps, where she could then cling and haul herself upwards to stand on feet that felt far steadier than she had presumed they would.

    Again, she ran her hands down her body, desperate for reassurance that she was indeed a person, a living, breathing person with arms, legs, and a head. Her hands paused on her chest, where her heart beat, an exceedingly quick thrum beneath her breast, but still, it was a pulse; she would take it.

    Turning, she looked around for the cause of her resurrection, expecting to find a witch with their hands raised and a circle of salt spilt on the grass, chanting whatever macabre necromantic spell they had found to revive her.

    But the only company she found was her own shadow. That, and the scuffling sound of a squirrel scampering up a tree and the lonely groan of branches, barren of leaves, swaying against one another.

    She tried to remember what she could of the before, of the in-between, or even what had happened just before awareness had bled back into her corpse. But her memory was as dark as her grave and she could think of nothing besides the gnawing hunger she had awoken with, her stomach as hollow and as empty as a dried, desiccated shell.

    Feigning courage, she tilted her chin towards the air and pursed her lips, determined, even with only herself for company, to appear entirely unphased at being brought back from the dead. Nor, react to the abject humiliation of having died in the first place. Though her chin soon dipped back down upon noting she was nude. Yet, another similarity to birth, entering the world without a stitch or scrap to her name.

    Nudity was the last of her issues though, as the ache in her stomach started to tighten, the empty hollow at her centre cramping fitfully at still being withheld any sort of nourishment. Her desire to eat compelled her to walk. Which she did, stumbling on knees no longer used to bearing the weight of a body towards the treeline, where her figure was quickly swallowed by shadow and lost to the darkness of the forest.
