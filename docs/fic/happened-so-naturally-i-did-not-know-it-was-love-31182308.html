Overthinking.

Overanalyzing.

That’s something Lena does a lot, something Sam has relentlessly warned her not to do, something that has led to spirals and consuming copious amounts of alcohol just to suspend all higher brain functioning. Perhaps she’ll find an answer at the bottom of this merlot.

Because overthinking can be dangerous, especially in Lena’s case, especially now she knows how harmful assumption making can be and the dark places that take root in her mind.

During her year of self-imposed isolation following Kara’s betrayal, Lena has assumed a lot, has overthought plenty. Most of it had been false, fabricated from a place of heartbreak and all the things she kept buried deep in those tiny, itty bitty boxes.

But the boxes are not sufficient, they never are when it comes to Kara.

Because what Lena feels for Kara is far too abundant, far too infinite, far too expansive and ever-growing. And for the past five years, remains undefined.

Trying to contain them in those ill-advised boxes is a foolhardy endeavour but it keeps Lena sane. Or so she claims.

They make it a little easier to remember the simple act of inhalation, something newborns do without a fuss, something Lena abruptly forgets to do whenever Kara shuffles closer.

It’s not that Lena minds the proximity. Or the physical connection. There was an adjustment period, an unnatural rigidity to her spinal column the first time Kara pulled her in for a brain-melting hug with little ceremony.

Lena remembers how shyly Kara had stepped back, gaze nervous and unsure, panicked that perhaps she had taken a liberty that was never given to her. Her lips were pursed, ready to deliver a long, rambling string of an apology but Lena just moved closer and restarted the hug without another word.

She remembers how Kara practically vibrated with excitement, humming contentedly as she wrapped her arms tighter around an equally delighted Lena.

That was then though. Those warm and loving embraces Kara would engulf Lena in are now replaced with something much more fervent. Hungry. Possessive.

It starts on a Thursday at Lena’s office. She’s riding off the high of staring down a boardroom full of middle-aged personifications of white male privilege. She even made a few of them squirm and loosen their Windsor knots in an attempt to ease the discomfort.

Things are just starting to return to some semblance of normalcy—or what could generously be considered as normal when you’re a Luthor— now that Lex and Leviathan are out of the picture and LuthorCorp has transitioned successfully back to L-Corp.

Lex’s shady dealings, sketchy associates and general villainy has left Lena with quite the mess, much like it had the first time around. Only now, it doesn’t quite feel as though she’s cleaning up after Lex but rather getting a second chance to do the sort of good in the world that has always been her intention.

The world’s reception to the Luthor name is as sceptical as it ever was, worse in some ways, but having National City’s resident Super fully by her side does lend Lena more credibility than she previously had.

She didn’t even need to ask. Kara eagerly volunteered, making herself present at every project launch and press conference, offering praise of the highest honour for the world saving genius, a title that trended on the internet for several weeks.

Still, Lena’s tentative as she sends off a text, inviting Kara for a last-minute lunch. She chews at her thumbnail, an anxious tick, as she waits for the tortuous seconds to crawl by.

Kara’s quick and enthusiastic reply is all it takes to undo the knot of irrational nerves that had wound itself up in Lena’s stomach, a result of her hard-to-kick habit of overthinking.

It’s been in complete overdrive since she mended things with Kara and the vision of their relationship seems to be under a much different filter. Whether that’s good or bad, Lena’s still deciding.

By the time Kara arrives, donning a white button down littered with little ship anchors tucked into navy slacks and brown Oxfords, Lena barely has the words Kara, hey out before she’s wrapped up into a tight hug.

As a warmth runs up and down the length of her back, one of the first things Lena notices is how strong Kara’s hands are, the raw and untapped power that lingers at her fingertips. The second thing she notices is the feverish heat Kara’s body constantly emits as she presses even closer, as if she wants to wear Lena like a second skin.

Lena’s never noticed this before but then she realizes it’s because Kara’s never touched her like this, never pressed against her like this, never held her with such urgency and freedom.

Lena can’t help melting like warm butter in the embrace as she feels Kara nosing into her silky mane and inhaling deeply like it’s a revelation. They stand there in the middle of Lena’s office, the minutes stretching by, wrapped up in each other and nothing else. Nothing else matters outside of their cocoon of happiness.

She hadn’t realized it, just how much she missed this, how much her body craved this like a drug, why on Earth she denied herself this for so long in the name of petty revenge and hubris. Was it worth it? A voice in the back of her mind screams out a resounding no, not if it meant not having this. Not having Kara.

“Hi Lena,” Kara says softly as she pulls back but she keeps her hands firmly planted on Lena’s forearms, as if she can’t stand the idea of not touching her anymore.

Lena releases an unsteady breath when she finally remembers how great and essential oxygen is and she internally revels in the feel of Kara’s calloused yet soft hands on her skin, how the warmth clashes with the coolness of her bare arms from hours in the air conditioned room.

She locks gazes with Kara, loses herself in the ocean-like depth of those blue eyes. She’s always been apprehensive about large bodies of water, a perpetual trigger for the misplaced guilt she feels about her mother’s drowning. But for Kara, Lena knows she will brave it, will brave the waves and choppy currents, the crisp smell of salt in the air. The exception will be there for Kara. It always is.

A soft knock at the door jerks them back to reality. Kara gives her a wary look, already tensed and ready to fling her invulnerable body between Lena and whatever assassination attempt about to walk through that door. Except assassins don’t do their targets the courtesy of knocking.

“Come in,” Lena croaks out, throat working just enough to utter something out. Jess enters with a large bag from Big Belly Burger, an equally large milkshake and a container of kale salad because Lena simply cannot abide or justify eating that much grease with no leafy greens to balance it out.

Kara juts her lower lip at the one green substance she abhors more than kryptonite. Lena can’t fight the grin etching it’s way across her face because of it. Kara is so fucking cute, her lips so full and soft and kissable….

A record scratch rips loudly in her mind. What? Stop it, Lena.

“Thank you, Jess,” she says once all the food is placed on the coffee table. Jess nods and smiles, eyes darting from her boss to the arms still wrapped around her before gracefully exiting with an expression Lena can’t decipher.

Or perhaps she can, if the heat creeping up her neck and spreading across her chest is any indication. Little boxes, like a hypocrite, she still has one that’s overflowing.

So she breaks away from Kara rather suddenly, catches the way Kara’s pout quivers slightly at the loss of contact and feels her heart tumble over itself in behind her ribs. Then she smiles, cocking a head towards their food, freezing for a moment when Kara’s hand quickly finds the small of her back when she turns to lead the way.

They settle down on the pristine white couch that tortured Lena for the better part of a year, ever on the periphery as a shameless reminder of all the precious moments she’s shared there with the one person she’s supposed to hate more than anything.

Lena tucks her legs underneath her, heels placed at the side of the couch, giving her feet a much needed reprieve. Kara shifts a bit closer, leaving a modest gap between them that could be easily overcome if either one twitches a single thigh muscle.

After Kara’s inhaled three burgers and is currently unwrapping her fourth, she says, rather sadly, “I missed you this week.”

Lena halts, fork suspended in the air en route to her mouth. Her brow furrows at the statement as she recalls, “I was at CatCo on Monday.”

Kara nods. “Yea, for like five minutes.” She ducks her head, giving Lena an open window to snatch two of her fries. “I know. I know that you’re really busy with work and all the projects you have lined up. Real world changing stuff and I’m super happy and excited for you. It just feels…longer. I’m sorry if I’m coming off as clingy or something. I just—”

She sinks her teeth into her plump bottom lip and Lena’s breath hitches in response, mind clouded with thoughts and feelings she simply cannot address.

Eyes like azure skies look towards Lena and light up timidly, a blush tinting her cheeks. “I like it better when I get to spend time with you. That’s all.”

Kara seems so uncertain, so small in this moment, as if she’s expecting Lena to say something to the contrary, as if she doesn’t anticipate that Lena feels the same, feels so utterly miserable and incomplete when Kara’s not with her.

“I like it better too,” Lena says, offering a smile she knows will reaffirm her point more than any of her words ever could because they both know Lena only smiles like this for Kara.

Kara beams and it’s one of the most beautiful things Lena has ever seen. “Yea?”

Lena scoots closer, nudging Kara playfully. “Yes. I mean, if this is supposed to be clingy then by all means, be clingy.”

“Oh? You might come to regret that,” Kara grins, eyes darkened and greedy as she captures Lena in a powerful gaze.

Lena levels a heavy gaze of her own, never one to be outmatched. “Doubtful. I don’t regret much when it comes to you, darling.” Kara’s grin stretches impossibly wider, cheeks burning a fierce red at the pet name Lena hasn’t dared to drop in months. It does feel nice to say it again.

They turn to light chatter, mostly Kara venting about some millennial click-bait tripe Andrea has her writing in favour of the hard-hitting journalism she actually wants to be doing.

Lena feels a little guilty, knowing that the way she weaponized her sale of CatCo to hurt Kara was causing her friend problems. If Lena was still in charge, Kara would have the freedom to write whatever she wanted, the important stories she loved bringing to life with her talented prose. Now, it’s all just a chore, not a passion.

Then Kara thankfully moves the conversation to something else, seemingly aware of the inner turmoil Lena’s facing and the needless guilt she’s feeling. It’s almost like another superpower, the way Kara seems to be so attuned to Lena while at the same time being blissfully ignorant of how Lena’s heart always beats faster when she’s around. And more importantly, the reason why that is.

(The reason that is still battened down for dear life in the last of Lena’s boxes. She’s not quite ready to sort through it even though every smile that break across Kara’s face pries it open further. The top is bound to fly off soon.)

Or maybe Kara does know, maybe she’s fully aware and she’s ignoring it because she doesn’t feel the same and doesn’t want to hurt Lena’s feelings, doesn’t want their friendship to go through the strain of rebuilding once again.

No. She can practically hear Sam’s voice in her head telling her not to overthink so for once, she listens to it and tries to focus on the TikTok video Kara’s showing her and lets her brain settle on trying to discern what the fuck Nia is doing in it.

 

Lena’s determination not to overthink is tenuous at best. A damn joke. She’s always had a propensity for it and who can really blame her when Kara’s hands somehow find themselves on some body part of hers at every given moment.

Whether that’s at the Tower while J’onn is debriefing Supergirl and Sentinel and suddenly the blonde has forgotten the English language until her arms enclose Lena while the brunette sits studiously at her workstation. Just minding her own fucking business, typing out a coding language she mastered at age seven and now something as rudimentary as CTRL + V is beyond Lena’s brain capacity.

As Kara presses into Lena’s back, she’s chatty and animated and Lena’s desperately trying to stifle the moan fighting its way up her throat when she eyes the bugle of muscle straining against Kara’s super suit. Lena’s ninety-nine percent—the closest to one hundred you’re allowed to be in science—sure Kara has a glint of self-satisfaction in her eyes when she takes in Lena’s response and she makes no attempt to give her a break.

If anything, she flexes more, leans in closer, pretending to look at the coding on the screen, as if she comprehends a bloody thing. Lena knows she does not, it’s just an excuse. A flimsy one at that.

“It looks good,” Kara whispers hotly and something tells Lena she’s not referring to the program.

Alex narrows her eyes at the pair, eyebrows pulled in a what the fuck expression that makes blood rush to Lena’s cheeks. Kara remains stationed behind her, carding a hand through Lena’s let-down hair, scratching lightly at her scalp.

Without thinking, Lena leans back and the keyboard in front of her is long forgotten.

 

Another time is on Sunday.

Kara had shown up after her morning patrol for brunch that Lena prepares from scratch every week. Today’s meal involves an obscene amount of frittatas, freshly cut fruits and thick slices of ham.

The blonde had wandered into Lena’s bathroom—the one attached to her room as if there isn’t one for guests—for a quick shower and emerged in grey sweats and Lena’s MIT jersey. A jersey Lena’s certain she had stuffed away in one of her drawers but she doesn’t comment, rather pleased by the sight.

It’s a bit snug on Kara’s arms, the blonde having much more muscle mass, resulting in the devastation of Lena perceiving her well-defined biceps and chiseled abs printing out against the fabric every time she fucking inhales.

Lena’s grip on the coffee mug loosens but it never reaches the linoleum flooring.

“Watch it there,” comes a voice from behind her. Kara’s hand had darted out and caught the cup, coffee and all, quite easily. No surprise or shocker there. What Lena focuses on is the fact that Kara could have and should have caught the cup from in front of her, given her position earlier.

But no, this damn Kryptonian took a fucking detour just to press herself behind Lena, to glide her free hand to Lena’s waist and press her lips against Lena’s temple to whisper sweet nothings.

What the actual fuck is Lena supposed to think about that?

Nothing, she decides. She takes the cup and places it on the counter.

“Thanks,” it comes out strangled and dry but Kara just hums and doesn’t move, opting to wrap both arms around Lena’s waist and pull her closer, nearly lifting her clean off the floor. “What are you doing?”

“Hmm?” Kara says so guileless.

“I said ‘what are you doing’?”

“Nothing,” Kara sighs into the junction between Lena’s neck and shoulder and Lord Jesus, Lena tries not to think about how that makes her knees feel like gelatine. “I’m just happy. Here. With you.”

Lena spins around and smiles because dammit, so is she.

 

Kara’s hand never leaves Lena’s from the moment the pair departs Lena’s building. There’s something comforting in it, having someone boldly step out into the world with her and not give a second thought to it. To be fully accepted for who and what you are.

“Can we get some ice cream?” Kara asks quietly. “There’s a parlour down the street.” Lena just nods, not trusting herself to speak. Kara leads the way, swinging their joined hands, squealing at the golden retriever that passes by.

Lena can definitely see the resemblance.

Kara opens the door and motions for Lena to enter. She greets the owner like a long-time friend and introduces Lena.

The owner smiles, genuinely and then he recalls how L-Corp devised a respirator that helped him and others acclimate to Earth’s differing atmosphere. How it saved his life. Lena blushes, Kara beams proudly and rambles on about how smart and wonderful she is. The owner nods in wholehearted agreement and offers the pair any flavours of their choice.

As they exit, Kara innocently requests a lick of Lena’s vanilla cone, the most conservative of ice cream choices, nothing that warrants wanting a taste. But Lena, bless her rattling heart, nods dumbly but before she can stretch out her hand, Kara closes the distance, curls nimble fingers gently around Lena’s wrist and brings the cone up to her mouth.

The irrational part of her brain seems to think there’s something deliberate and provocative in the movement and the unwavering gaze, despite how casually Kara moves onto her own cone that she eats with complete and utter decency.

She’d lasso the moon and stars and yank them out of the sky if Kara asked her to. She’d do anything for Kara.

Because Kara has to reply, “Thank you,” with absolutely no correction and Lena just gawks, cheeks red as tomatoes.

Okay, so Lena can allow the couple statement to slide because really perhaps people in romantic relationships have co-opted the word. It can just be referring to two individuals and nothing more.

Yes. Totally. For sure.

“Five years.”

That fucking box, it won’t stay closed this time and Lena almost wishes the ground would open up and swallow her whole. She can’t deal with this now. She jerks away from Kara and rushes out.

The blonde hurries behind her. “Lena!”

She doesn’t stop. Just keeps walking with no real direction in mind.

“Lena! Lena! Lena wait!”

And has she? Is what they’re doing wrong? Lena still hasn’t decided.

Kara’s always been a tad oblivious but never dense. Lena’s always been uncertain about some things but never dumb, never been the type of person to miss the details. That’s why she agreed to throw herself into the offer of friendship with cub reporter Kara Danvers, after weeks of dodging and sleuthing.
