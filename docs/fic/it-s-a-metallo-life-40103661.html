1. Doppelganger

“I will do everything in my power to stop you,” Kara said grimly, as she backed away to leave. “Just like any other villain.” With another rustle of her cape, she was gone.

Well fuck you too, Lena thought bitterly, glaring at the spot where the kryptonian had just been standing. Lena repressed the urge to throw something, to shatter her tea mug on the window that Kara had just been behind. To scream, to shout, to somehow deal with the rage that had not stopped for months. Fixing the world doesn’t make me a villain. You just think I’m a Luthor.  

Lena moved to put away the tea she had been preparing. This wasn’t going to be a night for relaxing after all. This was going to be a night for insomnia and scotch and anger.

Crossing her kitchen, her mind fixated back on Non Nocere. The animal trials had been producing perfect results, lighting a certain excitement in her scientific bones that perhaps this experiment really would work. It had felt like a longshot initially, but her dreams could become a reality. A reality that Supergirl wasn't going to fucking stop.

That said, she must still be cautious. Human brains were far more complicated than those of mice. But… if she succeeded? If humanity always acted out of good will, never caving to worry? What goodness, and creativity, and joy she would unlock.

Lena was so deep in thought that she missed the initial click of her condo’s lock, the familiar hum of her home’s shielding dropping, the slight creak of the front door opening. Lena jumped up, taking a few small steps to the other side of her kitchen island as she turned back, partially shielded by the granite.

“Who are you?” Lena shouted, more panicked than she’d ever admit. Her condo’s protections were extensive and vast. No one should’ve been able to enter, without warning, besides her. The energy field - strong enough to keep members of Leviathan at bay - should only be dropped with her DNA.

“Interesting,” said the figure, in a voice all too familiar, stepping from the shadowed doorway into the kitchen light. Chills ran down Lena’s spine, her body signaling danger, while her mind sputtered at the impossible. A familiar green glow accompanied the figure as she stepped closer, and Lena suddenly felt adrift, as though she were staring into a demented funhouse mirror. 

Her doppelganger spoke again. “I believe I’m you.”

Lena’s eyes dropped to the green stone in her doppelganger’s chest. “You’re not me.”

“Not in this timeline, clearly.” The doppelganger rounded the corner of the kitchen, curiously, her eyes trained on Lena as though she were prey.

Lena eyed the doppelganger warily. A few months ago, she would’ve written off the attacker as a fraud, a shapeshifter of some sort, a villain. But since then, she had learned that the multiverse was not just a hypothesis, but real - that travel was possible between the universes, when they had existed. So she was willing to entertain, if only for a moment, that this stranger might have been her from another world.

Lena knew her security system was good. She had built it herself. Occam’s razor - given what she already knew, the simplest solution to this question is that the doppelganger did, indeed, have her own DNA. 

It was an intriguing thought, as much as it was a terrifying one. Someone who knew her pain, her betrayal - who by definition could only want the best for herself. It wasn’t too odd to think that they could become allies.

Lena cleared her throat. “Scotch?” The question was simple. The doppelganger nodded.

Lena wasn’t naive. Only time would tell if trust could be built, if it was worth it - if she couldn’t have it with herself, after all, then there was truly no one.

Not that she’d ever admit it, the loneliness that ate at her… but perhaps, for once, she found the one person who wouldn’t betray her.

“This is uncomfortable,” Alex whined, from her place on Kara’s couch.

Kara laughed, before starting on her 8th Big Belly burger of the night. “Who knew fifth-dimensional imps had such good taste in food?”

Mxy’s parting gift - before heading off to some 18th century Mardi Gras party - had initially been well-received by Alex and J’onn. But while Kara’s ecstaticness was only slightly tempered, J’onn had gotten full pretty quickly and left, while Alex crashed on Kara’s couch in a state typically reserved for post-Thanksgiving-dinner.

Alex was no longer in a good mood. “Well, he owes you for nearly getting you killed-”

“It happens,” Kara says cheerfully, eyeing a donut. “And hey, you got free paintballing out of it.”

Alex grinned widely. “Yeah, that was fun. I kicked J’onn’s ass.”

“I figured. He was awfully quiet about it,” said a contented Kara, as she finally sat back on the couch, one last root beer in hand. She glanced at Alex with a smile before settling in, her expression dropping a little as her eyes became unfocused ahead. “You guys sound like you had a really fun day,” she said, her voice softening.

Alex sat up slowly, a bit of concern drawn on her face, as she noted the quietly forlorn expression on Kara’s. “I’m sorry it didn’t work, Kara.”

Kara glanced down at the soda bottle in her hand, picking slightly at the label. “She’s alone, Alex. She’s completely alone. She’s never had anyone in her corner. Just people who take advantage of her again and again…” Kara bit her lip, pausing for a moment. “Even me.”

“Kara-” Alex started, her voice firm, “You never took advantage of her. You were scared to trust her. You had me and everyone else telling you not to, for so long. That’s not the same thing as taking advantage of someone. You were just afraid to lose her.”

Kara’s eyes shifted towards Alex again, darting between her eyes. Kara sat silent for a moment. “You know, don’t you?”

Alex sighed, leaning forward to grab Kara’s hand. “No one tries to change history to save a friendship, Kara.”

Kara weakly smiled back, turning back towards her soda. “It’s not like it ever would’ve become anything anyway. I just… I just hope she comes back to us. I just hope she doesn’t get hurt in the meantime.”

Alex swallowed her own opinions. Not just of her suspicions of Lena’s feelings, but as to whether she would ever be okay with Lena coming back into their lives. Alex knew Kara was forgiving enough to move past the incident in the Fortress, but Alex wasn’t sure if she could ever forgive Lena for using kryptonite against her sister. But for now… that wasn’t the conversation Kara needed.

“Hey,” Alex said, giving Kara’s hand a gentle squeeze. “Why don’t we watch the Wizard of Oz?”

“A multiverse?” The doppelganger considered, her cold stare continuing to cause the hairs to raise at the back of Lena’s neck. “Perhaps that does explain how I got here. My own universe was collapsing. I was able to follow the fifth dimensional magic out. I do not believe there are other survivors.”

Lena forgot her wariness for a moment, instead snorting in shock. “Did you say magic?” The doppelganger’s eyes almost twinkled at the incredulity in Lena’s voice.

The doppelganger smirked. “Ever skeptical, as you should be. But magic is real, and I was able to channel it, use it to power my policing force. I ruled National City, able to quash down rebelling terrorists. I was about to kill Supergirl when my timeline collapsed.”

Lena’s heart stuttered, though she was able to keep her face unemotional in response to that sentence. Hell, on Earth Prime, Lex had apparently been some sort of alien-loving ultra-philanthropist. Who knows what Supergirl was really like in some other reality - anything was possible.

Still, Lena wondered, it felt as though she and her doppelganger were playing a game. Cat and mouse, though it was unclear who was really the cat. The more they talked, the more she was convinced that they were both trying to give the impression of tentative trust. But she felt… unsure. Unsure of her own motives, really, and definitely not sure of the doppelganger’s.

But even if they didn’t actually trust each other - perhaps that would someday be on the table, perhaps not - they could still use each other. Two Luthor brains were better than one, and she was fairly confident that this doppelganger was probably more trustworthy than Lex, at least. A real partner in Non Nocere.

Lena gestured towards the doppelganger’s glowing green chest. “A gift from Lillian, I assume?”

The doppelganger’s hand raised, gently tapping the kryptonite, her eyes ever trained on Lena. “Yes. How did you know? It doesn’t appear you got the same treatment.” Lena could detect a hint of bitterness in her voice. It made her want to shrink.

“My mother performed a similar procedure on a man named John Corben. We called him Metallo.”

“Metallo?” The doppelganger’s eyebrow quirked slightly, Lena again feeling unsettled by the familiarity. “Then that’s what I will be called, in this world. I haven’t truly been Lena in a very long time.” Each word was punctuated.

Lena’s disconcertedness aside, she was sitting across from someone with the ability to tap into unmatched power. Power that not only could broadcast Non Nocere to the world, but that might be able to solve the energy and environmental crisis of the entire planet.  

She wasn’t about to pass up on her goals simply because she was… unsettled. “Alright, Metallo,” Lena said unevenly, before taking a breath. “Let me tell you about Non Nocere.”

Metallo smirked.

2. Tensions

Lena stared in awe at the small sphere before her.

It had initially looked unimpressive - a silver golf ball, its surface lined with almost circuit-like traces. A neat design for a toy.

But when Metallo had spun the sphere on the surface of Lena’s lab bench - like a spinning top - the sphere had begun to glow, a shade of gold that Lena learned was signature of fifth dimensional magic. The device had operated as a resonant converter, changing fifth dimensional magic into a normal alternating electric current.

“And this has a mercury core?” Lena asked.

“Yes. The mercury acts as a ferrofluid for the magic.” Metallo hovered protectively over the sphere - she had called it a quantosphere - with rapt attention. Metallo had three on her person as her universe collapsed. Even a single quantosphere could power Non Nocere’s broadcast around the world. But immense power aside, the quantosphere could essentially recharge Metallo herself, keeping the synthetic kryptonite in her chest from exploding as it had for John Corben.

Kara Danvers believes in you. The stray memory is banished from Lena’s mind almost as soon as it arrives. It had been a difficult habit to build at first - particularly that memory, of Kara flying her out of the mountain fortress as an explosion gave rise to a shining green cloud behind them. But Lena had months of practice, shoving those echoes of another time into the box labeled “betrayal”, and moving on.

Lena turned to Metallo, nodding. “If you provide me with the schematics, I can get the equipment we need to replicate this. LuthorCorp doesn’t currently have a nanofabrication lab, but we should be able to construct one within a month.”

Metallo’s hard eyes met Lena’s. “I will provide you with a spec list for the equipment.” The message was clear. Metallo would not trust Lena with the design of the device itself at this point. 

Lena’s jaw tightened, but she nodded again. “Whenever you have the list ready.”

Lena’s phone pinged, much to her annoyance. Andrea had been bothering her all day about some damn celebration at CatCo for Lex’s contribution to launching Obsidian. Lena just didn’t have patience for it. She was sure Lex was starting to feel the distance - why deal with Obsidian, when the quantosphere could help everyone in the world, not just those wearing the stupid VR lenses - but at the same time, she wasn’t prepared to cut off Lex yet. He still owned LuthorCorp, after all.

Lena unpocketed her phone, pulling up the new text from Andrea, and froze.

“Kara’s stepfather died. She’ll be at the Obsidian launch tonight, if you want to talk to her.”

A pang of empathy rang in Lena’s chest. Kara had just lost her father. Or the closest thing she had to one since the age of thirteen, anyway. And Lena knew what that was like.

For all that Kara didn’t seem to care about Lena - not enough to tell the truth - Lena found it difficult to beat back her own empathy this time. Kara wasn’t some unfeeling monster, she was still huma– well, she still had a heart. And Lena knew it was hurting.

She sat, and she stared, until Metallo’s stern voice snapped her out of it. “Is something wrong?”

Lena looked up, shaking her head. “No, I just- I may as well go to the Obsidian launch after all, while you draw up the list.”

Metallo nodded, her eyes - which seemed to always be hard - narrowing in suspicion. But if she was bothered, she didn’t say anything. “It will be complete when you get back.”

Lena nodded, rising from her seat. The event was to start a little over an hour from now, and she had to find a book. 

Lena eyed Kara from across the room, ignoring Andrea’s impassioned speech about Obsidian or investors or whatever the hell she and Lex were talking about. Lena was feeling a bit nauseous with the whole Earth Prime “look at how good Lex Luthor is!” schtick. For Kara, it must be hell.

Kara made a dodge for the elevator, slamming the button multiple times as the crowd applauded Lex. Lena quickly followed, but she was too far back to make it in time to catch Kara’s elevator, so she waited impatiently for the next.

She could feel Lex’s eyes burning a hole in the back of her skull. She ignored him.

Kara startled as Lena appeared in the doorway, shuffling a photo under her hands. Lena realized, for the first time, that Kara never carried the weight of her emotion the way humans had, not while on this planet. The yellow sun made sure that, regardless of how much she cried, her eyes would be almost immediately clear of any red lines, her face remaining unpuffy. It was only the overwhelming tension in her body that indicated her recent tears… if someone knew what to look for.

Lena fidgeted awkwardly, offering her sympathies, offering the book that had helped her through her own mourning and the Luthor family mess. She was grateful that Kara didn’t seem to think her presence was an intrusion.

It opened up a horrible ache in Lena’s chest, as she fought the urge to step closer to Kara, to pull her into a hug. For a moment - just a moment - she wondered if it would be possible to get past all of this.  

Lena wasn’t ready to forgive, and certainly wasn’t ready to give up on Non Nocere. Not yet. But… this was an olive branch. And it had been given, and received. So maybe Lena was ready to hope.

“Thank you for saying that,” said Kara, her mouth quivering as she moved to adjust her glasses. “That means a lot coming from you.”

Lena stared ahead at her human subject, relieved to be far away from Metallo.

By the time Lena had gotten back from the Obsidian event, Metallo had already produced a list of all of the equipment needed to re-manufacture the quantospheres. She had moved on to coding the AI necessary for her robotic military force - a plan that made Lena nauseous. But if Non Nocere worked, the robot army would no longer be necessary.

And with Metallo’s cold eyes, Lena wasn’t sure she wanted to get in the way.

So Lena continued her work on Non Nocere from the live research lab, a few levels up from the basement Metallo worked in. Having completed the initial animal trials, Lena was now looking at the results of her first human subject.

Tapping on her recorder, she spoke clinically. “The brain anatomy of Patient 054 remains normal post-Non Nocere. The trial continues to exhibit positive results of Q-wave adoption.”

“Brava!” came Lex’s voice from behind her, causing Lena to startle. Lena let out an exasperated sigh. While Lex was less unnerving to be in the same room with, compared to Metallo, Lena was really hoping for some time alone - to concentrate, to get lost in the science, to not think about the rest of the world for two damn minutes.

Lena snapped at him, about to turn to tell him to just give her the night in the lab, when an alert popped up on the screen. Her Q-waves were being… suppressed? And the signature looked a lot like–

“Myriad.” Lena took a step back, shaking her head. “She’s using Myriad to interrupt my tests?” Lena’s rage welled in her chest. Myriad, the thing that was too dangerous for anyone to use?

Lex smirked, offering generously to help Lena, by going to the Fortress and turning the device off. Lena couldn’t help but roll her eyes at the obvious play.

“No. If she’s using Myriad… I want to see it for myself.”

3. Over the Edge

Kara felt sick to her stomach.

She had no choice, of course. And she wouldn’t have done it, if Brainy hadn’t said it were safe. Still, holding Myriad, powering it on, programming it to cancel the Q-waves…

Kara had trouble keeping the mental images at bay. Astra’s blood, as she died in Kara’s arms. Kara struggling to figure out how to say goodbye to Alex, as she headed off to fly Fort Rozz into space. Lena, walking away with the device, as Kara’s instinct to breathe fought against the sensation of knives piercing her lungs…

Truth be told, the Fortress was the last place she wanted to be right now.

Kara worked as quickly as possible. Activate Myriad, suppress the Q-waves, find the missing Obsidian victims, get out of the Fortress. And she had almost, blissfully, forgotten she was in the Fortress at all - until a familiar heartbeat snapped her back to reality.

Her heart sank. She knew how it looked.

“I thought it was forbidden to use Myriad, but forbidden for anyone but you.” Lena hardly registered what Kara was saying, what she was saying in response - all she could feel was the fury of Kara doing it again. The deception, the arrogance, the complete disregard for her own self-appointed rules. She has the fucking gall to try the sad puppy dog eyes, with every fucking excuse.

“You’re trespassing, and I don’t have time for this. You need to go.” Kara repressed a shiver up her spine, as she turned back to the console. No one had set up a Luthor protocol on Earth Prime. If Lena wanted to attack her with kryptonite again, she could.

“To think that I was sincere with you yesterday,” Lena growled coldly. I loved you and you lied to me. I loved you and I meant nothing to you. “Then I guess you're used to being two-faced.”

A portal buzzed to life, and Lena walked out.

Metallo eyed Lena curiously, as Lena set about her early morning routine of making two espressos. A quantosphere glowed on the counter, spinning quickly as it drew more magic into the room, invisibly absorbed by Metallo’s kryptonite.

It had only been a couple weeks, but Metallo had… perhaps not softened, but certainly become less guarded with the quantospheres. Lena had learned that Metallo had managed to bring back three from her universe. She wasn’t willing to give any to Lena, nor share the full schematic of the device, but she was no longer suspicious that Lena might try to steal one.

Another week, and LuthorCorp would have the equipment needed to manufacture more.

Lena slammed a quart of almond milk on the table. Apparently she was splurging on a latte today, Metallo observed. Lena had been quiet the past few days. Angry. It was a face Metallo knew all too well.

“Who betrayed you this time?”

Lena’s eyes darted up, as she huffed, then glanced back down to the mug she was about to steam. “No one. It’s nothing. Do you want a latte or just espresso?”

“Espresso,” Metallo answered simply. But she wasn’t going to let Lena off that easily. “I’m surprised you trust anyone anymore, enough to do this,” she said, with a small gesture towards Lena’s tense frame.

“I don’t trust her at all.”

“So there is someone.”

Lena bit her lip, pausing to grip the granite of her kitchen counter with her hands. She refused to look Metallo in the eye. “There’s not.”

But Metallo only laughed, not quite suppressing the glee she felt. Finally. This world’s Lena needed to be far more cynical than she was. “Let me guess - a lover?”

Lena glanced up, unsettled, unable to keep the vulnerability out of her eyes. Metallo tutted. “An unrequited lover.”

Lena grabs two mugs, dropping them forcefully enough on the counter that both women are surprised the mugs don’t break. “Her name is Kara. Yes, I’m in love with her. Yes, she broke my heart. She still doesn’t even know, doesn’t trust me with her secrets, like I’m nothing but a-” Lena stops, glaring to the side, unable to look at Metallo directly.

She doesn’t know why she’s saying this, other than knowing that Metallo will understand. After all, this Metallo knows… she’s dealt with Lillian, with Lex, with Andrea - with the same isolated childhood and cursed family name. Who else could know her better?

But when Lena’s eyes turn back ahead to Metallo, across the kitchen island, she’s startled to find fury, and contempt. Metallo’s eyes are wide, a sneer across her face.

 

Lena? It’s me. It’s Supergirl- It’s Kara.

Who’s Kara?

Just a friend. You don’t know me. Not really.

 

“Her,” Metallo hissed bitingly. “You fell for her? The terrorist?”

Lena shook her head. “No, she’s not like that here-”

“She’s the one that came to me,” Metallo said, as the kryptonite in her chest began to glow threateningly. “She spoke of doing no harm. She found her way into my world, and you are-”

Metallo cut herself off, the fury on her face dimming, as a sick, unhinged grin took over. “It will be a pleasure to kill one of the terrorist’s friends.”

Lena’s eyes widened as the kryptonite glow accelerated, finally beaming in her direction. She instinctively dodged, launching herself towards the quantosphere, swiping at it as she ran towards one of the couches.

But the quantosphere wasn’t constantly needed to power Metallo - she already had more than enough power for now. Lena cursed, pocketing the device. She had no defense for this. 

For a moment, she thought to turn, to beg. To reason with herself, that she was all she had left. That surely, with the same mind, if Lena was once close with Kara, Metallo would have to understand there was a good reason … 

But Lena chased the thought off, refocusing on her survival. It’s nothing she hadn’t thought about each night for the past few weeks. A version of her that was blown out of the sky, experimented on and in perpetual agony, pitted against a Supergirl who never met her - and therefore never knowing a Kara who could believe in her…

Lena knew she wasn’t going to survive by convincing Metallo to use reason. The only way to survive was to get help.

Lena’s eyes darted to the top of the fireplace, where her signal watch lay forgotten, seemingly eons ago. It was a long shot - who knows if Kara would come now, with all that happened between them. But it was Lena’s only option.

Dodging a second green blaze, Lena flung herself over her couch, running to the mantle and swiping the device. Metallo missed yet a third time, seemingly slow in her maneuverability. Or perhaps just playing with her prey.

Cat and mouse.

Lena hurriedly popped open the watch cover and clicked the button, turning her head over her shoulder to Metallo, as the kryptonite began to glow again.

And that’s when the rush of adrenaline gave way to sinking horror.

Metallo was using kryptonite. Kryptonite. Lena cursed herself, an idiot, a fool. In trying to save her own damn life, she had accidentally beckoned Kara into the perfect trap. Lena was damned either way - the only question was, fuck, Kara might follow her into the grave.

Don’t come, don’t come, don’t come, she prayed silently, clicking the watch again in warning. Kara wouldn't come, knowing it was a trap, right? Maybe after their argument in the Fortress, she wouldn't bother coming at all.

Lena fought back the tears beginning to form in her eyes, panic combining with self-loathing. If Kara came, she would die. And it would be Lena's fault. This can't be happening, her mind raced hysterically, scouring for possibilities - something, anything, that she could use to gain the upper hand.

Her eyes landed on her balcony door. Lena’s jaw tightened, her already-racing heart suddenly feeling as though it might burst out of her chest. There’s one play I have left.

As another green flash destroyed her fireplace, Lena thrust herself across the room, half stumbling and half ducking under the threat of further attack. She rounded the corner of the glass door on her balcony, frantically shoving the door open as she ran through, with no sign of slowing down as yet another flash of light brushed her side. She reached the ledge, bracing her arms on the railing, sloppily kicking her legs up, tumbling over the edge.

And then, she was falling.

 

4. Queen for a Day

Lena, what are you doing?!

Kara rounded the corner of another skyscraper, seconds away from Lena’s condo. But as she heard Lena’s heart racing, she could only feel like her own had stopped. Because Lena’s heartbeat was falling, falling, falling.

The sun had barely risen when the high-frequency buzz of the signal watch had yanked Kara out of her sleep. It took only fractions of a second for her to lock in the location as being Lena’s condo, leading to the tanktop-and-sweats-clad kryptonian immediately throwing herself out her window door.

But in the immediate following seconds as she flew halfway across the city, she found herself only more disoriented. She tried to lock on to Lena’s heartbeat - but there were two, identical in signature. One was pounding - the other unusually slow, and somehow fuzzy.

Even more disconcerting, Lena had set the watch off twice. Kara bit her lip as the buzzing tone turned into a pulse, meant to warn her off. But Kara wasn’t going to leave until she found out why Lena’s normal 61.3 beats per minute (not that Kara was counting) was skyrocketing near 170.

Kara shook her head. She couldn’t be sure why there were two heartbeats, but for now, she was going to worry about the one that seemed to be plummeting from Lena’s balcony. She squinted, tracking the figure tumbling on the other side of the building. As Kara rounded the final corner, she notched her trajectory down, noting the small rotation as Lena somersaulted through the air.

With a gentle oomph, Lena was safely in Kara’s arms. Kara breathed out a sigh of relief, as she slowly decelerated.

Lena, on the other hand, did not seem all that relieved. Her terror traded itself for a different type of panic, one that caused her to throw her arms unnecessarily around Kara’s neck, in what would assuredly be a gripping chokehold on any human.

“You’re okay, Lena. You’re okay. Let’s get back to your balcony,” Kara said soothingly.

“No,” Lena choked, as she fruitlessly tried to crawl up Kara’s body, as her instinct to not be flying warred with her common sense. “That’s where she is, she has kryptonite!”

Kara turned her head upwards, squinting towards Lena’s place, locking onto the somehow-identical heartbeat. Her eyes widened quickly to realize that the heartbeat was in an identically shaped Lena. And that her chest glowed with a nauseating shade of green…

No, no, no, this can’t be right…

Kara shook her head, turning back towards where she came. “It’s okay, Lena, I’ve got you. We’re going to my place.”

Lena didn’t say a word for the rest of the short trip to Kara’s apartment. The immediate threat was over, and her adrenaline had crashed enough to cause sheer exhaustion, but not enough to let her heartbeat slow. As talented as she is at thinking under pressure, right now, all she could think about was remembering how to breathe, as Kara nudged her into loosening her iron grip around the kryptonian’s neck.

Lena’s feet were finally under her, and her senses started coming back to her, including - oh, that was pain.

“Lena!” Kara shouted, panicked, catching her as she sank to the ground. “Lena, you’re bleeding.” Lena looked to her side in a daze, as nausea and dizziness began to creep into her being. She felt strong arms lift her again, as Kara stepped towards her bed.

Lena barely registered Kara’s words, until Kara’s attempt to nudge her arms up resulted in sharp pain. “I can’t, Kara - I can’t lift my arm,” she whimpered.

Worry lined Kara’s face, as she reached for Lena’s collar, rending the shirt into two. Lena had a single thought to spare on modesty, before turning her head down to try to examine the injury herself. The tension in Kara’s shoulders dropped the slightest bit. “It’s a clean wound,” she said, examining the fresh burn along Lena's left side. “I guess a kryptonite blast is self-cauterizing for humans.” Her eyes drifted up to Lena’s. “I’m going to cool it down, okay?”

Metallo didn't wait for an answer. With a grin, she reached out, abruptly grabbing and yanking at Lena's arm, using surprise as her advantage though her strength was rapidly failing. Metallo quickly pulled Lena into a chokehold, laughing as an off-balance Lena suddenly found herself struggling for breath.

“Your move, Girl of Steel,” Metallo taunted, winded in the effort of keeping Lena still, as the stone on her chest began to glow anew, gleaming ominously as it began to destabilize.

“Kara,” Lena choked. “Go! Please!”

“She’s precious to you, isn’t she?” Metallo asked Kara mockingly, as Kara’s eyes darted, desperately seeking an option to pull Lena away. One that she wasn’t going to find.

Lena recognized the situation for what it was. Metallo's kryptonite was rapidly becoming unstable, the quantosphere drain leaving only moments left - but a Luthor was never desperate. Metallo would be well satisfied with a pyrrhic victory. Yes, she would be drained, and die. But her death would be well worth it, if she could draw Supergirl into dying with her.

It was the same dark urge that led Lena to the Fortress of Solitude, months ago, holding Myriad. She could have trapped Kara in kryptonite instantly. And for her goal of using Myriad to make humanity better, it would’ve been the wiser choice.

It would’ve been some sort of vindication, some sort of acknowledgement about the fraud that served as the foundation of their entire relationship. But it had never come. Some small, dark corner of Lena’s mind was disappointed that she had been able to trap Kara and walk away.

Lena refused to be a trap this time. “Go! Please, Kara! The world needs Supergirl!”

But Kara was standing again, the warmth of the yellow sun in her body finally winning out over the pain of the kryptonite poisoning. The world needs Lena Luthor, Kara thought, as she launched herself forward, listening to the beginnings of the explosion as the kryptonite finally started to give way. And so do I.

When Lex rewrote the entire history of Earth Prime, he made certain of two things. The first, of course, was the ego stroke - Lex was the most respected and beloved public figure in the world. Second, the Luthors and Supergirl were not close. While they operated as business partners in their endeavor to save the world - his power over the kryptonian being a delightful form of torture, while he worked on his plans to kill her and her cousin - they were not friends. Supergirl had gained a reputation for being distant, impersonal, aloof. It bothered Kara more than she cared to admit, that the world now thought of her as distinctly alien, uninterested in the happenings of humanity.

So when Kara arrived at Massachusetts General Hospital - thankfully only a mile of flying - with nearly every vein glowing an incandescent green, anguish written on her face, and an unconscious and bleeding Lena Luthor in her arms, the media… lost it. Combined with the purported explosion at MIT (initially reported as the nuclear reactor exploding, though the reactor was quickly confirmed to be standing), no one had any idea what, exactly, had happened. 

And Supergirl wasn’t talking.

The kryptonian stood vigil over Lena’s hospital bed, unmoving, except for the time two officers tried to come in to take a statement of the happenings at MIT. After Kara physically lifted the first one and deposited him harmlessly outside the door, the second had simply nodded and left of her own accord.

The head nurse confirmed to the silently crying kryptonian that Lena Luthor was fine. The blood loss, mild concussion, and slight dehydration were easy problems to remedy. Kara had spared Lena the worst of the blast - in a few hours, she would wake up, no permanent damage done.

Kara's eyes never left Lena as she muttered a small “thank you” to the nurse, and she resumed her silent vigil.

7. Waking Up

Lena’s eyes fluttered open, a pounding headache causing her to groan. At least it wasn’t a migraine.

To her right, she saw the blurry shuffling of red and blue and splash of gold waves. “Hey,” said the warm voice, relieved. Lena felt the grasp of a hand on top of her own.

“Water?” Lena choked. Kara reached over, placing the small hospital cup directly to Lena’s lips. Lena took some slow sips, then leaned back, rubbing her eyes with her free hand as her sight slowly came back into focus. “Where am I?”

“Massachusetts General Hospital. You’re okay. They’re just keeping you for observation for a bit.” Kara’s smile was gentle, as her eyes finally met Lena’s.

Lena shook her head. She was at… MIT? The nano lab. Then she was flying. Kara’s face, pained, lined in green? Kryptonite? Metallo. Metallo!

“Sssh,” Kara said, stroking Lena’s forehead. “She’s gone. You don’t have to worry about her anymore.” Despite the comforting words and the soothing voice, Lena could detect the edge of pain and guilt in them.

They sat in silence. Kara’s eyes drifted up to the muted TV in the upper corner of the hospital room, as it reported the news of the events at MIT. Lena, head only half-turned towards Kara, watched the kryptonian. The quiet was... perhaps not comfortable, but it was peaceful, for now. For the first time in ages, Lena wasn’t tense. She took a deep, calming breath. It was over.

Lena watched as Kara's suddenly eyes grew wide, Kara's gaze landing on a warped orange and silver building that flashed across the screen. “Crap, we must’ve done more damage than I thought,” she said remorsefully.

Lena glanced at the screen, her lips tugging into a smile, as she turned her head back to Kara. “No, the Stata Center… always looks like that.”

Kara’s eyebrows crinkled, as she continued to watch the screen. “Huh,” she muttered, somewhat baffled. But then her eyes widened again, and she turned uncomfortably away from the screen as a distinct blush rose on her cheeks. Lena looked back on the screen, wondering why Kara would suddenly be feeling shame.

The TV now had cell phone footage plastered across it, the kryptonian entering the ER, shouting silently as some nurses ran up. It was surreal for Lena to watch herself be passed out, cradled in Kara’s arms until she gently lowered her on a gurney. The two disappeared behind the fast-moving medical workers, before the video started on loop again. And across the bottom ticker…

A SUPER AND A LUTHOR

Friends - or more?

Oh. Lena turned down to her hands, fidgeting as heat rose to her own cheeks. The peaceable calm between the two had shifted into a chasm.

She… should say it. This is the part where she should tell Kara, they have a heart-to-heart. The part where she admits she understands Kara now, that she had it all wrong, that Non Nocere was an overreaction.

Then the other three words, that would remove the vulnerable hesitance from the kryptonian’s face, and no doubt replace it with a wide smile. And where for so long, Lena had kept that carefully hidden away, eager to keep her friend close instead of risking the pain and awkwardness of rejection… but Kara felt that way too, her feelings confessed and in the open, and now Lena was aching to finally say it.

But the words got stuck in Lena’s throat. How could she admit to that, after everything she’s done? After fulfilling every one of Kara’s fears - stealing from her, manipulating her, trapping her in kryptonite.  

Lena would apologize, someday, soon. But love? 

Kara was sunshine and hope. She deserved… far more than Lena could ever offer her. Far more than the darkness of a Luthor, as distrustful and traitorous as she was raised to be. She couldn’t tell Kara. She would never tell Kara. 

Kara would get over her.

“The nurses said they’d discharge you as soon as you’re ready,” Kara said, her shoulders slightly drooped in uncertainty. “I can help you get home? Or I can leave you alone - whatever you’d prefer.”

Lena’s eyes met Kara’s again. She noted Kara’s tentativeness, and wondered if it reflected her own. “I want you to stay.”

Kara landed softly at Hanscom Field, as Lena held on for dear life. “We’re here,” she said gently, making sure Lena’s feet were firmly on the ground before she let go of her legs. Lena breathed a sigh of relief, casting her eyes on her personal jet, the one Metallo had taken to get to Boston.

The paparazzi at Mass Gen Hospital had waited impatiently at all entrances, eager to stop the Luthor or the Super for questions. But luckily, none had access to the helipad atop one of the hospital buildings, where Kara and Lena had taken off from. 

Hanscom Field wasn’t far - a little under 15 miles from the hospital by air. When Lena had attended MIT, her visits back to Luthor mansion had always been through Hanscom Field, which had plenty of infrastructure for the types of families with private jets. A Luthor wasn't going to wander alone through the public at Boston Logan International Airport, after all. So it was the obvious choice for Metallo - presumably still seeking some discretion - to land the jet here that day, and now Lena was simply going to take it back.

Sure, Kara could get them back across the country to National City in mere minutes, rather than 5 hours. But an airplane involved far less terror.

Lena tapped gently at the jet, her biometrics causing the door to unlock, and the airstair to pop down. Maybe I should reconsider biometric locks, she wondered to herself, as she shakily made her way up the stairs. On the other hand, how often does an evil version of me show up?

Lena made her way into the cockpit, as Kara followed up on the stairs, lingering in the doorway. “Are you sure you want me to stay?” she asked, fidgeting as the stairs behind her began to fold back into place.

Lena paused, her hand hovering over a checklist. “I… feel more comfortable with you here.” She turned her head behind to look at Kara, who nodded. “I’ll get us queued for takeoff. Feel free to take a seat?”

Kara nodded again, as Lena finished running through her checklist. A few minutes later, they were up in the air. Lena noted their arrival into class E airspace, before making her way to the cabin.

Kara - no longer in her Supergirl regalia, though not bothering to wear glasses - was seated, her knee bouncing nervously as she stared out the window. She didn’t look at Lena as Lena took her seat across.

“I almost told you, once. On the way back from Kaznia.”

Lena blinked, her memories seeping through. It had been about a year ago, hadn’t it? When Kara “very normal human reporter” Danvers had sat across from her, just like this, in this very same jet. 

“Really, I should’ve told you with Reign,” Kara said, as she continued staring out the window. “That’s when I knew. That’s when I knew my secret was only putting you in more danger, not keeping you safe.” Kara glanced down at her hands, fidgeting. “I’m sorry, Lena.”

Lena leaned over, her hand covering Kara’s. “Kara, I trapped you in kryptonite. I became exactly what you feared,” Lena said, pleading. Kara tilted her chin up, blue finally meeting green, as Lena continued. “I’m sorry. I’m sorry I didn’t believe you. I’m sorry about everything.”

And I love you too…

Kara stood, tugging Lena’s hand, causing her to rise as well. The two friends embraced, silent as the tears began to flow.

Hours later, Lena stood numbly in her home.

The place was a mess. The lights had been blasted out, casting the living room into the same darkness as the night sky. But even in the shadows, she could see scorch marks lining the kitchen, the fireplace, the ceiling. A couch that Lena vaguely remembered jumping over had apparently toppled. The glass running along her balcony had been blasted open. It had rained lightly in National City, leaving the condo smelling vaguely musty.

Two mugs sat peacefully on the kitchen island. It was too much.

“I… should call Jess,” Lena said, almost dazed. Kara watched her, concern written all over her face. Kara reached up to Lena, giving her shoulder a gentle, supportive squeeze.

Lena smiled weakly, turning to her bedroom to find the landline phone that hadn’t been blasted with kryptonite. When she returned from her call with Jess, she noticed Kara staring out at the evening skyline.

Or, rather, at the balcony floor itself.

Kara stood motionless as Lena approached. 

“Metallo said you jumped,” Kara said, turning towards Lena, their eyes meeting briefly. “Why? Why not go to the stairwell? Or wait for me?”

Lena shifted her gaze to the balcony too, remembering that feeling in her gut as gravity took over, as wind began to scream past her ear. “She had kryptonite, Kara. I wasn’t thinking when I signaled for you. I should never have…”

Kara pulled her in, holding her close, as Lena clenched her eyes shut, realizing she had started to panic. After a few moments, Lena melted into the embrace, her breathing slowed and calm. She never wanted to let go.

I shouldn’t ask, I shouldn’t ask, I shouldn’t ask…

“Will you stay with me tonight?”

Kara took a deep, calm breath as she woke. She wasn’t ready to open her eyes, but she knew she wasn’t home - wherever she was had stunningly little light compared to her bedroom. She felt her shirt pulled tight to the front of her, a fact that she’d investigate once she was a little more alert. But for now, she was content to breathe in the scent of flowers.

Lena.

Her eyes popped open, finding the brunette sleeping beside her. It wasn’t a totally unusual sight - but, Rao, she had missed this. Missed her. Kara breathed again to quell the knot that formed in her throat. They hadn’t talked about Kara’s confession at MIT - and Kara figured they’d never talk about it again. It was no longer a secret, and it would never need to be addressed. What did seem clear is that Lena was ready to forgive. Goodness knows Kara was, and she finally felt like their friendship was something that could be repaired.

Kara’s eyes drifted down to her own chest, where her shirt was being tugged. It seems Lena, in her sleep, had reached out to grasp the fabric just below Kara’s collar. Kara’s lips formed a small smile, as she reached her hand to cover Lena’s, feeling Lena’s grip relax in turn.

It would take time for them to heal, but… they could be friends again. The thought almost made Kara want to cry - the hugs, the coffee, the rushed lunches. The game nights and movie nights and sleepovers.

It was enough. It would hurt, when Lena dated - when she would eventually fall in love and marry. Rao, it had hurt so bad with James. But Kara would survive. There were no more secrets, and she had her friend back.

Kara’s eyes closed, trying to push the images out of her mind. Lena laughing shyly, as she talked about a first date that had gone particularly well. Lena excitedly showing Kara the new engagement ring adorning her left hand. Lena in a bridal gown, as Kara stood next to her - as maid of honor. 

Kara opened her eyes again, gently removing Lena’s hand, quietly shuffling out of bed, shifting her mind towards thoughts of a brief flight to Dublin, to checking in again at the DEO, to doing a final pass on that article. Things could go back to normal now. Whatever Lena wanted her to be, she would be. 

And it would be enough.

8. Choices

It was a cool morning as Kara flew back, stretched out in the sun. There had been some light rain that afternoon in Dublin when Supergirl had knocked politely at the window of the shop that sold Lena’s favorite scones. The shop owner took one look at the soaked Supergirl, and had been kind enough to meet her outside - scones carefully wrapped so as not to get wet.

Kara landed softly at Lena’s balcony, the breeze from the flight having thoroughly dried her suit. She lightly gripped the handle, hoping not to wake Lena as she snuck back into the apartment. But tuning in to Lena’s heartbeat, she realized - Lena was already awake.

Lena jumped up from the couch, rushing over to Kara, throwing her arms around her neck.

“Shh, it’s okay,” Kara said soothingly, as Lena burrowed her head into Kara’s neck. Kara gently wrapped her arms around Lena’s waist. “Be careful, you shouldn’t be barefoot with all the shattered glass.”

“I wasn’t sure if you were coming back,” Lena said, her voice tiny and withdrawn.

Kara closed her eyes. She didn’t think Lena would wake so soon, but maybe it would’ve been better to leave a note. “I’m right here. I’m not going anywhere.”

Promise? Lena thought. She shivered, as the light breeze made its way through Lena’s shattered windows. 

Lena shook her head, trying to get a grip. How utterly unbecoming of a Luthor, she thought, as she pulled away from Kara and gingerly made her way across her living room. Jess had said a crew would be there in the afternoon to deal with the damage.

Lena set about making coffee, before sitting back at the kitchen island, Kara sitting across and scooting scones towards her in exchange for the extra-sugary mocha. For a few moments, they ate their breakfast in a comfortable silence.

Kara worried the bottom of her lip. “Why aren’t you angry at me?”

Lena tilted her head up curiously. It’s you who should be angry at me. “For what?”

Kara’s eyes were downcast. “For creating her. For killing her.”

“Metallo… thought she was right. To control, to-” Lena gestures into the air, “To use control to avoid pain. I realized what I was becoming. Who I could become.” Lena shook her head. “All you did was show that I’m better off having you in my life.”

Lena paused, looking down at her glass. “I’m not sure why you’re still here, though. Knowing the capacity for darkness I have within me.”

“We all have that capacity,” Kara said earnestly.

Lena shakes her head. “Not you.”

“Yes, me. You’re not the only one who’s seen the darker side of yourself. At least your alternate self was motivated by something selfless.”

Lena blinked. “You… had an evil doppelganger? What was yours motivated by?”

Kara shrugged. “Power. It’s scary what I would’ve become, in a world without Alex, without Eliza.”

Lena tilted her head. “Do you blame Alex? For what happened to you in this other world?”

A confused crinkle formed across Kara’s brow as she squinted. “Of course not.”

Lena reached her hand across the table. “Then don’t blame yourself for Metallo’s choices. Or mine.” Lena bit at her lip. “She had a choice. In the end, we all have a choice.”

Lena stood nervously in the CatCo office, glancing down at her watch. It’s almost 5pm.

It had been five weeks since the events of MIT. Five weeks since Alex and J’onn had accompanied her as she destroyed Non Nocere. They had debated what was best to do with the quantospheres - truly revolutionary technology for the energy sector, though they decided to consult with Mxy first and move forward cautiously. Kara found that Kryptonians had dabbled in magic technologies in the past, and they decided to read through that research first before moving forward.

But still, Lena and Brainy had been free to investigate the quantospheres themselves, leading to bigger questions about the nature of magic and how it worked. Lena was spending a lot of time lately investigating the implications on quantum foam.

It had been an aching relief to talk to Brainy again. J’onn had been friendly to her, but they were never close. Alex had been… civil, but clearly did not want to be around Lena any more than she had to. 

Brainy had been a breath of fresh air, eager to debate the merits of various forms of scientific inquiry. Only one of which damaged the lab. Brainy had detachedly informed an exasperated J’onn that a 99.7% likelihood of success was not equal to a 100% likelihood of success.

Lena and Kara had started spending more time together, with Kara occasionally bringing in Belly Burger or some other food for lunch. On Lena’s busier mornings, she sometimes found a neat box of scones on her office balcony.

Kara hadn’t acted any differently, not really. Friendly, and warm, and… the occasional glance that lasted just a little too long. It made Lena’s heart stop. It made her want to collapse and tell Kara everything she felt. But every time the rush of words felt at risk of falling out of Lena’s mouth, she remembered the anguish on Kara’s face in the Fortress when Lena had trapped her, and the words died on Lena’s lips. Kara deserved better.

Kara would get over her.

“Lena!”

Lena glanced up again, watching as a cheerful brunette bounded towards her, opening her arms for a hug. She really couldn’t believe how easily most of the superfriends had accepted her again - had even gone out of their way, as Nia had this morning, when she asked Lena on an impromptu shopping trip.

“Ready?” Nia said, as she pulled back from her hug, a friendly smile on her face. Lena tried again to shake off her nerves, and smiled back. She was starting to believe in normal again. 

Lena twisted her hands nervously. She had finally convinced herself to go inside the apartment complex - the rain had helped - but she found herself reluctant to knock on Kara’s door.

Lena sighed. Get it together, Luthor. It really seemed like everyone was overjoyed for the reinstatement of game night, but Lena couldn’t help but be nervous. The shame was still overwhelming, and something about interacting with a group felt… raw. Exposed.

Breathe, Lena.

Lena paused, before knocking lightly on the door, hearing a muffled “It’s open!” from Alex inside.

Lena turned the doorknob. It was only the second time she had stepped into Kara’s apartment since their fallout in the Fortress, seemingly eons ago. At least I’m not bleeding from a kryptonite burn this time, she thought, casting her eyes inside the apartment.

Her heart skipped a beat when she realized… Alex was the only one in Kara’s apartment.

“She’ll be back soon,” Alex said, with a tone that couldn’t be called friendly, but wasn’t antagonistic either. “There’s a naval ship in need of a tow.”

Lena nodded, standing uncomfortably a few feet inside, not quite sure what to do. Alex paused to look over the Luthor, perhaps not thrilled to be alone with her, but… a little amused at her discomfort. Alex decided to take pity. “Please tell me that’s scotch,” she said, gesturing towards the bag in Lena’s hand.

Lena smiled weakly at the olive branch, stepping forward and grabbing the bottle out of the bag. Alex reached up into a cupboard to pull out two tumblers, setting them on the table.

Lena poured the glasses, Alex taking one for herself. The two sat in silence, Lena staring at her glass a bit too intently, Alex looking around the room and occasionally back to Lena.

Alex sighed, turning back to the windows, anticipating that her sister might barge in at any moment now. “I won’t hate you forever, Lena.”

Lena looked up reluctantly, giving a wry smile. “I wouldn’t blame you if you did.”

Alex paused. “You know, Kara always had a family who loved her. She’s been broken, thinking she lost her planet, her boyfriend. But she always had a warm home to return to. You didn’t.”

Alex shook her head, looking back down at her glass. “I get to be the protective big sister. I’m angry. But eventually, Kara’s going to tell me your entire life story enough times that I’ll have to forgive you, just so I don’t have to hear the whole thing again.” Alex let out a small huff, before taking another sip. “I’m just not there yet.”

Lena nodded slowly. Alex stared back out the window. Lena picked up her glass to take another sip.

“She said she told you how she felt.”

Lena nearly choked, blinking back tears as the scotch burned her nose. Fuck, not this conversation. “I’m not going to go after her, Alex,” she muttered rapidly, eager for this conversation to be over. “I know I screwed up.”

Alex turned to her again, jaw set in a scowl. “Don’t, Luthor. Don’t break her heart just because you think you’re being noble.” 

Lena blinked. “There’s no way you want me to be with your sister.”

“I want Kara to be happy.”

“She’ll get over me, Alex,” Lena said, somewhat disoriented. You’re not making any sense. “She deserves someone who wouldn’t trap her in kryptonite.”

“She deserves someone so idiotically in love with her, that she'd throw herself off a balcony to protect her,” Alex replied firmly, before taking another sip of her scotch.

Lena stared, dumbfounded. This… hadn’t been the conversation she had expected at all.

But before Lena could form a reply, another knock came at the door, with Alex shouting “It’s open!” again. As Nia and Brainy walked in, Nia threw Lena a quick smile before setting her boxes of board games down on the kitchen island.

Lena shivered slightly, as the twilight air cast a slight chill over National City. She ought to close the balcony door, and let her condo warm up again. But instead, Lena sat, deep in thought, Alex’s words ringing in her head. “I want Kara to be happy.”

Lena shook her head, glancing at the clock. In the week since game night, Kara had suggested resuming movie night - in which Kara showed Lena the movies Lena missed out on, growing up in the Luthor household. 

I should’ve said no.

Lena absentmindedly scratched at the back of her hand, rubbing it raw. I shouldn’t tell her. I shouldn’t! She deserves better. She shouldn't even want me.

Lena turned as she heard the click of the doorknob, as Kara’s voice and the shuffling sound of a bag spread through the quiet living room. “Alright, so I brought a copy of The Mummy, Pitch Perfect 3, The Devil Wears Prada - and a ton of ice cream-”

“You shouldn’t want to be with someone who would trap you in kryptonite.” Lena hardly registered her words as she said them. Well, fuck.

Kara froze, eyes wide, trained on Lena’s face as the door swung closed behind her. She took a tiny step backwards, as though she were trying to give Lena more space, though she was already all the way across the room. “Lena, I… if there’s a line I’ve crossed-”

"No! No, Kara-" Lena rose from her seat on the couch, making her way to the kitchen island to take a seat. “You didn’t cross any lines, Kara.” 

The tension in Kara’s shoulders seemed to dissipate, at least a little, as Kara watched Lena uncertainly. She shuffled awkwardly with the ice cream, before deciding to place the bag and DVDs on the counter. “Why… are we talking about this?” she asked, looking desperate to fold in on herself.

Lena fought to keep her lower lip from trembling. “Because… there’s a line that I want to cross. But I can’t, Kara. I can’t. I can’t be the person who gets too close and hurts you again.”

Kara’s brows furrow briefly, before her eyes widen again in realization. A soft, hopeful glow began to spread across her face. “What about the person who would step between me and the worst version of herself? The one who would take a kryptonite blow instead of me?”

“Kara…”

Kara slowly rounded the island, stopping as she reached the side Lena was on, but still feet away - as though she were approaching a bird, that might suddenly be scared off.

“I’m a super, and you’re a Luthor. Things get to be complicated,” Kara said in a low rumble. “It doesn’t mean they’re wrong.”

Kara lifted her hand forward, an offering, an invitation for Lena to close the distance.

Kara…

Lena reached out, placing her hand in Kara’s as she stood, taking a step closer just as Kara did. Kara tilted her head down, forehead pressing gently against Lena’s. They stood silently, eyes closed, Lena feeling warm against Kara despite the evening chill.

“I love you too, Kara.”

The words fell out, almost subconsciously. Lena kept her eyes closed, as Kara gently kissed her forehead, moving to her cheeks, Kara’s thumbs wiping away the tears that Lena hadn’t noticed had begun to fall. Kara’s lips dropped in front of Lena’s, her breath tickling Lena’s lips, but Kara stilled again. “Lena…”

Lena closed the distance, pressing her lips firmly against the kryptonian’s.

Kara took a deep, calm breath as she woke. She wasn’t ready to open her eyes, but she knew she wasn’t home - wherever she was had stunningly little light compared to her bedroom. She felt an arm wrapped around her bare waist, a fact that she’d investigate once she was a little more alert. But for now, she was content to breathe in the scent of flowers.

Lena.

Her eyes fluttered open, finding the brunette sleeping beside her. Lena looked snug, wrapped up against her. Kara smiled, tucking a stray strand of hair behind Lena’s ear. She softly rubbed Lena’s back, gently pulling her into waking - Lena would need to wake up soon, anyway, and much preferred Kara to her alarm clock.

Lena murmured something, tightening her arms around Kara. Kara nuzzled at the top of her head. “I didn’t understand that, Lena.”

Lena pulled her head slightly from its cradled nook in Kara’s arm, before murmuring, “I like waking up with you. Even if I don’t get scones.”

Kara laughed, and kissed the top of Lena’s head. “I think we can arrange both.”
