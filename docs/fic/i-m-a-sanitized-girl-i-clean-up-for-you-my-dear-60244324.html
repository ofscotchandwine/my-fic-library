    When you’ve been raised by a mother who hates you, or, in Rio’s case, not raised at all, there’s an unavoidable survival instinct fostered over years of mistreatment and nothingness. Agatha becomes covered in a layer of spines, poking Rio’s softest parts and making her impossible to hold. Rio shoots vicious thorns, clawing into the ether for a semblance of warmth and only finding her lover’s tender flesh turned ice cold.

    A pair of inconsolable women forced to defy their basest instincts to prove over and over again that they’re capable of the love they feel sparking between them every time they lock eyes. “The most beautiful flowers always have thorns, my love,” Rio would always say. “I’m not afraid of a little blood,” Agatha would always respond.

    This fight had started innocuously. Agatha noticed Rio pulling away, staying distant even when twined in her arms; she couldn’t quite break through the shadow that hung over her lover. But when the only thing Rio’s ever known has been the cold nothingness of the endless abyss and the disdain-filled gaze of those that passed on, how could she possibly convey her feelings to Agatha? How could she make her beloved see her point of view if she wasn’t even supposed to have one?

    “I don’t know what you want from me, Agatha!” Rio ran her fingers through her dark hair, a habit she picked up from the witch standing across from her in their quaint cottage’s kitchen, “I was perfectly content before you, I didn’t exist until your will demanded it, and now you have to reap what you’ve sown!”

    “You were content? Is your heart really so black that you preferred playing usher to millions of souls for eons over our love? Over our life that we’ve built together? Yes, you were nothing before you met me, but not because I came along and broke you. I fixed you, Rio!”

    Rio pressed her tongue into her cheek, pausing to carefully consider her words before deciding better of it and just letting her emotions burst forth, “You’re incapable of fixing anything! I didn’t want any of this, but now I’m here in this body and I care about you and I can’t imagine a world without you in it! Whenever you leave this house, our home , I count the minutes until you come back because the last thing I want is to be called to heel and find your body on the other end of the line. I can’t lose you, Agatha. And I don’t know how to deal with it because I’ve never had anything to lose before… I’ve never been anything worth having, either.”

    Agatha stood still, hands limp by her sides as Rio paced in front of her. She was unable to remain rooted to the floor like she usually was, she had to move with the frantic emotional energy that had completely overtaken her body, a still-foreign feeling.

    “Rio…” Agatha all but whispered into the thick air of their cabin.

    To stop her beloved from wearing a hole in the floorboards, Agatha walked towards her, grabbing her hand and pulling her towards the couch in their cozy living room. She pulled Rio down, wrapping her in her arms so tightly that her stubborn Death couldn’t squirm her way out like she does when she’s in these avoidant moods. Agatha wills their softest blanket to wrap itself around them, transforming the terrifying and beautiful creature in her arms into a docile, if slightly petulant, woman.

    “Lover,” Agatha breathes into Rio’s hairline, “I am not going anywhere for a long, long, long, long time. And when I do pass on, I know you’ll be there waiting for me. You cannot rid yourself of me that easily—I’m with you until the end of time, whether you will or no.”

    Rio turned her head to rub her nose along Agatha’s neck, feeling the blood flowing beneath the surface, counting the beats of her steady heart beneath her slightly chapped lips. She was always rendered speechless by the magnificence of human nature, of the incredible way sinew and blood and air and water and bone could be combined to create someone so soft and loving and vicious and headstrong. Agatha always awed Rio, but never more so than when they were just like this, with no bodies to concern them but their own and the only feelings to consider being the thrumming vine of love connecting Rio’s black bastardization of a heart to Agatha’s.

    “You are the best thing that’s ever happened to me,” Rio says, eyes dripping black tears down Agatha’s collarbone, leaving grey streaks that slither like snakes along every bump and wrinkle on her skin before disappearing beneath the folds of her robe.

    “As you are for me, my love,” Agatha replies, running her fingers through Rio’s soft hair and gently keeping her in place as she lowers further into the couch.

    As the two lay now in comfortable silence, Agatha’s eyes growing heavy with the comforting weight of her lover laid on top of her and the gentle warmth of the fire and soft blanket protecting them from the chill fighting its way into their shared home, Rio imagines a world without Agatha in it. She pictures the flowers losing their vibrancy, the rivers flowing backwards and then ceasing to exist at all, the winds betraying the travelers that dare brave them, the animals all collapsing where they stood. Rio will never let that world come to pass, not so long as she still welcomes the earth’s air into her mossy lungs.

    Rio doesn’t sleep, not really, but she closes her eyes and banishes those horrible thoughts. She instead pictures the world she and Agatha will build: vines and flowers decorating the outside of their humble cabin, thick fields littered with their tracks as they dance under the moonlight, and gentle pitter-patter of little feet on cold hardwood floors.

    Rio’s smile presses into Agatha’s chest, molding her handmade body to the marvel of flesh beneath hers.
