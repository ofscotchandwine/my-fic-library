    Chapter 1

    what happens if you think you don't have a family anymore? you build one yourself.

    Before Agatha Harkness, Death had a fairly straightforward existence. Simple wasn’t exactly the right word to call it, because it wasn’t, not really. Humans were born, they obtained the knowledge that none of them were making it out alive well before when they should have been concerned about their own mortality, then they died. Sometimes too early. Sometimes unfairly. Sometimes after periods of suffering long enough that it would have been a welcome embrace if it happened sooner. Never in the history of the universe had someone been born, fallen victim to attempted murder hundreds of times, often at her own provocation, had cause of death technically listed as “kiss” (had she been far enough on anyone’s radar for them to realize she needed a certificate), and taken up near-permanent residence in a stolen house that she hated, a few miles away from a former situationship she hated, half-raising the son of yet another situationship she hated. Rio had collected enough souls from enough corners of suburbia to know that this was a rarity bordering on insanity, and yet she stayed. What was more, she was slowly realizing, she wanted to stay there. For the first time in ages, maybe she could.

    His six years were too short as it was, but Rio had never gotten to truly step into her role as a mother with Nicky. She visited him from time to time, almost exclusively by pure coincidence. It wasn’t just human souls she collected and escorted from their plane of existence, so there were plenty of times that she arrived for a deer or, in the wildest cases, a bear, and found her wife and son before she found what she had come for.

    Appearing in a green glow in the middle of the night didn’t do her any favors in the ‘let the kid sleep’ department, but even when he was brand new, he never fussed or cried when she appeared. His dark eyes, achingly like her own, locked on to her with what was unmistakably Agatha’s intense stare. She’d stare back at him and make a point of blinking as few times as possible because truly, who could tell when that chance meeting would happen again? But without fail, her eyes would drift to her sleeping wife.

    Even asleep, Agatha would never quite settle. That much had been true far longer than the few months she had been a mother; even when they were living in the same space, every breeze was an intruder, the house settling was magic she wasn’t prepared enough to siphon before it could hurt her. It was infinitely worse in the woods where they were most vulnerable, when it wasn’t just herself she would need to protect.

    “Do you think I can’t feel you there?” she groaned one night. Rio froze in place, five-month- old Nicky still clinging to her skirt and babbling away to her in a language all his own, but made no move to leave.

    “I’m not here for him.”

    “I know you’re not.” Agatha sat up. “Just…just hold your son. Please. His first tooth is coming in and this is the first time he’s stopped screaming in days.”

    “Which means this is the first time you have slept in days.”

    “I didn’t think that was for you to worry about anymore.”

    “I do not have the capacity or ability to worry limitlessly about every being that walks this planet. But my rules do not apply to you. They never have and they never will. Surely you must know that by now.”

    “Maybe I was hoping it was untrue now.” Agatha was such a practiced, finessed liar that Rio almost believed her, but something in her eyes stayed hollow and sad.

    “Why would you ever hope for that?”

    She shrugged. “No one worried about me before you. I never thought a day would come that there would be an ‘after you,’ but logic would say that it would be the same.”

    “Nothing about my life with you is logical. Do not spend another second with that thought.” Rio tilted her head to keep a strand of hair out of Nicky’s reach; she could already tell that he was a puller with anything his tiny fingers could grasp. He screeched at the denial of access and she laughed.

    “Putting up a fight when he doesn’t get what he wants…he gets that from you, I think.”

    The second the words left her mouth, Rio knew she was down to seconds before Agatha snatched their son back and demanded that she leave. Making a joke about Agatha not getting what she wanted, when what she wanted was for the sweet boy in her arms to stay as warm and alive as he was in that moment…it was in poor taste, but something she didn’t consider until the words were already hanging in the air.

    “I didn’t…” she started, but Agatha was already standing with her arms outstretched.

    “If that is something you think is funny, you are even farther beyond help than I thought,” “It’s not. I’m sorry.”

    Nicky was fully wailing now, as though he could sense that the energy around them had changed into something he wanted nothing to do with.

    “Take whoever it is you came to take and leave. He’ll be at this for hours, I do not have the energy for this and you right now.”

    Their run-ins weren’t always as bitter as that particular night. She made a point of “accidentally” figuring out where they were every year on Nicky’s birthday and something about that would soften Agatha a bit, as much as it was possible for her to be softened at all. Nicky would look excitedly between them as though he wasn’t sure where to look first, because it wasn’t every day he got to have two mothers instead of one. When he was old enough to understand the concept of blowing out a candle, they would take turns gathering magic into the palm of their hands, letting it vanish when he took the deepest breath his little lungs would allow. It was good until it wasn’t, and the fact that it was good again a couple hundred years later, well…there wasn’t much that could surprise Death, but here she was.

    Surprised.

    The Westview house didn’t quite feel like home yet, but it was where she spent every free moment she had, playing Professor Rio to someone who still didn’t quite know how to be a ghost yet and listening to Billy’s magic lessons in the once again bewitched basement. He had potential, sure. But if Agatha was going to base that assessment on what she was hearing in these lessons, she would immediately be called on her lie.

    “I can’t get it, okay? If you don’t stop I swear I’ll try to banish you again.”

    “You know that won’t work,” Agatha said dryly, completely immune to a teenage temper, even if only because her ancient temper was worse. “I know you saw me pick up the locket. It’s on right now. You had some very strong feelings about that, if I remember correctly?” “Because you–ugh, never mind. Most of the stuff in this house is yours, isn’t it? I’ll literally bring Rio down and do it right now. She could be a personal item, right?”

    At that last question, the temptation was too great to resist; Rio let herself materialize right over Billy’s shoulder.

    “Personal item?”

    The noise he made wasn’t quite a scream, but she could tell by the look on his face that the pitch was infinitely higher than he had intended it to be.

    “Not a personal item, I don’t think of you as an item. I mean, I don’t think of you at all. Not that I don’t remember you’re around and appreciate your…deathness?”

    “Mi amor, I think it’s time to call it for the day.”

    As a ghost, Agatha didn’t need to breathe, but she made a big show of sighing deeply anyway. “I would like to actually breathe again someday and if being here right now is going to end in you sending me into another dimension or continuing to do…whatever that was…

    go home, and we can pick this up tomorrow.”

    “In the morning?” Billy asked hopefully.

    “You’re going to school.”

    “This is school. It’s just magic school.”

    “Actual school, William. And if you show up here a minute sooner than however long it would take you to get here after your last class, I’m going to…” She looked down at her transparent hands. “I’m going to ask Rio to lock the front door for me.”

    “You know, you really should be locking that anyway. Just…you know…safety and everything…”

    “That cannot seriously be all you got out of that.”

    “Yeah, whatever, school is important, education is superior, standardized testing means the most for my future.”

    “You can cut the attitude. I missed one chance to send my kid to school and I’m not making the same mistake with you.”

    “I...okay,” Billy said, his voice much smaller. “You know you just claimed responsibility for me though, right? I thought this was like babysitting for you, you put up with the kid for a few hours as long as the parents take them back at the end?”

    “Don’t read too much into it. Go home, be good, learn things tomorrow.”

    “What aren’t you acknowledging?” Rio asked once Billy’s Subaru was no longer in the driveway.

    “So you know about the Hex.”

    “I’m familiar with its work, yes,” she said bitterly.

    “Magic…on autopilot.”

    “Well, yeah. Scarlet Witch, that’s her whole thing, right?”

    “No.” Agatha ran a hand down her face. “Magic. On. Autopilot.”

    “You…no. No you didn’t.”

    “Just once, but how was I supposed to know that would happen? I didn’t even know until he showed up in my house, I thought she did it solo or built Vision some new parts that had… special features, if you know what I mean.”

    “I always know what you mean.”

    “On a scale of one to…I need to watch for flying sinks again…how mad are you?”

    “Death isn’t thrilled. But Rio…acknowledges that we were divorced and not speaking and you had the Darkhold, except for that one night we met up. Rio thinks he turned out a lot like you. As upsetting as it is, Rio seems to win a lot when it comes to you. I guess maybe Billy now, too. God save us when there are two of them.”

    “I could balance it out if you want.”

    “For the last time. As much as I love your enthusiasm. You killing two random people doesn’t balance out those boys. There are…there are higher authorities on this than just me. It’s possible that a case could be made. We’ll cross that bridge another day, okay, mi amor?” “It’s a lose/lose no matter what, you know. Make the case now, you show up with the ghost you’re married to. Make the case later, you show up with your freshly resurrected wife. I don’t think they look too kindly on either of those options.”

    “Since when do you care?”

    Agatha’s face betrayed her focus for a split second before she successfully tossed her hair. “I don’t care.”

    “Don’t worry about it yet,” Rio soothed again. “If it makes you feel better, I might even let you haunt Jen for a few minutes.”

    “Yeah, tried that while you were gone yesterday. She was telling someone about the Road and it hit me…she thinks it’s real. She was in a teenager’s delusion and she thinks it’s real. I had to leave before I laughed or I would have given myself away. I guess no one ever told her, but I was busy dying, it took a lot out of me.”

    Rio shook her head and sank down into one of the living room chairs.

    “What’s that face about?”

    “You have two more kids. You get another chance.”

    “But you don’t,” Agatha finished softly.

    “Can it really be called another chance if you never got a first one?”

    Rio didn’t know enough about ghosts to know if they could cry, but she could have sworn Agatha’s eyes welled up a little.

    “You were his mother too.” Her voice was low again, that same danger versus devastation, but this time the devastation was clear. “You lost him too.”

    “I think I’ve needed you to say that for a long, long time.”

    “I knew every second was borrowed. Every time I saw you in his face, I remembered that someday, I’d see your face and his, and it would mean I’d never see his again. I’m not good at feeling things like that. I thought if I kept him away. Well. You know the rest”

    “His life line was always shorter than most. I wanted you to have what little time you could have with him. It did occur to me that you’d spend it trying to get more instead of enjoying it, I guess I just hoped you wouldn’t do that.”

    “You don’t know what it was like.” Agatha’s voice shook, but from what Rio could see of her face, she still couldn’t tell if there were ghost tears there. “You didn’t fall asleep with him warm and alive and wake up…I would have stayed up that night. I would have held him. I would have done something for him besides being absolutely fucking useless.”

    The lightbulbs got noticeably brighter, a clear warning sign.

    “He kissed you goodbye,” Rio said softly, hoping it would defuse the situation even a little bit.

    “He…what?”

    The lightbulbs flickered a little, but didn’t shatter.

    “I made sure he…I couldn’t let him leave without saying he loved you one last time. He’s safe. I went the whole way with him, I held his hand the whole time. He was never alone, not for a second.”

    They both fell silent. This marked the first time they’d ever had any form of this conversation that didn’t end in knives, blood, tequila, and broken furniture.

    “You scare the shit out of Billy,” Agatha said finally, clearly unable to speak about Nicky any longer. “But he does really like having you around. I don’t remember as much about Tommy. I guess that’s another bridge we cross when we get to it.”

    “Did you find him?”

    “Yes and no. There’s a boy named Thomas Shepherd whose circumstances were the closest we’ve found so far. There are only so many drownings and miraculous resuscitations happening at public pools these days. But if a baby witch and a ghost are going to corner a stranger and say they’re a long lost family member, you really do have to be one hundred percent sure you’ve got the right person. We’re not going there yet. I don’t usually plan, I’m more of a schemer, but this is still part of Billy’s Road. I can’t go all…Agatha…on it.”

    “I always saw us with a girl after Nicky,” Rio said thoughtfully. “Teenage boys. Teenage boys with two other sets of parents. That’ll be. Wow.”

    “You saw…me…with a girl?”

    “Oh, yeah. Blue eyes, crazy curls. Your little twin.”

    “It was pretty unfair that I carried Nicky for nine months and he popped out looking like he didn’t have any of my DNA.”

    “You don’t think he had your smile?”

    “I’m not sure. I haven’t done a lot of smiling.”

    Rio shook her head. “You’ve smiled plenty, that I’ve seen. You’re lucky you’re a ghost right now or I’d tickle that spot in your rib cage to prove it.”

    “You wound me.”

    “Practice solidifying yourself a little harder, test my theory.”

    “Put me back in my body, test it yourself.”

    Rio moved from her chair and settled on the couch, somewhat giving the effect that Agatha was on her lap when in actuality, Rio had just passed right through her legs.

    “I’ve missed enough other things that when that time comes, I bet I won’t even remember.

    I’ll be too busy.”

    “Thank god, do you know how long it’s been since my last good orgasm? Why are you looking at me like that, are we not being sexy right now?”

    “Not really,” Rio said. “I’m thinking about that little ridge on your nose. The way I used to kiss it to wake you up if you were asleep when I came home. I hate to break it to you, but those were even more smiles. You’d be smiling before you ever woke up."

    "You want me back in my body so you can kiss my nose again?" Agatha wrinkled said perfect nose, looking at Rio like she had lost her damn mind.

    "Among other things. But you know how humans say you never really appreciate something until you don't have it anymore?"

    "Humans say 'you don't know what you have until it's gone.'"

    "That is the exact same thing." Rio sighed. "I miss little things. I miss you trying to cook and almost starting a fire. I miss you insisting you aren't tired and passing out in my lap a few minutes later. I miss how no matter how big the bed was, you could have been in my skin and it wouldn't have been close enough. So, yeah, I miss kissing your damn nose. Sue me."

    She almost pretended she was trying not to, but Agatha was giggling. "Did Death just say 'sue me?'"

    "I've been in this neighborhood for too long. I'm becoming one of them."

    Agatha closed her eyes and held her focus until she really was (mostly) sitting on Rio's lap. "Here. Try now."

    "No one on earth can tell you what to do, but I have you eating out of the palm of my hand. Interesting."

    "Ghosts can't eat," Agatha deadpanned. "Now, would you shut up and kiss me before I lose the hold I have on this?"

    "Always so bossy," Rio murmured, but her lips were already finding that little ridge she loved so much. She planted a feather-light kiss there, one on each cheek, and found Agatha's lips. She would never forget the last time she had tried that, so busy enjoying it that she didn't come up for air in time to see the streaks of black across Agatha's face or feel her starting to float upward. Her heart sank when she felt empty air on her face; that was the longest hold on a corporeal form so far, but she supposed all good things had to come to an end eventually. "Hey," Agatha said softly. "Give it time. I won't fade every time forever. Besides...you love it, don't you? The anticipation."
