The halls were less of a maze than the palace in Alexandria, but they were no less complicated. In the end, Kara resolved to tail the attendants. It wasn’t long before she figured out where Lena was staying. She was still awake reading, most likely, and had fruits delivered to her rooms.

The attendant knocked, intending to step inside to deliver the platter, but Kara heard a faint Thank you, leave it outside, please. The attendant acquiesced, leaving as swiftly as he arrived. 

It was as if the gods set up the opportunity for Kara. Fortuitous wouldn’t even begin to explain the circumstances. So, she picked up the platter and knocked. She could hear Lena getting up and seemingly preparing to berate whoever had the gall to interrupt her again.

“Was I not clear—Kara?” The door swung open and Lena’s mouth dropped open.

“Surprise?”

“How—?”

Kara nudged her inside, setting the platter on a table. “Well, you see, Kal had a change of heart, and um, I’m the ambassador now? So, uh, I am to remain by your side and report—”

Her rambling was cut off by Lena launching herself into her arms. “Gods above, I’ve missed you so much.”

Kara laughed, picking her up and spinning her around. “I think I’ve missed you more.”

“Impossible.” 

Kara hummed, “I disagree. It’s entirely possible, you know.”

Kara could almost hear Lena’s pout. “I missed you the most. Just ask Samantha and Jess. I think I’ve driven them beyond mad the last two years.”

She set Lena back on the ground, holding her tighter. Lena tucked her chin in the crook of her neck, cold nose brushing against her jaw.

“Lena?” Kara said, pulling back. Lena hummed, waiting.

“I love you.”

Lena grinned in response, double-dimples etched into her cheeks.

“And I, you, darling. And I, you.”
