For as long as Lena had run Luxe Luthor , no restaurant had ever lasted in the space directly across from her. 

It wasn’t like she went out of her way to force them out, contrary to the image that some press liked to paint. She never said anything, did anything, just simply kept her own restaurant running and the others came and went like leaves on the breeze. 

There was a rumor that had spread around to most of the local restaurateurs of the area: don’t move in the space across from Luxe Luthor . It's cursed.

Lena certainly isn’t disappointed. But, well- competition makes inventors of us all, or something. 

There was, at first, the gourmet butchery that moved in nearly five years ago, shortly after Luxe first opened, the minimalist (and wildly overpriced) French bistro that soon followed; both lasted only one year, respectively. There was the Peruvian-Japanese fusion place that had lasted a whopping seven months, and then had quickly shut its doors following a string of bad press reviews. The rest that had followed- one elitist niche eatery after another- had rarely lasted six months apiece without closing up permanently and vacating for the next restaurant to follow in its footsteps. 

So when Lena leaves Luxe one evening, locking up the front doors and waving goodnight to her team, she barely bats an eye at the brand new sign being strung up across the street that reads “ZOR-EL’S”.

Lena is gently spooning pomelo relish over two sashimi-style pieces of fresh Santa Barbara uni. She basks in the scents of the salty seafood and cutting citrus, and finishes the whole plate with a small bed of house-grown shiso leaves, a dusting of lime caviar and a smear of her new house yuzu sauce. 

She turns to Jack, Luxe Luthor’s resident sommelier and her best friend. He’s sitting on one of the bar stools he dragged into the kitchen, eyeing her whole process, watching rapt intrigue as she finishes off the dish. She gathers a bite on a clean spoon that incorporates all aspects of the dish, and passes it over to his grabby, eager hands for him to try. 

“I think it’ll be a strong addition to the spring menu,” Lena says, gesturing at the plate, “but I think there’s something missing in the yuzu sauce. Do you think it needs wine? Maybe a white or one of the new whiskeys, a Yamazaki, perhaps?”

“I think you need to take a break, because I’ve tasted seventeen versions of this dish and it’s been astounding each time. However-” Jack wiggles his eyebrows in a way that Lena assumes to be conspiratory, “if it means I get to keep trying bites of this, I will continue to tell you there’s something wrong with it, if that's what you want to hear.”

Lena slipped back into her kitchen and pulled out her notebook. She set out her knives, briskly prepared some of the ingredients for a new asparagus salad she was creating, and got to work. For the first time in a very long time, Lena had a moment of peace in her kitchen, albeit only for a few hours, but she fully intended to make good use of it.

Any irregular beat or palpitation, Lena feels and she knows, and it’s her job to either respond to the situation accordingly or give her restaurant the food-industry equivalent of a physical and get it working in peak c0ndition again.

Accuse chef Kara Danvers of stealing her customers? A spectacle at one of National City’s hottest restaurants right now is certainly the press Luxe needs to bring back its usual crowds. Realising she’s starting to look like she might actually be enjoying the drink, Lena forces her face into a mask she hopes conveys disinterest and puts the glass back down, reaching for the plate next to it.

When she bites down, her mouth floods with the powerful, but not overwhelming, taste of garlic and spices, perfectly complementing the meat’s rich flavor. Kara had already announced her intent to step away from Zor-El’s , so Alex is prepped to take over as head chef, but when she tells the rest of the staff if they need her they can find her right across the street at Lena’s restaurant everyone finds the whole ordeal hilarious. Kara takes a couple of weeks to ensure that the transfer of duties occurs as smoothly as possible, but once Alex is settled in as head chef and everyone else has found their new routine, Kara officially dedicates her schedule to Luxe Luthor and their new menu. So while the interior gets re-done, Kara and Lena spend sun up to sun down sequestered away in the kitchen, hunched over two battered notepads and a countertop of ingredients. 

Inevitably, the menu becomes a bit pizza-focused.  

It isn’t Lena’s fault that her intent for the new menu is elevated comfort; it certainly doesn’t help that every new topping combination she tries and every new style of crust she develops leaves Kara practically drooling on the counter. They manage to cement a couple of other non-pizza additions, like a citrus-prawn linguine that Sam instantly adores and a fresh tuna crudo with avocado mousse and ponzu sauce that makes Jack bang his fist on the table with glee. 

They incorporate a lot of things from Lena’s garden. The radishes with a wintery salad, her wild ramps as a side and garnish to their signature steak dish, all of her herbs stuffed in local Jidori chicken. Slowly, with Kara beside her and the help of her staff, the new menu comes together.

They spend so much time together, working on the menu and getting every asset perfect for Luxe’s reopening, that more often than not Kara ends up crashing at Lena’s house afterwards, or Lena at Kara’s. It’s a habit they don’t attempt to interrogate— especially with their quickly growing relationship— but Lena admits there’s nothing better than coming home with Kara after a day of hard work. 

It isn’t perfect. With the new challenge of both managing and operating the restaurant, Lena feels the pressure raining down on her sometimes, but Kara is just as kind as she is passionate, and she always listens, whether it’s to Lena’s ideas, frustrations, or outbursts. 

They make it through, and when Luxe Luthor reopens under a new name that reflects the shared effort they all underwent to get here, Kara holds her hand tight as they reveal the name Luxe & Lend on the restaurant's freshly painted wall. 

It’s around the same time of the reopening that Kara floats the idea of not renewing the lease on her apartment in the spring. 

“Are you sure?” Lena asks, still not completely convinced that everything that has happened these past few months is entirely real. “You know what our lives are like— I work long hours, and I’m not always the most… well, agreeable.” 

Kara shrugs, like she hasn’t spent nearly every waking hour of the last few weeks with her. “I know you, Lena. I love working with you, but I want to keep building a life with you outside of work. I want us to order takeout after a rough day and relax on the couch together. I want movie nights and board game parties and late nights and sick days. I want everything with you.” 

Lena can’t argue with that. So when Kara’s lease ends, their friends all come to help relocate her things to Lena’s apartment, and then Jack pops a couple bottles of chianti. What started off as a simple moving party quickly dissolves into an actual party , and Kara and Lena get a chance to host that game night she had talked about pretty much immediately. 

It’s everything Lena has ever wanted. The restaurant is flourishing— her restaurant, she reminds herself— the crowds they’re pulling are bigger than ever, their menu winning awards. But Lena knows there’s something else waiting for them— something the two women have been putting off for some time.

They both start to pull away from their duties to Zor-El’s and Luxe. Alex has all but cemented her management of Kara’s creation, and Kara trusts her sister and staff implicitly to uphold the same standards that Kara championed in her restaurant. Leaving Luxe is a little bit harder— Lena’s trust is not as steadfast as Kara’s, as anyone who has been within a five mile radius of her may know— but as she watches the joy on Sam’s face as her sous begins to take over more and more of the responsibilities of Luxe’s opening Lena knows there will be no struggles there. 

Her staff doesn’t even bother to appear shocked when she announces her departure, temporary and partial as it may be; she still expects to be kept in the loop for all major changes and menu adjustments. It’s a simple transition, really— easy in a way Lena isn’t used to, may never be. 

So it all comes together quite smoothly in the end. Kara and Lena talk about it, widely, not really coming up with a solid plan for the now empty days ahead of them. Kara wants to travel, Lena knows, yearns for it just as much as she yearned for Lena before they met. Lena wants to travel, too— wants to be wherever Kara is. 

Kara comes home one day with a stack of mail in hand and a bag of groceries in the other. She loads up the pantry, then collapses onto the couch next to Lena, envelopes tossed haphazardly on the coffee table. Lena shuts her laptop and grabs half the stack, wordlessly helping Kara comb through their junk. 

“Farmers market was busy today,” Kara hums, her head dropping down naturally to its place on Lena’s shoulder. “I think there was a local high school on a field trip.”  

Lena nods. “That’s lovely,” she says, imagining all those youths with their hands full of fresh fruits and market stall snacks. “A great way to get them invested in local business and agriculture.” 

“Listen to you, liaison to the community,” Kara laughs, and Lena nudges her playfully. “Oh look, a letter from Cat!” 

Kara holds it up between them. It’s in a plain white envelope of nondescript but visible quality, adorned with a few stamps and an address in looping script topped with the names Ms. Danvers and Ms. Luthor. Kara slides her thumb under the lip and pulls out a single sheet of elegant stationary and a folded piece of printer paper. 

The words on the stationary are brief, succinct, and in a cursive kindergarten teachers would worship. It reads— to the household of Kara Danvers and Lena Luthor, 

I wish to congratulate you on the continued success of your two enterprises and commend you, Luthor, on the wrenching of your business from your brother’s dry fingers. It is a pleasure to see how the restaurant has flourished without the presence of a man who knows nothing of how good kitchens are run.

I have heard word of your shared temporary retirement from my network of rumormongers— even as I traverse across the country, location scouting for the next season of “Cat Grant’s Culinary Travels” it remains important that I keep a close eye on all of my charges. I know the both of you to be people, like myself, who prefer to keep their hands moving, and so I have sent a small gift that may hopefully jumpstart your travels together and lead you to many fascinating culinary finds, which you may then detail back to me on an episode of the show. Consider this an early honeymoon present. 

I’ll have my new assistant Liam forward you any details regarding both your gift and your future guest appearances this season. As Ms. Luthor may know from her time on Chopped (a show which I wholeheartedly detest, to be frank) the nature of this business is one which requires much discussion in regards to scheduling, permissions, and contracts, and I simply do not have the time nor the energy to be bothered with organizing any of that. 

Enjoy your trip. Admittedly, I look forward to seeing the both of you soon. 

Cat Grant. 

Kara and Lena both reach the end of the letter at the same time, but Lena is the first one to reach out and remove the piece of printer paper, heavy with ink, from behind Ms. Grant’s note. She unfolds the sheet before them, revealing a printed voucher for airfare to anywhere in the world, of not insignificant credit value. 

“God, this is kind of her.” Lena passes the voucher to Kara for her eyes to pass over. Then, remembering the letter, she snorts. “An early honeymoon present. She must know something we don’t.” 

Kara smiles. It’s true, Cat does know something Lena doesn’t— she knows about the wooden box, stuffed in the heel of Kara’s non-slip boot, knows about a silver band with a teardrop emerald, the same color as Lena’s eyes in the light of the sun.
