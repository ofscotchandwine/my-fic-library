Summary

When Lionel Luthor destroys Zor El ship and family, Kara strikes a deal with Davy Jones that grants her invulnerability to most weapons. Earning a reputation as the Girl of Steel, Kara turns to piracy as she hunts down the Luthors to bring them to justice. After saving a drowning maiden, she's captured and sentenced to death. While she awaits the noose, an unlikely ally comes to her rescue, Lena Luthor, who offers her a deal; if Kara is to help Lena find her recently kidnapped brother, Kara secures complete pardon for her crew. Kara quickly agrees. Little do they know, they will both find more than they bargained for out on the sea.

Chapter 1

The thunder of cannons and crunching of wood shocked Kara Zor El out of her sleep in the dead of night. Splinters of wood drug across her face and neck, piercing her flesh as she stumbled out of her bed. Just before her hand grasped the door handle, the hair on the back of her neck stood on end as a low whistle screeched through the air; her knees hit the floor just in time to miss the force of the cannonball whizzing overhead, crumpling into her bed and dragging the thin mattress and blankets out into the water just on the other side of where the ship’s wall once stood. 

They were under attack. But why? They were nothing but a cargo ship. They had no cannon or weapons on board. What was to gain from sinking them rather than raiding?

Hands now shaking, Kara pushed open her door to see a hallway rapidly filling with brown foamy water. Clutching her nightgown in her hand, Kara sloshed through the water in her scramble to make it to the upper deck. Red liquid pooled around her feet as she ran among the splinters of wood and broken glass, salt stinging her wounds, but she gritted her teeth through the pain. Crashing of cannons and shouts of sailors echoed around her as she pushed against other frantic men attempting to find the upper deck. She knew the ship was sinking; it was already taking on too much water to be salvaged. At the cannon's current rate, Kara knew her only chance was to make it to the lifeboats and pray that her family was already there waiting for her. 

But her father was the captain. And the captain always went down with the ship.

Her lungs gasped in the cool night air as she finally emerged on the upper deck, but the smoke and gunpowder burned her throat and eyes as she whipped her head around in desperate search. Men, both complete and parts, laid along the deck as others ran around them. The cannons sounded louder up here, so much so that on the first round, Kara winced and covered her ears, but she still heard the creak and splintering of the mast just before it fell into the sea with a crash that splashed water on her cheeks. As she turned her head though, she finally spotted a familiar face.

“Father!” Kara cried out as she stumbled towards him, tripping on debris and slipping on the blood from her feet.

“Kara!” her father gasped, quick to grip on to her arms. “What are you doing up here?”

His normally dapper attire and suave hair now hung in tatters and disarray. Red and brown splotches and streaks stained his blue coat and pants, and water and blood smeared through dark hair stood it on tall and unruly. The kind smile and eyes Kara had grown up with now held fear and desperation that chilled Kara to her core and increased her fear tenfold.

“I heard the canons and thought I could help.” They both flinched as another projectile screeched by and sunk into the ocean behind them. “Who is attacking us? Are they pirates?”

“No, not pirates,” her father shook his head before locking his gaze with her. “It’s Lionell Luthor.”

“What? But he’s a nobleman! He would never--”

“It’s because of that noble status that he can!” Her father’s fingers dug into her bicep so hard that she feared a bruise. “You need to hide, Kara. Find a lifeboat. It’s your only chance.”

“But mother and Clark--”

“They’re already gone. There’s no hope for them now. You have to go. Now!”

“Father, I--”

Just then, the ship swung to the side as the filling water in the hull brought on a capsize. Loose objects and debris now rushed to the side and overboard to the swallowing sea below. With a desperate push, her father flung Kara into reach of a rope that she quickly grasped. It burned her palm and fingers as she wrapped her hands in it, but her shout and tears came from watching her father disappear into the water below.

The sun scorched Kara’s face as she squinted against it and steered her small make-shift vessel towards the land just underneath it. Her muscles burned from a night of near constant rowing and goosebumps still broke out across her skin when she heard the clattering of bones from the corpses of pirates past strung up along the rocks outside of the city as a warning to those like her. When dawn broke and National City Port appeared on the horizon, she had found the motivation to fight through the burn in her muscles and row even faster.

Especially when the morning light showed just how much water the small vessel had already taken on. Serves her right from stealing a boat from a drunkard. She should have stuck with her normal rule of only stealing from loyalists or government officials.

The closer to the harbor, the lower her dingy surrendered to the ocean. Waves creased and threw more water in faster than Kara could throw out. By the time the boat made it to the dock, all of it but one of the handles of the oars were underwater. Given that most of her boat was now underneath the dock, Kara forwent the docking fee and held her head high as she sloshed down the harbor and into town with water filled boots.

Surprisingly, National City Port was abuzz with activity. With the large military presence in the city, many of the citizens lived blissful carefree lives believing no pirate or criminal would dare set foot on the island. Today just so happened to be the day in which the military was to be celebrated in a day of ceremony, presentation, and promotions with a ball that evening. Also known as the perfect time for someone to take advantage of the distracted military and commandeer a ship to track down a certain English nobleman.

With a city this size, Kara had more than plenty of ships to choose from. Some large, some small, some old, some new. Since her odds of finding a crew here were minimal, Kara’s options were limited to those small enough to be manned by one person. Still, that shouldn't prove to be much of an issue since most of the guard posts had been abandoned in favor of attending the ceremony. With any luck, Kara and her new ship would be halfway back to Gotham Port before anyone in National City Port noticed.

Much to her misfortune, the dock with the ship that caught Kara’s eye just so happened to be the one posted with two guards. Apparently not all the soldiers had been invited to today’s celebration. Still, Kara’s curiosity only peaked more as she began to wonder what was so special about this ship that still proved the need of two guards on a day like today.

Forgoing the warning signs that the dock was strictly for military personnel only, Kara sauntered down the stone steps and then wooden dock holding her head high as if enough confidence would let her slip past. Surprisingly, that was all it took half the time, but these guards appeared to be a little more attentive than some as they hurried in front of her to block her path.

“This dock is off limits to civilians,” the soldier to her right in front of her said, his head held high and eyes unfocused.

“Oh, I’m so sorry I didn’t know,” Kara gave a stiff smile. “But tell you what, if I see any, I’ll let you know.”

She went to step around the two men, but they only shuffled to stand in front of her, their expressions still hard and neutral.

“Miss, we cannot let you further on to this dock.”

Kara pursed her lips. Perhaps it was her wet clothes or tangled blonde hair, but her normal play of confidence and looks were falling flat. A different strategy was needed then.These two were still men after all.

“I understand.” Kara said slowly as she reached out to brush the nonexistent dirt off the shoulder of the soldier to her left, pleased when a blush colored his cheeks. “I just hope you can forgive a silly young woman like myself. Sometimes I just see something so wondrous that all my rational thought goes out the window.”

Both soldiers immediately puffed out their chest, exchanging brief looks to gauge just who stood the tallest and broadest between them. Kara couldn’t help but smirk. Men, especially soldiers, were all too easy to manipulate in a woman’s hand.

“Something like what?” one asked in a deeper voice than he used earlier.

“That ship there,” Kara nodded past them to the ship floating in the water. “She’s marvelous.”

“That’s the Manhunter,” the soldier to her left said, his shoulders sagging. “It’s the fastest ship in Her Majesty’s Fleet.”

“Rather small, don’t you think?” Kara frowned with an exaggerated tilt of her head.

“Silly girl,” the other soldier laughed. “That’s what gives it its speed.”

“Interesting,” Kara pondered as she drummed her fingers along her chin. “You don’t think you could take me on it, could you?”

“Oh, yeah--” the soldier on the left grinned before he grunted in pain as his companion hit him with the butt of his gun.

“Are you mad?” the other demanded. “Lord Luthor would have our heads for any sanctioned use of the Manhunter.”

Kara’s clueless sweet demeanor vanished and her blue eyes widened. “Lord Luthor… he’s here? I thought he was tending to the wars far north.”

The right soldier rolled his eyes. “Of course, he’s here. He owns half the city after all and no soldier gets promoted here without his approval.”

Well, this certainly changes everything.

“Personally, I can’t wait for him to leave,” the other soldier grumbled. “He’s been so serious and demanding since his father passed. Almost even unhinged if you ask me.”

His companion narrowed his eyes and glared at him. “Unhinged? How exactly would you take it if your father was hunted down, robbed, and then killed by a crew of pirates captained by a woman so evil that hell itself spat her back out?”

“The Girl of Steel?” The other soldier threw his head back and scoffed. “Come on, you can’t tell me you actually believe in that drunkard tale. She’s nothing but a myth.”

“Are you saying a myth killed Lord Lionell Luthor?”

“I’m saying something legendary had to kill him. I don’t think anything from this earth could have killed him or any other Luthor for that matter.”

“Oh, come on,” he shook his head before nodding towards Kara who watched them intently with pursed lips. “Miss, do you believe in the legend of the Girl of Steel? Yes or no?”

Kara gave a tight smile. “Sometimes legends are based on more truth than you think.”

“Typical,” the soldier on the right rolled his eyes. “You can’t trust a woman to ever think logically. They’re passive creatures not meant for such complex thought.”

“That’s why we leave the mentally taxing job of guard duty up to the men,” Kara’s tone dripped with enough salt to be seawater as she spun on her heel. “Well, thank you gentlemen, you’ve been most helpful.”

“Wait! Did you possibly want to talk some more?” the soldier to the left of her asked as he put his hand on her shoulder.

“Sorry, but I have some other pressing matters to attend to,” she replied, twisting out of his grip as gently as possible. The last thing she needed was to cause a scene in a place as miltiterant as National City Port.

“But--”

A loud splash into the water next to them cut him off, snapping all heads to attention. Frowning, Kara rushed to the side of the dock, craning her neck to try to make out just what it was. A beige figure with a dark black tuff pillowing out now bobbed below the waves as it fell deeper into it. It couldn’t have been a person, Kara thought. No one in their right mind would risk a jump into a harbor with this many rocks. It was suicide. 

When she looked up to the top of the cliff to see where it came from, she saw sets of human heads in military caps peering down at her and her heart skipped a beat when she realized this may not have been quite as intentional afterall, at least not on the woman’s part anyways. 

“It’s a damsel,” one solider gasped. “Can you swim?”

“No,” the other shook his head. “Can you?”

Kara never heard his answer as her head was already underwater as she dived down after the young lady who now floated lifelessly down to the dark sea floor. 



Chapter 2

Her maids laced the corset with enough force that robbed Lena from a complete breath. She wanted to hate them but couldn’t. It was her mother who paid their salary, and it was her mother who told them to tighten the corset this much. God forbid Lena didn’t starve herself in grief like her mother. How much grief even was there for a man who spent far more hours at sea than with his family. If only he had simply taken her with him like Lena had so desperately wanted.

But Lena was a woman and according to her mother, a woman’s place was a home, not anywhere near the drunkards or salacious sailors on ships and ports across the sea and along the coast. A woman’s place was in a home, stomach swollen with child and heart full of longing to the man whose seed she carried. It was what Lillian had done; Lena’s life should be no different. Dreams and fantasy were just that - not reality. 

Lena took slow steps downstairs. Nothing too fast as she feared her lung’s support when she took too many steps at once. Afterall, it wasn’t as if a woman needed to take so many complete breaths if their only purpose was to stand and look attractive until they wed and their purpose morphed into child bearing. As she was currently single, Lena had no use for the latter, and all that really mattered was how pleasant of a smile and how tiny of a waist she could present to her suitor and, more importantly, her mother. 

“I just don’t understand why you can’t just shoot the woman.” Lena overheard her mother’s harsh tone carry up the stairs.

“What did you think I did, mother?” Lex spat back. “Bullets and blades don’t have an effect on her. Why do you think she is called the Girl of Steel?”

“Using the legend as an excuse again, Lex?” Lena cocked an eyebrow as she stepped from the stairs to the foyer. “I see you’re already using that Lord title to push the blame of your failures on to others.”

Her brother narrowed her eyes at her as she approached to stand by their mother. “I can assure you that most legends are based in truth, little sister. It just so happens that this one’s invulnerability seems to be credible.”


“Oh pish posh,” Lillian rolled her eyes and waved her hand. “You say that as if you’ve ever seen her. Besides, she’s a pirate. It’s not as if anything about her is credible.”

Her nostrils flared. “Her fleet of mercenaries and criminals are responsible. He was on a mission of diplomacy; of course, he was outnumbered. They rose against him like a pack of wild dogs. He never stood a chance. I’ve heard what their kind does to defensely trade and diplomatic ships. That Silver Banshee ship has set more ships on fire and killed more people than any amount hurricanes have. There isn’t an ounce of chivalry or decency among them.”

“You say that as if you were there,” Lena mumbled, smiling slightly when she saw her brother turn his head in agreement.

But, of course, it wasn’t her brother who received a slap across the cheek.

“How dare you dishonor your father that way,” Lillian hissed before stepping back to grant wider access to the front door. “Now get in the carriage. We don’t want to keep the Commodore waiting.”

Her cheeks and eyes now burning, Lena held her head high as she walked past her mother and ignored the apologetic glance from her brother as she approached the waiting carriage. She knew what she said, and she held no remorse in saying it. Her mother could say all she wanted, but Lena knew the truth. Anyone who paid attention to his reports did.

The carriage rode rough along the cobblestone road that winded them up to the fort positioned at the top of the cliff as an overlook to the sea. Heavy gray rocks covered in moss lined the exterior while the interior maintained a surprisingly clean and fresh look with a combination of stonework and wood flooring and beams. The wide expanse usually full of cannons and other artillery had been cleared and done up so the soldiers and guests could mingle without obstacles. Of course, this unfortunately meant there were less structures Lena could hide behind as her mother pulled her into the crowds.

“Oh my, Lena. Doesn’t the Commodore look handsome in his uniform? His promotion suits him.”

Lena arched a thin brow as she turned her gaze towards her mother, nodding before frowning in distaste.

“I will never understand why the white powder wig has to be part of the uniform,” Lena said before gritting her teeth as her mother rammed her boney elbow into her rib.

“You would do best to hold your tongue, daughter,” Lillian hissed in her ear. “I will not let that mouth of yours ruin the best thing that will ever happen to you.”

Lena’s scowl only deepened. What a sad, meaningless life she lived if marriage to a man was the best thing to ever happen to her. Perhaps that was how her mother’s life had gone. It seemed to be how most women here lived their lives: waiting for marriage, raising the children, maintaining the home. If only she had been born a man and gotten to sail the seas like her father and brother and leave this whole world behind. From the stories they told, Lena could never understand why they ever came back.

“Lex,” Lillian barked. “Watch your sister while I go to tend to one of the serving girls. Someone needs to show her the proper way to open a door.”

Resisting the urge to roll her eyes, Lena attempted to share a frustrated look with her brother who didn’t meet her gaze. Annoyed, Lena inhaled through her nose to sigh but realized too late that her outfit made that impossible. Sweat prickled along her neck as she felt parts of her dress beginning to stick to her arms while the stiff corset held her back stiff and lungs deflated. Opening up her fan, Lena began waving it, flexing her wrist to its full extent as she desperately sought relief from the heat.

“You know, I quite like the wigs,” Lex said, taking a glass of wine from a passing waiter. “They do a great job at keeping my head warm.”

“Says the bald man,” Lena scoffed. “I’m sure you welcome anything that keeps your scalp protected from the sun.”

“Another perk of the wigs!’ Lex smiled humorously before it softened. “Mother’s only trying to look out for you, Lena. You don’t have to fight tooth and nail with her on everything. Be patient with her. Humor her.”

“Says the man who is gone for nine months out of the year,” Lena scoffed, padding her forehead with the back of her hand and finding it clammy.

“Excuse me, Lord Luthor.”

“Commodore Lord,” Lex grinned as he turned to address the approaching man. “What do I owe for this pleasure?”

“I can assure you the pleasure is all mine,” the Commodore said with a slight bow. “And I was hoping I could have a word with your sister if you didn’t mind.”

“Not at all.” Lex took a step back and gestured to him towards Lena. “I have other people I need to speak with so if you would excuse me.”

Lena’s jaw clenched as she watched her brother turn away and disappear into the crowd of other military men. Seeing the hopeful and intent eyes of Commodore Lord, Lena chewed the inside of her cheek as she did her best to work some sort of smile on her face in case her mother was watching.

“May I?” he held out her arm which Lena slowly took.

“Of course,” Lena said, closing her fan in her hand as she let him take the lead.

“You look lovely today, Miss Lena,” he said as he led her up the stairs to a more private section of fort with a short wall, providing full access to the harbor beneath and glistening sea beyond.

“Oh, thank you,” Lena gave a tight smile as she attempted to subtly lean against the wall. Her vision blurred around the edges, but she rapidly blinked that past. No doubt just sweat from the heat. It would pass. She fanned herself faster. 

“I apologize if I seem forward, but I must speak my mind.” Commodore Lord said in a far away voice. Lena blinked and realized that he was actually further away, his eyes thankfully trained on the sea beyond them rather than her undoubtedly dripping red face. 

“This promotion,” he continued. “It throws in sharp relief that which I have not yet achieved. A marriage to a fine woman.”

Oh, of course, now he looks, Lena grumbled to herself as she attempted to keep her shallow breath subtle while she did her best to throw on a flattered smile. Perhaps if she just fanned her face faster, neither he nor her mother would be able to make out any sort of distress in her features. If only the lightness in her head wasn’t making that so difficult.

“You have become a fine woman, Lena.” Commodore Lord offered a sincere smile that Lena suddenly found herself unable to return.

“I can’t breathe,” Lena finally confessed hoarsely, her fan beginning to slip from her fingers.

“Yes,” Commodore Lord let out a laugh before turning his attention back to the ocean. “I am rather nervous myself as well.”

When her knees went weak, Lena leaned her weight against the fort wall for support but unfortunately, her shoulder never found purchase. All she felt was air and a pressure against her legs as she tumbled slowly as if it were a dream. Wind rushed past her ears and her dress billowed around her, and naturally, Lena closed her eyes because that was what one did in dreams. 

As she hit the water below, the shock didn’t wake her. Instead, the cold sank into her bones like a numbness and a warmth soon followed as she drifted further into the depth. No one ever told her how quiet the ocean was. So peaceful. Just her. Perhaps the ocean would be granting her the freedom she craved afterall.

It probably would have if those firm arms didn’t wrap around her waist.

The arms held the strength of an octopus but the warmth of a fire. She lacked the strength to open her eyes, but these arms possessed enough to tear her from her heavy gowns. It wasn’t until those arms pulled her to the surface did Lena realize just how much her chest burned for oxygen and that her blacked out vision wasn’t from the dark ocean water. 

Unfortunately, when their heads finally broke water, Lena’s body lacked the relief she sought. Her lungs still burned as her chest was too weak to move, and her sight remained bleak. Thankfully, her rescuer had more than enough strength to share and managed to swim through the current with her on their shoulder. Lena couldn’t help but wonder if Commodore Lord had enough muscles underneath that white powder wing to do that.

Another set of arms came about on her own as she found herself being pulled out of the water and the hard wood of a dock pressed against her back. Boots thudded along the wood around her as calloused fingers gently pushed her hair off her face.

“Not breathing!”

“Move,” the surprisingly feminie voice ordered sharply.

The dull end of metal pressed against her chest and tore downwards, freeing her lungs to finally expand. Saltwater burned Lena’s throat as she retched it from her lungs and heaved in the glorious oxygen her body burned for as it supplied the strength to blink the salt and sting from her eyes.

“Never would have thought of that,” one of the men in military garb said, In his hands he held her now slashed corset. Lena could only hope that was the only one her mother ordered.

“Clearly you’ve never been to Singapore,” the woman beside him muttered.

Lena’s chest heaved as she slowly pushed herself up onto her elbows, acutely aware of the fact that she was currently wearing only wet undergarments in front of three strangers. When she met the gaze of the two soldiers, they had the decency to act flustered and turn their backs to her, but the blonde woman only held her gaze.

Those blue eyes looking back at her possessed the same strength of the woman’s arms and legs, and Lena found herself powerless to look anywhere but.

“You saved me,” Lena whispered. 

“You needed saving,” the blonde grinned.

“Seize her!”

A thunder of boots descended on to the dock, and Lena suddenly found herself being pulled away from the muscular blonde woman. Immediately, the woman put her hands up, showing no intention of grabbing a weapon, but the soldiers still grabbed hold of arms regardless. With the adrenaline still pumping through her veins, Lena whipped her head around before she spotted both Commodore Lord and her brother rushing down the docks.

“Lena,” Lex gasped as he immediately pulled her wet frame into arms before slipping off his jacket to put on her shaking shoulders. “Thank goodness you’re alright.”

Commodore Lord, however, pulled out his sword and wielded it before the woman. 

“What is your name and business here, woman,” he barked.

“Commodore,” Lena gasped sharply. “Is that really any way to treat my rescuer?”

Commodore Lord narrowed his eyes before shoving his sword back in its sheath.

“I believe thanks are in order,” he said finally, holding out his hand to the sopping wet blonde.

The woman eyed the pale smooth hand shrewdly before reluctantly placing her own hand in it. Not two seconds later, the man’s limp hold became a vice and jerked the woman’s arm forward so he could push aside the sleeve of her shirt, revealing the pink raised skin of a P on her forearm. 

“Had a brush with the East India Trading Company, have we?” Commodore smirked. “Pirate, I presume.”

Closing her eyes, the woman sucked her teeth, but remained silent even though her silence told more than enough.

 Lena failed to hold back a faint gasp. So this was what a pirate looked like; the ones that pillaged, robbed, and made her father and brother’s life so difficult. Pirates were the definition of villainy. However, looking over this woman, Lena struggled to see this woman as anything but a strong hero she would gladly see off on any voyage.

“Hang her,” Lex barked.

Lena stiffened next to him, but her brother only tightened his grip on her.

“Keep your guns on her men,” Commodore Lord ordered. “Someone fetch me some irons.” He pushed her sleeve further up her arm only to see that it was bare. “Do you have a name, pirate?”

“Captain Kara Danvers,” the woman confessed without blinking.

Lena narrowed her eyes. The woman’s voice went high at the end just like her mother when she was lying. This woman was already set to be hanged. What good would it do to lie now?

“Captain?” Commodore Lord scoffed. “Well, I don’t see your ship.”

“I’m in the market,” the so-called Kara Danvers said with a tight smile.

“So you’re not a captain yet, then. Nor will you be.” Commodore Lord took a step back and jerked his head. “Search her.”

The two soldiers holding the woman immediately began patting her down. Lena’s stomach rolled and she gritted her teeth as she saw their hands linger in certain areas that they wouldn't have if the pirate was a man.

“Alright so let’s see,” Commodore Lord said as he picked up the items the soldiers confiscated. “A pistol with no additional shot or powder. A compass that doesn’t point north. And not even a sword.” He shook his head and let out a laugh. “You, Ms. Kara, are without a doubt the worst pirate I’ve ever seen. It’s a wonder how you even got that brand.”

The pirate only smirked. “You’d be amazed at what a little hard work and dedication will achieve.”
