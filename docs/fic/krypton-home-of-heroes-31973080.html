Krypton: Home of Heroes

Krypton is still closed to the world on the morning of opening day. 

 

There's a thrumming sense of excitement and anticipation in the group of people waiting beyond the tall doors. The low buzz of conversation filters through the crowd as they examine the unopened entrance. The tall doors guarding the place are majestic and elaborately carved with scenes depicting the heroes that call this place home. 

 

There's Aquaman battling Ocean Master in the underwater depths, the scales of his armor painted a shimmering green and gold on the wood. There's Wonder Woman soaring over Themiscyra, the star on her tiara signified by a glittering crystal embedded in the wood of the doors, catching the light of National City's morning sun. 

 

The last children of Krypton rise above them all, high in their places of honor. The strong, noble features of Superman intricately and painstakingly carved into the wood as he flies high above the landscape of his home planet. 

 

Beside him is a new long-awaited addition to Krypton.

 

"They added Supergirl!!!"

 

There are eager exclamations from children in the crowd, little hands pointing up to the proud, elegant figure crowning the ornate doors. The smiling face is familiar and beloved from Saturday morning cartoons and comics. Golden hair glinting in the sun, and blue eyes bright and hopeful, the depiction of Supergirl elicits admiration and excitement from fans below.

 

“Wow, we’re gonna see Supergirl!!!”

 

"Oh my gosh, Supergirl’s finally in Krypton!"

 

"How long has it been? Forty years since Krypton opened?"

 

"Nah, Superman was Leopold Luthor's first comic. Supergirl's only been around like 25 years."

 

"She was in the comics and the cartoons, but they never added her to the theme park until now."

 

"I can't believe it took them this long."

 

"I heard they were planning to years ago, but old Leo died before he could put Supergirl in the theme park."

 

"I loved the comic reboot. Crisis really did a number on her character."

 

"She's beautiful..."

 

Somewhere behind the doors, there is a small building, cleverly hidden by one of Krypton's many new attractions. Inside the Department of Effects and Operations, four people are gathered around a large collection of screens, watching the crowd outside the doors.

 

"Feeling nervous yet, sis?"

 

Alex Danvers smirks as her sister jiggles her leg while looking up at the screens, listening to the audio from the crowd. Kara's head whips around to fix Alex with a dirty look. "Shut up, Alex."

 

"Stop moving!" Nia chastises as she tugs Kara's— Supergirl's — cape closer to where she's kneeling on the ground. "Do you want me to stick a needle in you?"

 

It's a testament to Kara's nerves that she doesn't have more of a comeback. She just obediently moves back into position while Nia makes last-minute adjustments to her costume. Through the pins held between her lips, Nia pipes up. "It is a lot of pressure. Supergirl's fanbase is enormous."

 

"Yes, and it is the first time Supergirl is being introduced to the park since Krypton opened forty years ago." Brainy adds helpfully somewhere to Kara's left, clasping his hand behind his back. He observes the growing crowd on the screen serenely. "There is a lot of legacy and history attached to the role. Not to mention Supergirl is Lena's brainchild, so her ambitions are not only hinged on Krypton, but on how the guests receive Supergirl."

 

"Not helping, guys." Kara cringes. It's all well and good for them, they're not going to be out there in public representing Supergirl , her childhood hero. And Lena's too. Supergirl is a hero for so many people, and she doesn't want to let anyone down. So yeah, she knows how much is at stake. Not just for the park, but for Lena.

 

Kara doesn't want to let Lena down.

 

Alex must have noticed that Kara looks a little green around the gills because her smirk turns into a softer smile. She sidles up to Kara's side and puts an arm around Kara's shoulders. "You've worked so hard on this for a whole year, Kara. We all have, but you and Lena have worked the hardest."

 

Alex gives her shoulders a shake. "You can do this. You deserve this, okay?"

 

Kara gives her sister a small, grateful smile and nods. "Okay."

 

"Okay!" Nia swats Alex's arm away so she can fix Kara's cape, and Kara laughs. Nia can be even bossier than Alex when she gets going. "Now that we've had our little moment, can you please let me get back to work on my masterpiece here?"

 

Alex holds up her hands with a wry grin and slowly begins dispersing the other DEO members by barking orders. Kara looks down at the suit that Nia is fixing. It's an amazing suit. Sleek, elegant and yet dynamic and comfortable. Nia’s done a beautiful job on it. 

 

"There. I'm done." Nia straightens up and gives Kara a critical once-over. As she finishes her assessment, Nia nods and gives Kara a smile.

 

"You look..." Nia shakes her head with a low whistle, her eyes wide. "You look like Supergirl."

 

For Kara, there's no higher praise than that. 

 

"Thanks." Kara smiles, her nerves receding as she smooths her hands down the suit. It feels good, familiar. It feels like her childhood days of putting a towel around her shoulders to pretend she was her favorite superhero— and at the same time, she feels the weight and the joy of everything it symbolizes. Everything they've all been through.

 

"It's time to bring her home."

ONE YEAR BEFORE OPENING DAY

 

It's past ten when Kara finally looks up from the model she'd been constructing. The office is darker than when she had started, and everyone else has gone home.

 

She sees a little sticky note on the edge of her desk with Winn's messy scrawl in thick black Sharpie: "GO HOME :)" 

 

He must have passed by her desk to say goodbye without Kara noticing. He's used to that— most everyone here is. The whole DEO knows that whenever Kara Danvers is working on a project, she tends to forget everything else around her. Sometimes she'll look up and flash the others a smile and a wave as they head home, but most of the time when they know she's busy, they'll just goodnaturedly poke her or leave her a note.

 

Alex is constantly teasing her about it. As if she's not the same way. Kara rolls her eyes to herself. You walk into a pole one time , because you were busy thinking of designs for a new ride, and your sister never lets you live it down.

 

She thinks she can be forgiven in this case. It's been a really long time since she's been able to work on anything that's sparked her interest and ignited her passion.

 

The thought and the memories it stirs up cause resentment and anxiety to spike in Kara's gut, and she frowns. It's a familiar feeling, and while it's been bothering her less since she's been back at Krypton, it still makes her feel uncomfortable.

 

It's been a year since Kara was practically tossed out of the DEO, standing outside the glass doors with nothing to show for her years of dedication and hard work but a cardboard box bulging with her desk clutter, and a grudge against Lex Luthor burning like bile in her stomach.

 

Kara's not a person who holds grudges. She likes to think she gets along with most people. She likes them, and they seem to like her too. And she’s always lived by the philosophy that she'd learned as a child from the Krypton comics, and later from all the time she spent at the theme park— there's good in every person, and the world is better when you bring that goodness to the surface and let it shine through.

 

Every person except Lex Luthor.

 

Sometimes Kara thinks she took things too personally, got too emotionally involved. Krypton is a business, and it's just a job, after all. She could have gotten a job somewhere else when she got fired from Krypton. After a decade of working with the DEO, she's certainly qualified enough, and she'd gotten multiple offers.

 

But Krypton is, and always has been, her dream.

 

Reading the comics, watching Supergirl cartoons every Saturday, all the birthdays when Eliza and Jeremiah had taken her and Alex to the theme park. Krypton was her childhood, the place where she always belonged. 

 

Those early years with the Danvers, when she and Alex were sworn enemies rather than sisters, they never fought when they were at Krypton. She and Alex would always race each other to the Superman ride, one of the few things they could agree on as their favorite. 

 

When they were sitting beside each other at the very top of the ride, staring down the 200 feet drop with their hearts racing, scream-laughing in anticipation — Alex wasn't ignoring her or being mean to her. She'd be holding Kara's hand tightly in excitement, while Kara gripped back just as hard.

 

Krypton was where Kara first lost the squirt gun game and failed to get the stuffed Krypto the Superdog she always wanted, and Alex, feeling bad for her, had rolled up her sleeves and shot that squirt gun like a madwoman possessed until she won the stuffed animal for Kara. Kara still thinks that the only reason Eliza hadn't complained about the amount of money she spent on that game was because Alex was doing something nice for Kara and they were finally getting along.

 

Kara can still remember being a kid, and talking to the people who worked at Krypton— from the sweet old man at the popcorn stand who would hand her a bucket of her favorite blueberry-flavored popcorn, to the operator at the Themiscyra ride. From her first visit, the inner workings of the park had fascinated her, and the magic of Krypton had captivated her.

 

It hasn't let her go since. Not through her teenage years, when it was no longer cool to be so into superhero comics, Not even through all the drama of art school. When she'd started as an intern at Krypton's Department of Effects and Operations right out of college, it had been a dream come true. She's been a part of the DEO family ever since. 

 

Krypton is her home, and her love for this seemingly limitless universe has always been part of her.

 

That's why being forced out had hurt so much.

 

Kara never liked Lex Luthor, but she'd tried to respect him at first. He was her boss, after all. Sure, he never shared the creative vision that his grandfather had. He didn't hold the same love for Krypton that Kara did. No, he wasn't interested in the spirit of innovation and creative freedom that J'onn encouraged, or the drive for artistic excellence that Cat constantly roused out of them. 

 

But Lex was still the owner of Krypton, and he was a businessman who lived what must be a demanding and high-stress life. Kara could understand that. Could even work around it. She and the rest of her team at the DEO had worked around the budget he'd slashed down drastically. When he'd trimmed her team, Kara had grit her teeth, but she had adapted to it. When he shot down every idea she'd pitch at meetings because he claimed there was no money, she held her tongue with great difficulty.

 

Working with Lex had meant working in a constant state of anxiety and repression. Every creative idea was ruthlessly squashed, funds were drained. No new projects were allowed, and there was a constant push to commercialize on what they currently had rather than create something new. Everything seemed to be about merchandising, about how they could milk every existing thing on Krypton for money. 

 

Under him, Krypton had stopped being about inspiring people, and became more about making profits.

 

That wasn't even the worst of it.

 

Every few months, there would be massive layoffs. When Kara had started at the DEO a decade ago, there were at least five hundred people working in the building alone, and Krypton had at least ten thousand employees. But when she had left, the DEO had been trimmed down to sixty people, and the park was constantly short-staffed.

 

The worst was around the holidays. Kara still fumes whenever she remembers how Lex would deliberately fire people just before Thanksgiving, and people were left without jobs. She remembers Antonio, who used to work as an operator for the Rondor beasts, crying because he wouldn't be able to give his kids a Christmas that year. She remembers the old lady who used to make the delicate El-shaped chocolate treats worrying about her medical bills that always piled up at that time of the year.

 

There was also the constant threat that Krypton would close. Krypton had always been open, always welcomed everyone who passed through its doors. Kara still remembers old M'yrnn J’onnz telling her that in the forty years since Krypton had opened, it had never closed, not even for a single day. Not even on the day that Leopold Luthor, its founder, died. But with Lex, his own grandson, at the helm, the threat of Krypton going bankrupt and closing was very real.

 

Working under Lex meant you constantly had a noose hanging over your head, and you spent half your time worrying about whether you would be the next to go, and the other half scrambling to keep your job.

 

As much as she'd tried to hold her tongue, that just wasn't something Kara could stand for, so she'd spoken out. The next thing she knew, she was banished from Krypton and out of a job. 

 

Maybe she should have taken the job offer in Metropolis, or moved back to Midvale. But Kara just didn’t have the heart to leave. She was too attached to Krypton. 

 

So when the scandal around Lex had exploded a year later, and news of his schemes had all but broken the internet, Kara had still been around to watch it all unfold.

 

She's not gonna lie and say she was above feeling a sense of triumph as she watched him on trial on her screen. Hearing him pronounced guilty of fraud and embezzlement brought no small sense of relief and satisfaction. She and Alex had celebrated with two whole pizzas and a bottle of wine.

 

When J'onn had offered her her old job back, there really wasn't any other answer Kara could give. 

 

And now she’s here. For better or for worse, she's back in Krypton. With a new boss. 

 

It probably doesn't bode well that the new head of the company is yet another Luthor, albeit one who's much nicer to look at than the bald asshole who fired her, but a Luthor nonetheless.

 

Kara has to admit, she had some reservations about Lena Luthor.

 

The first time she saw Lena walk into the DEO meeting room, it had felt like all the air had been sucked out of the room. Every other motion stopped, and every head turned to this newcomer. Not because she was striking— though she admittedly was. Elegant and imposing in her severe outfit and sharp heels, Lena had cut an impressive silhouette and immediately drew the eye.

 

But the attention she had received in the room was in no way positive. 

 

Still stung from the wounds Lex's time had inflicted on them, and bristling with defensive suspicion of yet another Luthor having power over them, the entire DEO regarded Lena Luthor's presence among them as a trap. The whole of the DEO was, and still is, wire-taut with tension from the lingering fear that they would be laid off or closed down. And it was barely-concealed (and sometimes bare-faced) hostility, instead of respect or admiration that greeted Lena that first day.

 

Kara's not going to lie and say that she was overly welcoming of Lena either. 

 

It's not that she doesn't like her. 

 

Kara knows her fandom history. She knows Lena Luthor was the one who had created and inspired Supergirl at the tender age of six. There’s stories of it online— articles and interviews from when Supergirl first debuted, and old Leopold Luthor had proudly proclaimed that Supergirl was created not by him, but by his little granddaughter. Even the titles on the Supergirl cartoons credit Lena as the creator of the series. There’s even theories that some of the illustrations of young Supergirl were actually based on Lena as a little girl.

 

That alone would’ve been enough to make Kara geek out, since Supergirl has always been her favorite superhero. She has every issue of the original Supergirl comics that ran up until Leopold’s death, and then she’d collected every issue of the reboot illustrated by Jack Spheer. She’d watched every episode of the Supergirl cartoons as a kid.

 

But Kara’s an adult now, whatever her collection of Supergirl models (they’re not “toys” if they’re for work, Alex) might indicate. And her opinions are based on observation from professional interactions.

 

Lena herself was magnanimous enough when she first arrived, quick to assure everyone that her mission is to undo her brother's work. And so far, Lena is committed enough to Krypton that Kara sees her around every day, even though she probably has a million other things to do and who knows how many other Luthor Corp subsidiaries to oversee. 

 

Sometimes Kara will see Lena talking to a few DEO engineers, clearly still fresh from a meeting with investors, rolling up the sleeves of her delicate sapphire-blue silk blouse to reach into a panel crammed with wires and circuit boards, just to show the other engineers how they can fix a specific problem. Sometimes, Kara will see her talking to J’onn, who is pretty much the only member of the DEO who treats Lena like she’s a friendly presence.

 

Besides, Lena is... magnetic. Yes, that's the best way to describe her. Despite the wide berth most of the DEO people have been giving her, Lena has a presence that draws people to her, and she immediately takes command of a room. When she speaks, people, though begrudgingly, begin to listen.

 

But Kara’s been down this road with a Luthor before, and that didn’t end well. And she’d like to keep her job this time. So she’s kept her distance. Once bitten, twice shy, and all that.

 

Kara lays down her paintbrush and sits up, her body only now realizing how long it's been in a cramped position. It's been good to work on a project again, to have something that can occupy her thoughts so much that she gets in the zone. 

 

Although, her aching muscles aren't as happy. And neither is her sister. She's gotten six missed calls and ten messages from Alex telling her to go home, all ranging from playful to threatening physical violence. Kara chuckles and packs up all her things. This can wait until tomorrow.

 

She leaves a short note with a smiley on it for the night cleaning crew, and turns off the lights. As she navigates the dim hallways, Kara notices the lights on in the conference room. They’re not usually on, so she decides to make a quick detour on her way out to turn the lights off. 

 

As she approaches the door, she stops short.

 

The conference room isn’t empty. There’s someone standing at the head of the table, surrounded by blueprints and concept designs, looking down at the small-scale model of Krypton. 

 

It takes Kara a minute to realize that it’s none other than Lena Luthor herself.

 

Lena’s hands are flat on the table, and her shoulders are not squared in refined Luthor arrogance, but hunched over as she stares at the miniature landscape of Krypton. Something about the way she looks at the model, her gaze intense but unfocused, tells Kara that she's looking at it without seeing it at all. 

 

Kara bites her lip, silently debating whether she should make her presence known. She and Lena Luthor have never spoken more than two words outside of a professional conversation before, and truth be told, she’s a little intimidated by this powerhouse of a woman. 

 

There’s also the fact that her brother was the selfish, unimaginative, egotistical megalomaniac who screwed over so many people, practically ran Krypton to the ground, and oh yeah, kicked Kara off the DEO for daring to disagree with him.

 

But seeing Lena now, lit by the dim glow of a desk lamp, her shoulders bowed and her brow furrowed, she looks less like a Luthor and more like a human.

 

"Working overtime?" 

 

Lena’s voice floats toward Kara, cool and low in the semi-darkness. She must have known Kara was there the entire time. Kara startles, blushing as Lena tips her head toward her. Kara adjusts her glasses awkwardly, and Lena acknowledges her with a small wan smile before turning back to the model of Krypton. "I don't want the union at my throat because I'm overworking you like my father overworked his employees." 

 

There's a note of bitterness in her otherwise light tone, and Kara elects not to laugh at the weak joke. She doesn't think Lena's looking for an audience anyway. 

 

Lena gestures at the model of Krypton with a careless sweep of her elegant hand. "Look at this park, Kara. Tell me what you see." 

 

Kara follows her gaze, unsure of what to say. "I'm sorry?"

 

"This park. What do you see when you look at it?" Lena rolls her hand carelessly. 

 

Kara doesn’t know what answer Lena is looking for, so she turns to look at the other woman instead. In the dim half-light, Lena’s eyes look tired. Underneath her makeup, an artist's eye can see the lines and sunken shapes of fatigue and sadness. 

 

"I'll tell you what I see, Kara... I see my grandfather's vision being eroded by my family. I see his dream peeling away little by little like paint off a wall. I see a paltry attempt at restoring that dream that will inevitably fail, because it's being built patch by patch by an impostor who will never have the artistic vision that he had."

 

There’s a wealth of bitterness and self-loathing apparent in every note of Lena's voice, and Kara wonders if she has ever said these words out loud before. If they weren't two people alone shrouded in semi-darkness looking Lena's vulnerability straight in the eye, would she have even said it out loud?

 

"My grandfather always believed in me, you know… Even when the rest of my family didn't.” Lena whispers quietly, and something inside Kara trips at the heart-hurt softness of her voice. “He always encouraged me to build things, to create things. I remember telling him once when I was fourteen 'I'm not an artist like you. I'm an engineer'. .. And I remember so clearly what he said to me: 'I'll tell you what you are: you're a dreamer. Whether you're an artist, or an engineer, or a mechanic like I was, if you have a dream and the guts to make it happen, you can do anything, Lena' ." 

 

Lena scoffs, her hands balling into fists on the table. "I used to think he was right. I know better now."

 

In the face of such blatant self-loathing, Kara can’t help but feel the urge to say something back. Words of comfort push against the back of her teeth, trying to get out, but something stops her. Lena shakes her head, and Kara can see her green eyes dim as her listless gaze sweeps over the model. 

 

Lena picks up the miniature Supergirl from the middle of the model, running her fingers over the tiny figure. She shakes her head bitterly. “At this point, it might not even be worth investing all this time and money and effort into rebuilding the park. We might as well forget it."

 

Her words trail off with a heavy sigh, and Kara studies her. If she could trace the lines and bows of her, all that would show would be defeat. 

 

Lena Luthor looks utterly defeated. 

 

It's in this moment that Kara regrets ever thinking that Lena is anything like her brother. How could she have ever thought that? Seeing Lena's heartbreak, it's clear to see that this woman is as far from her brother as one could possibly get.

 

"Can I tell you what I see?" Kara comes up beside her, and Lena looks up at her with tired, red-rimmed eyes. Kara reaches out to take the Supergirl figure from Lena’s hand, and she studies it like Lena did.

 

"When I look at this park, I see my childhood.” Kara smiles as she looks at Supergirl’s hopeful face, her proud and noble posture. When she looks up at Lena, there’s still a trace of that smile left.

 

“I see myself waking up on Saturday mornings so excited to watch my favorite cartoons. I see myself as a kid buying the latest Supergirl comic and copying the drawings so I could learn to draw. I see myself coming to the park for the first time and being in absolute awe that I could live in the world that only existed on screen and on the page, even if it was just for a few hours. Me, the scrawny nerd from Midvale that everyone made fun of.” Kara chuckles a little at the memory, and out of the corner of her eye, she sees the smallest smile quirk Lena’s lips.

 

“When I was in Krypton, I could be Supergirl and still be me , Kara Danvers. I felt like a superhero, like I could do anything. And I see myself dreaming that one day I could truly be a part of it… I see myself applying to art school, just so I could work here and make the same kind of magic for others that your grandfather created for me and countless other people like me."

 

Kara meets Lena's eyes, and sees that the green gaze is glassy with the sheen of emotion. Very gently, Kara takes Lena’s hand and presses the little Supergirl into her palm. 

 

"I think your grandfather was right. All of us here at the DEO, we may be a bunch of misfits, but we're all dreamers. You may not be an artist like your grandfather, but I think you are a dreamer. I may not be an engineer, but I'm a dreamer. Maybe all of us can work together and restore your grandfather's legacy. I mean, what was it Supergirl always used to say? Stronger together ."

 

Lena contemplates her for a long moment, then she huffs a small laugh. "I was the one who thought of that line when I was six." 

 

Kara gives a little squeak and her eyes bug out. "Okay, you cannot just say things like that! You're gonna make me geek out." 

 

Lena laughs, full and genuine this time, and Kara grins at her over the dim light. The rich, delighted sound trails off into a softer smile, and Kara nudges her companionably with her shoulder.

 

"You're not an impostor, Lena. You belong here in the DEO. You belong here on Krypton. We can do this. Stronger together." 

 

In the dim light, Lena's eyes soften, but there's a spark of light there that had been missing earlier. She nods. 

 

"Stronger together."

6 MONTHS BEFORE OPENING DAY

 

Krypton keeps them so busy, Kara nearly forgets her own birthday. 

 

She spends so much of her time in the DEO, moving from one project to another, the days kind of blur together. Her days have mostly become brainstorming with Lena, then talking it out with the other members of their DEO team. It’s an invigorating process of design and planning, followed by construction and testing. Then failing. Then going back again to fix things. Over and over until they get it right.

 

It’s a frustrating task, but at the same time, it’s refreshing. For the first time in a long time, she and the rest of the DEO are finally allowed to flex their creative muscles. It’s like being able to stretch again after spending so long being restrained and stifled.

 

And when they do get things right, the feeling of accomplishment is indescribable. 

 

Like the first time they test the Supergirl ride. 

 

The graphics for the ride, showing Supergirl returning to Krypton after fighting her enemies, are still rudimentary. Kara is still working on polishing it, with Winn’s help. But the emotion they evoke is powerful enough. 

 

Kara watches the scene of Supergirl bursting through the pale crimson clouds of Krypton’s red skies with a deep, unknown feeling rising in her chest. Tears form in her eyes as she watches Supergirl’s graceful flight bring her closer to the warm, welcoming glow of Rao. Kara’s tears begin to flow freely when she hears the hero’s joyous laughter swelling until it rings across the vast expanse of scarlet skies. Supergirl spreads her arms as she looks down at Krypton, as if to embrace the planet. 

 

As if to say she’s come home.

 

When the flight simulation ends, Kara leans back on her seat. She’s trembling, she realizes. She feels breathless, choking on an emotion that feels more real and more overwhelming than anything she’s ever felt.

 

She hears a few sobs and sniffles around the now-dark chamber as the music fades and the lights come back up. On her right side, Alex is swiping at her eyes with her forearm. A few seats over, Nia is openly weeping while Brainy, whose own eyes are suspiciously glassy, hands her a tissue.

 

On the seat beside her, Lena is taking off her 3D glasses slowly to reveal tear tracks on her cheeks. She doesn’t wipe them away. Instead, she stares unseeingly at the blank canvas where Supergirl’s image had been. She doesn’t say anything, just stares silently.

 

Kara reaches out, taking one of Lena’s limp hands in hers. The moment their fingers touch, Lena looks up, and Kara sees the same raw emotion she feels reflected in the clear green of Lena’s eyes.

 

Indescribable.

 

And so with everything that’s been going on, it’s really no surprise Kara didn’t remember her own birthday.

 

In fact, she only remembers it when she walks into the conference room for their usual meeting, and the deafening yell of “ SURPRISE! ” nearly makes her jump out of her skin.

 

“Happy birthday!”

 

Once she gets over the fight-or-flight response, Kara’s eyes light up as she sees nearly every member of the DEO, and some of the employees from Krypton, all packed under a sparkly banner. J’onn and Winn are holding up a huge cake with the Supergirl crest made of red, yellow and blue icing.

 

“Oh my gosh, you guys!”

 

Alex steps forward with a huge grin. “You didn’t think we’d forget about your birthday, did you?”

 

“Honestly, I forgot about it myself.” Kara laughs as she hugs her sister.

 

“I figured.” Alex snorts as she releases Kara and playfully pushes her closer to her cake. “You’re becoming as much of a workaholic as Lena is.”

 

“Where is Lena?” Kara looks around, hoping to spot the brunette in the crowd. When she doesn’t see her, Kara feels almost disappointed.

 

“She’s running late. Something about finding you the perfect gift.” Alex rolls her eyes. Kara tries not to let her disappointment show as J’onn and Winn bring over the cake. Everyone cheers as she blows out the candles flanking the El sigil.

 

Lena doesn’t arrive until after the cake is cut, and the others are already pouring drinks. Kara brightens immediately, holding her arms out to her friend. “Lena! You’re here!”

 

Lena sets down the paper bag she’d been carrying to embrace Kara, sinking warmly into their hug. “I wouldn’t miss your birthday.”

 

Kara’s arms tighten around her friend, and she sighs happily as her cheek presses into Lena’s soft hair. Lena’s hands graze her shoulder blades, fingers pressing against Kara’s back. “Thank you for coming.”

 

Somewhere behind them, Alex clears her throat. Lena pulls away slowly, a soft smile on her face.

 

“Of course.” Lena bites her lower lip. She always does that when she’s nervous, Kara’s noticed. “Will you come with me to my office for a second?”

 

At her quick, reflexive nod, Kara hears Alex snort into her beer and Nia giggle behind her cake slice. She sends them a look before following Lena into her office.

 

Once they’re alone, Kara immediately notices that there’s something almost… twitchy about Lena today. She’s fidgeting with her hands and worrying the handles of the paper bag she’s holding. Kara gently leads her to the couch where both of them have crashed more than once after 14 hours of going over ride designs.

 

Lena chews her lower lip, and before Kara can ask her why she seems so nervous, a box wrapped in pretty gold foil is thrust into her hands. Lena blurts out, "It's for you."

 

Kara looks up at her, eyes wide, and she lets out an involuntary squeal of delight. "Lena, you didn't have to!" 

 

But she's already excitedly shaking the box to hear what's inside. Lena laughs, some of the nervousness easing out of her as she waits patiently for Kara to untie the bow and lift the lid. As she peeks at the contents of the box, Kara's brows furrow slightly in confusion. 

 

It's... red fabric? Kara looks up at Lena, her forehead crinkling a little in confusion. "Are you giving me swatches from costume design?"

 

"Take it out." Lena insists, two bright spots of pink appearing in her cheeks. She looks half-nervous, half-excited. This is about as eager and uncomposed as Kara has seen her, and it makes her smile. It's adorable. Lena's adorable. "Let's see it on you."

 

Kara complies, and as she lifts the garment out of the box, her eyes widen. "... Is this—?"

 

Lena wrings her hands, another nervous habit, Kara has noticed. "It's Supergirl's cape. I wanted you to have it. And..."

 

Lena takes the smooth fabric from Kara's hands and runs her fingers over it. Then she unfurls it, reaching over to drape it over Kara. Her hands smooth the material over Kara's shoulders, and Kara feels her mouth go dry. There's a soft look on Lena's face and a tiny smile plays around her lips as she watches Kara. 

 

"And I also - I wanted to ask you if you would be Supergirl for opening day." Lena's hands drift away from Kara's shoulders and she begins to wring her fingers again.

 

Kara stares at her with wide eyes.

 

"You wouldn't have to do a lot." Lena hastens to reassure her, as if she's already sure Kara will say no. "It would probably only be for an hour or so, just to open the ride with me. It's fitting, don't you think? To have Supergirl there to introduce the ride? And you'll probably just have to say a few things from a script, but not a lot." 

 

Lena's babbling. Kara's never heard the eloquent and precise Lena Luthor babble before. But it's like the other woman can't seem to stop the stream of words from her mouth. It seems Kara's rubbed off on her, and it makes Kara chuckle a little to herself. 

 

"I just..." Lena's voice becomes gentle, almost tender, and she regards Kara with eyes that are so... soft . Kara opens her mouth to speak, but no sound comes out. Everything she wants to say gets caught in the lump that’s starting to form in her throat.

 

Lena bites her lip again and averts her gaze. Instead of meeting Kara's eyes, she turns her attention to the cape on Kara's shoulders. She strokes her fingertips on the edge of the cape, and as she does, the back of her hand brushes Kara's arm softly. Kara swallows.

 

"You told me once that when you first came to Krypton, it gave you the chance to immerse yourself in the world you loved." Lena keeps her gaze studiously on the cape, but Kara watches her intently. "You told me that being in Krypton made you feel that you could be both Kara Danvers and Supergirl. And I... I just wanted to give that to you again. You've been such an important part of this story— our story."

 

Finally, her eyes lift up to meet Kara's, and the green of them is tender yet clear. "I may have thought up Supergirl, but you've made Supergirl who she is now, Kara. I owe that to you, and I just wanted to give you a piece of it back, and… and..." Lena's eyebrows wrinkle nervously. "... please say something."

 

Kara looks away from Lena for a moment, her gaze travelling to where Lena's fingers are wrapped around the edge of the cape. Her grip is careful and light, but the tendons of her hand are taut, like she's holding back, and there's the slightest tremble at her fingertips. She's nervous. Lena's nervous.

 

Kara reaches out to calm those skittish fingers first. She smooths over tense knuckles first, carefully easing the tension in the anxious hand before she tangles their fingers together. Once Lena's hand is securely held in her own, she raises her eyes to meet Lena's.

 

"Thank you."

 

She pulls Lena to her, holding her tight and pressing her close. Lena's arms come up gently to her back, pressing light and careful, so careful. Kara presses her face to Lena's soft hair. 

 

"Yes, I’ll be your Supergirl."

 

With Lena in her arms, it takes her a moment to realize what she’s said. When she does, Kara chokes, all but tripping on her words. “I- I mean, I’ll be Supergirl for you.”

 

“I know what you meant.” Lena laughs, her voice fond and teasing. Kara’s stuttering seems to ease Lena’s tension, and the look on her face is amused but soft, so soft, when she pulls away.

 

“Thank you, Kara. This means more to me than you know.”

OPENING DAY

 

Krypton at night is even more spectacular than Kara could have dreamed. And she has dreamed it, every minuscule detail from start to finish of this whole incredible year. Tonight, every single dream is coming spectacularly to life.

 

One of the zipline operators helps her into the harness, securing all of the fastenings and testing everything one last time. There’s still some vestiges of nerves in Kara, which Alex has assured her is normal. Behind her, she can hear the staticky voice on the other end of the transceiver say that across the park, Lena has taken the stage.

 

That’s Kara’s cue. Once she steps onto the perch high above Krypton, all of the nervousness falls away, to be replaced by a sense of pride and joy that fills Kara’s entire being. 

 

This is it. The culmination of everything they’ve worked for.

 

One deep breath, and Kara lets herself momentarily forget all about the mechanics of this controlled flight. 

 

She steps off the ledge and lets go.

 

Kara has done this a hundred times before, but it’s an exhilarating feeling everytime. The wind rushes around her as she flies over the park, air displaced by a body that wasn’t there before, stirred and working around her, with her. There’s an immense sense of freedom in it, as if she’s left everything behind her the moment she let herself leave the ground. 

 

She feels light, like she can do anything.

 

The mechanism keeps her on the track, and Kara knows that she’s not really flying. It’s basically a step above a zipline. But that’s easy to forget when she’s gliding a hundred feet above Krypton

 

It takes her breath away, looking down at everything they’ve built. She feels the salty spray from the Great Kryptonian Sea on her face, and she forgets that it’s controlled by a switchboard in the control room underneath. 

 

She sees the lofty spires of Kandor, and she forgets that the glittering golden lights in each tower are not people living in each building, but are switches scheduled to go on at a specific time at night. 

 

She crosses the Rainbow Canyon, the giant arch painting her in iridescent colors, and it’s easy to believe that she’s flying under a real rainbow, not some colored lights projected up at the sky.

 

She marvels at the Scarlet Jungle glowing in the dark, its trees bathed in a soft crimson light. It’s easy to forget she and Lena had spent months agonizing over the colors just so a red forest would look natural in real life. And now here it is, the dense thicket radiant in the night time.

 

Kara can hear people exclaim as they look up at her. In the familiar red cape, she’s easy to spot, even in the night sky. She waves at them with a bright smile as she passes by, and their cheers drown out the motorized whir of the zipline. They only grow louder as she soars past them.

 

She spots the crystalline face of the Jewel Mountains, and she angles her body forward, the harness giving her enough slack to move through the air while keeping her supported. She and Brainy must have worked through this a thousand times, trying to find the most aerodynamic body positions that would match Supergirl’s style.

 

Below her, Kara can see the crowd at the foot of the ride. Lena stands on-stage with her head held high, a resplendent smile adorning her face.The eager white dog they’d gotten to play Krypto is sitting proudly beside her, nuzzling her hand, his adorable little cape tied around his collar.

 

“Many years ago, in his comics, my grandfather Leopold Luthor, created a world where anything is possible— where myths come to life, and people are capable of great things. He created a universe where people can fly, can move mountains and run faster than the speed of light— superheroes who could save the world.”

 

Kara can hear the pride and love suffusing every word of Lena’s speech, and the wave of affection she feels for the woman at the podium nearly overcomes her. Lena delivers her speech with deep feeling, her rich voice stilting just slightly out of emotion. 

 

“Forty years ago, my grandfather lifted this world straight from his inked illustrations and brought it to life. He built Krypton for everyone, to give people a place where anything can happen, even if it’s just for a few hours. He made a place that inspired people to be the best they can be, to be their own heroes. He wanted to spread this inspiration, this hope, to everyone. Including me.”

 

Lena smiles, soft and fond. “When I was six years old, I imagined a hero for myself— a hero who stood for hope, help and compassion for all. Her name is Supergirl, and I think you all know her.”

 

A wave of cheers ripples across the crowd, and Lena's smile is so bright, it could light up the night. Krypto barks at the audience, causing another wave of cheers, this time littered with coos. Lena bends down to scratch behind his ears. “No, we haven’t forgotten you, Krypto.”

 

Lena looks back at the audience, her eyes shining. “My grandfather brought Supergirl to the comics and the cartoons, and he always wanted to make her a part of this park, of Krypton. Sadly, he passed away before that dream could become a reality. Today, in honor of the man who gave us this beautiful world, we’re going to bring his dream and mine to life.”

 

And that’s Kara’s cue. She reorients her body for landing, just as the zipline slows and gradually lowers her. Right on time, she glides within view of the mountain, and she can hear whispers growing louder in the crowd. 

 

“What is that? Is that a bird?”

 

“No, it looks like a drone. Or some kind of plane.”

 

Somewhere in the audience, a child gasps in excitement and exclaims “It’s Supergirl!”

 

Kara grins as she touches down smoothly, her boots making contact softly on her mark on the platform, a far cry from the first time she and Winn had tried this when the harness had jerked her and left a mark like a seatbelt rash on her waist. A roar of excitement rises from the audience, but Kara really only has eyes for one person.

 

Lena turns the moment she arrives on-stage, and the smile she gives Kara is blinding.

 

If her flight over Krypton had taken her breath away, it’s nothing compared to Lena’s effect on her. Lena is radiant, her face suffused with pride and so much… love . Kara knows it’s because she’s finally seeing the childhood hero she had created come to life in front of her eyes, but she can’t help it. The vibrant emotion sparkling in those green eyes makes Kara feel weak, but at the same time, it makes her feel like she can do anything. Like she can fly, for real.

 

It makes her feel honored that she can give this to Lena, that Kara can make her dream come true.

 

“Welcome home, Supergirl.”

 

The words make a lump form in Kara’s throat, and it may just be that she’s inhabiting her character a little too much, but the words mean so much to her. After a year of hard work and late nights, of dedicating all her time and energy to this place, Krypton is home. For Kara, and for Lena.

 

“Thank you, Miss Luthor.” Kara smiles at her, wishing she could take Lena’s hand to show her how much she means those words. She’s reciting from a script, but every word feels significant to her, and she wants Lena to know that. Instead, she puts her hands on her hips and looks out at the sea of excited faces in front of them.

 

“When I was a child, my planet Krypton was dying. But today, Miss Luthor has lovingly brought my world back to life. Now Krypton can once again be a place that brings joy and inspiration to everyone who comes here... To all who step into this world of hope and wonder— Welcome. Krypton is your home too.” She casts one more bright smile at the audience, and a cheer erupts among them. 

 

But Kara hardly hears the roar of excited voices as she turns to Lena and holds her arms out. It’s not part of the script, and Lena looks a little surprised, but she leans into Kara warmly just like she does whenever they hug. As her arms settle around Lena, Kara clasps the harness in place.

 

She’s not going to lie and say she’s not nervous.

 

It’s not the rig Kara’s anxious about. She's run through this a hundred times with Winn. She's done a hundred trials, taking turns carrying pretty much every member of the DEO through the zipline. Except for Brainy who had refused, and J'onn. Kara's strong, she can deadlift 225lbs but even she can't carry J'onn a hundred feet in the air for a full minute.

 

Every DEO member had been in on it. When they’d found out it was a surprise for Lena, they had enthusiastically volunteered to help Kara test it out. Alex had been as stiff as a board the first time Kara lifted her, but by the fourth time, she was raring to replace Kara as Supergirl. Winn had been twitchy and scared at first, and he'd screamed a little on Kara's first lift-off. Nia had been harder than she expected. She's smaller than Kara, but she'd been squirmy, all flailing limbs and whooping fist pumps as they soared over Krypton. Her enthusiasm had been infectious, though, and once Kara got a firm grip around her and she was sure the harness would hold them both securely, she was whooping along just as loudly.

 

Kara has every confidence that the suspension rig will work. She would never risk Lena's safety on something she's not sure of.

 

And yet, it's all Kara can do not to let her voice shake when she turns her head slightly so she can whisper in Lena’s ear. “Do you trust me?”

 

“What—?” Lena pulls away a little, enough for Kara to secure the front buckle of the harness, nearly invisible against the black of her dress. 

 

Lena looks up at her, not comprehending for a second, and Kara uses it to her advantage. She smoothly tugs the belt - and Lena - forward, so she can clip the harness now around Lena’s waist to the one just hidden by her own suit. 

 

Slowly, realization dawns on Lena, and Kara grins at the way those crystal-green eyes widen.

 

“Kara…”

 

There’s a note of fear in the warning way Lena says her name, and the apprehensive stare she gives the harness between them makes Kara relent a little, giving the line between them some slack. Her own nervousness increases tenfold. She doesn’t want Lena to feel forced into doing this.

 

Kara knows what she's asking from Lena. Relinquishing control is the hardest thing for her— her greatest fear. Allowing herself to place her trust in Kara enough to give up control and allow herself to be cared for. To be held instead of holding the world like she does.

 

But Lena is still and soft in her arms, the crowd is cheering for them both, and Kara wants to do this for her. Lena deserves this. 

 

Kara tips her head a little so she can meet Lena’s eyes, and she hopes Lena can read all the warmth and affection she feels for her in her gaze. “You don’t have to if you don’t want to.”

 

Lena looks up at her, glass-green eyes searching hers. All the deep feeling in her gaze is at once familiar and unknown to Kara, and it makes her heart clench dangerously. Lena's voice is little more than a whisper when she speaks, and only Kara can hear it over the din of the waiting crowd. “I want to.”

 

Kara’s mouth goes dry at the clarity in Lena’s eyes. Her arms tighten around Lena’s waist. “Trust me?”

 

Lena’s eyes soften, and she nods. Her hands trail over Kara’s arms and her fingers catching slightly on the material of the suit before pressing firmly. 

 

“I do.”

 

It’s all the permission Kara needs. A cheer erupts when Kara lifts Lena, and the moment she sweeps Lena into her arms, something inside Kara settles. Lena’s arms wind around her neck, her grip nervous, and Kara hooks her arms securely around her, keeping her close.

 

Somewhere behind them, one of the other DEO members gets ready to swap out the real Krypto for an animatronic hooked to the same rig. And this is Kara’s cue to distract the crowd. She turns to them with the brightest smile she can manage. 

 

“Come fly with us!”

 

The audience goes wild, especially when Kara whistles through her teeth, and the animatronic dog is hoisted up on the zipline. “Up, up and away, Krypto!”

 

And then it’s their turn. Lena’s grip on her is tight, and her face is tucked into Kara’s neck. Kara bows her head over Lena’s and she murmurs reassurances in her ear. “I’ve got you.”

 

Kara hears Lena’s intake of breath as she launches off the platform, and her arms tighten around the other woman. 

 

If she’d thought flying by herself was amazing, flying with Lena is indescribable. There are simply no words for it.

 

Fireworks erupt above them as they soar into the air, far enough away that the explosions won’t harm them, but close enough that they paint the entire sky around them in spectacular colors. Each spark dazzles like a meteor streaking across the night sky, dancing in graceful arcs and imploding into millions of little embers around them. Kara leans over Lena, her lips just barely skimming the shell of Lena’s ear. 

 

“Open your eyes.”

 

Lena complies, and when she does, the sparkling light reflects in her eyes— reds and golds and silvers all shining in that clear gaze. Kara feels more than hears her intake of breath as she looks up at Kara then at the spectacle behind her. Her eyes go wide as she takes it all in, the fireworks like stars exploding all around them.

 

All the fear seems to leave Lena at the sight, to be replaced by wonder etched into every line of face. Lena looks down at the park— this world she’d breathed life into, that they’d both created through their hard work and dedication. When she sees the wonder and the pure emotion in Lena’s face, Kara knows her surprise was worth it. 

 

After everything she has done for Krypton, Lena deserves to see how amazing it is.

 

Lena’s enamored gaze moves from Krypton to Kara, and this contact leaves Kara breathless. She desperately wishes she could paint Lena right then, that she could capture the way Lena’s eyes shine in the myriad of colors from the lights, the way her lips are parted in something like awe at the spectacle around them. 

 

But at the same time, Kara wants this to belong only to this moment. They have worked so hard for this, for Krypton. All the sleepless nights, each painstaking decision, every sacrifice, every little bit of themselves they’ve poured into making this place special. 

 

It feels as if every moment has led to this. The two of them, flying together, the night sky brilliant and explosive around them, Krypton glowing and radiant below.

 

It feels… significant, to an unknowable degree that Kara can’t quantify or articulate. She doesn’t even want to. To give it a name feels almost sacrilegious. 

 

Instead, she lets the moment settle between the two of them, a thread of stillness in the midst of the tumult around them. The rest of the world falls away below them, and there’s no one else around. Just the two of them flying over Krypton. The peaceful sense of freedom that Kara has come to associate with flying like this envelops her once again, but this time, Lena is wrapped with her in that same cocoon. They’re sharing it together, and Kara finds there’s no better feeling than this.

 

The hand that had been tightly gripping Kara’s shoulders eases slowly, drifting carefully to settle at the side of Kara’s neck. Lena’s thumb soothes circles on the curve of Kara’s jaw, and Kara wonders vaguely if the other woman can feel the pulse thudding wildly at her throat. Under the dazzling light from the fireworks, Kara sees the soft smile blooming, vibrant and delicate, on Lena’s face. 

 

“Thank you.” Lena’s eyes are bright, and her voice is so soft, Kara can only hear it because of how closely they’re pressed together. “Thank you for helping me bring Supergirl and Krypton to life.”

 

Kara feels her heart squeeze at Lena’s words. It feels like everything inside Kara clicks into place as she looks down at the woman in her arms. She lowers her head and touches their foreheads together.

 

Kara can feel the other woman’s warm breath against her cheek, slipping past parted lips. “Thank you for giving me my home back."

 

Kara desperately wishes her hands were free, so she can take Lena’s hands in hers, to show Lena in some tactile way just how much this means to her. Instead, Kara just pulls Lena closer and presses a soft kiss to Lena’s cheek. With the way they’re pressed together, Kara’s lips touch the corner of Lena’s mouth, and her heart pounds wildly, scared she’s done something to disrupt the delicate balance of this moment.

 

The moment Kara pulls away, Lena’s eyes open, and under the curtain of Kara’s hair, the green of them is dark and deep enough to drown in. Kara’s breath is caught, still and enraptured, in her throat. 

 

All around them, the sky erupts with blinding colors and a cacophony of sound, but Kara doesn’t notice. The world has narrowed down to the way Lena feels pressed against her, the way her lips are parted and her eyes watch Kara, almost as if she’s waiting, waiting...

 

But the moment doesn’t last.

 

Their flight comes to an abrupt end when they reach the top of the mountain, and both she and Lena are jolted to a quick and jerky stop at the end of the line. Somewhere below them, the zipline team is unharnessing the animatronic Krypto. Someone adjusts the rig and they’re both lowered to the ground.

 

Slowly, Kara eases her grip on Lena, little by little, so she can stand on her own feet. Kara knows she should let go, their flight is over, no one from the crowd can see them up here. 

 

Her arms are sore from carrying Lena’s weight a hundred feet in the air, but her whole body protests against letting Lena move away from her, and she’s still holding her close when the zipline operator has to step in and pry them apart so they can remove their harnesses.

 

“Kara, you can let go now. You’re both on solid ground. You’re both safe.” The operator, a woman Kara knows only as Vasquez speaks to her in a calm voice that filters vaguely through her senses. Vasquez looks pointedly at the way Kara is still gripping Lena’s waist.

 

“Oh.” Kara looks away sheepishly, and with great effort, she manages to peel her arms off Lena. The other woman doesn’t say anything, but she’s looking at Kara with an indecipherable look in her eye.

 

“Right. Sorry.”

 

Kara’s cheeks flame with embarrassment, and despite holding her in her arms a hundred feet in the air just seconds ago, she now finds it hard to look Lena in the eye. She clears her throat and pretends to be absorbed in watching Vasquez talk to her walkie-talkie. “Supergirl and Miss Luthor are on the ground.”

 

A cheer erupts on the other end, familiar voices whooping and laughing, and then Alex’s voice, relieved and satisfied. “Good work, Vasquez. You and Demos can bring them and Krypto back to ground level. The first riders are going in. The Girl of Steel is now open and fully operational.”

 

Both Kara and Lena are ushered into the elevator with Vasquez and Demos. It’s cramped quarters, given that they’re now at the top of the mountain, but Kara studiously avoids Lena’s eyes. Under the harsh fluorescent lights of the elevator, so jarringly different from the brilliant fireworks above them, panic begins to grip Kara. It’s all well and good when they’re a hundred feet in the air, and Kara’s holding Lena in her arms, but something about being back on solid ground makes her feel awkward and stilted. 

 

That flight had been exhilarating, indescribable. Perfect. Doing this for Lena, giving her this experience, has been the highlight of an amazing day, the day when all of their hard work has finally come to fruition. 

 

But… what if Lena doesn’t feel the same? What if she’d just gotten swept up in the moment, caught up in the high of the flight, and pulling off the Supergirl stunt, and finally revealing the park they’d all worked so hard on?

 

Squeezed together in the tiny elevator, Kara can feel the warmth of Lena, even through her suit, and she wants to press against her, be as close as they were during their flight. But Vasquez and Demos are there, stoically awkward in the way elevator passengers are whenever they’re stuck in the same space. Demos holding onto a large animatronic dog adds to the almost-comical absurdity of it all. 

 

Except Kara doesn’t really feel like laughing, because everything tonight has felt heart-poundingly big and overwhelming. All she wants is to either have Lena pressed against her again— close, close , just like it was before she had to let go— or not to look her in the eye for the rest of her life.

 

Lena isn’t looking at her either.

 

It’s almost a relief when they get to ground level. The elevator opens, and they squeeze out. They’re greeted by most of the DEO, and almost immediately, both Kara and Lena are swept into the fray. J’onn has Kara in a bear hug, and Alex is pounding on her back and laughing. Winn and Brainy are yelling about wind velocity, drag and momentum. Nia is squeezing Lena around the waist and seems to be attempting to lift her.

 

Being surrounded by her family, Kara’s mood eases, and she chances a look at Lena. 

 

The other woman is smiling and attempting to keep up as Nia squeals in her face, revealing how Kara had planned this surprise for her all along. When she hears about the surprise plans, Lena looks up. Her eyes meet Kara’s, and Kara swallows a little nervously.

 

But then Lena’s face breaks into a lovely smile, her eyes soft around the edges, and Kara feels her heart skip a beat. Through the mass of people around them, Lena lifts her hand and extends it to Kara. Over Nia’s shoulder, Lena mouths “Thank you.”

 

Everything inside Kara relaxes. Her fingers find Lena’s over J’onn’s shoulder, and she’s finally able to tangle their hands together. Unable to fight the smile that spreads across her face, Kara squeezes Lena’s fingers gently, never wanting to let go.

 

“Always.”
