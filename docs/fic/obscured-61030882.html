Author’s notes

The two chapters of this fic tell the same story. Chapter 1 is from Lena's perspective, Chapter 2 is from Kara's perspective.

Lena

“Are you going to try to kill me?”

It was a knife through Lena’s heart to hear that question fall from Kara’s lips. I suppose I’ve confirmed her nightmares, she thought, staring back at the trapped kryptonian, I guess I used kryptonite against you after all. “No, Supergirl, I’m not going to kill you.”

She could’ve done it differently. Could’ve come back to the Fortress later with her portal watch, stolen Myriad in the quiet of night. But she didn’t. She needed to see Kara’s face, she needed Kara to understand. “I’m not a villain,” Lena said, “And you shouldn’t have treated me like one.”

It’s not that Lena thought of herself as a good person, or above human impulse. She had said as much to Hope earlier - trying to excise her own dark imaginings from her mind. “People are governed by feelings,” she had said as she exited the simulation, “Anger, hurt, sadness, and I'm guilty of that too.”

“I wouldn’t blame you,” she heard Kara whisper, just as Lena stepped through the portal. The kryptonian’s words tugged at Lena’s heart as the portal closed, and she had half a thought to turn back.

But her heart hardened. She was going to fix humanity first - she had to. And then, maybe, she and Kara could have a real conversation.

What is it she had said to Hope in Mount Norquay? “This is a life lesson to learn. Sometimes, the good guys don’t win.”

But now she wondered if the good guys would ever win again, when the crisis was about to erase everything that ever is or was, across all Earths. 

She had helped Alex and the team as much as she could, evacuating billions of lives across thousands of ships to Earth-1. There was a glimmer of hope as she and the rest of the stranded refugees hovered above the other Earth… waiting and hoping for answers, wondering if the paragons - Kara among them - would succeed in saving them all. 

But the antimatter wave came again, and there was nowhere to run. Lena watched in subdued horror as the stars in the distance were snuffed out, one by one. There was nowhere to flee, not as the wave was closing all around them. When the wave hit the first of the fleet of ships, Lena knew it was over.

She knew there would never be a reconciliation with the woman she loved. Part of her admitted that she could’ve forgiven Kara, eventually. But she wasn’t ready yet, and there simply wouldn’t be a tomorrow anymore.

She died clutching the superwatch to her chest.

Fuck.

Lena cradled her head as she woke, fighting off a splitting migraine as she forced herself off her couch. “Hope, close the curtains,” she demanded, stumbling towards the kitchen for some water, dropping whatever was in her hand onto the counter as she reached for a glass. 

But her living room didn’t dim like she expected, and she heard nothing respond to her. She forced herself to face her bright windows, noting that the curtains weren’t moving as commanded. “Hope?”

That was when she noticed that everything in her condo seemed… off, somehow. Her TV wasn’t quite the model she had bought. Her couch was slightly longer. The marble countertop of her kitchen was just a shade lighter. How did I get home? Lena wondered, Where was I before?

That’s when her eyes landed on the object she had just down on her counter. Her superwatch. 

The crisis, she realized, her heart starting to pound. How the hell am I alive…

Lena reached for the TV remote, clicking on the news to see if the rest of the public was acting as chaotic as she was feeling. But she only felt more disoriented at the image she saw before her - Kara, in her supersuit, shaking hands with her brother. The ticker that ran below the story only disoriented her further: Lex Luthor to accept Nobel Peace Prize

What the fuck.

LCorp was somehow LuthorCorp again, but the unexpected signage didn’t stop Lena as she burst into the CEO office. “Lex-”

“Sister!” Lex said cheerfully. “How are you doing this fine afternoon?”

“Don’t,” she said warningly, “If I have my memories back, you do too.”

“Of course I do,” Lex said, a characteristic smirk on his lips. “I got a hold of the Book of Destiny. And now I have everything I’ve ever wanted.”

Lena stilled, glancing at her brother. She supposed it was true, from what she had seen in the brief news clippings - he was loved by the world, at the helm of LuthorCorp again, even taken over the DEO as a subsidiary.

“I know what you’re thinking,” Lex said, rising from his seat. “But I promise, I have no reason to lead your kryptonian astray.”

Does it count as a veiled threat if it’s obvious?, Lena thought. But in that line was an implication far more frightening than Lex’s plan to kill Kara - the kryptonian was more vulnerable than Lena realized. “She doesn’t have her memories,” Lena said.

“The Book of Destiny had a clause, where the paragons would keep their memories on this new Earth,” Lex said cheerfully. “I almost missed it. But luckily, I was able to override it.”

“Luckily,” Lena deadpanned. “That still doesn’t explain why you gave me my memories back.”

Lex winked. “It’s more fun this way.”

Lena spent hours scouring news archives. His background is impeccable here, she thought. If I start telling people that he’s evil, they’ll think I’m insane.

She paced her living room floor, holding the cracked superwatch in her hands. I don’t need to tell her yet, she thought to herself. I just need to give her some protection. For now.

She glanced down at the watch, remembering Kara’s instructions. One click for danger, two clicks for trap, three clicks to talk. Swallowing harshly, she clicked the button three times.

She wasn’t kept waiting long. “Miss Luthor,” Kara said as she landed at Lena’s balcony.

“Supergirl,” Lena said.

Kara was bright-eyed as she landed, harking back to the distant friendliness of years ago - after Lena had proven herself with Medusa, before Reign’s arrival had made everything go wrong. She really doesn’t remember, Lena thought, watching as the kryptonian stood curiously before her. Lex wouldn’t write us to have a close relationship on this Earth… “I have to ask you something,” Lena said, “And I ask that you hold this in confidence.”

“Of course, Miss Luthor,” Kara said, her brows furrowed in concern.

“Has my brother been acting strange lately?”

“No,” Kara said, tilting her head. “He’s a great partner to work with.”

Lena frowned - both at the fact that Kara wasn’t sensing anything off, and that Lena had just shown one of her cards. Kara knows I’m concerned about Lex, she thought, hopefully she doesn’t tell him. “Alright,” Lena said, “I just need to give you something before you go. For kryptonite.”

It was the best she could do on short notice, sneaking into the LuthorCorp labs and getting something for Kara without triggering much scrutiny from Lex. At least I still remember the blueprints, Lena mulled, remembering how the same design had kept Kara safe in Stryker’s Prison. The only difference in this design is that it would embed into Kara’s suit - hopefully to keep Lex unaware of its existence.

Kara took the shield, examining it for a moment, before pressing it to her chest. They both watched as the shield disappeared onto the El crest, and Lena prayed it would be enough to keep Kara safe until Lena found a different way forward. “Thank you, Miss Luthor,” she said. 

There was something in Kara’s smile - a flitting curiosity, something warmer than Lena expected. “What is it?” Lena asked.

“You just remind me of someone.”

“Who?” Lena asked, as her heart skipped a beat. 

Kara’s brow crinkled, as the kryptonian’s expression grew confused. “I’m… I’m not sure.”

Kara still remembers, Lena realized, feeling a flutter of hope in her chest, somewhere in there, her memory lives. “Good night, Supergirl.”

“Good night, Miss Luthor.”

Lena wasn’t expecting a summoning to Lex’s office.

More than that, she wasn’t expecting a full anti-kryptonite suit design projected onto the office hologram when she walked in the door. “What is this?” Lena asked.

“That should be obvious,” Lex said, “I’m making an anti-kryptonite suit.”

Lena swallowed. So Kara told him-

“She didn’t tell me about the shield,” Lex said cheerfully, as though he knew Lena’s thoughts. “My scanners could detect it. But no matter, she needs a stronger anti-kryptonite suit for the task I have in mind.”

“Task?” Lena asked, with a sinking feeling in her stomach.

“We’re going after the Children of Liberty’s kryptonite stores tomorrow,” Lex said, with deadly calm, “She’ll need to be prepared.”

Lena’s head snapped towards Lex, eyes wide. There’s a flaw with the suit, she worried, this is a trap.

“You won’t find anything wrong with it,” Lex said matter-of-factly, as though he could read her mind. And Lena believed him - her brother was smart enough to hide the flaw well enough. She might be able to find a runtime bug that would deactivate the suit, some obscure memory error that would crash the code. But it would take time. More time than she had.

“You don’t have to kill her,” Lena said, “She’s doing everything you ask! She trusts you!”

Lex smirked. “Of course I wouldn’t kill her.”

Lena could feel the harsh rise and fall of her chest, watching as Lex turned. “Though it would be tragic,” Lex said, “Imagine the show of support that people would have for LuthorCorp and the DEO, having lost our star hero.”

Lena turned on her heel, and left.

“I need to ask you not to help Lex tomorrow,” Lena said, begging the kryptonian on her balcony. “Please. He’s not- he’s not the man you know.”

Lena knew how absurd she sounded - frantic and panicked, a madwoman who was losing touch with reality. In a sense, she was… losing a reality that she knew wasn’t real in the first place, but having no way to prove it to others. She had hoped to catch evidence of Lex’s misdeeds over time, build a case to show the amnesiatic Kara that Lex wasn’t to be trusted. But Lex was moving too fast, and she had no options. 

Kara’s face twisted with confusion and sympathy, as the kryptonian stepped forward, subconsciously placing her hands comfortingly on Lena’s arms. It was a familiar, warm gesture - reminiscent of other times.

Kara’s brow crinkled again, as though she were trying to decide if she were acting too familiar, too awkward to this woman that she barely knew. She senses it, Lena thought, watching the uncertainty play out on the blonde’s face as Kara pulled away. “Sorry,” Kara said, taking a step back as though to give Lena space. “I just-”

“He’s going to try to kill you,” Lena said hopelessly.

Kara watched Lena, clearly at a loss for what to do. Lena knew Kara didn’t want to leave Lena so distressed, but there was no reason for Kara to trust what Lena was saying. “Why do you think that?” Kara asked.

“I’m going to tell you the impossible,” Lena replied. “The timeline we’re in isn’t real. You’ve forgotten the true timeline, but you were enemies on my Earth.”

“Miss Luthor,” Kara said, her eyes cast in concern that would’ve been touching at almost any other time, “Are you feeling okay?”

“Damn it, Kara, I’m not ill!” Lena shouted.

Kara widened her eyes. “You know my real name.”

Oh, Lena thought.

Kara stilled, wide-eyed and curious, taking in Lena’s face. Oh, Lena thought again, I… need to tell her what I know. “Your favorite movie is the Wizard of Oz,” Lena said softly.

Kara tilted her head.

“You love potstickers and Britney Spears,” Lena continued. “You’re a terrible cook. You have a beautiful singing voice. You’re claustrophobic - but you try not to let people know.”

“On your Earth… you knew the real me?” Kara murmured.

A stray thought flit across Lena’s mind. Supergirl isn’t the real you? “I knew you,” Lena said, “And I know my brother wants you dead.”

Kara bit at her lip, glancing to the floor. “There’s something we can try,” she said, “I’ll be back soon.”

“Okay,” Lena said.

Kara turned on her heel, ready to depart to the skies. But she paused in thought, turning back to Lena. “Can I ask you something?”

Lena nodded curiously.

“Were we in love?”

Lena hesitated, eyes darting between Kara’s. “We were friends.”

“I can search your mind,” J’onn explained, moments after he and Kara both arrived on Lena’s balcony. “If I can find the memories you claim exist, I may be able to use it to find any walled-off corners of my mind. If I can find those, I can use it to awaken others.”

“Including me?” Kara asked curiously. “Your powers don’t work on me.”

“My powers don’t work on you, but they may still work on whatever block someone else has placed in your mind,” J’onn said, and Kara nodded.

J’onn reached up to touch Lena’s temple, and Lena swallowed nervously. “Will it hurt?”

“No,” he said reassuringly, and she closed her eyes.

As warm fingers pressed to her face, she felt a flood of thoughts - almost as though she could see them dance before her eyes. Her childhood, her mother, her first time stepping into the Luthor Mansion, Andrea-

It didn’t take long for J’onn to find the jumble that led to Supergirl. Her mind seemed to skip between memories, occasionally jumping back. She could feel her heart pounding as Kara shouted at her to climb, feeling wind all around her. She could feel her friend’s soft embrace as they stood in an elevator. She could hear Kara’s laughter…

She could see each tear roll down Kara’s face in the Fortress.

J’onn pulled back, and Lena crumpled to the ground - J’onn and Kara jumping forward to catch her. “Are you okay?” Kara said.

“Yeah,” Lena said shakily, rising to face a somber J’onn. “Do you remember?”

“Yes,” J’onn said, eyes roving curiously over Lena, “Yes I do.”

J’onn turned to Kara. “This may be painful.”

Kara’s brow furrowed. “I thought the process wouldn’t hurt-”

“It’s not the process,” J’onn said, lifting his hand. “Not all of these memories are pleasant.”

It all passed in seconds - she watched Kara’s face shift from bright, to astonished, to wary and wistful. All those thoughts and feelings were in there somewhere, Lena mulled, feeling a depression settle over her as Kara stumbled backwards.

Long moments passed as the blonde caught her breath, seeming desperate to collect her thoughts. “We need to awaken the others,” Kara said, looking up to J’onn, “We should move quickly-”

“I’ll find Brainy and Nia, you go find Alex. I can set up my ship as a headquarters,” J’onn said, watching Kara’s nod before he launched himself off the balcony.

Kara glanced to Lena, and Lena could’ve collapsed again under the familiarity. Gone were the bright eyes of a cheerful super. The Kara who knew the pain of betrayal - her Kara - was back. “Thank you,” Kara murmured gently, turning towards the city in preparation for launching into the sky, “I need to go.”

“Wait-” Lena said, causing Kara to pause, back still turned away. I shouldn’t ask. I shouldn’t ask…

But Lena only knew half of the equation, and she couldn’t handle that. “Are we in love?” Lena whispered.

Kara turned her head slightly over her shoulder - not enough to reach Lena’s eyes, but just enough to see the outline of her face. “Yes,” Kara confessed.

Lena swallowed harshly, her gaze falling to the floor as a sense of loss washed over her.

She heard a step ahead of her, and suddenly felt gentle fingers on her chin, tilting her head up. “After,” Kara said, and Lena’s heart swelled with the promise. A hope that, maybe when this was all over, they could talk. “After,” Lena agreed.

Kara smiled softly, before turning on her heel, launching off the balcony to find her sister. And Lena found herself alone again.

There would be no after.

Lex had made sure to televise the whole thing - Supergirl, partnered with LuthorCorp and the DEO, chasing down dangerous weapons by deranged terrorists. “Kara, what are you doing?” Lena shouted at the TV, watching as the caped super made her way into the warehouse. “It’s a fucking trap-”

The building exploded in green mist, stealing her breath away. She watched in horror as the cloud grew around the walls of the falling buildings, as the camera shook from the aftershocks, as startled birds flew away. She couldn’t process the words of the newscaster, but could only take in his shocked expression and the panic in his voice. 

Lena had been too late. Kara was dead.

She muted the TV, trembling as she took a seat on her couch. I should’ve said it, Lena thought, eyes landing on the superwatch that sat ahead of her. She leaned forward to pick up the device, fighting the tears that she felt she didn’t deserve to shed, I love her, and I should’ve said it…

A knock came at her door. Lena almost wrote it off - she just didn’t have the will to deal with anyone, especially if Lex had come to gloat. But she found herself checking her tablet for the video, just in case.

And was surprised to find Alex Danvers there. 

“We need to talk,” Alex said as Lena opened the door, “I need you to come with me.”

“Where?” Lena asked. “Did you see- Why are you-”

“Not here.”

As they went across town, walking into an unfamiliar building and entering an unfamiliar elevator, Alex explained. J’onn’s ship - set up as an impromptu headquarters - was called the Tower, and only the superfriends knew the location.

None of that mattered as the elevator doors opened again. Lena gasped as she saw Kara standing on the other side, clearly alive and uninjured, in deep discussion with Brainy and J’onn.

But she ignored the others as she launched herself across the room, only half hearing Kara’s words as she turned. Only part of Kara’s there wasn’t time to warn you made it out of her lips when Lena’s hands reached Kara’s collar, pulling the blonde’s face down to her own.

It wasn’t the first kiss she imagined, somewhere quiet and private after an evening of dinner and wine and shy glances. This wasn’t timid. In a way, it didn’t even feel like a beginning. It was crucial - a confession to feelings that had been left unsaid too many times, to the world ending and a faked death that sent panic along Lena’s veins.

Kara returned the kiss, pulling her close with warm arms as Lena finally melted into them. She could feel the desperation in Kara’s hands, the devotion in her lips, the promises of the future where they had both failed in their past.

“I love you,” Lena said as she broke the kiss, reveling in the fact that she could say it - that she wasn’t Kara’s enemy in the Fortress, that she wasn’t staring at a gravestone. Kara was real and alive, and that was all that needed to be said. “I love you.”

“I love you too,” Kara said softly.

The two held each other tight, and all Lena wanted to do was stay - to understand how Kara escaped, to figure out what happened next. After the whiplash of emotion between her television at home and walking into the Tower, Lena almost couldn’t bear the thought of walking away.

But Kara had faked her death, and Lena still had a role to play. “I have to go,” Lena murmured, “I have to yell at Lex for killing you.”

“Will you come back tonight?” Kara murmured.

“Yes.”

Stars twinkled in the evening light when Lena stepped into the Tower again.

Kara was alone this time, as the others filtered home. There would be no going home for her for a while, not until she no longer had to fake her death. But Lena would be there, helping the team come up with a plan.

Lena took her seat on the couch next to Kara, waiting for a friendly gesture before curling up in Kara’s arms. Gone was the desperation from before, leaving only a shyness. “How did you do it?” Lena said, “The kryptonite was everywhere.”

“J’onn pretended to be me,” Kara said, “He turned into a bird at the last moment.”

“So Lex wouldn’t see a heat signature after the explosion…” Lena was relieved. She knew Lex had bought her reaction - it hadn’t been hard to summon the tears, the anguish, the rage. And her threat at the end had been real. 

But now she knew he probably believed Kara’s death for himself. After all, why would the trusting and loyal Supergirl have a plan to betray him?

“You saved my life,” Kara murmured, resting her head on Lena’s, “I would’ve gone in if not for you.”

The Tower bed was small, but Lena didn’t want to go home. In recent weeks she had faced her own death, been reborn to a strange universe where her hostile brother had unprecedented control - and then, in the span of a few hours, she had her best friend back only to watch her die. Nausea and anxiety were rooted deep inside her, and she was grateful that Kara didn’t mind sharing the bed.

“He won’t bother us for a while,” Lena said, as her arms wrapped around Kara’s neck, as Kara snuggled closer, “But he’ll eventually catch on.”

“We’ll be ready for him when he does,” Kara promised.

Tomorrow would have to take care of itself. Tomorrow, Lena would be ready for Lex’s mind games, for the battles she’d face. She’d be with Kara every step of the way, part of a team where trust would eventually build again. For the first time, she had faith in that.

But tonight… the only thing that mattered to Lena, as the couple settled comfortably on the bed, was the overwhelming tenderness of Kara’s gaze.

Lena leaned forward, pressing her lips softly to the kryptonian’s, heart aching in the new freedom between them. She reveled in Kara’s warmth, letting relief finally settle into her, as they exchanged kisses and whispered about ancient feelings, holding each other tight in the night until they finally drifted into dreams.

For the first time in months, they both slept soundly.

Chapter Summary

This is the same story as the first chapter - but from Kara's point of view.

Her dreams were strange.

She had tossed and turned as she dreamt of Lex Luthor - yes, the Lex Luthor - fighting her, for reasons she didn’t understand. The dream quickly morphed into an image of him hovering in the night sky, holding a dead body that looked identical to her own - and she couldn’t make sense of why the incredible philanthropist would murder her.

Dreams didn’t have to make sense, of course, and she should’ve shaken it off. The partnership between LuthorCorp and the DEO had been a wild success in recent years, and Lex would soon accept the well-earned Man of Tomorrow award and his first Nobel Peace prize. A dream was just a dream.

But why couldn’t she shake it?

She didn’t expect to be summoned by Lex’s little sister. She didn’t know that Lex had given his sister a superwatch at all. “Miss Luthor,” Kara said, as she landed on the balcony.

She had rarely spoken to the younger Luthor. Though something was different now - the way sunlight glint brilliantly in green eyes, the gently tousled hair, soft lips that drew Kara’s attention… Keep it together, Danvers.

“Has my brother been acting strange lately?” Lena asked.

Kara’s brow crinkled - concerned for the woman in front of her who was clearly hiding her nerves. But there was something else washing over Kara, an odd feeling that she should be concerned, that her dream was more real than she could remember. “No,” Kara said, tilting her head. “He’s a great partner to work with.”

Lena looked disappointed by her answer. But the Luthor offered her an unexpected gift - a kryptonite shield that sank softly into Kara’s suit, drawing a tender memory from when Lena had shown such care to Kara before. Kara glanced up, smiling warmly.

“What is it?” Lena asked.

“You just remind me of someone,” Kara said.

“Who?” Lena asked, as Kara heard her heart skip a beat.

You, Kara wanted to say, but she realized that was absurd. She barely knew the younger Luthor. She had never received a gift like this before. Why does this feel so familiar? “I’m… I’m not sure.”

She couldn’t read Lena’s expression. But part of her hoped that Lena would call on her again. “Good night, Supergirl,” the woman said.

“Good night, Miss Luthor.”

Another nightmare. She was fighting Lex this time.

“You betrayed Lena!” she had shouted, “Left her with nothing but a ruined name and a broken heart!”

Kara woke up gasping, drenched in sweat. It felt like her whole world had ended in that dream. It was disorienting - she could remember every detail, far more vivid than any other dream she had ever had. And Lex was her enemy. Again.

She tried to write it off. Lex had proven himself over the years, her trust in him was reasonable.

But… she wanted to trust Lena.

Kara showed up to the DEO that afternoon, trying on a new anti-kryptonite suit that Lex had made for her. “We found a trove of kryptonite from the Children of Liberty in a warehouse,” he said, “I want you to destroy it tomorrow morning.”

Kara nodded, flexing her new suit. Nothing is wrong, she told herself, they’re just dreams.

Lena summoned her again. “I need to ask you not to help Lex tomorrow,” she begged, shortly after Kara landed on her balcony. “Please. He’s not- he’s not the man you know.”

Kara could feel the stress rising in her. It was just another mission. Lex gave her a suit for it. The dreams meant nothing. Lena-

Lena…

“Sorry,” Kara said, taking a step back, trying to collect her racing thoughts. “I just-”

“He’s going to try to kill you,” Lena said hopelessly.

Kara watched Lena, trying to shake off the clear break from reality that they both were having. “Why do you think that?” Kara asked, exasperated.

“I’m going to tell you the impossible,” Lena replied. “The timeline we’re in isn’t real. You’ve forgotten the true timeline, but you were enemies on my Earth.”

That’s absurd, Kara thought, another Earth? Enemies? With Lex Luthor?  “Miss Luthor,” Kara said, “Are you feeling okay?”

“Damn it, Kara, I’m not ill!” Lena shouted.

Kara widened her eyes. “You know my real name.”

She watched as Lena’s eyes widened, flitting expressions as Lena went from frustrated to resolved. “Your favorite movie is the Wizard of Oz,” Lena said softly.

Kara tilted her head.

“You love potstickers and Britney Spears,” Lena continued. “You’re a terrible cook. You have a beautiful singing voice. You’re claustrophobic - but you try not to let people know.”

“On your Earth… you knew the real me?” Kara murmured. Is this why I’m drawn to you?

Kara examined Lena for a moment, taking in everything about her - the tension in her body, the fluttering of her heart, the desperation in every word. Why do you feel so familiar?, Kara wondered, thinking again to her dreams against Lex, to her own defense of Lena. Why do I feel this way about you when I barely know you?

Is this real?

Kara bit at her lip, glancing to the floor. “There’s something we can try,” she said, formulating a plan in her head to ask J’onn for help. “I’ll be back soon.”

She made her move to depart - but she needed to know. If this other-universe Kara… “Were we in love?” she asked.

She didn’t know why she felt disappointed by Lena’s answer. “We were friends.”

She almost felt relief when J’onn said he had found the memories. She could certainly hear the relief in Lena’s breath, an affirmation that she was finally believed. It made Kara itch with anticipation, to understand the mystery - will this answer my dreams?

“This may be painful,” J’onn said, as he reached towards her.

“I thought the process wouldn’t hurt-”

“Not all of these memories are pleasant.”

Kara closed her eyes as J’onn’s fingers pressed to her temple. She realized that this wasn’t like remembering - it was like waking up for a bad dream.

Or fighting her way out of a black mercy.

The memories she wanted most were the ones that first slammed into her. “I’m just a woman trying to make a name for herself” and “I didn’t see your name on the byline” and “Kara Danvers, you are my hero” flashed through her mind like water running down a stream, easy and melodic in the way it wrapped around her heart and made it glow again. I’ve been numb this whole time.

She could feel Lena’s hand in her own - holding half a plane in the other - as they floated above the reservoir. That was the moment I realized I loved you.

Then there were the memories she rejected, like how a human might instinctively look away from the sun. She didn’t want to remember Red Daughter dying in her arms. She didn’t want to know the sting of kryptonite in her lungs. She could almost stand to look at Lex as they fought during the Crisis, because at least she had defended Lena then. But there was so much she wished she hadn’t done.

What passed as mere seconds held an eternity for her, forcing herself to look into her past, breaking away from the fog she had lived under- until she stumbled away from J’onn, gasping for breath. “We need to awaken the others,” Kara choked, “We should move quickly…”

J’onn made his departure, and Kara threw Lena a quick thank-you before turning away. She doesn’t want to see me now, Kara thought, when this is over, she may never want to speak to me again-

“Wait-” Lena begged.

Kara hesitated.

She could hear the fluttering of Lena’s heart, the shallow intake of breath. “Are we in love?” Lena asked quietly.

Kara froze in place, swallowing harshly before turning part way towards Lena. An anguished realization washed over her, if Lena was asking…

There is so much I need to say. “Yes.”

She could hear the hammer of Lena’s heart, and turned to see the defeated expression on her face as the Luthor stared resolutely at the floor. 

Kara bit at her lip, taking a step towards Lena, tentatively raising her fingers to Lena’s chin. “After,” Kara promised. If you still want this, I will be here.

“After,” Lena agreed. Kara smiled, feeling hope - true hope - for the first time in ages.

She launched into the sky.

Chaos.

Kara found Alex quickly, dragging her to the Tower - as J’onn called his new ship headquarters - to have her memory awakened. Brainy and Nia were there, too, already back to normal.

Soon Alex followed. “You’re saying Lena got this to happen?”

“She warned me about Lex,” Kara said, “We can trust her. The issue is, I need to be on site soon-”

Kara’s LuthorCorp-provided watch began to beep. “He’s summoning me now…”

“I’ll go,” J’onn said, as he stepped towards the Tower balcony. “Lena’s worried about a kryptonite explosion, it won’t hurt me. I’ll transform and get out of there.”

“I have to talk to Lena-” Kara said.

“No,” J’onn said, blocking Kara with his arm, “You’re going to stay here and play dead. We can’t have you be seen after the explosion.”

Kara hung back, watching him depart. “I’ll go,” Alex said quietly from behind her, “I’ll bring Lena here.”

“You won’t make it before the explosion,” Kara argued, “Lena’s going to think I’m dead.”

“I’ll get there as soon as I can,” Alex said firmly, “Otherwise, this is all for nothing, Lex’ll be after us next.”

Kara tightened her jaw, and nodded.

“I think that worked,” J’onn said, landing on the Tower balcony less than an hour after he left.

“I concur,” Brainy said, rising from his station to walk over to where J’onn had taken his place next to Kara. “Your transformation took place 1.38 seconds after the explosion. I believe Lex Luthor will interpret that data as Kara’s body being burned up in the blast.”

Kara sighed, partially in relief, partially in impatience for Lena’s return. “What did you transform into? To fly away?”

“There was a flock of finches outside of the building, I was one of them.”

Kara nodded. “I guess we bought time for now-”

She could hear the elevator open.

Kara turned as she heard the clatter of shoes against the floor, as Lena practically darted her way across the room towards Kara. “I’m sorry, Lena, there wasn’t time to warn-”

It became apparent that Lena’s first interest was not in talking, not as the Luthor grabbed Kara’s collar, pulling her into a kiss. Lena, Kara thought desperately, wrapping her arms around the other woman, reveling in how she melted into her arms. Lena, Lena, Lena…

“I love you,” Lena said, as she broke the kiss.

Kara smiled. “I love you too.”

“I can stay, Kara,” Alex said.

“I’ll be fine,” Kara said, hoping Lena’s conversation with Lex wouldn’t last long, “Lena said she’d come back tonight. J’onn showed me the backroom.”

Alex frowned. “You’ll be living here a while.”

“I know,” Kara said, “But Rao, at least I’m alive.”

Alex smiled tiredly, giving her sister a tight hug before turning to head home. Everyone else had already gone for the night, minds all churning about what to do next, how to defeat Lex, how they had somehow walked around brainwashed for who knows how long.

Kara sank slowly onto the Tower couch, head tilted up towards the ceiling. It all happened so fast…

She heard footsteps again, turning to see Lena as she walked tiredly across the floor, eyeing Kara with a twin sense of relief and uncertainty that Kara mirrored. Kara opened her arm, welcoming Lena as she took a seat next to her, the Luthor’s head dropping to Kara’s shoulder in exhaustion. “J’onn pretended to be me,” Kara explained quietly, Lena murmuring in response that Lex would believe it.

Supergirl, for now, was dead.

“You saved my life,” Kara whispered back, resting her head on Lena’s.

Night fell.

Kara could sense that Lena was too anxious to leave - and truth was, Kara felt the same, so shaken by the new reality she had woken up to, feeling unmoored by the fraudulent reality she had lived. 

So when Lena asked to share the Tower bed, Kara could only respond in relief.

“He’ll eventually catch on,” Lena said, as the two curled up in each other’s arms.

“We’ll be ready when he does,” Kara promised.

Lena curled up tightly, pressing her lips to Kara’s again - no longer with the franticness from before, but with a soft ache instead. Kara found herself returning the kiss, settling into a rhythm against Lena’s lips, before migrating to her jaw, to her neck, peppering her with kisses that were held back for far what had felt like an eternity.

“I’ve wanted this for so long,” Lena whispered.

“Me too,” Kara confessed.

In the quiet of night, there was relief in their exhaustion, a feeling of calm in knowing the team had united again - and at least they had one another. The two held each other tight as they murmured softly into the dark, until eventually nodding off to sleep.

Kara dreamt of Lena's smile.
