    penitence

     

    Metropolis never really felt like home to her.

    But to be fair, the first time she truly felt a place was truly home, was in the last couple of years in National City. With friends and game nights. Wine and laughter.

    Or at least she thought it was.

    She doesn't think so much about it anymore.

    She's back at Metropolis. Working at L-Corp East, following a schedule.

    Lena would completely immerse herself in her work if it weren't for Sam Arias, and her daughter.

    Sam who waited for her at the airport the night she landed. Even when it was past midnight. Sam, who drove her to her own home, even when she knew for a fact, Lena owned several properties, including the old mansion at the outskirts of the city. Still, Lena was grateful for the option of not being in any of those places. She didn't want the cold and the loneliness those places offered.

    So she appreciated Sam's presence that night. And every night since. As Lena was still mustering the will to leave Sam's place.

    And Sam... Sam wasn't pushing.

    Quite the opposite. She talked about their afternoon as if Lena being there was a given. They arrived together at L-Corp, everyday. And left the building the same way.

    Lena had forgo-ed the use of chauffeurs, as Sam basically drove her everywhere, without complaint. She never asked what happened to Lena to make her move across the country again. She was just there, supportive, and willing.

    And maybe it would have been easier if she had fallen in love with her instead of Kara.

    It’d been over a month now, and while Sam hadn’t asked about why Lena left National City, she had started to ask about Kara, Alex… wondering if she should visit soon. And Lena knew, her period of grace was ending, and Sam would question her soon.

    It happened as they were going back to Sam's on a Friday night.

    “I would not bother,” Lena said this time, as Sam questioned if she should visit the Danvers, unable to stop the bitterness she still felt.

    “Why not?” Sam asked, a frown in her face, as they drove to her house.

    Lena didn't say anything for a while. She hadn't revealed Supergirl's identity to Sam. She didn't plan to. But it made it all difficult to explain why she was being so grouchy with the Danvers sisters.

    "Lena, I've not— I don't want to intrude in your life," Sam said. "But what happened? It's been great having you here, but you are not… you."

    The secret to be a good liar, Lena learned quite early on in her life with the Luthors, was saying something close to the truth.

    "Kara told me she was in love with me," Lena said, not looking at Sam, but at the window next to her.

    "Lena," Sam started, surprised. "That's great! I would think you'd be jumping on one feet, happy. Why are you here, then, instead of with her?"

    "Lex," Lena replied easily.

    "He's dead. You separated yourself from his rhetoric by not attending his funeral," Sam easily retorted. "Am I missing something?"

    'That I killed him because I was protecting my best friend, and it turns out she'd been lying to me for three years about who she was', Lena doesn't say.

    "She's the one that exposed him," Lena said, quietly. "And I'm unsure if she does care for me, or if she simply got close to me as a source."

    Close enough. Lena thought. Or at least, as close as she was willing to go.

    Sam chuckled, shaking her head.

    "Are you kidding me?" Sam asked, so earnests and clearly surprised that Lena would even think like that. If she knew… "Kara cares deeply about you. I'm not entirely surprised she told you she's in love with you. I'm surprised she took this long. The way she was working to prove your innocence when kids started getting sick… that wasn't her searching for dirt on you, quite literally the opposite."

    "Yes, probably to butter me up," Lena let out.

    "My god. What happened? You are cynical, but not like this, not with Kara." Sam said with finality. "All of your usual rules don't apply to Kara. I could tell even back then."

    "I don't want to talk about it, anymore," Lena said. "I— I'm not ready yet."

    Sam nodded, "I hope you know I'm here for you, whenever you are ready."

    Lena nodded, and looked at the window again.

    She wasn't ready to talk about it. But that never stopped her thoughts before.

    She still thought about every single moment with Kara, debating with herself if it was real, or if it was just a Kryptonian, getting close to a Luthor to keep her in check. Or if it was, as Kara said, every single moment had been honest between them.

    Lena has known for years she was in love with Kara Danvers.

    She still remembered the way she angled her face, and looked at her eyes, and asked: "What about you, Ms. Danvers? I didn't see your name in the byline?" With the right amount of playfulness that indicated some sort of sexual interest.

    How she'd filled her office with flowers, for Valentine's day. How she'd paid for the food whenever they had lunches. How she moved her entire schedule around to fit those lunches with Kara.

    And how when she realized she wasn't being reciprocated, she took a step back and accepted the friendship, because she was already too deep in.

    And maybe the amount of food Kara ate should have been a clue. Or the way she'd said: "I flew here… on a bus," the first time she went to interview her. But she never thought Kara would have been Supergirl, and keeping it a secret from her, her best friend.

    Still, Lena had known she loved Kara, since very early on. She just… wasn't willing to let the warmth she felt around her go. Even if it was just resigned to a friendship. That was enough.

    And now, now that Lena is confronted with the fact that Kara had been lying to her since the beginning. That she never trusted her enough to tell her who she was, an alien, a Kryptonian, Supergirl… It sting like nothing has before.

    Not even Andrea's betrayal stung in the same way. Nor Lex's. It was… heartbreak.

    And then, there was everything that Kara had said in their last conversation…

    "—That I'm stupidly in love with you." Kara had said it with such desperation, only to be surprised that she said it. Looking at the Truth Seeker fastened to her right arm, making her say everything with sincerity.

    And yet, Lena couldn't help but not believe.

    Lena didn't want to believe.

    Because how could Kara actually love her, and still lie to her day after day? How could she love her, and not share her real name with Lena?

    They had lunch together at least once a week, since they became friends. Kara would sometimes rush, with nonsensical explanations, and maybe that should have been another indication that she was hiding something, but Lena always assumed it had to do with some secret source for an article. She knew all about how whistle-blowers worked. She never thought to connect meek, mild mannered Kara to Supergirl.

    Didn't matter how many times they seemed to be in constant communication with the other. How they always referenced the other.

    Maybe Lex was right. She didn't want to see what was right in front of her. The thought made her stomach shrink.

    She was still with the presence of mind to realize that they had arrived at Sam's house. Ruby had greeted them, with a stack of papers scattered around her at the table. A scene too close to her own memories past and present. And Sam was debating aloud what they should have for dinner.

    "I'm not hungry," Lena announced. "I think I'm going to sleep early today. Enjoy your evening." And with that, without waiting for an answer, she retired to her assigned room.

    It's been over a month, and Sam's guest room looked exactly the same as the night she arrived there. Besides, the bed sheets that had been changed regularly. Her clothes were inside the closet, and drawers. But there were no pictures, no trinkets, no books visible.

    But in Lena's defense, it wasn't home.

    It wasn't cozy blankets thrown over furniture. Or half finished acrylic paintings in the corner, the smell of baking going slightly wrong, and pot-stickers as consolation prize. The sound of some cooking show, always playing and the muffled sound of traffic moving downstairs.

    As much as she loved Sam and Ruby. They weren't surprisingly strong arms, engulfing her every time she was close enough. Blonde hair softly caressing her cheek, and Lena's first line of defense.

    Or, realistically, they offered a very similar sentiment.

    But they weren't Kara.

    And that was the problem.

     

     

    Kara had written a scatting article about Lex. How he had orchestrated the Kaznian invasion of the U.S, how he was behind the exposure of President Marsdin as an alien, and the step into power of President Baker. How he was the one giving orders to the President of the United States to pardon him. How he had used his money, connections and status to collude with Kaznia and train the Supergirl clone who had impersonated her to attack American soil, experimented on aliens and funded Agent Liberty's crusade.

    She had listed all and every crime Lex ever committed, as if telling everything bad he had done would excuse her own sins.

    Even when the voice at the back of her head, with its Slavic accent, keep telling her that it wouldn't bring Lena back to her.

    "Everything is always about Lena to us…" the voice said. "It was always about Lena to Alex too."

    "His name is Lex," she almost screamed out-loud.

    "Was," the voice somewhat kindly retorted.

    It wasn't grief. Kara was intimately familiar with that, she had been grieving since she was 13 and lost in space with no parents, no planet, no culture, no one to talk to. She have been grieving longer than she has been on Earth. So she knew what she felt about Lex's death wasn't grief, but it felt uncomfortably close to it. What's worse, whatever it was, it made her miss Lena even more.

    If that was even possible.

    Lena occupied every single waking thought. And, now it was evident that she'd been feeling like that for longer than she admitted to tell anyone. Her days started early, and ended late.

    A routine that humans would have perished from in less than a week.

    "It's like you are punishing yourself," Alex said one day in week three since Lena had left. It was barely 6 am, and Kara had been already patrolling for hours. "Did you even go home last night?"

    Kara didn't reply that she did; because arriving at her empty apartment at 3 am, only to fly out the window half an hour later, didn't seem like a good answer to give to her sister.

    "I'm fine," she replied, instead.

    "No, you are not," Alex retorted. "I know you aren't, you don't have to lie to me. But I can't let you destroy yourself. I told you she would need time to process, and that was before you dumped your feelings on her."

    "I didn't mean to," Kara defended herself. "I didn't know…"

    "And that's okay. But you also have to process that you are in love with your best friend," Alex kindly said. "And that she might not forgive you for lying to her."

    Kara's knees almost gave out at the insinuation. And the worst, its how possible it really was.

    "You have been talking to Kelly about this," Kara presumed.

    Alex reluctantly shrugged, "I'm worried about you, of course I've been talking with my girlfriend about it."

    Kara didn't say anything. And hoped her jealousy about her sister's relationship wasn't too obvious. She hated that it felt similar to the last time Alex was in a serious relationship. It wasn't that she didn't want her sister to have a good relationship and be happy and in love.

    It's that Kara wanted it for herself too. And she wanted it with Lena, as it turns out.

    She almost wishes she never figured it out.

    It was easier to simply bask in Lena's presence, her kindness, smile and joke with her, in the illusion that they were just best friends. The sting of not be around her again would hurt less.

    Of course, she wouldn't be in this situation if she'd been honest with Lena. If Lex hadn't revealed her identity first.

    "I'm okay," Kara said aloud. Seeing in Alex's face that she could read her like a book. Like a very unsurprising step-by-step manual. "I'll just go to Catco, and then… I'll just chill at my apartment. I promise."

    Kara didn't wait for an answer, just flew out the balcony, with every intention of breaking her promise.

    Silence was never something she could stand, since her stay at the Phantom Zone. And it was something she refused to endure during Lena's absence.

    She probably just wouldn't use her primary colored suit to go out that night.

     

     

    "If you ever need me, all you have to do is call. No expectations." Kara had said.

    And Lena had been tempted to take that offer. Just to listen to her voice. She didn't even know what she would ask, or say. Where could she start.

    It's been another month, and she had finally moved from Sam's house. Much to her friend's frustration, Lena had been unable to open up to her.

    Lena couldn't face Sam anymore than that. Her worried face still haunts her at work, but there she can distract both of them enough to avoid speaking. She couldn't say the same if they were living under the same roof.

    Lena knew it was Sam's caring nature, but to be fair, Lena doesn't know how to be cared for.

    Now, while Sam made sure that Lena at least had lunch everyday, and left the office before the clock even came close to 8 pm every night, Lena could imbibe in the comfort of her own solitary penthouse, as it was her preferred method of auto destruction and self flagellation.

    She'd unearthed a picture that she must have packed without realizing. Her and Kara, smiling. Embracing each other with no care in the world.

    The glasses affixed to the blonde's face, made Lena wonder how she could be fooled by them.

    Then she remembered how she used to sneak out of Mount Helena's by using glasses similar to Kara's own, and not straighten her hair. Maybe throw a baseball cap for good measure. It always worked wonders.

    The whiskey burned down her throat, just as she liked. Bitter and punishing. Kara's smile in the picture made her miss her even more. Made her remember the feeling of her arms around her, warm and inviting. As if with a simple movement of her head they could be kissing.

    She looked at the skyline, almost expecting to see the blue and red clad figure flying outside the windows. But the landscape was wrong. And even if she managed to see a similar figure, she knew it wouldn't be her.

    It was past midnight in National City, Lena had been drinking for hours, and the idea of taking Kara's offer…

    "Fuck it…" Maybe she could get answers if she was brave enough.

    She pressed on her favorite contacts, Kara's picture occupying most of the screen. Kara smiling at the camera as she stuffed her mouth full of pot-stickers.

    Kara didn't want that to be her contact picture; Lena adored it even more because of it.

    It barely ringed once, before Kara answered the call, "Lena?"

    "I asked for you name," Lena said. The first thing that came to mind, as she had ruminated in every interaction between her and Supergirl. Her and Kara… "You said it wasn't a good question for a Luthor to ask…"

    The silence was overwhelming, the Kryptonian wouldn't even give her the balsam of her voice.

    "I panicked," finally, Kara mumbled. "I was mad at you because of the Kryptonite thing, and I panicked. I didn't want to tell you like that. But I was mad and hurt, even when I knew it wasn't fair."

    "So you hurt me, instead," Lena whispered back.

    "I'm not proud of it. I would take it back if I could," Kara said, the conviction in her voice… Lena would be inclined to believe her— if she hadn't been fooling her since they met. "I made a mistake with you, Lena. I understand it, more than ever."

    "What does that mean?"

    Another stretch of silence made Lena almost finish the call. She couldn't even be sure Kara was even telling the truth, anyways. The truth seeker was no longer connected to her arm. Presumably.

    "The clone?" Kara asked unsure, yet she didn't expect for an answer to continue explaining. "She… bonded with me, again. She died, but I— I guess, I absorbed her. And she was molded— almost raised by— by Lex."

    Lena surprised herself with the involuntary inhale she did.

    "And… It doesn't really matter." Kara said. "He was your brother, and you still took the difficult decision. You outsmarted him, you defeated him, you were brave, and valiant, and I couldn't even tell you the truth. The most important person in my life." Kara said.

    Lena wished she could see her. She said nothing.

    "I think I know how it must feel," Kara continued. "You must be thinking of everything I said to you as both Supergirl, and Kara. Dissecting every single interaction and trying to decide which one was true."

    Lena grasped the tumbler with a little more force.

    "'You got it backwards. The Luthor name doesn't deserve Lena.' I told Mercy when she broke into LCorp," Kara said. "I wasn't angry, I wasn't hurt anymore about the Kryptonite. I wasn't trying to stop you from asking too many questions. I was trying to keep you safe. And it's exactly what I think about you."

    "You could have told me then…"

    "I could have. Many times, I think," Kara conceded. "But I always talked myself out of it, because I was afraid that you would leave."

    Lena could feel the sad smile in Kara's lips, somehow. Self deprecating and self punishing, sardonic, even. But she said nothing.

    "I miss you," Kara confessed.

    Lena wanted to say that she missed her too, like she had missed no one.

    The words got stuck somewhere in her throat.

    "I have to go," she muttered, and without waiting for an answer, she finished the call.

    Sleep never came that night. Time tickling by, as Lena maintained her gaze fixed at the ceiling above her bed.

    She was hurt, no doubt. But was it worth it to self isolate, feeling worthless everyday, giving the upper hand to Lex who wasn't even alive? Especially when Kara was there offering a way for the both of them to learn from this?

    Lena thought about Andrea; how Lena had never seen her again since they found themselves in London, and she discovered Andrea had taken the medallion, even when they move in similar circles.

    How Lena had left the first friend she ever knew, her first real love, never to look back.

    Was she willing to leave Kara for ever, and forget about her?

     

     

    Kara had been benched.

    And, to be fair, this time she would be the first to admit she'd crossed the line.

    She just… was angry.

    There was some sort of word exchange, and then one of them said it.

    "Why are you defending the human bitch? You are one of us," the one closer to her asked.

    "You are lucky they were Somahturians. You are lucky they are even alive!" Alex voiced, pacing the D.E.O. cell she had to put her in after subduing her with Kryptonite.

    "I'm sorry… I—" Kara started.

    "I don't wanna hear it. Sleep it off." Alex interrupted her, walking towards the door. Then stopping. "I know you are sad, frustrated, heartbroken. But you can't become what Lex has been saying for however many years. Don't let him win, even in his death."

    She went to work, the human way. She wrote two proposals for articles, at human speed, and left to check on sources the same way she arrived.

    When she got home that night, walking up the stairs and using the door instead of her window, she wished alcohol had any effect on her. She wanted to wallow today.

    She'd never lost control in that way, besides the Red Kryptonite incident. It was not who she was, to resort to violence that way.

    But it had felt good, to let loose, and hurt something, someone as much as she was hurting.

    "He shouldn't have said that about Lena," the voice at the back of her head said. The accent still there, even if it sounded a little more like Kara herself everyday.

    "No," Kara said at no one. "But I should be able to control myself. My existence in this planet hinges on me controlling my powers."

    There was no response.

    The picture was starting to show signs of the wear and tear Kara had put it through the last three months.

    She was starting to lose hope that Lena would ever return to National City. She'd betrayed her trust. She'd broken her promise to protect her, she'd broken her heart, by making Lena trust her, walking past her insurmountable high walls, making herself important to Lena.

    And not trusting her with the most basic of her secrets. Lena thinks she doesn't even know her name.

    Kara wished Lena knew she only wanted to be Kara to her.

     

     

    Lena had made a routine. At least, she had stopped drinking to the point of passing out. She made plans for the weekend. And she went to every single soccer game Ruby had.

    Sam had even managed to make her go to two blind dates.

    Lena made Sam promise she wouldn't try a third time.

    She knew more than ever that there was no one else who could compare to her. To Kara.

    It was just so difficult to let her in again.

    Jess had called from National City with the news that Supergirl had frustrated the robbery of her own office. She had assured her that nothing had been taken. But that Supergirl didn't seem like herself when she left LCorp, escorted by the agency that seemed to follow the hero around.

    She wasn't sure what that meant. But still, after not being able to stop thinking about Jess' words for over a week, she logged into the security camera of her office. The one that was outside the central network of the company, and that was hidden behind several encryptions, that only she knew about.

    The scene was mundane enough. Three figures working in the darkness, trying to open the safe in her office. Then, glass flew everywhere as the city's hero arrived, taking down all three of the thieves, seemingly with her mere presence.

    Her eyes glowed with heat-vision, and her fist plummeted over all of the humanoids, with an abandon she had never seen in her. Or her cousin for that matter.

    The one that had said, whatever it was, had gotten the worse of it though. Then Alex Danvers was there, with a squad, giving orders to Supergirl that were promptly ignored, until Brainy arrived, from god knows where, giving something that Alex injected what could only be Kryptonite into Kara's neck, knocking her down.

    That was not really a Kara she knew. Or a Supergirl, for that matter. She wondered what could have upset her in that way. That anger, seemed foreign in Kara's face. But maybe she didn't really know her that well.

    She wished she did.

    But she needed more time.

     

     

    Supergirl wasn't a full time thing, anymore.

    At least, Kara had stopped patrolling, and only helping when she was called. Brainy having fine-tuned his algorithm.

    Instead, she had dived into her work at Catco. She was following leads, checking sources, developing articles. Nothing related to her other job.

    She had done that in the last month and a half. Lena left almost six months ago, and Kara was pretty sure she wouldn't come back.

    She'd started praying to Rao again. Not for her return, but for her safety.

    Kara knew she was in Metropolis. Daily Planet having reported on her return to that city almost as soon as it happened. And from time to time she would find an article about some donation to the hospital that had her name all over it, even if it was not mentioned.

    She had half a mind to ask Kal about keeping an eye on her, when he came back from Argo. But she realized that both of them could miss-interpreted her intentions as 'keeping an eye on Lena, because she's evil.'

    Game nights had stopped. At least she stopped hosting them, and she managed to find an excuse to not assist whenever someone else was.

    She didn't think there was much of a point if her partner wasn't there.

    "Kara, boss is looking for you," Franklyn said, one Wednesday late morning as soon as she entered the bullpen.

    He was on his way out, Kara had just returned from a coffee meeting with one of her sources. "Sure, thanks Franklyn."

    She took two steps, and that's when she heard it.

    The sound she'd been seeking for months, hoping, wishing, yearning to hear again.

    Her legs took her in automatic past the bullpen, past her desk, past James himself, standing at the door like a guard, with a weird smile on his face.

    She wasn't looking in her direction. She was distracted looking at something in the desk, her hip propped on the corner of it. Her hair was a little bit longer, in soft waves, rather than the severe ponytail, or the straight middle part.

    "You are back," Kara exhaled, almost by accident.

    Lena's heart started, the only sign she'd heard Kara, as she still was with her back at her.

    "Would you mind closing the door?"

    Kara said nothing, she simply complied with the request.

    With the click of the door, finally, Lena turned around, to look at her.

    "Congratulations are in order," she said.

    "What?"

    "James submitted your piece of Lex's for the Pulitzer Prize," Lena explained. "They just informed me that you are officially a nominee."

    Kara truly did not care.

    "You are back?" She asked instead.

    Lena cocked her head to one side, and Kara has never wanted to kiss anyone more in her life.

    "For now."

    Kara had never before appreciated more those two words, strung together, than in that precise moment.

    She could finally breathe again.
