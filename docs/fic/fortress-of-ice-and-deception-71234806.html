    Fortress of ice and deception

     

    Trapped in the place she'd just called Home on Earth, in front on Lena, felt like karma. Trapped by the same best friend, using the Fortress' defenses and a shard of Kryptonite against her. Maybe she deserved it.

    She probably did.

    She definitely did.

    She had no one else to blame but herself.

    She was the one unable to tell her best friend, 'I'm not who you think I am.'

    She was the one who couldn't let go of the entire synthetic Kryptonite thing, who made her best friend's boyfriend break into her personal vault behind her back to make sure she'd given her all of it.

    James of course told Lena about it, which made the breach between them even bigger. Making the difference between Kara and Supergirl much profound, deep and extremely difficult to come back from.

    Still, it was Kara's fault, she could have come clean so much sooner. When they were facing Reign, right after Lena was poisoned, when Kara was poisoned by kryptonite or after coming back from the coma Reign put her under, when Lex had come back… but at the end she didn't.

    She didn't. And she will regret it for the rest of her life. She didn't, because she was scared to dead to lose her.

    As she saw Lena leave the Fortress of Solitude through a purple portal, with Myriad in hand, she realized she didn't know how to be without Lena anymore.

    And now she'd apparently would have to learn how, anyways.

     

     

    She couldn't accept it. Of course she couldn't. New universe be damned.

    She'd been selfish, she knew. But her selfishness was not born out of malice, nor to keep Lena close and surveilled, because she was a Luthor, as she surely thought. Kara was simply… not ready to lose her best friend.

    She'd never felt connected to someone in that way. It felt addictive, almost. The same way she felt when she flew for the first time after years of not doing it.

    The sense of euphoria when she first see her, or whenever she got a text from Lena. That excitement whenever something happened in her life, and the first person she wanted to tell was Lena. And yet… sometimes she knew she couldn't. Because Lena would never forgive her.

    Everytime she wanted to say something, Lillian's voice played at the back of her mind.

    "Eventually, she'll find out on her own. Find out that you've been lying to her all this time. And when she does? She'll hate you for it."

    But it prevented Kara for ever uttering those words. Because the more she got to know Lena, the more she realized Lillian Luthor was right. And she didn't want to lose Lena too.

    Not after losing her childhood, and her parents, her mission of taking care of Kal, not after losing her planet and her culture.

    Losing Lena would be losing too much.

    Lena would be furious her mother knew her that well.

    There was a new universe, and that changed nothing.

    She had a magical imp willing to change history for Kara to not lose Lena, and that didn't work either. But it made Kara come to a fatal conclusion. Lena was working with Lex on something.

    Something that wasn't good. And she wasn't sure she would be able to stop them. But she knew she would have to try. Even if she had to stop looking at Lena as her best friend.

    "—if you continue to work with Lex If you go through with whatever it is the two of you are planning, I will do everything in my power to stop you just like I would any other villain." She told Lena, before taking flight.

    Of course, Lena didn't want to listen to her. And Kara understood. She wouldn't want to listen to herself anymore either.

    But there was a shard of hope, when Lena gave her a book after Jeremiah's passing. Kara read the book fast, but savoring the warmth of Lena still caring enough about her. And yet, Kara ruined it once again by using Myriad.

    At the fortress, Lena confronted her, again. Kara wasn't entirely sure of how Lena knew she was using Myriad, but there were lives at stake and that seemed to be a more pressing matter than her feelings about Lena.

    "There are other ways to find people than Myriad," Lena said. "I can think of four off the top of my head, but you, you do what you want."

    The petty, 'But you are not on my team, anymore, so…' never made it past her lips.

    "What I'm doing is finding people your brother disappeared," she said instead.

    "Do you have any proof that it was Lex?" Lena asked.

    Of course she didn't. That was the frustrating thing. Except for his infuriating, teasing smirk, she had nothing. It was defeating, and the more time Lena spent working with Lex, it was like pouring salt into that particular wound.

    Her silence was enough of an answer to Lena.

    "No, I didn't think so," she said. "You just return to your default position of blaming a Luthor. You did tell me you'd treat me like a villain..."

    "If you acted like one, yeah, I did say that," Kara replied, just tired of losing to that insufferable bald man. "And right now, you're trespassing, and I don't have time for this. You need to go."

    "I'll leave when you stop using Myriad," Lena challenged her.

    And Kara will be the first one to admit she can be stubborn and impatient sometimes. Much later she would accept this was one of such times. She would realize she was just playing into Lex's hand.

    "That's not gonna happen," Kara said, instead.

    "To think that I was sincere with you yesterday," Lena lamented. "Then I guess you're used to being two-faced."

    And then she left.

    And Kara felt the defeat, the cold of the Fortress, even when she shouldn't.

    However, there wasn't much time to think about it, because there was a Morae attack, that consequentially let the baby sun eater escape and steal her powers.

    Maybe she truly had lost Lena.

     

     

    There wasn't much time, every minute was borrowed against an immortal Lex, like the very annoying thorn he was. At least they had a plan.

    "I may be able to reinforce the anti-Luthor protocol," Lena said walking towards the quantum computer, and Kara felt the warmth returning once again to the Fortress of Solitude. "It won't kill Lex now that he's immortal, but it might slow him down."

    "Lena..." Kara whispered, she knew they had little choice. She had little choice, but as long as they stopped Lex it would all be worth it.

    "Kara, he's gonna try and kill you. All right?" Lena pleaded. "I won't let you turn this into a suicide mission. I can't."

    "We've got no other choice," Kara argued, a calm smile on her face. She could do it now. Now that Lena was by her side, even if everything was fixed between them. "It's okay. I've come to terms with it. As long as you guys stop the satellites, and help everyone with the Myriad, it'll be worth it."

    They may never get the chance to fix things, to say everything she wanted to tell to Lena. But she would be safe, all her loved ones would be safe without Lex, and Kara was okay with that.

    Lena inhaled a breath that seemed to make her swallow whatever retort she had, and seemingly came to accept the same conclusion as Kara. "When Lex comes, you're gonna wanna take the higher path, but you know him," she said. "He plays dirty. Mean."

    Kara hummed.

    "Maximizing the pain wherever he can," Lena continued. Her eyes fixed on Kara.

    And if Kara wasn't sure time was indeed passing, and every second should be focused on defeating their common nemesis, she would have swore time stopped just between them. And she didn't want it to ever end.

    "Well, I was stuck in the Phantom Zone replaying the destruction of my planet for over two decades," she said, anyways. Because time doesn't really stop on Earth, as much as Kara cheated it with her superspeed. She knew that them, Kara and Lena had very likely run out of time. The chances of her getting out unscathed from her battle with Lex were slim. "Bring it on." There was a pause, a moment when Kara wanted to memorize every detail of Lena's presence anyways. But time… "We should probably—" She pointed towards the armory.

    "Kara," Lena said, low. and it was like music to Kara's ears. "I know it wasn't easy to trust me again. Especially with Myriad—"

    "You're the only person in the galaxy I know would do right by it." Kara interrupted her. Surprising even herself about how fast, and how real her words were. There was no time to hash out everything that went down between them. All the mistakes and all the words they said that hurt each other.

    But Kara realized, in that precise moment, that even if she died, Lena would have that truth. She would trust no one in the galaxy, more than she trusted Lena. Not with Myriad, not with her legacy, not with her heart.

    'You are the only person in the galaxy,' her lips said. Her heart said, 'I love you'.

    She'd learn her lesson. Lena deserved the truth always. This time, however she hoped Lena could wait a little more for that truth. She didn't want to burden her with not having a meaningful conversation about it. She would say everything once Lex was defeated, if she survived it.

    "Come on," she said.

    She knew the chances were slim, but it wasn't impossible. She was the Paragon of Hope; she had to trust that the next time she stands at the Fortress with Lena, it will be a happier situation.

    She hoped she could deliver that to Lena.
