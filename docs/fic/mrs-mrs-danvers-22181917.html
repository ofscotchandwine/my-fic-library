“I do require proof, dear sister. This is a new era. I need to know what side you’re on. With the press of a button,” he snaps, harsh, “you’re dead. This isn’t the time for games.”

Lena considers what he's asking. The typical parlor tricks or stalling methods aren't the right play, not for her brother.

“I know who Superman is. Is that proof enough?”

It's her ace in the hole, and Lex turns to her, his sharp jaw in profile. Lena holds her wrist up, revealing the Super watch.

"What do you mean, 'who he is?'"

"You were right," it pains her to say. "He has a civilian identity, just like Supergirl. And I can call him at any time. He's yours for the killing."

“That’s more like it," Lex indicates one of the rolling chairs facing his desk. Lena takes this to be the true beginning of their bargaining. "Take a seat."

She takes the seat, and he pulls zip cord from an opened cabinet and begins to methodically tie her to the chair. Lena doesn't want to think about why he has knotting rope in his office, criminal reasons or otherwise.

"Pragmatism and all."

Lena lifts an eyebrow. "Oh, sure."

"This country. This planet," Lena feels the rope go taut around her wrists. She inwardly grimaces. "It’s ours. We could fix the germination of this alien virus for generations—"

Lena zones out at this part. Lex tends to wax poetic when he gets into the nuts and bolts of disemboweling Superman or whatever. She glances around the room. Had Lex hired a new decorator? It's a little bland.

"—I'm trying to protect mankind from an extinction event—"

She feels another zip of tightening cord, cutting into her skin. Lex has slipped the knot, though. She wants to roll her eyes. This is the guy who runs LuthorCorp, who hopes to rule the world through fear. When he's done tying, he continues his tirade, looking back out over the city, and Lena makes quick work of getting herself out of the bindings. She silently heads to his wall of cabinets, curiously sniffing through his aged scotch.

"—the devils don't come from hell beneath us. Oh no, they come from the sky—"

She continues to rifle through the cabinet, fantasizing about the new house she and Kara will build. They can really start from scratch, a fresh canvas. She can pick new themes, styles, everything. Their last home was a single-minded devotion to country cottagecore, but this new one could be modern. Lena's thinking those pretentious Eames leather chairs, an inner courtyard, overlarge windows and doors—

“Lena, are you listening?” Lex seethes.

Had Lex compared himself to some great mythological figure yet? Napoleon? Epicurus? Hannibal?

"And how did you get out of that chair?"

“Sorry,” she apologizes with exactly zero sincerity. “Did you get to the part where you’re going to kill Superman?”

“I am going to kill Superman.”

“So, no.”

"We don't have time for your insolence," he raises his hand, clicking a button.

For a split second, Lena's worried it's a bomb. She hadn't prepared for that, but fortunately, the sky behind him goes a crispy, smoky red instead. It's unnatural. It puts a spur in Lena's stomach, but she feigns mild interest and surprise.

She glances back at Lex.

"Your work, I presume?"

He nods. "Now sit back down, and call Superman with that watch of yours."

"Is that really necessary? The sky is red. I'm pretty sure he'll come running."

Lex shows his teeth again, this time menacing.

"We already spotted a Super in the city. Call him."

Lena moves to press the button, but at the last second, she pauses, finger hovering.

"You’re going to kill millions of people. The world can’t live under a red sun."

"Neither can Superman," he says, like this is a clincher. There's audible screaming, sirens outside the windows.

"They’re cheering," he says, holding up his arms like a victor.

Oh brother, Lena thinks. And now that Lex isn't looking at her, she turns back to the cabinet.

"We must put faith in ourselves, but we put faith in him. There's only before Superman and after—"

God damn it, where are the power bars? Lex usually keeps snacks in here.

"When I'm president—"

"Have you thought about marrying him?" Lena interjects, finally finding a power bar and ripping it open.

Somewhere, Lillian Luthor is telling her to close her mouth while she chews, but current company regards her, eyes wide in affront.

"What?"

"Consolidating all that power? It worked for me. Oh," Lena snaps in remembrance. "I forgot he's taken. Shame. I've heard he's got a really big—"

"Lena," Lillian suddenly enters the room, just in time to censor Lena's language. Her preternatural ability to interrupt Lena's crassness is uncanny.

Her eyes narrow. “What are you doing here?” they flick to Lex. "She isn't supposed to be here. She's supposed to be dead."

"A curiosity, isn't it?" Lex comments. "She just dropped in, unannounced. Fortuitous, wouldn't you say?" He reaches for his water again, sipping, green eyes watching over the crystal clear edge.

"Fortuitous," Lillian repeats like it's anything but. "Or suspicious."

“Hello to you, too, mother. Have you seen what your son has done to the sky?"

"I don't see why man can't be as cruel as nature," Lex chips in.

Lillian rolls her eyes, placing her bag on a side table, beige coat wrapped around her and tailored to perfection. Burberry? Lena wonders.

"Try not to quote Hitler, darling, it's not even 5 PM."

"It's apt," he gestures to the red sun like Lillian isn't impressed enough. And honestly, Lena doesn't think she looks very impressed. She looks angry.

"I thought we agreed to wait."

"It doesn't matter if Lena's a traitor, mother. She can't stop the plan."

"A traitor? Me?"

The only thing that betrays Lena in that moment is the timing. The room rings with warnings, the screens of Lex's office going a high alert red. That will be the DEO having hacked Lex's systems with Lena's access codes.

"Power offline," a disembodied voice informs the room. The sky slowly takes on the ambient hues of yellow again, and Lena smirks.

"Oh, it doesn't matter, does it?" Lillian directs to Lex.

Both Luthors turn on Lena then, individual LexoSuits materializing over their bodies.

"Now, this seems a little extreme," Lena decries. "How could this be my fault? I've been right here the whole time."

"You failed your mission," Lillian condemns. "You didn't report in. You're working with the government."

"What was your play here?" Lex adds "To get yourself caught, cornered and unarmed?"

"Well, actually, yes. I needed to stall you while we shut down all of your systems. And lure you into the open, of course. Mother, I didn't know you'd be here, but this is really working out most excellently."

She smiles, Lillian sneers. There's a beat of silence as Lex takes in her words. Lena holds up a hand as the cannon engages.

"You might want to get an antidote before you do that. I poisoned your water."

Lex frowns.

"I poisoned that energy bar," he indicates the half eaten bar in her hand, and Lena immediately spits it out.

"What? Arsenic?" she wipes her mouth.

"Thallium."

"Well, I developed an immunity," Lena states haughtily, but then she doubles over and throws up in his waste basket.

Alright, so that was a bluff. Looking up, she notices Lillian has already closed her helmet, face disappearing behind green light and metal. Lex's face is still on display, however, smirking.

"Unlike you, I actually did develop an immunity to Arsenic."

"That doesn't exist," Lena says before throwing up again. She thinks she's gotten most of the power bar up and out of her system, so she should be in the clear.

"A lovely little city in the Andes," Lex boasts. "And that's a shame," he motions to the vomit. "I wanted you to die painfully."

Then, surprisingly, he coughs. Lena coughs, too, covering her mouth.

Uh oh.

"Did you—?" Lex turns to Lillian.

"I poisoned the air," she says through the suit.

Lex reaches to activate his helmet, but it fails to engage, and Lena knows in an instant it's been sabotaged by Lillian.

Brava.

"Really, mother?" Lex coughs again. "Your one and only son?"

Lillian doesn't move, waiting for him to fall while Lex scrambles to override his systems. Lena presses the button on the Super watch and just as she's feeling light headed and delirious, Kara busts through Lex's office wall in a suit of Lena's making. She'd had to alter her own LexoSuit to do it, but it's worth it for the fashionable entrance Kara makes. Poison-free air comes rushing in through the broken glass and twisted steel, cool and fresh.

"Oh hi!" she greets the three of them.

God, she's adorable.

"Alex says the air is heavily laced with carbon dioxide? That's not cool." She tosses Lena a high tech face mask. Lena rips it over her mouth while her brother glares up at Supergirl.

"You don't look dead. Imagine that, my sister, a liar. No matter," he rises into the air, jet thrusters a menacing orange glow. "This will be easy."

Shockingly fast, he aims a shot at Kara's chest. Kara doesn't take evasive maneuvers, instead allowing the green blast to be absorbed into the suit. She grins wide, not even flinching. Lena had made her effectively immune to Kryptonite, and she can't help but smile. In this new design, she'd used pants and shoulder pauldrons, too. Lena personally prefers the skirt. Easier access. But either way, Kara's looking daunting drifting above Lex, evolved and superior in every way.

"You can thank your sister for this," she taunts.

Lex snarls, hurling himself at Kara, and they disappear through the hole in his office and out into the sky above Metropolis.

Lena's eyes travel after them until she remembers, oh yes, her stupid mother is still here. Lillian's already targeting by the time she turns, firing a burst of rounds into Lex's desk as Lena rolls behind it.

"Are you defending him or trying to kill me?"

"Does it matter?"

Lena rips Lex's desk drawers off their tracks, assisted by the hole Lillian blasted through the middle. She spots a revolver in the top shelf and grabs it, checking the ammo. Only six shots.

"Filicide seems bad for business," Lena calls back to her mother, hedging while she comes up with a plan.

“Competing agents living under the same roof is bad for business." Lillian fires another volley of shots at the desk.

Lena turns at the tail end of the fire, taking aim for the thinner metal at the hinge of Lillian's neck, the chink in the armor of the LexoSuit. The bullet strikes, and Lillian hisses, faltering. That'll take care of the AI in her weapons systems.

"You really going to support that psychopath?" Lena calls out again. "Everything you've built will crumble because of his frankly gay obsession with Superman."

"Don't confuse yourself with Lex, darling."

Okay. Lena deserves that.

Lillian rises into the air now, done playing with her prey. She takes aim at Lena over the desk, but Lena slings a small knife over her shoulder, one she'd hidden just in case in her boot. It lodges in the arm of Lillian's cannon, sparks flying.

"I'm warning you," she stares up at her step mother. "You're backing the wrong horse. It's not too late to change sides."

“I told him to lay low."

“He's not that smart.”

"But there's only one side: mine. I want you both out of the picture," Lillian rips the blade out of the cannon, chucking it into the wall. "You're insubordinate, difficult, and a constant disappointment. Parenting has frankly been a huge mistake."

"You're telling me," Lena grunts.

Another cannon rises out of Lillian's back. Her helmet moves to the side, watching Supergirl barrel roll through the sky.

"I'm guessing that's your LexoSuit modified? And you left yourself completely vulnerable? Tsk, tsk. Not wise, Lena."

"Hope, the Nanites, please."

Lillian's cannon glows red, but just as her step mother fires a swarm of silver and grey overtakes the shot. The Nanites begin to crush Lillian's suit, slowly pinning her back down to the floor.

"Firepower isn't always the answer. There are more elegant solutions," she lords over her stepmother.

Confirming the Nanites can keep her down, Lena checks the sky to find Kara flying past in a blur of red and blue. Lex is in hot pursuit and they pause, just beyond, close enough that Lena can hear their trade of insults.

"And how do you plan to stop me from telling everyone who you are, Kara Danvers?"

Kara's fists ball, clenched tight by her side.

"They won't believe you."

"And why not?"

"Thanks to Lena, we've hacked your systems. We've deleted the evidence. And CatCo just published "How Lex Luthor Divided and Betrayed America" written by yours truly. It'll be your word against mine, and I'm pretty sure you'll be thoroughly discredited."

Lex gnashes his teeth.

"I'll kill everyone you've ever met."

He aims a blast at Kara's head, but she darts past it. She flies high, and he follows. She fakes, dives, catches him by the suit and rips out his arm cannon. She eye lasers the back cannon, his jet engine thrusters. It's extremely attractive.

And it’s over. She has Lex by the arm, the metal ripping and close to letting him fall. Kara holds out a hand.

"Let me help you."

"Saved by a Kryptonian? I’d rather die."

He lets go, pitching into the wide open expanse below. Lena watches him fall fast past the broken glass. She rushes to the window framing and sees the resulting large and fiery explosion, but there's an unmistakable purple flash of a portal.

She grumbles.

Turning back to her step-mother, she finds Lillian free and reaching for a metal box from her handbag. It's a dispersal device, one Lena is intimately familiar with.

"Always have a back up plan, Lena," Lillian sneers.

But when she hits the button, nothing happens.

"I do," Lena taunts. "Did you forget who designed that, mother?"

Lillian sighs heavily, rolls onto her back on the floor.

"You made the virus inert. You changed the isotope."

"I did."

Sensing that the hard work is already done and that they can quickly claim the credit, the DEO agents finally arrive, bursting into the room, Alex at the head. Lena wants to roll her eyes, but she's got one more thing to take care of.

"That's my cue, mom," she waves at her mother's sour face. "Gotta' go."

And with the press of her Rolex watch, she portals out of the room.

Using the portal is always a little jarring, a little disorienting, but Lena is so dead set on her goal, that she shakes it off within seconds.

"Hello, again," she greets her brother, smoking and sparking and in an absolute mess on the floor of his secret hideout in the mountains.

He stares up at her with a face as pleasant as a snarl can be.

"You fixed the watch. I thought it was offline."

"We're not all as stupid as you think."

"Obviously. You found me."

"What can I say? You’ve become predictable."

Lex lets out a mirthless laugh.

"Give me a hand," he holds his out, attempting to stand, but Lena raises the same gun she found in his desk. She's got five shots left. That should be more than enough.

His eyes widen slightly in a look of recognition, and it's one of the rare moments Lena actually believes he's surprised. Or afraid. He drops his hand.

"They want me to bring you in," she tells him impassively. "But no one would know what happened to you. They'd never find your body, and you deserve it for everything you've done."

Lex narrows his eyes, stares down the barrel of the gun until he meets Lena's.

"You're being a fool, siding with them. They're aliens. She lied to you for years. She knew who you were."

"No, she didn't."

"You can only count on blood," Lex continues, attempting to stand again. "Now, stop being stupid and help me up."

Lena cocks the trigger of the gun. Lex stops moving, looking more angry now than afraid. Surprised to be defied by the person who's always been kept down on the rung below him.

"I turned over all the evidence and our correspondence, all of it, to the government. I've been leaking everything for years."

Lex actually laughs at this.

"No one trusts you," he says, the certainty in his voice an indication of the true depth of his arrogance. "You don’t even exist. You're not going to shoot me, Lena. I'm all you have left."

But that's what he doesn't understand. Lena would shoot her brother in the heart. She'd be positively gleeful to kill him. He's a blight on this planet, not the aliens. Killing Lex would make Metropolis, National City, and the world safer. Safer for Kara. But she wavers. Thinking of her wife, what would Kara think? Would she ever trust Lena again if she lied about what she'd done? Did she trust her now? What they had was tenuous and new, but how long would it last?

"Do I have to remind you of everyone you've hurt and killed? You're a thug. The hired muscle. You'll never be anything more than that. Stop pretending like there's a way out for you. They'll turn on you eventually. Everyone does."

Lena considers this. It's one of her deeper fears, that Kara will lie again, be unfaithful. But if she has to choose between putting her trust in Lex or Kara, she knows which way she'll fall.

She lowers the gun.

"You don't get to define who I am any longer," she tells him. "They're going to lock you in a hole so deep, you'll be in a different time zone."

After everything is said and done, Lena has a new name. It's familiar, the name she went by for most of her life. Lena Kieran. And Kara keeps her identity, too, despite it being the world's worst kept secret. Not that Lena's extra bitter that even the door guard at the DEO knows who she is.

“No he doesn't, Lena! Pat doesn't know," Kara always likes to argue. She's wrong. "Anyways, I can't lose my job. I’ve worked so hard! You're talking to a Nobel Peace Prize winner, after all.”

Nobel Prize or not, Lena’s still pretty sure Kara is wrong. She put in maybe four full days of work in the last year. Kara eats at least a third of her entire work day, a suspicion absolutely confirmed now that Lena knows she's Supergirl. She's found the secret pizza parlor that Kara's single-handedly kept in business.

Regardless, her secret is safe thanks to some whiz named Winn at the DEO. Her identifying data has been permanently wiped from the LuthorCorp servers, every trace of evidence gone. They've installed Sam as the interim CEO (she keeps asking about Alex), and that only leaves the eye witnesses: Lex and Lillian. They're being held in a cage, nice and tight, until the DEO decides what to do with them.

Lena's just happy it's not her. It could have been if things had played out slightly differently. If Kara hadn't believed in her, hadn't taken multiple punches to the throat and still kept coming. And they get to work together now, too, which remains a joy and a torment when it involves Kara botching many of their sneak attempts.

“You see, the key is subtlety,” Kara tells her over comms when they're investigating a radical right wing activist group against alien rights.

Lena’s not sure a being that traipses around in primary colors and can bulldoze a skyscraper with her pinky finger knows the meaning of subtlety. And she's proven right moments later when Kara introduces herself.

“My real name is Kara Liberty. I’m here searching for my birth dad… Agent.”

Lena's groan over their comms is audible for two city blocks. Predictably, things devolve from there, but at least Kara is good one thing: punching.

“We did it!" Kara celebrates with Lena after, pressing in tight for a bone crushing hug. She smells like smoke and gun powder, and it's becoming a weird turn-on for Lena.

“We took out Agent Liberty. Us.”

"We're a good team."

And Lena's happy. For once. Designing their new home, working with Kara, cleaning up Lex and Lillian's more die-hard supporters, visiting Mon-El in solitary to torture him because she misses the routine of it. He’s terrible at chess. The sick thrill she gets from beating him every single time and never letting up is unparalleled.

"Can we watch Great British Bake-off tonight?"

"Of course, love."

"Can you make potroast?"

Lena laughs. Her wife is a goofball and permanently thinking with her stomach, but she wouldn’t trade her for anything else in all the world.
