It took all of five minutes for her to complete the task, casting one last look around the room, more gleeful than melancholy.

    She launched back around the corner of the staircase, panting.

    Rio was standing casually with her thumbs tucked into the pockets of her overalls. Her expression was so neutral and measured that Agatha fancied she might start whistling a tune. There wasn’t a single hair out of place on her head; every lock of it remained in those two perfect braids. The only hints that anything at all had just happened were the two subtle spots of colour blooming high on her cheekbones.

    Rio smiled up at Agatha, extending her palm.

    Once Agatha had assured herself Rio was alright, she let her gaze fall to her mother. Evanora was slumped on the hallway chair – a chair Agatha had never seen used, purely for decoration. Her eyes were scrunched up in what looked like a nasty symbiosis of fear and pain, her face mottled and wet with sweat, or tears, Agatha couldn’t make it out from here. Her robe hung open, revealing her thin, ascetic nightdress. The pallor of her skin was sallow - tinged yellow - like she was about to throw up.

    What the hell did Rio just do?

    Agatha refused to swoon like a princess being rescued from her tower by a handsome knight. But a little part of her wanted to.

    ‘Shall we go?’ Rio questioned, with the same intonation as if she was asking Agatha whether she wanted a coffee.

    Agatha descended the final few stairs, crossing the hall swiftly to lace her fingers into Rio’s. She offered her a shaky smile.

    ‘Let’s go.’

    ‘Great.’

    Agatha chanced a glance at her mother. Her voice seemed to stick in her throat, and she had to forcibly swallow twice before she felt she could talk again.

    ‘Bye, ma.’

    Evanora didn’t look up, simply lifted one hand in a gesture of surrender, farewell, or both. Her fingers were trembling.

    Rio opened the door expertly, as if she’d been there a thousand times, as if the whole experience wasn’t dysregulating for her, unfamiliar and anxiety inducing. Agatha spied the minute tremor in Rio’s right hand as it grasped the doorhandle, and she squeezed her left in thanks.

    She was staying strong for Agatha.

    No sooner had the door clicked shut behind them, and the outside air was on their faces once again, did Agatha heave in a ragged breath, bending at the waist like she’d been punched. ‘Fuck, Rio, fuck. What about your job? You love your job. What about Halloween? Jen? Alice? Lilia?’ Then, in the tiniest voice possible, fresh tears drowning her baby blues, ‘What about my mom?’

    Rio steadied her with a palm to her lower back and the other cupping her cheek. ‘It doesn’t matter.’ Gentle, reassuring, grounding. ‘They don’t matter.’ Agatha’s breathing slowed, nodding at Rio, who was taking her bag from her and sliding it onto her own shoulder.

    ‘Agatha,’ Rio said, cradling her name in her mouth like it was the rarest and most precious of delicacies. ‘You matter.’

    Agatha kissed her, and Rio tasted the salt of her sorrow on her mouth. She looked into Rio’s eyes, and Rio saw the very moment Agatha’s last wall came down, the physical retreat of her defences made visible by the barely perceptible softening of her gaze, the minor growth of her pupils.

    Rio thought suddenly of Lenny, and the skin they shed every month or so, leaving behind a papery husk and emerging like a butterfly, vibrant and new. Rio imagined Agatha would always be a palimpsest, bearing the scars of her early life, but right here, right now, she was shedding the skin of her past, discarding it crumpled and empty onto her mother’s doorstep. Agatha took one shaky step down, the first official step away from her house. The sun chose that moment to break out from the heavy cover of cloud, illuminating her face, so beautiful and light that Rio felt it like a gunshot wound to her breastbone. She tugged Rio down the step until they were level again, the older woman helpless but to follow her like she probably always would, devoted and sure.

    Agatha cleared her throat softly, tossing her hair over one shoulder and shaking her body like a wet dog just in from the sea, physically eliminating the distress from herself. ‘Let’s go get breakfast from that place with the Colombian beans you like.’ Rio beamed. ‘Okay.’

    Agatha stalked towards Rio’s motorbike, left parked haphazardly in their haste. She threw Rio a smile over her shoulder, reaching out to tug on a braid, making Rio keen. ‘I’m driving.’
