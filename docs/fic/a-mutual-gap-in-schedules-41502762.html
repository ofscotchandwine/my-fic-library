    “Um.” Kate looks at Anna, who looks at Kate.

    Yelena gestures to the short haired woman on the floor - “This is Sofia. Sofia is also very sorry for trying to kill you.”

    Sofia smiles surprisingly politely for someone with cuts slicing through their arms. “Very sorry.”

    Kate nods, trying not to swallow her tongue in amazement. “I’m Kate.” She tries to look somewhat pleasant.

    “They know who you are, Kate. They were trying to kill you, remember.” Yelena waves an arm behind her, “Bathroom, for your stitches.”

    Sofia nods, Anna stares with those glazed eyes at the dead Widow once more.

    Kate sees the outside of the safe house for the first time when Yelena makes the call.

    It looks as unimpressive outside as in. The full extent of the wood rot is clear in the sunlight, logs stacked around the foundations to hold the damn thing upright. The grass surrounding is overgrown, knee height, almost, with nothing but yellowing fields in every direction. There is no established road in sight, simply a wide dirt track that sinks into sludge a few feet on - a deep blue Volvo sits an inch deep in mud a quarter mile along. Kate doesn’t think it’s a particular leap to assume it was stolen.

    There are random stacks of planks and cobbled concrete half toppled around the property, sinking unappealing piles of fungus and debris into the earth. Kate tells herself that the fresh air compensates for the view.

    Sofia and Anna stay inside, left to mourn, wrap the body - Kate is unsure of the bond the Widows share, whether they are sisters or rivals or strangers amidst a shared victimhood.

    They look at Olga’s body with expressions that could almost resemble grief, but fall flat amidst the clear confusion they both feel.

    Sofia made quick work of her stitches - it’s an unsettling thought, how practised her hands must be at sewing her own flesh. Kate tries not to consider Yelena too much, with that revelation.

    When the blonde in question nods at her to step through the front door, Kate recognises the companionship - there is trust offered, to stand alongside the assassin as she makes contact with others in this network of spies. She thinks Yelena might be cautious, even, as her eyes flick to the ex-Widows in clear warning, shoulders set and gun still hanging by her side, even with Sofia’s back turned, ducking into the bathroom, and Anna hunched on the floor.

    The look Yelena gives them over her shoulder as she crosses the threshold is fleeting, heavy with distrust - Kate is very aware that something could be said of how much the assassin resembles a retreating cat, ears flattened as she steals glances on the way out.

    “Melina is… an old ally.” She mutters as they walk around the safe house.

    Kate nods, toes a rotten chunk of wood out of her path. She is quickly growing accustomed to these details rarely being her business, and doesn’t ask any questions. Yelena steps onwards, a few feet, hesitating only to holster her firearm before dialling from the burner.

    It takes three rings for an answer.

    “Melina. There is a problem.”

    It is instantly clear to Kate that this is not a call Yelena enjoys. She stares at the sky with clear restraint, breathing visibly slow. “This is important.” A hum, unenthusiastic. “Левиафан.” Kate can hear garbled speech on the other end, high pitched and tinny, impossible to dissect. Yelena snaps, interrupting, “Well, the Widows sent to kill us did. So, what is it?” She turns around and begins to pace. “You think I don’t know that?” Exhale. “There is one dead, two freed.”

    She brushes past Kate, steps growing agitated. “They will be soon. Stop avoiding the question, we are against a clock.”

    Kate stares at her nails, pretends to assess her cuticles.

    Yelena reaches a particularly unimpressive pile of rotting wood, this assortment featuring mushrooms and squirrel shit. Pauses in her tracks to ask - “So why the hell did these Widows know of it.” Sighs, lengthy, after a response. “Do you have any names? Anyone involved?”

    Paces back past Kate. “Well- make it your jurisdiction.”

    The following silence is the longest yet, Yelena hesitating and grinding her lower jaw from side to side, contemplative. “Thank you.”

    She lets her words sit on the dead line for a second before humming and flipping the phone shut, no pleasantries offered. Stares at the sky for a few moments, not acknowledging Kate standing awkwardly alongside the wall with hands to her hips as she tries to find something more interesting to look at than the strange assassin.

    Kate swallows, wishes it wasn’t so loud, “You, ah… anything?”

    Yelena turns over, with all the leisurely manner of someone who had not a care in the world. “You have gotten us both in very deep shit, Kate Bishop.” She huffs. "You owe me drinks for this."
