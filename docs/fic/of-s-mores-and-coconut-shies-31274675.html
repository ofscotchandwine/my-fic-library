“Oh my god, Alex, I’m so bored,” Kara complained from her spot on one of the sofas, where she was sitting polishing cutlery. “These spoons are so reflective that a vampire wouldn’t be able to see themselves in them.”

Alex snorted a laugh from behind the counter, and Kara looked over just in time to see Alex straightening her back out. “It’s late Tuesday morning in November Kara, what were you expecting?”

“Something a bit more fun than polishing cutlery.”

Alex propped her hands up on her hips and raised an eyebrow at her. “Would you rather be on your hands and knees cleaning the bottom shelves? Because I can definitely trade places with you if you want.”

Kara winced. “Absolutely not.”

“I gave you the nice job, so quit complaining,” Alex said, throwing a balled-up napkin at Kara. They both watched it as it landed on the ground halfway between them, and Alex shrugged. “Eh, it went further than your paper plane did.”

“Napkin aerodynamics are a bitch.”

“Tea?”

“Yes please,” Kara replied with a cheer, waving her tea towel and a spoon in the air.

Tuesday mornings were always quiet in Rilassante, the coffee and cake shop that she and Alex owned and ran. Kara always supposed that it was because Tuesdays were like less fun Mondays. Mondays sucked, but at least most people got back to their classes or offices and shared stories about what they did with their weekends. Tuesdays had none of that. When they’d worked out that it was their quietest day of the week, they’d decided to designate it a cleaning and paperwork morning.

Alex brought her drink and a stack of paperwork, and sat opposite her to go through some invoices for cake orders. Kara was nearly finished with the cutlery, wrapping each set in a napkin and placing it back in the basket.

She glanced outside and saw people hurrying past, bobble hats pulled down low over their ears and their hands jammed in their pockets to ward off the freezing air. It was always cold this time of year, but for the last few days it had been especially cold, and Kara had even seen a little frost on some of the plants in the basket outside her windowsill.

Their café was located just off the National City University campus, in an area which seemed to cater to the students more than anyone else. There were several bookshops that sold only academic books, stationary stores, computer repair shops, coffee shops, and even a store that sold nothing but NCU merchandise. It was a dynamic place to work, and there was always something happening. Kara especially loved the students who came in sometimes and sat for hours in the corner reading worn paperback textbooks and making notes in them. It was what she’d done when she’d been an English Literature major, camping out for hours in her local coffeeshop with a pile of books, her laptop, and an endless supply of food and drinks. Now, working at the other end of the spectrum, she was happy to be the one providing joy to stressed students.

But today there were no stressed students, and it was so quiet that she’d actually run out of jobs to do now that she’d finished her cutlery. The piped music, the scratching of Alex’s pen on the paper, and the occasional sound of a car going past on the square were the only things she could hear. She stood up and stretched, taking her time about it, then mooched over to the counter to put the basket of super-shiny cutlery underneath the till.

After Kara’s fourth sigh, Alex dropped her pen on her paper and looked over to where Kara was leaning over the counter.

“Why don’t you go and see your girlfriend?” Alex asked with a withering look.

Kara felt herself blush immediately. “She’s not my girlfriend!” she protested, standing upright and fiddling with the cloth Alex had left behind on the counter.

Alex sat back in her seat and folded her arms. “Ah, but I know that you want her to be.”

“That’s—”

“Honestly, Kara, you’ve been going on about this Lana for—”

“Lena,” Kara interjected. “Her name is Lena.”

“Ahummm,” Alex said, gathering all her papers into a near pile. “It’s been what, a month, or six weeks? Six weeks of you two flirting with each other in the most hopelessly shy ways. I’ve seen you when she comes in here to get her hot chocolate; you blush just like you’re doing now. And I know for a fact that you haven’t read a single one of the books you’ve bought from her shop whenever you go over there to see her. Why don’t you just ask her out already?”

“Because …” Kara started, then snapped her mouth shut. There really wasn’t any good reason why she hadn’t asked Lena out yet. No good reason at all. And yet she still hadn’t managed to gather her courage enough to actually do it.

The first time she’d seen Lena, it had been at the end of a very long, hectic day. Kara was feeling frazzled and flyaway wisps of her hair kept getting in her eyes, but she didn’t have a minute to fix it. Alex and Nia were frantic in the kitchen trying to keep pace with all the food orders they’d had, and Kara was serving drink after drink after drink. They were run off their feet, but all that seemed to fade into nothingness when Kara turned to face her next customer.

Dark hair twisted up in a messy bun, piercing green eyes behind a pair of black-framed reading glasses, and a huge blue and white scarf wrapped around her neck and trailing over a black pea coat. There was a small, rainbow pin on the lapel of her coat, a tote bag with the obvious corners of books poking out of it, and she was wearing the prettiest smile that Kara had ever seen. Her whole bad day was forgotten as the world around the woman in front of her seemed to dissolve into nothingness.

“You look like you’re having a busy day,” the woman said, and dear god, her voice? Her accent? Kara pinched herself behind the counter to check if she was still awake or if she’d fallen asleep standing up. Yup, she was definitely awake, and this woman is definitely real and oh shit, get it together Kara!

Kara recovered herself enough to find her voice. “Yeah, yeah, it’s been a day, that’s for sure,” she croaked, then cleared her throat quietly. “What can I get for you?” she asked, brushing those damned wisps out of her face once more and wincing when she felt herself smear something damp on her skin.

The woman glanced over her shoulder. “I can give you a minute, if you like? I’m the only one in the queue.”

“You are?” Kara asked dumbly.

The woman laughed. “I am,” she said, leaning forward and reading Kara’s nametag, “Kara.”

Kara slapped a hand on her forehead loudly enough that the customer picking up their takeout drinks at the end of the bar looked up in confusion. “Hah, sorry. Yeah, it’s really been a day. It’s okay though, thank you. What would you like?”

“A hot chocolate wiiiith …” the customer ran her eyes over the syrups on offer on the shelf behind the counter, “hmm. That’s quite the selection. With rose syrup, please.”

“Turkish delight hot chocolate coming right up. And uh,” Kara felt herself flush a bit and hoped that it wasn’t as obvious as it felt, “and what name should I put on the cup?”

She smiled again and raised an eyebrow, her eyes bright with interest and curiosity. Oh damn, look at those dimples. Kara felt herself start to sweat.

“My name is Lena.”

Lena. A name as pretty as she was. Kara was smitten immediately, in a way that she’d never been before. The coffee shop was busy and she met a lot of people, but never had she been struck breathless in the way that she’d been with Lena.

It was only a few days after that that Lena had come back to Rilassante, this time with a red scarf and a thick archaeology textbook under her arm, asking for more hot chocolate and a few pastries. She learned that day that Lena was doing her part-time postgraduate degree in early modern history, and was doing an elective on archaeological principles and techniques so as to better understand the excavations currently happening in the old Jamestown site in Virginia. During that visit, Lena also told her that she worked in one of the smaller academic bookshops around the corner from here, and smiled hopefully when Kara said she’d come by some time.

Kara headed over to the bookstore once a week on Lena’s workdays, Wednesdays and Thursdays. It was a cozy, dark wood paneled bookstore, with a large squashy sofa to the side of the till area; Lena was always curled up in it reading something if there were no customers near the till. There was a little rainbow flag on a stand next to the till, and together with the pin she’d seen on Lena’s lapel that first time, they gave her hope. She swallowed her nerves and straightened her shoulders.

“Is it … is this yours?” she’d asked, pointing at the flag on her first visit while Lena scanned Kara’s book and slid it into a branded canvas bag.

Lena nodded, hearing the hidden question. She was clearly biting the inside of her lip, and it made her smile a little lopsided. “Is that okay with you?”

Kara felt a rush of giddy relief at Lena’s admission and just grinned. She toed off one of her Vans then picked it up to show Lena the rainbow laces. “Mhmm, more than okay.” Lena’s bright smile had kept her feeling happy and optimistic for the rest of the day.

Ever since then, they’d struck up a friendship. Lena would come to the coffee shop twice a week and order the same things every time even though she always asked about the specials. Between customers, they’d talk about whatever book she was reading, the weather that day, or whatever else came to mind. Travel, the biggest news stories, funny customers that Kara had served since she’d last seen her. Kara couldn’t get over the warm feeling that she got every time Lena pushed through the door with a shy smile on her face and a new book in her hands.

Gradually, it became obvious to Kara that Lena was only coming to Rilassante to see her; she suspected that Alex (or more likely, Nia) had told Lena when the best times to come by were, because she always managed to appear when there were fewer customers and therefore when they’d have more time to talk. And Kara tried to time her visits to the bookstore when they were both on their lunch breaks; she’d bring Lena her favorite treats and Lena’s cheeks would pinken behind her reading glasses. She always bought a book, but the books were just excuses to see Lena, and they both knew it. Lena’s smile was knowing every time she rang her up.

Every shy smile they shared, every time their fingers brushed when Kara passed Lena her treats, every disappointed look in Lena’s eye when Kara had to go back to work. They all built, and built, and built, and sat in Kara’s chest with a wonderful weight of hope and anticipation that she’d never felt before, with anyone. She liked Lena, really liked her; every interaction with her just made her want to learn more about her, but she was too shy to do anything about it.

And then there’d been the Marshmallow Incident last week, as Nia called it. Lena had come striding into Rilassante with her hair in a sleek ponytail, wearing slacks and an open suit jacket over a shirt. Kara could hear her heels clicking across the floor as she approached, and she’d promptly spilled tiny marshmallows all over the counter and the floor. That day, Lena seemed to have her self-confidence firmly in her grasp, teasing Kara with bravado that she’d never seen before. She’d rolled the sleeves of her jacket up and leaned over the counter, and ordered her hot chocolate to go because she had to teach a seminar in twenty minutes. She’d lowered her voice when she’d ordered, making her drink sound like sorcery, and Kara had promptly forgotten every word in the English language. Lena had only chuckled, the sound rich and deep, but her smile when she’d dumbly passed Lena her drink was the same soft, shy smile she saved just for Kara. Lena had boldly held her gaze for a long moment, during which Kara felt her heart hammer in her chest and her breath catch in her throat.

It was almost like Lena was testing her somehow, and she’d got the answer she wanted when Kara stammered her goodbyes with her face heating up. And when she’d come in on Friday afternoon for her regular order, Lena was back to her normal pea coat and giant scarf, green this time, and her hair back up in that messy bun. But there was a glint in her eyes that hadn’t been there before, and even though Lena was back to the slouchy cozy student look that Kara was more familiar with, the memory of the rolled-up sleeves and the throaty chuckle wouldn’t leave Kara’s mind. Alex teased her, Nia teased her, Winn teased her. By now, they all knew about Lena and the spilled marshmallows.

And then there was yesterday morning. The morning had been a disaster; the delivery people had been late, they were missing some items and had doubles of others, and on top of that, one of their ovens was on the fritz. Kara’s mind felt a little frazzled from all the stress, and it wasn’t helped when Lena walked into Rilassante looking as beautiful and soft as ever. Her cheeks were pink from the cold and her hair was loose and wavy, and she was wearing a dark blue bobble hat on her head. It was warm in the shop, and she undid her coat, revealing a stripy red and blue sweater underneath.

“Hi, Kara,” she said, pulling her hat off and stuffing it in her pocket, before fluffing up her hair and smiling gently at her.

“Hey,” Kara replied, feeling the familiar breathlessness that just came with the territory whenever Lena came around. “No uh, no suit today?”

Lena let out a breathy laugh. “No, that was just for the other day. I have to teach a few seminars throughout the year for my postgrad, and that just happened to be one of the days. I have to dress up.”

“You looked … you looked so different. In a good way!” Kara blurted, then panicked. “Um, not that I don’t like the way you usually look because I do! I didn’t mean to imply otherwise! I really like how you usually look, you know, like this and— and when you’re all soft in your cashmere sweaters and things,” Kara babbled, feeling heat rise in her face and pressing her hand to her forehead. “Oh, I just …”

“Yeah?” Lena prompted when Kara lapsed into silence, the dimples deepening in her cheek as she tilted her head. “You liked the suit but you like this more?” she asked, sweeping a hand over herself and indicating her casual clothing.

“Mhmm,” Kara replied articulately, her face crimson, and Lena’s smile brightened.

“I’ve got a stock check to do all day today and tomorrow, so I’m in on weird days. But it means I’m off on Wednesday and Thursday,” she said. Her words were not quite a question but there was a definite uptick to the end of her sentence that had Kara wondering what she meant by it. “No classes, either.”

There was a noticeable, but not uncomfortable, silence between them while Kara made Lena’s drink to go. Alex popped her head out of the kitchen at the back and waved at Lena, before giving Kara a surreptitious questioning thumbs-up with her eyebrows raised. Kara waved her off, and Alex rolled her eyes before ducking back into the kitchen.

Kara was just adding extra marshmallows to Lena’s drink, because she knew by now that Lena had a sweet tooth, when she heard Lena clear her throat. She turned to look at her, and noticed that Lena’s face was pink now from more than the cold. She looked a little more timid than she had earlier, as though she wasn’t sure of something.

“You know, I’ve never seen you wearing anything other than this uniform,” Lena said slowly. Her voice was warm, as usual, but there was an undercurrent of nerves that Kara hadn’t heard before. She pointed at Kara’s black jeans and shirt with her branded apron over the top, and Kara looked down at herself in confusion. Why is Lena talking about my uniform?

“I guess that’s true,” Kara replied with a laugh, putting Lena’s drink on the counter and sliding it towards her. “We only see each other on the days I’m working.”

Lena reached out for her drink, her fingers brushing Kara’s then settling on top of them. “Are you okay?”

Kara blew out a breath. “Sorry, yeah. We’re having a stressful day. Everything seems to be going wrong and … ugh.”

It took Kara a moment too long to notice that Lena was stroking her fingers with her own, an affectionate look on her face. “I’m sorry that you’re stressed,” Lena murmured, and Kara was transfixed by the low timbre of her voice and by the tingles that Lena’s touch was sending up her arm and down her spine. “Is there anything I can do to help?”

Kara swallowed, and looked into Lena’s lovely green eyes. “I— n-no, but … this helps.”

“Does it?” Lena asked almost in a whisper, sliding Kara’s hand from the drink and holding it between hers on the counter. “How about this?”

“Yeah,” Kara managed. Lena’s hands were so soft …

And then Lena’s phone rang in her pocket. A flash of irritation zipped across her face, and she let go of Kara’s hands in order to fish her phone out. “I’m really sorry,” she said, looking at the screen with her finger hovering over the answer button, “but it’s my boss, and I’ve got to take this.”

“Oh, oh, it’s okay,” Kara said, straightening back up and pulling her hand off the counter, missing Lena’s immediately. “You gotta do what you gotta do.”

“Thanks, darling,” Lena replied, shooting her one last smile before picking her drink up and answering the call. “Hey Sam, I’m just on my way back now. Yeah, yeah … no, I put the box of chemistry books next to the biochemistry ones … they’re upstairs. I’ll be back in a minute, don’t try to move them by yourself with your broken arm …”

Kara was still reeling from the darling by the time the door swung closed behind Lena.

With great difficulty, Kara pulled herself away from her memories and back into the present. She was still leaning over the counter with Alex staring expectantly at her, her paperwork all in a neat pile on the table next to her cup of tea. When Kara finally met her eyes, Alex raised an eyebrow.

“What?” Kara asked suspiciously.

“Kara. I’m going to be a good big sister here and give you some real talk, okay? Lena just about threw herself at you yesterday. I could hear you both from the kitchen. I don’t know how much clearer she could be. I saw her holding your hand over the counter. She basically told you that she wanted to see you outside of work. She told you that she’s free Wednesday and Thursday,” Alex said, standing up from her chair and picking the balled-up napkin from the floor. “Just … ask her out! I know that you want to. She clearly wants you to. She probably would’ve asked you herself yesterday if she hadn’t had to leave. You’ve been dancing around each other for weeks. She’s beautiful, intelligent, kind, and friendly. Just … Kara, just ask her out and get to know her better.”

Kara gaped at her sister, who was by now standing in front of her with an expression on her face that was full of fond exasperation. “I don’t want you to miss this chance with someone you obviously like very much, especially when she obviously likes you too.”

Alex is right. Alex is totally right, Kara thought to herself. Why hadn’t she been braver? All of Lena’s smiles, her gentle blushing, their wanting to spend time together. Lena’s absolutely blatant flirting, she now realized, while wearing that suit. Lena holding her hand.

I want to hold her hand.

“What … what,” Kara spluttered, “where would I even take her?”

“It’s November. Take her to a carnival or something.”

Suddenly, an idea flashed through Kara’s mind, and she had the perfect idea for a date. She stood up straight, feeling both determination and nervous energy thrumming through her.

“I’m going to do it. I’m going to ask Lena out!” Kara stated, her hands trembling. “I’m going to— oh, do you mind if I just, y’know, go to Reign’s Books? Can’t ask her out if she isn’t here.”

“I’ll drag you there myself if you don’t go there on your own two feet right this minute,” Alex muttered. “Apron!” she yelled at Kara, and, by the door, Kara laughed as she undid her apron and tossed it onto the back of a nearby chair.

Reign’s Books, the bookshop where Lena worked, was around the corner and down the street from Rilassante, on the opposite side of the block. She made her way towards it at a clip, her heart racing in her chest. Her courage was up and she was determined not to it fail her before she had a chance to ask Lena on a date. She was halfway down the street before she realised that she’d run out without a coat and it was bastard freezing outside. Thankfully, she could see the bookshop coming up on her right, and she stopped just at the side of it to look at her reflection in her front-facing camera. Since it hadn’t been a busy morning, her hair didn’t look like quite the disaster it usually did with all the flyaways and streaks of chocolate and coffee, so she pulled it out of the hair tie and fluffed it up as best as she could in the frigid wind.

She took a deep breath, and opened the door to the bookshop.

For a shop that was in the middle of a stock count, it was remarkably tidy. There were a few boxes stacked up near the till, and some of the bookcases were empty, but other than that, it looked mostly the same as it always did.

A woman with a sling over her shoulder stood up from behind the till when the doorbell dinged. She was tall and pretty, with a kind face and a warm smile. “Hi, welcome to Reign’s Books— oh, are you Kara?”

Recalling Lena’s phone call the day before, she realized that this must be Sam Arias, the owner of the store, with the broken arm. “Hi, yes, I am. Are you Sam?” she asked, walking up to the till.

“Guilty as charged,” Sam replied with a cheeky grin. “I take it you’re here to see Lena?”

“How did you know that?”

Sam raised an eyebrow, her grin widening. “Blonde hair, glasses, cute, dressed all in black, and … yeah, those are your eyes, too. Lena’s talked about you,” she said, holding her good hand out over the till for Kara to shake. “It’s nice to meet you. I feel as if I know you already.”

“Ohhhh,” Kara replied, drawing the word out and feeling hot under the collar. Lena talked about her to her boss?!

Kara must have looked as confused as she felt, because Sam started laughing. “In addition to being Lena’s boss, she’s also my best friend in the world. And as such,” she said, leaning forward over the desk, “I do have to tell you that if you break her beautiful heart, I will break you, and I don’t need both arms to do it.”

“Understood,” Kara squeaked, “but, is it normal to give someone a shovel talk when they haven’t even asked— I haven’t even asked Lena out yet! She might not even say y—”

Sam gave a one-shouldered shrug and a good-natured smile. “Eh, I don’t often get to give someone a shovel talk on Lena’s behalf. Thought I’d take the opportunity when it presented itself.”

“Fair en—”

“LENAAAA!” Sam suddenly bellowed, startling Kara. “Don’t worry, sunshine. She’s not going to say no to you,” Sam said with a wink, just as Lena’s muffled whaaaaaat? came from the back of the store somewhere. Sam turned to face the direction of Lena’s disembodied voice. “Kara’s here!” she shouted.

There was a thud and the sound of a door creaking open, and Lena walked onto the shop floor, half covered in dust with her hair in a messy ponytail. She was wearing blue jeans and a green shirt with a speech quote that said don’t make me repeat myself – history. Kara snorted a laugh, because of course Lena would wear a geeky history shirt.

“Hey,” Lena said when she reached the till.

“I like your shirt,” Kara said, and Sam snickered behind her hand.

Lena let out a burst of laughter and looked down at her shirt, brushing dust off it and batting at her thighs. “I’ve been sorting out the storeroom by myself because someone broke their arm,” she said, levelling an unimpressed glare at Sam. There was no heat in it whatsoever, the reprimand offset entirely by the clear love in her voice for her friend.

“What happened?” Kara asked.

“I fell out of my daughter’s treehouse,” Sam replied, rolling her eyes at herself. “It was only about four feet to the ground, but I landed awkwardly and here we are. Now I get to direct Lena as if I’m a cartoon supervillain and she’s my goon. Move this box over here. No, three inches more to the left,” she ordered in a mockingly deep voice, and Lena slapped the top of her good arm.

“Go away,” Lena said with a smile. “There are some invoices with your name on them.”

“Right, riiiight, got to give you two some time alone,” Sam said, dodging out of the way of Lena’s hand and throwing a hugely exaggerated wink to Kara. “Those invoices aren’t going to file themselves.”

Kara decided that she liked Sam.

When they were alone, Lena turned to Kara, her shoulders relaxing. “Hey,” she said, smiling affectionately. “I wasn’t expecting to see you today.”

For a moment, with Lena in front of her looking tired but happy, she almost forgot why she was here. All of a sudden, her nerves returned in full force as she remembered what she’d come here to do. She cleared her throat and shifted her weight from foot to foot, Lena watching her with slight concern, before she stood up straight.

“So yesterday you said you were free on Wednesday and Thursday,” she started, clearing her throat a second time, “and I— I wondered whether you might want to spend one of those nights with me. I mean, oh god, that’s not what I meant at all,” she pinched the bridge of her nose and squeezed her eyes shut for a few seconds. She could hardly bear to look at Lena. Had she really just asked her to spend the night? For shit’s sake.

She looked up and saw that, rather than being offended or something by Kara’s inept insinuation, Lena was standing up straight too, and- that was hope there on her face! It bolstered Kara, who shook off the rest of her nerves.

“What I meant was, for these last few weeks, I’ve really enjoyed getting to see you on the days you come in, and the days I come here. I think about you all the time and you fluster me but in the best kind of way. And I love that you love Turkish delight hot chocolate because up until now I honestly believed I was the only one. And I think that … I don’t think I’m the only one feeling this, so I was wondering whether I could ask you out on a date. I would really like to get to know you better. So— so, Lena, would you like to go on a date with me?”

“I would love to,” Lena breathed, her eyes shining with delight. The corners of her eyes were crinkled, and her dimples were deepening in her cheeks as her smile grew on her face. “Kara, I’d love to. I think about you all the time, too. I like you. Seeing you is always the best part of my day,” she said, then blushed as if she hadn’t meant to say it. She shrugged shyly and held her hand out, and Kara took it. She linked their fingers together, sugar mixing with dust.

Kara’s heart was singing. She hadn’t read the signs wrong: Lena liked her too! She liked Lena, and Lena liked her back. Cool, cool, cool. God, she felt like a high schooler again. She wanted to dance around the bookshop, start singing, or shout from the rooftops, but there were more important things to do. Like confirm the date.

“Is this week okay?”

“I was trying to hint to you yesterday,” Lena said, swinging their hands a little, “but I think you were too stressed to notice. Did everything get fixed?”

“Yeah, everything’s fine, and I’m sorry I didn’t pick up on it until too late. But I’m here now,” she replied with a smile, bumping Lena’s shoulder with her own.

“You’re here now,” Lena murmured, turning towards her and laying her other hand on Kara’s shoulder. “Is tomorrow too soon?”

“Tomorrow’s perfect,” she said, unable to keep the smile off her face. “I know just where to take you.”

Lena raised an eyebrow. “Oh really?”

“Yes, but it’s a surprise.”

“I like surprises,” Lena said, her voice low. She leaned up and pressed a lingering kiss to the corner of Kara’s mouth. Kara closed her eyes and sighed happily. “I want to kiss you properly. I’ve wanted to for some time now,” Lena whispered near her ear, sliding a hand into her hair, and Kara felt herself shiver all the way down to her toes at the feel of Lena’s lips on her skin.

She let out an honest to god whimper when Lena pulled away from her with a devilish smile and a brush of her fingertips on Kara’s cheekbone.

“I’ll give you my number,” she said, backing away and picking up a pen from next to the till. “Text me the details? For our date.”

“For our date,” Kara repeated, knowing she was blushing but not caring one bit about it. “I’ll text you. Wear something comfortable. Relaxed.”

“No suit?” Lena asked with a smirk.

“Let’s save that for the second date.”

“Confidence,” Lena said, folding the piece of paper with her number on in half and holding it out to Kara between two fingers. “I like it.”

“It is pretty cold,” Lena confirmed, shivering a little when Kara let her go. When Kara moved towards her again with her arms up, intent on warming her up, Lena laughed and held her hands up playfully. “No, no, I very much want to kiss you senseless and if we start again we’ll be here all night. And, freezing parking lot isn’t exactly romantic!”

Kara stuck her bottom lip out in a pout, but spoiled the effect by laughing. “Alright, alright. Text me when you’re home?”

Lena smiled softly at the concern, and hesitated by her car door. “I will. But, I kind of don’t want to go.”

“I don’t want you to go either. It’s kind of cheesy but I wish that this night wouldn’t end,” Kara admitted, shoving her hands in her pockets. “But I also don’t want you to freeze. I’d prefer you not to have frostbite on our second date.”

Lena chuckled. “Good point. I might fancy a hot chocolate tomorrow, keep myself warm and all that until Friday. Know anywhere good?” Lena asked cheekily. She opened her door and tossed her coat onto the passenger seat, then climbed into the driver’s seat.

“I might know a place that you’d like,” Kara replied airily with a wink. “I’ll see you tomorrow, then?”

“Until tomorrow, darling,” Lena replied, and Kara stepped back from the car. Lena plugged in her phone and turned on some kind of rock music, reversed out of her space, and with a last smile at Kara, made her way towards the parking lot exit. Kara waved when she reached it, and Lena waved out of the window.

By the time Kara reached her own car, she was practically walking on air. She was so happy that she had practically danced across the parking lot. She was probably on Red Forest’s security cameras, but she found that she just didn’t care.

Alex was going to be so insufferable when she told her how well the date had gone, Kara thought with a smirk as she settled into the driver’s seat. Kara was willing to let her take a little of the credit though, for practically shoving her out the door in order to finally ask Lena out. Alex was going to be so smug. Kara couldn’t wait to tell her.

And, perhaps, to introduce her to Sam.

Kara turned the key, and thought once more about Lena. She could feel in her bones that this evening had just been the start of something great, and she couldn’t wait to see where they ended up.

And tomorrow? Tomorrow she’d see Lena again. See her smile, hold her hand, and kiss her, and hopefully not spill marshmallows all over the place when she made her the best Turkish delight hot chocolate that had ever been made.

She couldn’t wait.
