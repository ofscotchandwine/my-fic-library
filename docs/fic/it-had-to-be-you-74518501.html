Summary

agatha harkness did not want another friend, thank you very much. she definitely didn't want to be set up to spend an evening with a stranger at a party when she could have been at the bar, or better yet, at home in her pjs. but sometimes, even if you don't want something, you get it anyway. that's just the way of the world. and in the rarest of cases, that thing you didn't want...it might just be exactly what you need.

    “I’ll need to write lesson plans and a syllabus. It might not be a whole summer.” “Then we go get coffee, you write, I’ll draw. Problem solved.”

    Agatha couldn’t help but smile. Jen was the only friend she really had left post-Wanda, and much of that was because they had always acted like boomerangs for each other. One pushed the other away and with the force of a hurricane, they came right back. When they were stable together, they were like two cacti—they could stand next to each other for years and coexist peacefully, but any closer and they would start jabbing each other with their spines. Rio was softer, the only person in Agatha’s life besides Lilia who was willing to hug a cactus without worrying that she might bleed.

    “You’ve got yourself a deal,” she said finally.

    “The more important question,” and the teasing note in Rio’s voice returned. “Do you drink coffee the way you drink hot chocolate?”

    “You mean does it take me twenty minutes at the counter to order it, or a mobile order a mile long?”

    “Uh huh.”

    Agatha sighed in defeat. “If they have oat milk, iced latte with two pumps of caramel. If they don’t, cold brew, half and half, light ice, but I switch up the flavors sometimes. That’s not that bad. I just like things the way I like them. You and your black coffee, that is a tragedy.” “They don’t have hazelnut creamer at a diner,” Rio argued. “My coffee isn’t usually so soulless but come on, desperate times.”

    “Fine, fine. So coffee. But what if I don’t want coffee?”

    “Pizza,” Rio said. “Or sushi, I know a really good sushi place.”

    “Raw fish?”

    “Yeah, see, I think you tricked me with your chicken caesar wrap. I think that’s something you order to make it look like you’re spontaneous and adventurous. But at home…I have no proof of this, but I think you’re a buttered noodle adult.”

    “They’re good,” Agatha mumbled.

    “I can’t talk. The number of nights I’ve been too tired to make dinner and ended up having pretzel bites and homemade cheese sauce that I justified by telling myself cheese has protein…I don’t even want to admit to how many times it’s happened.” Rio paused. “Okay, what about breakfast instead of coffee sometimes? You seem like a woman who likes chocolate chip pancakes and I don’t have to be at work until 10 most days.” “And I only teach three days a week during the school year. Those days are stacked with classes, and I’ll still have grading and office hours, but if you ever want to get breakfast and not do our jobs during rare hours off. There’s this rumor I keep hearing that a work/life balance is good for you, or whatever.”

    “You do have workaholic energy.”

    “Yeah, well. Easy to have when you don’t have anything else.”

    Agatha had spoken before she could stop to think and paid for it immediately with a sick sinking feeling that settled somewhere in her lower abdomen. And you wonder why you don’t have anything else. She forced her eyes to stare at the TV so intently they started to water a little, praying if she was quiet enough, Rio would assume she just went back to watching the movie. For a while, that seemed to be the case, but as had grown to be her norm, Rio didn’t stay quiet for long.

    “Everyone has something,” she said softly. “By default, that makes you part of ‘everyone.’ And one of the best things we get to do in our time on Earth is find people we like to have somethings with. I like having somethings with you, Agatha. So we can work or we can watch more movies or we can have more adventures, but you’re not getting rid of me that easily.”

    “You’ve known me for four days.”

    “I have,” Rio agreed. “And call me crazy, but I’d like to know you for a lot more than that. Any of my friends can tell you, I bond for life. Like a…I don’t know. What animal does that? Is it a penguin? Sea otter?”

    Agatha snorted. “I’d give you shit for knowing that, but it would look really bad when I admit to knowing that wolves do, too.”

    “Makes you think about calling someone a lone wolf,” Rio said thoughtfully. “Lonely wolf might be more accurate.”

    On the TV, the credits started rolling, an “up next” display on the screen signaling the start of a late-night marathon of the show.

    “Keep it on,” Rio said. “We’ll plan our next adventure. I was thinking a late movie if we’re going for something fun.”

    “I really do have to write this syllabus. If I don’t do it early in the summer, I’ll be pulling all nighters in August trying to get ready.”

    “The drive-in is out of the city, so maybe we grab dinner somewhere casual, get some work done, but when it’s movie time, all the work gets put into a bag and doesn’t get touched for the rest of the night. What do you think?”

    “Drive-in?”

    “Never been to one? Oh, Agatha…I’m gonna teach you how to live.”

    “Okay, so dinner. I pick what books I’ll be tormenting my students with, you get your blind bag designs done. I like it.”

    “Work and play.”

    “Work and play,” Agatha echoed softly. “Could be nice, right?”
