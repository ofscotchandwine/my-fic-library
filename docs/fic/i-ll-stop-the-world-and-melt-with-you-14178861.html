An Instagram check at a red light, a single heart of a notification prompted Kara to take off faster than before as soon as the light turned green. A few slight interpretations of bike traffic laws later, she tossed her bike down in the backyard and yanked open the sliding back door. “Samantha!”

    “Kitchen!”

    Kara dropped her backpack at the kitchen threshold, pulling her phone out of her pocket. “You need to look at this.”

    “Hey.” Sam was at the island, sleeves pushed up and chicken thighs on the cutting board in front of her.

    “Nice to see you too. Be careful, I’ve got chicken hands.”

    “Hi,” Kara greeted begrudgingly, pulling up Instagram and holding the phone in front of Sam’s face. “Will you please look at this?”

    “That is a very nice photo of dirty green powder.” Sam squinted. “What am I supposed to be reacting to?”

    Kara checked the screen to make sure the right thing was still up. The photo she had taken of the ingredients for the Matcha Lion’s Mane Mushroom swirl popsicles was showing as was intended. “Look at the likes.” She held it up again.

    “LkLuthor?” Sam quirked a brow, grabbing the chefs knife set aside and cutting into the chicken. “That Lena?”

    Kara huffed. “Yes, that’s Lena. Lena who kissed me two weeks ago. Lena who kicked me out and Lena who has ignored every form of contact I have tried using since. Until now. Liking my dumb fucking Instagram.”

    Sam finished chopping one of the thighs, tossing it into a bowl and starting on another. “Oh, so you’re finally ready to talk about what happened between you and Lena, huh?” That stopped Kara. She groaned, collapsing onto a stool and dropping her head onto a clear section of the counter. “Yes,” she mumbled into the granite. Immediately after everything had gone down at L-Corp, she had gone straight to Alex and Sam. A little too shocked for tears, all she had disclosed was the kiss before refraining from talking about it entirely.

    “I’m all ears,” Sam said. “Oh, but please chop while you spill.” She motioned her elbow to the colander of green beans.

    Kara obliged, pulling everything her way. “Why would she like a photo if she doesn’t want to talk to me?”

    “Maybe she’s scared.”

    “She’s the CEO of a billion dollar company.

    She can have the decency to respond to a fucking text.

    Liking a photo instead of initiating a productive conversation is petty and immature.” The latest piece of green bean to meet the force of Kara’s knife flew to the floor.

    Sam shrugged a shoulder up to itch the side her face, hands still chicken occupied. “Are you this mad because you’ve let it stew for two weeks or are you actually super pissed?” Kara put her knife down. “Both?”

    “My sweet Kara.”

    “Samantha.”

    “You have the emotional intelligence of a cardboard box.”

    Sam finished up with the chicken, taking the knife to the sink. “And I say that with love.

    What happened with Lena?”

    Cutting the veggies gentler, Kara groaned again. “It’s stupid.”

    “Okay.” Sam flicked the water on with an elbow. “Why don’t we just finish this up, pour some red wine and you can tell me what’s going on.”

    Kara grabbed another handful of green beans from the colander.

    “As long as it isn’t a Bordeaux.”

    “It’s not what she said, it was how she said it,” Kara drawled, draining wine from her glass.

    Arroz con pollo in the oven, Sam leaned forward to grab the Tuscan red bottle from the coffee table, topping herself off.

    “And what did her tone say?”

    “To get the fuck out of her office immediately.

    It was borderline mean.” Kara tucked a knee to her chest.

    “Do you know who the woman who walked in was?” Sam asked.

    Kara let her mind wander back.

    She hasn’t really been focused on the person doing the interrupting.

    She remembered the panic in Lena’s eyes, her scrambling to get out from beneath her, and the bite in her voice. “I don’t know.” She gulped down another mouthful of wine, burning her throat slightly. “I was more concerned in actually getting out.

    I barely buttoned my shirt. I basically did the walk of shame through a corporate office building and then had to ride my bike .”

    “I’m sorry sweetie.”

    The soft sympathy in Sam’s voice was enough to prompt tears to Kara’s eyes. “Fuck.” She tipped the wine glass back to her face, sliding a finger underneath her glasses to swipe at each eye once she put it down.

    “I really don’t want to cry about this.”

    “Cry, don’t cry.

    Do whatever you need to do.” Sam scooted across the cushions so she could wrap Kara in a hug.

    “She hurt your feelings. You’re pissed, you’re sad. It’s okay.”

    Kara let herself stay with her head on Sam’s shoulder.

    “I just want to be able to talk with her about this.

    That’s the worst part, that I want to fix this and I can’t do anything unless she responds.” She successfully kept her tears at bay, the telltale lump in her throat dissipating. “Liking my damn Instagram post…”

    Sam laughed.

    “Do you think maybe that was her olive branch?”

    “More like olive fucking twig.”

    “Okay, so it wasn’t a great gesture.” Sam agreed.

    “But it is a gesture.

    Do you feel like if you reached out now you’d get a response?”

    “I don’t know, that’s why I’m talking to you!” Kara eyed her wine but didn’t go for it. “You and Alex have the perfect relationship.”

    Sam nearly snorted into her glass.

    “Excuse me, whose relationship have you been watching for the last ten years?”

    The oven timer beeped, Kara lifting her head so Sam could get up to take the pan out. “You’re still together, aren’t you?”

    “You’re derailing,” Sam reminded, talking louder over the clang of the oven door shutting. “This isn’t about Alex and I, this is about you.”

    Kara slumped over the arm of the couch so she could properly yell back.

    “What am I supposed to do then?”

    “Ever humored the idea of trying to talk to her in person?” Sam poked her head out to the living room, fork in hand.

    “Also, come get some food.”

    Keeping the wine with her, Kara dragged her feet to the kitchen.

    “What, am I supposed to wander the streets and hope I run into her.

    It’s not like she’s going to show up at the market, this isn’t a Hallmark movie.”

    “She has an apartment.”

    Kara switched the wine for a plate, serving herself.

    “She’s barely ever there, she basically lives at the L-Corp office.”

    “So go there,” Sam said plainly, spearing a piece of chicken.

    “Scene of the crime.”

    “Just show up at her office?” Kara sucked a stuck grain of rice from her thumb, taking both the plate and the wine back to the couch, Sam following.

    “Worst she can do is kick you out. Best case scenario, you guys can hash out all your emotional problems.

    Or make plans to.”

    It wasn’t reassuring that the worst-case scenario was all Kara could picture. Jess refusing let her through and citing that Lena had taken her off the visitor list, being let through by an unsuspecting Jess and Lena demanding she leaves, or walking in on Lena with another woman. Kara knew it was stupid to let her mind wander and create unrealistic situations to talk herself out of doing what she needed to do.

    The uncomfortableness settled tight in her chest.

    “Part of me wishes I could just shake this off, forget about it.

    We kissed and she didn’t call, it’s not the first time I’ve been rejected in my life.”

    Sam waited until she finished chewing, washing her bite down with wine.

    “Probably because you genuinely, really and truly like her.

    Kara, you were friends first.

    God, at brunch the other week- I was telling Alex this.

    It was like you were exuding this positive, beautiful energy just because you were around each other.”

    “My life’s not a movie, Sam.”

    Sam let her fork drop.

    “Maybe not, but I really don’t think this is something you can brush aside. You came in here, guns-a-blazing, about Lena being scared and hiding behind social media. If something that small is going to set you off, you owe it to yourself to do everything you can to address and resolve the issue.”

    Kara sighed, holding her wine between her knees as she picked a large piece of red pepper from her plate and flicked it onto Sam’s.

    “So I gotta show up at her office, huh?”

    “Mmhm.” Sam poked a foot into Kara’s thigh.

    “Extend your olive twig.”

    Samosas.

    That was what Kara chose to bring with her Monday afternoon.

    They were one of Lena’s favorites and she was almost guaranteed not to have eaten yet, it only being a bit after one. The elevator ticked up to Lena’s floor, Kara tightening her grip on the brown paper bag the closer it got. Doors opening with a ding, Kara stepped into the familiar office space, first receptionist waving her over to Jess.

    Jess had a phone tucked to her ear, typing quickly at her computer.

    Kara waited through a few, ‘uh-huh’s’, and finally, “No, like I said before, she is not taking any calls this afternoon. I will tell Miss Luthor you reached out and pass that all along.”

    She spotted Kara after that, eyes widening and holding up a finger.

    “Yes, yes, sounds good.

    You have a good one, thanks, buh-bye.”

    Jess hung up the phone.

    “Kara.”
