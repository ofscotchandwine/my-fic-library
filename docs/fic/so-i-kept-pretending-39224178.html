Coffee

Lena let out an irritable huff as she made her way down the sidewalk. Project Non Nocere had looked so promising. Steve and his enemy inmate had, briefly, gotten along… before all hell broke loose. Before Steve’s body had ramped up the mental pain of suppressing his survival instincts into utter physical agony.

Lena only had a couple of strategies left to try, feeling the doubt bubble up in her throat. She did not have much faith in the last-ditch efforts she would soon be making. Project Non Nocere would be a failure. Maybe it simply wasn’t possible to fix humanity’s basic personality. Science wasn’t about faith, of course - but her analytical instincts were often correct. 

Maybe there was no way to avoid the hurt.

Lena yanked hard on the door to Noonan’s, still caught up in her own brain on the multiplexing issues involved - how were the Kryptonians able to produce such high purity optic fiber for Myriad? - as her caffeine withdrawal headache caught up with her. She should have just made some coffee at home, but perhaps the walk and the familiar coffee shop would help jog better ideas.

“Haven’t seen the two of you in a while! Same as usual?”

The barista at the counter smiled, somewhat uncertainly, as a startled Lena glanced up. Two of you? Lena whirled around to find a familiar blonde’s face, an awkward and unusually guarded glance to match Lena’s increasingly exasperated one. “Yeah”, the Kryptonian said quickly, stepping forward with her $20 in hand. “Same as usual.”

Lena held back the urge to roll her eyes, and stepped to the side of the counter to wait for her order. The barista eyed them briefly, before handing Lena’s cup to her coworker, and beginning work on Kara’s sugar-filled monstrosity.

“You know buying me coffee doesn’t make up for calling me a villain, right?” Lena hissed.

“This is awkward, Lena, I want out of this as quickly as you do.” Kara said stiffly.

As adept as their usual barista was at making Kara’s drink, the other came back with Lena’s espresso first. Lena grabbed her cup and bolted for the door.

And that’s when the screaming started.

Two men had entered the shop, wearing distinctive black jackets, with a platinum star on the left breast. Their hoods were pulled up, silver masks covering their faces. One held some sort of assault rifle in his hands, while the other had a pistol holstered - along with zipties - on his belt.

Lena halted in front of the doorway, eyes wide. Do I still get quarterly assassination attempts on this Earth?, she wondered dimly, at least vaguely grateful that Supergirl was nearby. Her heart in overdrive, and mind working like sludge, it took her a moment to recognize the masks as what must be some sort of Earth Prime variant on the Children of Liberty.

The second man stepped forward, grabbing Lena’s arm. “Ms. Luthor, you’re coming with us”, he said gruffly, as he jerked her arm forward to throw her between the two men. Grabbing her bag, he reached in to find a phone, and smashed it on the ground. He shoved her forward, and she stumbled outside towards a waiting black van, feeling her arms pulled behind her and being clasp in the zipties as she panicked.

Supergirl, she kept thinking, her heart racing. Kara, where are you?  

A twisting knot formed in her stomach, as she was tossed into the back of the van. As angry as Kara was at her - as she was at Kara - she still had somehow assumed that Kara would defend her, when push came to shove, when her life was on the line. But doubt crept in her mind, a vision of trapping her best friend in kryptonite, leaving her in a cold fortress. Kara wasn’t going to stop this. Why would she?

“Wait!” cried out a familiar voice behind them, Kara pushing past the cafe door before coming to a halt. “You want to take me too.”

“Are you crazy, lady?” said Lena’s escort. She couldn’t see Kara out the door, but could see where the two men had turned back towards the cafe.

“Whatever leverage you need Ms. Luthor for, you’ll want me too. I- I’m her friend. And I wrote the CatCo piece on Agent Liberty last year, with Supergirl’s help.”

The men looked bewildered, but the second muttered “Boss, we gotta go” to the first, and the first gave a nod. The sounds of shoving, zipties, another shattering of a phone - and suddenly the blonde tumbled into the van behind Lena, before landing with a thud on the floor. The doors shut behind them, and Lena could hear the heavy steps as the men walked to the front of the van.

“Kara, what are you doing?!”

“Uh, following you?” Kara said, wincing as she glanced at the scrape on her arm. Unlike Lena, her hands were tied haphazardly in front of her instead of behind. Kara gathered herself, taking a deep shaking breath, noting the two benches in the van, and the barrier between them and the drivers up front. Kara brushed a hand behind her ear before pulling herself towards Lena. She gingerly reached out, cupping Lena’s face, eyes searching with meaning that Lena couldn’t quite process at the moment. “Are you alright?”

Given the recent betrayals, Lena was thrown off by the sudden show of affection - but she noted the curious feel of warm metal pressed behind her ear. “What are you-”

“NO TALKING”, shouted the man now loaded in the passenger’s seat, from the other side of the van cage. “And get to the other side, blondie.” An engine rumbled to life. 

Kara obediently pulled back, scooting to the other bench, flexing her hands nervously and eyes dropping down to examine where she was scraped. Lena stared in a daze, her brain slowly catching up. She was being kidnapped by the Children of Liberty - of course, Luthors were alien lovers on this Earth. And Kara was here, and had put something on Lena - a tracker? Didn’t the DEO have some sort of tracker on Supergirl? And Kara was bleeding, which wasn’t weird after the rough treatment… except, she was Supergirl. 

Lena’s voice dropped to a whisper, numb and confused, “Kara, is your… friend going to show up?”

Kara furtively glanced towards the men in the front of the van, unsure if they were listening. Concern and resolve were written on her face as she glanced back at Lena. “She’s, uh, away for a few days. On a business trip.”

That’s when it dawned on Lena. Rumors of what had happened to Superman a few years ago, brief concerns voiced in conversations she had overheard when the DEO was trying to figure out how to tackle Reign.


Kara had solar flared.

Liberty

“She says she knows Supergirl - and that she wrote that article about us.” A pause, a rumbling of voice on the other end of the line - that frustratingly, Kara couldn’t hear. The man in the passenger seat turned around to look into the back of the van. “Hey blondie, are you Kara Danvers?”

“Yes,” Kara answered. If I survive this, Alex is going to personally find a way to throw me into space.  

“She says yeah.” Some more chatter, a goodbye. The passenger turns to the driver. “Liberty says this turned out better than he hoped - that Danvers bitch is a real thorn in his side.”

Kara thought back on what “she” had written - when she first arrived on Earth Prime, she went through the entire catalog of her past articles, to commit them to memory. It had been an utterly bizarre experience, hearing her own voice describe events she had never witnessed, people she had never met. She knew on this Earth, Lockwood’s identity hadn’t been outed yet, if he were the leader at all. Her article had described the inherent fascism in the rhetoric of the Children of Liberty. The group seemed smaller on Earth Prime compared to Earth 38 - the joint humanitarian work with the Luthors and Supergirl had not left as much appetite for raging xenophobia in the general public. But the group also seemed more extreme, feeling backed into a corner from the Super-Luthor alliance. According to Kara’s unpublished notes, she felt the group would devolve into terrorism, sooner or later.

Aching and sore, Kara glanced over to Lena, knowing she must feel the same. They had been in the car about an hour, on opposing hard wooden benches that made it difficult to keep their balance, on a rumbling and bumpy highway out of National City. The caging made it hard to see where they were going, but Kara thought it looked like they were heading north.

She tilted her head back against the wall of the van. Kara didn’t like wearing her communicator day-to-day, but Alex had insisted that even as a civilian, Kara at least needed to wear her tracker. The DEO had no reason to monitor her when she was out of commission, but someone would eventually notice she was missing. At the very least, she had dinner planned with her sister that night.

Kara again looked over to Lena, who was half turned away, withdrawn. Exhaustion, fear - guilt? - written on her face. Kara sighed, wishing they could talk. Priority #1 would be to figure out what exactly Agent Liberty wanted to do with Lena. From a journalistic perspective, the purpose of political kidnappings usually fell into two categories - to acquire some sort of ransom, or use as a warning.

After what seemed like an eternity, they pulled up a driveway and rolled to a stop. Kara peered out the front again, seeing the shape of a small house in front of them. The doors of the van opened, and the two prisoners were dragged out.

“Put blondie in the kitchen. Liberty wants to talk to her,” said one man to the other. Lena was dragged away, wide-eyed, starting to struggle to stay with Kara until Kara shook her head. There was just no point. Lena let herself be led away, her anxiety-lined face turned back to Kara.

Kara's zipties were cut, actual handcuffs replacing them, her arms now behind her body as she was pushed down into a kitchen chair. Another masked figure emerged from what looked to be a living room.

“Kara Danvers,” said the voice she had heard before.

Kara gave a small nod. “Ben Lockwood.”

Ben froze. Though she couldn’t see his face, his mental math could be assumed. Denial? Feign confusion? Or maybe just shoot, and move on? After a moment of stillness, he landed on a decision. “How?”

“Well, I am a reporter.”

Ben removed his mask, and Kara wasn’t sure how to feel. Earth 38’s Ben had been a stooge for Lex - charismatic, but ultimately, not particularly intelligent. It wasn’t wise for him to remove his mask and confirm that he was Ben Lockwood. On the other hand, if he was planning to kill her, removing his mask wouldn’t matter either way. If he were smart enough, he’d know that her reporter notes were likely stashed in a place that could be recovered in the case of her disappearance, but she couldn’t be certain she could give him that much credit.

“So, why are you here? Some sort of death wish?”

“What do you plan to do with Lena?”

Ben scoffed. “Setting an example, for all those alien-lovers out there.” He pulled out another chair from the kitchen table, straddling it as he glared at her. “People like you. People like Lex Luthor.”

She would never get over how weird Earth Prime was. “You’re not going to do yourself any favors by executing her - people will see your tactics before your message. You’re too fringe and too unconventional for it to do anything but cause people to write you off as insane.”

“Yeah?” Ben said, in smug amusement. “And what does little reporter Kara Danvers propose instead?”

Kara bit the inside of her lip. She was absolutely going to regret this, but she didn’t have much of a choice. “Ally with Lex.”

“You’ve missed the entire-”

“Lex and Supergirl are fighting. Lex thinks Supergirl has developed… a God complex. Become too big for her britches. She wants to be invulnerable, to have no weapon made against her for even the worst case scenario, complete power.” Kara leaned forward, face steely and grim. “You can drive that wedge deeper.”

Oh, she was definitely going to regret this.

Ben stared at Kara, nonplussed, as though she had said the sky was green. Kara wondered if that’s what her face looked like as she sat next to Nia, who had been adamantly clapping for Lex as he received his peace award. “And you know this how?”

Kara shrugged, leaning back again. “Confidential.” 

Ben stood suddenly. “How do I know I can trust what you’re saying?” he demanded angrily. She had offered him a silver platter of opportunity, but only if the absurdities she was spouting were actually true.

“You don’t,” Kara replied simply. “But you can ask Lex for something you want, and see if he gives it to you. If he doesn’t want a deal, you can still make an example out of Lena, if you want.”

Ben crossed his arms, leaning back against the kitchen counter. He considered Kara for a moment, before replying, “And what should I do with you?”

Kara’s knee bounced nervously for an instant, before she stilled, swallowing hard. “Lex might give you something for me, too.”

Fables

Kara’s heart skipped a beat, as Ben’s lackey shoved her through the basement door, and she nearly took a stumble down the stairs. “Hurry it up, blondie” he taunted in her direction, Kara rolling her eyes in the dim light back. She made her way to the bottom, and looked up to find Lena.

A modicum of stress left Kara’s aching shoulders, as Lena looked up back at her, face mirrored in similar relief. Lena was sitting on the floor, seeming no worse for wear from when they got out of the van, her wrists chained around a radiator. Kara’s exhaustion was starting to sink in as one of her cuffs was undone. She didn’t bother to struggle as she was forced on the ground, her wrists bound again on the other side of the radiator.

The guard stomped his way up the stairs and closed the door, leaving the only light source in the basement as the small rectangular window on one side. Kara was grateful for the light. She didn’t know when she’d begin metabolizing sunlight again (the DEO hadn’t been able to figure out a pattern from the few times she’d flared, and active experimentation seemed unwise), but at least she wouldn’t be trapped in the dark - her least favorite feeling.

Lena and Kara sat in silence. Kara’s head rested against the radiator, eyes closed, trying to control her breathing.

“Kara…”

Lena couldn’t think, could barely breathe while Kara was gone, uncertain about what was happening to her or when (if) she’d see her again. Now that she was here, and Lena’s adrenaline was dropping, she didn’t know what to say. What the hell were you thinking, following me?

“You solar flared?” Lena said weakly.

Kara opened her eyes, looking at the ground, and nodded against the radiator. “A Jarhanpurian. She had kryptonite, so it had to be fast. My stores were already low.”

Lena worried her lip. “Lockwood?”

Kara took a breath. “I tried to convince him to ransom us to Lex.”

“Both of us? Kara, why?”

Kara closed her eyes again. She used far less energy after solar flaring, but her caloric needs were still higher than a human’s, if only due to body temperature. She was tired. “Lena, everyone knows us at Noonan’s, and we were very publicly kidnapped. Lex was going to ask about me anyway. I just… I just need to buy some time.”

Lena thought to the tracker affixed behind her ear. Either Kara would get her powers back, or the DEO would find the tracker. Either way, it was best if they stick together, Lena knew that much. “Time? Then why not stay with the Children of Liberty?” She watched Kara, still resting her eyes, seeming content to remain silent - or at least too exhausted to speak.

“God, if I could go back in time, I would’ve stayed home,” Lena muttered. 

“That works less well than you’d think.”

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Kara turned to her side, opening her eyes, facing Lena. “I never told you that you were right. About the kryptonite.”

“Kara-”

“No, hear me out. I… I was scared. But I was dumb.” Kara took a breath, shook her head. “Did you ever hear about the day I terrorized the city?”

Lena froze, then slowly nodded.

“I had been exposed to red kryptonite. It- it’s terrible, it makes the worst thoughts you have feel real, every doubt or resentment…” Kara paused. “The DEO didn’t have a way to stop me. I set stuff on fire. I threw Cat Grant off a building just to taunt her. I broke my sister’s arm. We’re lucky I didn’t murder anyone. That the DEO was able to synthesize a cure in time.”

Lena started to speak, tried to say something, but-

“Did you know, on some of the other Earths, the supers used to band together? And they each had a backup plan, a failsafe, for if any of them turn evil? So that the rest of them can take the brainwashed one down. I’ve experienced red kryptonite, I’ve seen silver kryptonite - I know one person shouldn’t be invincible, not if they can be brainwashed. I just never wanted to be a weapon.”

Kara shook her head, sliding back to rest against the wall, stretching her legs out from where they were curled under her. “I meant to tell you that you were right, after I told you I was Supergirl, after I could tell you. But then… things just seemed good, and I just couldn’t bring myself to-” Kara cut herself off, looking down. “I didn’t want to fight. But we should’ve fought. We should’ve talked about me throwing your last name in your face. Asking James to search you. All of it.”

Kara turned towards Lena again, her voice deepening from fighting unshed tears. “Lena, I’m sorry.”

Lena trembled, an ache in her chest, trying to find the words she needed, but stumbling across different ones instead. “I trapped you in kryptonite. I stole from you. I tried to brainwash the world. Kara, why did you get in that van?”

Kara paused, eyes shifting between Lena’s, her mouth parting slightly before closing again. It unexpectedly made Lena’s heart ache - a hope buried long ago, before things got complicated. Kara turned away, staring resolutely at the ceiling. “I thought Lockwood would want to kill you, make an example of you - and he does. That’s why we need to get you to Lex, Lena.”

Lena wanted to scream. Her life or Kara’s? And Kara had chosen her? She pushed her head against the wall too, taking deepening breaths, trying to calm down. 

In the silence, it occurred to her that - for all that Kara didn’t seem to care - she left her piece hanging. She blinked back tears. “I’m sorry too, Kara. For everything.” 

For a few moments, everything was quiet. A tranquility seemed to linger between them, weight lifted from the air, a sense of comfort that had so long been lacking. Lena’s mind drifted, from an exploding planet, to a family full of secrets and conditional love. “You hide things so you don’t lose people. I run from people who hide things. I guess we were bound to explode.”

Kara smiled softly. Lena smiled back.

“So you… threw Cat Grant off a building?” A barking, crying laugh reverberated from Kara, and Lena quickly joined in on the cry-laughing too. Their voices echoed slightly off the walls, a brief reprieve from their tense day as their bodies shook and their smiles landed on each other. This, it had been too long since this had happened. Their faces softened in the shaded light, as the eastern-facing window no longer caught the direct rays of the afternoon sun.

A sudden bang caused them both to jump, as the door to the basement swung open. A familiar figure made his way down the steps, as his eyes landed on both women, prompting an unusually wide grin.

Otis.

One of Lockwood’s men shuffled down behind him, unlocking each woman from the radiator, and locking their wrists up again. Lockwood’s man pushed Lena up the stairs first, and Kara made her way up not too far behind. As Otis shoved Kara to the top of the landing, her eyes rose over Lena’s shoulder ahead, landing on Lockwood shaking hands with a certain egomaniacal lunatic.

Kara took a deep, shaking breath. The danger for Lena - the mortal danger, anyway - was over. He wouldn’t kill his sister. Lex turned, meeting Kara’s gaze, a smug and delighted grin across his face. She, of course, knew she wouldn’t be so lucky.

The Kryptonians had a phrase, from their own sort of Aesop’s fables, where a space traveler struggles to not burn up while reentering an atmosphere - uwedh chiv shom vav Daxam. Earthlings had various similar ones, of course, with a common English version being: Out of the frying pan, into the fire.

Lex

Lena awoke with a jerk, a blinding headache making her groan. She fought the dizziness and the nausea, trying to remember… anything. Opening her eyes, she saw Kara sprawled next to her, still knocked out. Lena’s mind raced - Lockwood, Lex, the whirring sounds of a helicopter outside. Otis striking Kara, Kara crumpling to the floor.

Lena looked around, realizing with another jolt that they were back at the Luthor mansion. She hadn’t spent much time at the Earth Prime location, making being “home” all the more uncanny. Her hands and legs were bound in twine, making it difficult to sit up. Lena struggled to push herself against the wall, closer to where Kara was laying.

A pained whimper escaped Kara’s lips, causing Lena to hover over her, concerned and hoping to talk. But Kara was still unconscious. It was then that Lena noticed the sickening green glow of Kara’s bindings, the small trace of radioactive gleam in her veins. Lena let out a small cry, as she pulled Kara into her lap, stroking her hair and wondering what to do next.

The tracker, Lena thought, reaching behind her ear. Another fragmented memory of Lex reaching behind an unconscious Kara’s ear, frowning. A scoff before Lex turned to Lena for another search. Some snarky comment to Lockwood about evacuating.

The DEO tracker was gone.

“Hello, sis. Have a nice nap?”

“Lex, let her go,” Lena said, turning to the sound of the voice as Lex entered the room, a sheathed knife in hand. He stood comfortably in the center of the living room, having all the time in the world.

“And why would I do that?” Lex smirked. “I have a captured, flared out Kryptonian. I have a nice store of kryptonite. I want to see how the two get along.”

“You said you weren’t murdering anyone-”

“I said no more unnecessary homicides.”

“Killing her isn’t necessary! ”

“Come now, Lena, did you really think that I’d let her live?”

Lena floundered. No, she hadn’t thought Lex would let Kara live - but the goal of their partnership wasn’t to capture Kara, it was to make Non Nocere work and use it on the Earth. No one was supposed to die. And before this morning… she thought it would be the right thing to do. 

Back in the Liberty hideout, as she had sat in the dimly-lit basement alone, she could only panic about Kara. The Kara who Lena had once abandoned to breathe toxic, burning fumes. The same Kara who would intentionally get herself kidnapped, just to give Lena her only lifeline to safety. Lena feared the worst - that Lockwood would kill the extraneous captive, or figure out who Kara really was.

Lena had believed that you could never hurt someone you truly cared about, and Non Nocere was the solution for an essentially heartless world. But as she sat on that cold basement floor, Lena knew that, for all Kara had hurt her, it wasn’t true to say that Kara didn’t care for Lena. And Lena knew - when she wasn’t suffocating behind the wall of her own cognitive dissonance - despite her revenge on Kara, she still loved her too.

If she had gotten Non Nocere to work, and flipped the switch, numbing the world into falling in line - god, she really had become Lex - what would love be, and would there still be a heart left to warm it?

Another groan sounded from Lena’s lap, as Kara’s eyes fluttered open. She immediately fixated on Lena’s face above, letting out a gentle breath.

“The Maid of Might awakens!” Lex exclaimed in amusement. Kara crawled up to sit, back against the wall, casting a concerned look at Lena before her eyes found Lex. She looked entirely unenthused.

“I owe you my gratitude,” Lex said casually, with a flippant smile. They may as well have been talking about the weather. “I was planning to re-engage the Children of Liberty - thank you for making that so easy. They now have a stockpile of weapons, and I have a wonderful incoming Assistant Director at the DEO.”

“I’ll make sure to bring a fruit basket on Monday,” Kara answered sarcastically. Her eyes drifted down to Lex’s hands.

“Mm, I have a gift for you,” Lex said, unsheathing the knife. Nausea filled Lena’s stomach as Kara shivered at the glistening green blade. “Otis! Would you assist me, please?”

Footsteps approached from a neighboring room. Otis waltzed in, unperturbed, as Lex gestured towards Lena. Lena unsuccessfully tried to shrink away as Otis grabbed at her, hoisting her over his shoulder; he took a few steps across the room before dumping her unceremoniously on the couch. Lex nodded towards Kara, and Otis yanked her up by her kryptonite bindings, arms above her head, hanging her wrists on a hook on the wall. With one last wink at Kara, he stepped back towards Lex.

“Any changes in status?” Lex muttered to Otis. The two men talked briefly, confirming the security precautions around the invisible mansion. Not that it mattered. While Lena had lent the Luthor mansion to the superfriends during the Reign era, none of them knew where it was on Earth Prime.

Lena shifted her attention to Kara, before blinking in confusion. Kara’s lips were turned slightly upwards, seemingly in a smidge of relief. What on Earth could she be smiling about?, Lena thought, watching Kara stare down at her feet. Kara was wiggling her toes in her shoes.

Lena tilted her head in wonder. Kara had once mentioned to her offhand - after coming out, before Lena’s betrayal - what it felt like to metabolize sunlight. But relief didn’t make sense. Even if Kara was making record time to un-flare, it would take hours to get anywhere near super strength. Lex would kill her sooner than that. Immediately, even, if he noticed her powers returning.

At an unknown location, with no powers, no tracker - no way out.

Kara looked up at Lena, face serious again, but with a hint of reassurance. I know you’re the paragon of hope, Lena thought to herself fruitlessly, but I think our luck has run out.

Lex finished his conversation with Otis, who shuffled out the door. As Lex turned towards Lena, she said the only thing she could think to say, remembering back to the truthseeker on Lex’s arm. “You don’t want to be alone, Lex. But you won’t have me in your life, not if you do this.”

Lex smiled. “Oh, Lena, I’m the only one you have,” Lena clenched her jaw in anger, before opening it again, but Lex continued first- “Do you really think she’s told you everything?” 

Lena scoffed, looking up at Kara, who would assuredly knock down this nonsense. But Kara only stared back at Lex, eyes wide, a bewildered crinkle forming on her face.

“Kara?”

Kara looked back at Lena, guilt and shame playing across her face, realizing that denial might have worked before the long silence. Kara glanced down, in both pain and puzzlement, before eventually looking back up at Lex. “Red Daughter?” she whispered. Lex nodded.

Lena felt her throat closing, the panic of an unknown betrayal setting in again. But there wasn’t a contest this time - trust, conviction, rage winning out. “No, Lex, this is what you do. You drive a wedge. It’s not going to work this time.”

“More’s the pity,” said Lex with a grin. “But it’s merely temporary. I have a backdoor in your tech, Lena. If you can’t come to your senses on your own, I’ll just make you.”

He stepped further towards Kara’s side, Kara trying to squirm away as the kryptonite blade came closer. “Any last words, Girl of Steel? And don’t make a big, stalling speech - I’ll cut you off if I get bored.”

Kara stared up at the ceiling, refusing to look at Lena, barely fighting back her tears. “Lena-” she whispered, closing her eyes, a war between what she really wanted to say and what she thought she should. She cleared her throat, starting again. “I’ve been living on borrowed time since I was thirteen. Everything, everything since then, has been bonus.” 

Kara’s eyes dropped from the ceiling, finally meeting Lena’s gaze, and Lena could see the tears gathering as she shed her own. A sad, wistful smile played across Kara’s lips, an ache filled her voice. “And I’m glad I got to spend part of it with you.”

Kara huffed and looked away, composing herself again. Her jaw set, she turned her face towards Lex, staring him down. He sneered, whispering a word quietly enough so only Kara could hear. “Coward.”

Lex took one last step - as Lena screamed - and plunged the kryptonite knife into Kara’s abdomen.

Wounds

Kara screamed, head thrown back, thrashing wildly as the knife pierced her skin. Her veins, already slightly green from the shackles, darkened as the effects of the embedded knife took hold. Lex stood back, leaving the knife in, to watch as blood began to pool at Kara’s side.

Lena screamed, kept screaming, not knowing what she was saying other than telling Lex to get away. Kara choked, coughing up blood onto her shirt. After some moments of strained heaving, Kara’s eyes rolled back in her head, and she collapsed against her restraints, her legs no longer holding any weight.

Lex laughed. Lena could only sit and watch in horror, as her friend’s life hopelessly slipped away.

Without notice - no more than a millisecond’s worth of rumbling, anyway - a loud series of crashes rang out as dust filled the room. Lena instinctively turned away, closing her eyes as chaos broke loose. She heard shouting behind her - and the distinctive buzz of a portal opening. The sounds of guns and punches started to ring as the portal closed, as Lex’s men came storming in.

Lena opened her eyes, finding the ceiling collapsed in, bright lights and more dust from above partially obscuring the scene ahead of her. But what she could make out was J’onn and Alex, the former pulling Kara off the hook and laying her on the ground, the second examining. 

“If you remove the knife, she’ll bleed out.”

“No, J’onn, we know she’s metabolizing sunlight again. At this point, the kryptonite will kill her faster than bleeding out will.”

Alex decisively pulled out the knife, immediately following with pressure against Kara’s abdomen. Alex looked agonized as she rapid-fired instructions at J’onn, before gesturing to him sharply. He quickly reached down, hoisting Kara’s unconscious body in his arms, and flew away.

Alex turned around, briefly surveying the ongoing fight behind Lena, who was hearing snippets of Brainy and Nia. This must be a small operation, thought Lena, normal DEO agents couldn’t be trusted to properly attack Nobel Peace Prize winner, Lex Luthor. Alex’s eyes quickly darted to Lena’s, with no look of surprise on her face, sprinting over and drawing out a knife of her own. Given the sheer magnitude of stern fury on Alex’s face, Lena was more than a little concerned, but Alex quickly swiped down the twine bindings holding Lena’s hands and feet together. Alex yanked Lena up from her position on the couch and dragged her backwards. “You really had to pick today to get kidnapped, didn’t you, Luthor?”

That’s fair, Lena thought numbly, watching Alex press the comm on her face. “We got them both - WITHDRAW!” Alex yanked Lena across the room, towards the collapse, and Lena could make out an opening in the middle of the debris.

Is that a ship in the middle of Luthor mansion-

Doors shut, and Lena looked around, wide-eyed.

“Hello, Lena”, said Brainy, in his perpetually calm, smooth cadence. “Welcome to the Tower.”

Nia had been tasked with Luthor duty, as the others worked in another room on Kara.

She didn’t say much, or do much. Lena was expecting to be chained up somewhere. Instead, Nia left her alone for a few minutes, returning with a box of cookies and bottles of water. Lena was starving, but she was also too nauseous to eat. She merely sat on the floor next to a quiet Nia, back against the wall, legs pulled up into her arms, staring numbly ahead. Numb… she’s been feeling a lot of that lately.

My god, were we really getting coffee this morning?, Lena wondered, watching the sun set out a Tower window.

“How did you find us?” were the first words she spoke. Nia looked over, with a sympathetic smile. “Kryptonian heat signature. We tried Kara’s tracker, but we were… locked out of it, for most of the day.” Lena’s eyes widened, realizing that Lex’s connections in the DEO meant he would have been able to gain control of the encrypted tracker data as soon as he heard about the kidnapping. 

“We eventually found it in a small town north of the city. We’re guessing you were there before?” Lena nodded mutely, bitter realization that Lex had left the re-enabled tracker behind as a taunt. 

“We were trying to find records of aircraft appearing on primary radar in the area, when we suddenly saw Kara’s signature light up. We weren’t expecting it back so soon.” Lena breathed out. Nia continued. “Primary radar isn’t that great - Lex had ADS-B turned off in his aircraft. Kara’s signature was distorted by the cloaking of Luthor mansion. But between the two, we were able to find you.”

Lena scratched at a tar stain on her knee, left over from the collapse of Luthor mansion. “Why aren’t I locked up right now?”

Nia frowned, putting her hand on Lena’s, giving it a supportive squeeze. “Alex wants to, honestly. But she’s pretty sure Kara wouldn’t be happy with that once she wakes up. And J’onn knows you intend no harm anymore.” Nia paused, as though weighing her options, before pressing forward. “Full disclosure, he can read your mind. When he wants.” For the first time, Lena looked directly at a hesitant Nia, eyes wide in shock. But she quickly wilted. “Bet he doesn’t control people’s minds though,” Lena said dejectedly.

Nia was going to leave that one alone.

It was a long hour or so when the crew filtered out, dispersing in different directions. Alex made a beeline for Lena and Nia, a tempered anger as she looked down. “She’s stable. She’ll be unconscious for a while - the kryptonite interfered with her cellular regeneration. But she’ll be fine, with more light.” Alex paused, sighing. “Do you want to see her?”

Lena leapt up, not quite believing the words she was hearing. As Nia broke off to talk to Brainy, Lena followed Alex to a back room, where Kara lay beneath a sun lamp.

It was odd seeing Kara like this. Her hair down, her glasses off somewhere, in Kara’s clothes. Lena would think she was peacefully sleeping, if not for the rumple of bandages beneath her shirt, and the health monitor to the left. Lena felt her breathing accelerate, before trying to pull it in again. She felt a hand land on her shoulder, and turned her head towards Alex. “She’ll be fine, Lena.”

Lena nodded, slowly dropping onto the stool next to Kara’s bed, tentatively reaching out to hold her hand. Alex stood stiffly next to her, her face hard.

“I’m really pissed at you.”

“You should be.”

At some point, Nia led Lena to a shower, and brought a change of clothes. Lena couldn’t be sure that time was really passing. She eventually ambled out, crashing on the couch next to Kara’s bed, facing away to relieve the muscle spasms that had developed on her other side. Being chained to a radiator had not done her body any favors. But at least she could drift off into a fitful sleep.

“Kara?” said a familiar voice, now with warmth instead of vitriol. Lena tensed awake.

“Alex?” 

“Kara!” Sounds of body meeting body, a long-overdue bear hug.

“Lena?” Kara croaked. “She’s safe, Kara. She’s asleep.” Lena could hear Kara’s relieved sigh. There was a short pause and clinking, as Alex passed Kara a glass of water and let her drink.

“It was really a boneheaded move for you to-”

“C’mon, Alex, I’m literally in recovery, do we have to do this right now?”

“Then it’s going to become ‘Oh Alex, it’s over, do we still have to argue about it?’ Kara, you could’ve died! ”

“I didn’t want to lose her!”

Lena's heart slammed, words from an ancient time making their way back from her memories. Lena had once convinced herself that Kara was lying - crocodile tears and all - over who she really was, that “Kara” was the mask over the self-righteous Kryptonian hero who could never trust a Luthor. If I could be Kara, just Kara… 

“I know, sis, but-”

“I didn’t die! I’m fine! I mean... I’m exhausted, but I’ll be fine, right?”

“Sure, you’ll be back to normal - another 5 hours, give or take. Though you’ll have a kryptonite scar above your belly button to match the one you got in high school.”

“Scars are cool, Alex.”

“Kara, this is closest you’ve gotten to getting murdered-”

“Earth X was pretty close, twice- ”

“Kara-”

“Can we at least get pizza to do this? I’m starving.”

Lena trembled silently, overwhelmed by their sibling bickering, by the fact that this seems to be a conversation they have fairly often, by Kara’s cavalier attitude towards nearly dying. 

She knew who Kara was now. And she desperately wanted to roll over, to gather her in her arms and hold her close. But she wasn’t ready to face Kara, not yet. And she knew she wouldn’t be disturbed… at least for the next few hours, so long as they thought she was asleep. So she kept pretending.
