    marry me, today and everyday

    It’s five o’clock in the evening. Alex is huddled on the couch with a chest cold, swaddled in three blankets with a bag of honey-roasted peanuts clutched to her chest and a collection of tissues growing in the trashcan on the floor beside her. She can hear Kelly in the next room making dinner with Esme, who’s recently decided that her singular dream in life is to be a chef. (Unfortunately, her six-year-old palate is limited, and she’s really only interested in mac-and-cheese.) It’s been a long day of full-bodied coughing fits, cold medicine falsely advertised as bubblegum-flavored, and a lot of pathetic, nasally whining.

    And then, her sister shows up with snacks and comfort movies, and settles in for the evening like she’s here to make things better. Instead, Kara drops a bomb on her head five minutes into The Emperor’s New Groove that sends her spiraling into a frenzy of hacking, violent coughs.

    “I asked Lena to marry me last night,” Kara says casually, and Alex inhales a mouthful of honey-roasted peanuts directly into her lungs.

    “What?!” She chokes, and Kara, only mildly concerned, hands her a glass of water from the coffee table as she slams into her aforementioned coughing fit.

    “Are you okay?”

    “Am I okay? Are you okay?” Alex asks, incredulous.

    Kara seems confused. “Why wouldn’t I be okay? I get to marry Lena. I literally could not be doing any better.” She shovels a handful of potato chips into her mouth and chews loudly.

    “When I told you to tell her how you felt, I meant like, ask her on a date? And what, you decided to ask for her hand in marriage instead?”

    “I mean, she said yes.”

    “Oh, sure, okay, that changes everything. It’s no longer absolutely insane to ask someone to marry you instead of asking them on a first date if they say yes.”

    “I know it’s not conventional—” Kara ignores Alex’s scoff “—but who cares? I could’ve asked her on a date, but I’d have spent the entire time trying to stop myself from asking her to marry me and I’d eventually fail, so I just cut the middleman and asked right off the bat.”

    Alex shakes her head in shock. (And she finds herself a little disturbed by how much Kara’s explanation is making sense in her fever-addled brain.) Her sister, who was single yesterday, is engaged today.

    “Okay,” she says, because she cannot fathom what on earth else there is to say. She shovels honey-roasted peanuts into her mouth and chews thoughtfully. “Does anyone else know?”

    “Just you,” Kara says. “I wanted you to know first. We’ll tell everyone else soon.”

    And then, she forgets to tell literally anyone else.

     

    -

     

    In her defense, Kara has a lot on her mind. Well, one thing, really, but it’s a big one: Lena is her fiancée. She’s going to marry Lena. There is not much room left in her head to think about anything else, even a plan to tell their friends.

    Truly, she just . . . forgets. They both do. It’s a minor oversight.

    Having told Alex almost immediately means that Kelly knows, and probably Esme as well since she’s developed a habit of hiding around corners and eavesdropping—a result of her new dream of being a spy when she grows up (the chef dream did not survive past dinner), but other than that? Her thoughts of shouting it from the rooftops are forgotten, and it takes her three entire days to realize they have not told their friends they got engaged.

    Embarrassingly, she doesn’t even figure it out on her own.

     

    -

     

    “Did I forget a meeting?”

    It’s about lunchtime and Kara is starving. Her plan was to meet Lena at the Tower and go to Noonan’s, but when she arrives, everyone is gathered together on the couches and armchairs, and her brow crinkles in confusion. She seems to be the only person missing aside from J’onn, who she’s pretty sure is on patrol across the city. Five pairs of eyes look up at her in the doorway.

    “Not at all,” Kelly says, her smile welcoming and warm as she beckons Kara over to join them.

    Nia offers her a relaxed grin and a little wave, and adds: “Your timing’s perfect, actually. Lena and Brainy want to test the shock absorption stuff in my new suit. I don’t see the need, but apparently I get tossed around too much and they’re worried about head trauma. Wanna watch me throw myself at stuff full-speed to make sure it works?”

    Kara falters mid-step. What?

    “What?” She asks as she draws closer, leaning against the back of the couch above where Lena’s sitting, legs curled under her and a mess of blueprints and loose papers scattered between her and Brainy.

    “You’re not throwing yourself at anything,” Lena says, fixing Nia with a ‘don’t you dare’ look that Kara herself is incredibly familiar with. Nia grumbles under her breath but says nothing, and accepts a sympathetic shoulder pat from Alex, who has made a full recovery from the cold she thought was killing her barely three days ago.

    “Just science stuff,” Lena tells Kara, her entire body language changing like the flip of a light switch as she turns toward her, tipping her head back to look up at Kara with a happy smile. “Don’t worry about it.”

    So Kara doesn’t worry about it. Not because she isn’t concerned, but because Lena is smiling at her and it’s really very distracting. Nia and her head trauma can wait.

    “Hi,” she says, ducking her head to press a quick kiss to Lena’s lips, realizing she hasn’t properly greeted her fiancée. (And if she’s been completely unable to stop calling Lena her fiancée both in her head and out loud—so often that Alex has, rather aggressively, started keeping tally in a notepad—well then that’s between her and Rao.)

    (And Alex’s notepad.)

    “Hi.” Lena’s voice is sweet and warm as she smiles back at her, eyes crinkling around the edges. Her dark hair is tied back in a loose bun, the kind that only happens when she’s distractedly pulled it away from her face while engrossed in work, and Kara reaches out to tuck a loose curl behind her ear. She makes a mental note to scrounge around for a hair-tie, because Lena’s using a stray rubberband and Kara knows how much that can hurt. Even invincibility is weak against the pull of a rubberband coming out of long hair.

    “Um. What.”

    Kara jumps at Nia’s voice. Nia, who’s staring at them with an eerily blank face, waving a finger between them like doing so will help her process . . . whatever it is she’s trying to process.

    Oh, shoot. Did they ever tell her?

    No, Kara thinks. We may have forgotten to do that.

    “What was that.” It’s a question, but only grammatically—her tone is so flat that it doesn’t sound like she’s asking. “You kissed. You guys don’t kiss. Are you guys kissing now?”

    “I know, it’s disgusting,” Alex says at the same time Lena, unhelpfully, says: “Well, not anymore.”

    There’s a beat of complete silence. Kara climbs quietly over the back of the couch and drops down next to her fiancée, never taking her eyes off Nia, who’s sitting completely still, so much so that Kara’s starting to worry.

    “Nia?” Lena’s voice is soft and hesitant, laced with a hint of the special kind of concern she saves just for Nia, who she’s become fiercely protective of.

    Sometimes, the Superfriends laugh about it—how Kara and Lena have taken her under their wing and all but adopted her. They joke, but Kara knows that Nia looks up to them, and while Lena quietly does her best to take care of their whole family, her mother-hen tendencies are especially strong toward Nia.

    And though Kara won’t say it out loud, seeing Lena like that does things to her heart. Crazy things that make her dream of a future with her and Lena and children. Beautiful things that feel almost in reach, that only several days ago seemed they could never come true. (Lena would be a wonderful mother. Kara is so sure of this that her whole chest aches with it.)

    Just when she’s really starting to worry about Nia’s reaction, the girl’s shoulders drop and she slumps against the back of her chair and says, with a voice full of great tragedy: “I just lost so many bets, oh my god.”

    That, she was not expecting.

    Lena’s shoulder bumps against hers as she scooches closer, tilting her head to the side and asking, incredulously: “You guys bet on us?”

    Her eyes dart from person to person, landing on everyone in turn, but no one seems willing to look her in the eye. Alex has taken an intense interest in fiddling with the safety on her gun (which feels a little like a casual threat, but Kara’s not going to analyze that out of fear) and Kelly is staring at one of Lena’s blueprints, which she definitely does not understand any of but is acting like she does. Brainy is fixated on one as well, spinning a pencil between his fingers, but he had never actually looked up in the first place and since he helped write them, Kara figures he’s not pretending.

    “Who won?” Lena asks, with some morbid type of curiosity.

    “Nobody. We all lost—by a lot. We had too much faith in you.”

    “Jess came the closest,” Nia follows Alex up, seeming to have mostly recovered from her previous dismay. “But her bet was on almost five months ago.”

    “I have to admire her persistence,” Kelly says, almost like an afterthought. “She never wavered—not even when you two were fighting.”

    “What a fool,” Alex scoffs.

    “You lost by a lot more than Jess, Alex.”

    “You are supposed to be on my side. For better or worse—this is the ‘worse’ part.”

    “I’m confused,” Lena says weakly, and Kara, privately, completely relates. Outwardly, she takes Lena’s hand in support, perched strong and steady at her side. After a brief pause, Brainy speaks up, but doesn’t tear his eyes away from the large sheet of paper he’s been scribbling something that looks like math on the corner of.

    “You were all going to lose anyway,” he says, but does not explain further. They wait for a moment in confused and uncomfortable silence before Nia demands says: “Elaborate.”

    Finally, he looks up at them.

    “The quantity of bets you have placed on Kara and Lena’s romantic relationship is . . . quite comprehensive, however much an invasion of privacy—” Alex shrugs, undeterred “—but those placed on how that relationship would officially begin were limited by your assumption that they would follow traditional human dating customs.”

    “Oh shit, that’s right,” Alex breathes, torn between devastation and glee. “We didn’t plan on you guys U-Hauling so hard you went straight from best friends to walking-down-the-aisle. We thought you’d be at least sort of normal.”

    “Wait, back up,” Nia says, her face twisted up into something both incredulous and confused. “What is she talking about. Are you engaged?”

    Kara’s high-pitched, squeaky: “Uhhhhhhh,” is met with disbelieving silence.

    “Let me get this straight.” (Kara can tell, based on Lena’s suddenly pinched expression, that she’s resisting the urge to make a gay joke, so she offers her fiancée [her fiancée!!] a consoling pat on the arm. Lena catches her hand and laces their fingers, and her heart explodes a little in her chest.) “You decided to get married before you’ve ever been on a date?”

    “Oh, Nia,” Alex says, her eyes sparkling with chaos, “they’ve been engaged for years.”

    Kara lets out a strangled noise that seems to encourage her sister further. All she wanted was lunch with Lena. (Lena, who is going to be her wife. ) Perhaps a short walk through the park so she can hold her hand and stare at her a lot. Instead she’s being bullied.

    “Those bracelets they’re always wearing? That’s the Kryptonian version of engagement rings.” Alex looks entirely too pleased with herself, and Nia looks on the verge of flipping over a page of her own suit’s blueprints to plan their wedding on the back.

    “What does this mean for your bets on their engagement and subsequent marriage?” Brainy asks, his gaze focused back on the plans for Nia’s suit. He writes something in the margins before looking up, glancing between them all like he isn’t sure where to start. “If the timeline of their engagement overlaps with that of the bet on when they would start dating, which I’m sure you all recall losing—”

    “We do, yeah, thanks,” Alex grumbles.

    “—then any bets you had placed on when Kara would propose would either be lost very terribly, or become null.”

    “Oh. I hadn’t thought about that.”

    “I’m sorry, did you all just automatically assume Kara would be the one to propose?” Lena says, looking deeply affronted. In response, she receives only blank stares.

    “Of course,” Nia says, like there was never any debate. “Six years and you couldn’t even make a move; what makes you think you could manage a proposal.”

    “Kara didn’t make a move either until three days ago!”

    Nia shrugs. “She was too busy staring at your chest.”

    Lena flushes pink and opens her mouth, but closes it without saying anything. It’s a fair comment, and since Kara has no counterargument, she just gawks. Alex, on the other hand, looks rather ill, and says, her voice weak: “You could have explained it any other way. And that’s what you chose.”

    Kelly, ever the mediator, clears her throat and offers both Kara and Lena a warm smile. 

    “We’re all very happy for you both,” she says, and Kara is so grateful for her calm, gentle support. Affection for her sister-in-law bubbles up inside her. “I know you’ll bring each other so much joy.”

    “Thank you, Kelly.” Lena’s voice is not quite wobbly, but Kara can hear how Kelly’s words have pulled an overwhelming swell of emotion up inside her. Sometimes it still surprises Lena to be on the receiving end of love. Kara hopes this fades with time, but hopes the awe when Kara tells her she loves her never does. She meets Lena’s soft gaze, reveling in the warmth it spreads through her body like it’s inside her veins.

    Somewhere in the background: “God, you guys are disgusting.”

    “Shut up, Alex, it’s beautiful.”

    “No, it’s nauseating.”

    “So are you and Kelly,” Nia teases, and there’s a quiet thump and a loud yelp—Alex definitely threw something at her. 

    “Hush, you two,” Kelly cuts in, a light chide in her voice, and Kara glances over to see her grab the decorative pillow back from Nia and place it far from Alex’s reach on the other end of the couch. “They’re getting married. Leave them be.”

    Warmth spreads through Kara’s bones and she turns to Lena—tells her in a whisper: “We’re getting married.” She leans close, smiling like she’s drunk on the words. Her heart stutters as Lena beams back at her, all sparkling green eyes and double-dimples and pink cheeks as she whispers back: “We’re getting married.”

    She’s going to be my wife, Kara thinks, and it knocks the air out of her lungs. Lena’s fingers slip between hers and she clasps their hands, and there is more love than she could ever possibly contain inside her body as she looks at Lena. Her heart beats with it. The words repeat themselves in her head. Over and over, I get to marry this girl.
