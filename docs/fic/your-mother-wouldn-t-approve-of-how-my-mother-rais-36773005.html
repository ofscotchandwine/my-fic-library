    She doesn’t do it right away, because Jack needs more cheering up and Kate is woefully bad at it. No, she waits until she’s back at her place.

    It’s late, but Clint doesn’t grumble at her when he picks up the phone. Ever since Yelena got shot and he heard her having an emotional breakdown – twice – he’s been nicer to her. More patient. It’s kind of a nice side effect, even though Kate misses his gruff attitude a little bit.

    “Evening, Hawkeye,” he says, and Kate sits in front of her couch, her legs spread on the carpet and Pizza Dog walking over to sprawl out over her lap. He’s warm, and Kate smiles as she runs her fingers through his fur.

    “Hi to you too, Hawkeye,” Kate shoots back, wondering if he already knows about the decision with her mom’s trial. “How’s the fam?”

    “The fam is just fine,” Clint replies, and a creaking and crinkling in the background makes Kate think that he’s probably sitting in his old leather easy chair in front of the television. Definitely with something dad-approved and boring playing on the screen. “Lila’s been asking about you. She misses you – are you still planning on flying down here after that kerfuffle with your mom is done?”

    Affirming it with a hum, Kate lets her eyes draw up to the slowly growing collection of postcards on her wall.

    They talk a bit more about Lila and the others, and Kate occasionally hears Laura’s voice in the background. She slowly works up the courage throughout, but she’s pretty sure Clint knows there’s more to her call without her having to say anything.

    When their conversation lulls, Kate breathes in deeply.

    “Can you find out why my mom’s trial was postponed?” she rushes out as quickly as she can, leaning her head against the seat of her couch and closing her eyes, her jaw working as she considers what she’s asking. “I know you’re retired, or whatever, but you must still have ties to SHIELD, and they have access to court records, right? I know my mom has done some really awful things, but she’s been in solitary for, like, ever, and that’s not fair, you know? Solitary confinement is torture—she doesn’t—my mom doesn’t deserve torture.”

    He’s quiet for a couple of seconds while Kate tries to catch her breath and her escaping sanity. “No, she doesn’t,” he agrees, and some of her anxiety melts away. She loves her mom. In spite of what she’s done, she loves her, and she’s tired of pretending otherwise.

    “So—can you?” she asks, feeling younger than she has in a while.

    “Kate…” he starts, and she knows she won’t like what he’s about to say next. “I don’t have to ask anyone. I already know why it’s been postponed.”

    Whatever she thought he’d say, it’s not that.

    “What the fuck?” she asks, and he lets out a sigh.

    “I was going to tell you,” he says, and she sits up straight.

    “What the fuck, Clint?”

    Pizza Dog looks at her, his one good eye full of confused energy, sensing her distress.

    “I didn’t go looking for it, trust me, but I’ve been keeping my feelers out after everything that went down between you and Fisk,” he explains, and Kate feels her anger deflate ever so slightly. “I didn’t want you to end up as collateral damage, just because he can’t get to your mom.” He lets out a fresh sigh and Kate feels weirdly touched by his admission.

    “And what did you hear?” Kate asks, pushing on Pizza Dog’s belly until he rolls over and lets her slide out from under him. Shaking out her thighs, she wanders over to the kitchen and pulls a box of cereal from a cupboard.

    “That I didn’t have to worry about you at all,” he says, and Kate’s hand stills so suddenly that several stray Cheerios bounce off the side of her bowl and onto the floor. “Someone else has put you under their protection and told the whole criminal underbelly that you’re not to be touched.” Kate’s hands shake as she sets aside the cereal box and slowly turns to her wall of postcards.

    If you read this, it means I have decided to do something very foolish.

    “Your mom’s trial has been postponed because Fisk’s associates have been turning up dead and the prosecution is trying to figure out if your mother or Bishop Security have anything to do with it,” Clint continues, while Kate grips the edge of her kitchen counter to steady herself.

    I wish I could have stayed, but I am in the middle of sorting something out, and I do not want to drag you into it.

    “Clint?” Kate says, voice as unsteady as her heart.

    “I know, kiddo, it’s a lot,” he reassures her, clearly missing most of the puzzle pieces that Kate’s mind is quickly putting together to create a larger whole. “Like Laura said. If you want, we can come visit you for the trial. You don’t have to do this alone.”

    “Clint,” Kate says again, closing her eyes and massaging her temple. “I think we have a problem.” “What?” he asks, and Kate briefly considers not telling him anything and simply laughing it off. His relationship with Yelena is still fraught at best.

    But Kate is going to need all the help she can get if she’s right.

    “I think I know who’s been killing mobsters,” Kate says, and he makes a half-surprised harrumphing sound that Kate thinks should be off-limits to Avengers who are responsible for saving the world and returning it to its pre-snap population.

    Their conversation lasts for a while longer, but Kate is only partly listening. Her mind is already whirring with the implications of what this might mean. It certainly explains why Yelena was so very interested when Kate told her about that useless tracksuit guy who attacked her on her night out at the start of February.

    She wonders if Yelena put out the word that she’s not to be harmed before or after, but then decides that it doesn’t really matter. At the moment, the truth appears to be that Yelena is hunting down Kingpin and his ilk. And that she got herself shot for the trouble.

    Shot.

    As a highly trained, supremely skilled assassin.

    If Kate wasn’t already determined to help before, she sure as hell is now.

    Her hypothesis is too strong to ignore even for Clint, who decides to help by promising to reach out to his SHIELD buddies. He says some stuff about wishing that Tony Stark was still around, because he was the only one who really knew how to work those surveillance things he kept all over the place, as he puts it, but Kate starts tuning him out again at that point.

    Because if Yelena is out there hunting down the tracksuits and anyone else who’s helping them, and she’s doing it for Kate, then Kate just might have to go out and buy a ring.

    Kate doesn’t sleep that night. Not right away.

    Instead, she kisses Pizza Dog’s sleepy little head, grabs all the things she considers she might need, locks the Fenstermacher, and legs it to her mom’s apartment. She doesn’t run the whole way, because she’s been cutting back on gym hours in favor of lying on the floor and crying about Yelena, but she very nearly manages it.

    By the time she meets Clive the doorman, Kate is sweaty and a little bit miffed at herself that she didn’t just wait for an Uber. But then again, time is of the essence, probably. She has no idea how many more mobsters Yelena can kill without Kingpin retaliating. Possibly worse this time. Kate is thinking less bullet wound and more grenade launcher crater.

    Clive greets her with a friendly smile, despite all the shit that’s gone down with her mom that he must have heard about by now. She’s come into the penthouse at much odder hours before, so he doesn’t even question it, and Kate tries not to grimace and show how winded she truly is. She’s only skipped the endurance training for two weeks, Jesus.

    Okay maybe three.

    Thankfully, this elevator is never broken, and Kate soon finds herself on her mom’s floor.

    Rushing inside, she takes a moment to consider how utterly normal the place still looks. It’s not even dusty – probably because Jack has been having a maid come in and clean it up. Somehow, that makes the whole thing a lot more eerie. Her mom isn’t here, but her belongings seem alive. Well- cared for. Even without their mistress.

    “Okay, Kate, focus,” she tells herself, moving left and down the hallway towards her mom’s study. After it caved in during the Battle of New York, her mom had it completely remodeled so that it wouldn’t remind either of them of what they’d lost. And that her dad died among his most treasured belongings – his books. “If I were Eleanor Bishop, criminal mastermind and loving mother of one, where would I…”

    She slides open the door and startles again at how much it looks the same. Down to the haphazardly placed pieces of paper on her mom’s desk. Right where she left them the last time she put down her work and didn’t pick it up again.

    Kate has barely been back to this place in the last two-and-a-bit months, and before that, she didn’t use to spend much time in her mom’s study. When she was much younger, she would sometimes sit in an armchair in the corner and watch her dad work, but when she got older, she was always too busy with her martial arts training or her archery to watch her mom work instead.

    Now, she almost regrets it.

    Her mom’s laptop is placed neatly on the little holding tray. A small lamp imbedded in the side blinks inconsistently, telegraphing that it hasn’t been powered off since the last time it was used. Much like the paper and the clean surfaces all over the penthouse, it makes Kate feel just that little bit creeped out.

    Prison hasn’t touched this little bubble of normality.

    She almost doesn’t want to sit in her mom’s leather desk chair.

    After another minute of fighting herself, Kate takes the plunge, her fingers immediately going to her mom’s laptop and opening it. She’s not surprised to see that it’s password protected, and she spends several precious minutes trying to enter the mind of Eleanor Bishop. Something she’s tried at several points in her life and failed at over and over again.

    “Come on, mom,” Kate whispers to herself, her fingers hovering over the keys. “Talk to me.”

    Even after several attempts, she can’t guess it. She tries her dad’s name. She tries her dad’s birthday. When she starts feeling sappy, she tries her own name and birthday and a variation of the very same, without any success. Maybe her mom just isn’t the sentimental type.

    Leaning back in the leather chair, Kate is assaulted by the remnants of her mom’s perfume and decides then and there that maybe she needs to try something different.

    Which definitely has nothing to do with Kate being a sad little girl who misses her mother.

    Nope.

    Bending forward, she tries the several drawers of the desk, only to find them all locked. The desk is wonderfully and beautifully carved and disgustingly expensive mahogany, so Kate has no qualms whatsoever about using her bow to break them open.

    “Sorry,” she whispers to the splintering wood as she checks each drawer.

    When she breaks open the fifth one – of six, thanks – she finds what she’s looking for, and leaves her mom’s penthouse. Though not without grabbing another expensive bottle of bourbon from the liquor cabinet and a backpack from her old bedroom.

    She goes back home to her apartment for the night because she’s tired and worn out from the last couple of weeks.

    Tomorrow, she has more work to do.

    In the morning, she picks up a new postcard on her way out. It’s another close-up of a painting, but Kate doesn’t read what it says. Not just yet. Not while she’s on a mission. She already walked Pizza Dog before the sun was fully up, and now that he’s neatly tucked away on her new mattress, Kate can put more of her vague plan into motion.

    Tucking the card into the left inside pocket of her overcoat, she decides to walk this morning, because she has so much nervous energy stored up, she’s not sure what to do with it. Now that she’s fairly certain that Yelena is somewhere in the city, she feels almost rejuvenated. The postcards are also a good sign, she finds. They prove that Yelena is still alive out there; that she’s still hammering away at her own little pet project. Kate doesn’t think Sonya is the one doing her bidding this time. The morning air also keeps her awake and aware. She’s slept a sum total of two and a half hours and woke up in the middle of a particularly vivid dream of her and Yelena meeting up in that abandoned toy store-warehouse the tracksuits took Clint to when he got himself captured on purpose.

    It takes her almost no time to reach the building that houses Bishop Security.

    The woman at the front desk recognizes her immediately, and makes an odd face that Kate has no trouble deciphering. Kate hasn’t been back to her mom’s company once since her mom was thrown in jail for using her company’s assets to supply Kingpin with information on certain persons he wanted dead.

    “Darleen,” Kate greets her, and she raises both eyebrows and nods at her.

    Technically, her mom is still the head of the company, even though she’s temporarily given over the decision making power to her second in command, so Kate should be welcome. And, just like she thought, Darleen does not get up to stop her.

    Bishop Security occupies most of the floors between the twenty-third and the thirty-fifth, but Kate is focused on the thirtieth.

    When she gets off the elevator, most of the people sitting closest to it look up to see the new arrival, and Kate finds herself nodding and waving to the few who send her a greeting. There are several cubicles on this floor, and Kate catches a couple of curious eyes above the temporary walls as she walks through the main workspace and past offices with glass walls. At the very end of it, she sees the glass door with the all-important gold-plated name.

    Eleanor Bishop.

    Her mom’s second-in-command hasn’t taken over the space at all. Kate can already see that everything is just as she remembers it even before she reaches the scanner next to the door. Before trying to get inside, Kate takes in the tasteful cream-colored couch and the glass coffee table that’s laden with two-month-old magazines. The cleaning staff has been inside here as well.

    Again, Kate feels uncomfortably certain that her mom could stroll right past her at any moment and say something beautiful and kind.

    You’re the only thing that matters to me, Kate hears for what feels like the one-millionth time repeating in her mind.

    Behind her, she hears footsteps.

    Turning her head, she catches surprised steel-grey eyes.

    “Kate?” her mom’s second in command asks, stopping in front of her own glass-encased office.

    “Gosh, it’s been months already, hasn’t it? Happy New Year, kiddo.” The woman is wearing a charcoal pantsuit that underlines her striking features and high cheekbones.

    “Hi, Irina,” Kate replies, reaching out instinctively and catching the woman’s outstretched hand in a shake. It’s firm, but not crushing, and Kate suddenly feels fifteen again. Like she’s meeting Irina for the first time and falling in love with her. “Happy New Year.”

    “How are you doing?” Irina asks, letting go of Kate’s hand and indicating to her own office with a wave of her hand, like she’s hoping Kate will follow her in for a chat. “I’m so sorry about your mother. She’s a strong one. She’ll pull through all this, you’ll see.” She presses her hand to the scanner by her door and it unlocks with a little snag and pull. “Come on, I sneaked some candy past the ravenous masses out front.”

    It’s a tempting offer to take her mind off everything that’s going on, but Kate resists.

    The postcard in her pocket feels like it is pulsing with life, but only just.

    She has important things to do.

    So she waves off the offer, and gestures to her mom’s office.

    “I need to get something I left in there before—you know, all the stuff. And, uhm, I didn’t come here in the last two months because, well—” She’s desperately searching for an explanation that sounds believable, but not like she’s emotionally so damaged and deranged that Irina will insist on a chat about it all. “It’s so awkward without my mom. Walking around in here like I own this place —I mean, I kind of do, a little bit, but—”

    Irina smiles, and Kate knows she’s rambled too much.

    Still, the woman is nothing if not discreet, which is probably the reason she was her mom’s second in command in the first place. Briefly, Kate considers whether Irina had an inkling of what her mom was doing, or if she was perhaps even involved but got away with it because her mom didn’t give her up. The thought tastes sour, and she pushes it away. She can’t think about that, not when she’s on a mission to save Yelena from herself.

    “Okay, kiddo,” Irina says, opening her office door and walking smoothly inside. Before she closes it behind herself, she places her hand on Kate’s shoulder and squeezes. “But if you change your mind, just stop by before you head out.”

    “Sure,” Kate says with a nod, swallowing thickly as Irina removes her hand and fully closes the door to her office. For a second, Kate watches her walk to her desk through the glass walls, then she returns her attention to her mom’s own fingerprint scanner.

    Before, Kate was always allowed to go inside.

    What about now?

    She takes a moment to reach inside her overcoat and slide her thumb along the edge of the postcard she has yet to read. And then, she reaches back out, her fingers settling on the cold glass of the scanner, heart beating thickly in her throat, choking her.

    When the lock snaps and whirrs, Kate briefly considers she might be imagining it.

    The screen flashes green, and Kate grabs the handle of the door out of instinct, pressing it down slowly and entering with hesitant steps. Her mom’s study was bad enough, but this is a place where Eleanor Bishop spent so much time, Kate came to think of it as her mom’s second home.

    In truth, Kate has spent more time in this room than in the study.

    Closing the door behind herself, Kate takes a deep breath of the familiar air.

    “Hi, mom,” she whispers, letting her eyes sweep the wall of windows to the right. “Just stopping by to see how you’re doing.” She imagines her mom standing in front of the windows and surveying her kingdom. She used to wear such beautiful outfits to this place, and when she would see Kate in her grungy sweaters and jackets and ripped jeans, she would crinkle her nose and laugh. And then she would hug her and kiss her cheek and cup it and brush back Kate’s hair. I love you, she would say, and Kate would look back at the hallway and hope no one else had seen her mom treat her like someone so uncool.

    Feeling her throat grow tight with emotion, Kate quickly moves her attention to her mom’s desk and the computer that sits atop it. This time, when she looks back at the hallway, it’s to see if anyone else is paying attention to her, but no one is. At least not overtly.

    “Okay,” Kate says to herself, rounding the desk and sitting down in her mom’s ergonomic chair. It pushes against Kate’s back as she tries to slump into it, and she curses her mother’s desire to stay healthy and have good posture. “Let’s see if we can get this working.”

    She takes off her hat and puts it down behind the keyboard, which draws her attention to a framed picture. It must be new, because Kate hasn’t seen it, and before she does anything else, she picks it up with trembling fingers. It’s a picture of Kate at the last archery tournament her mom went to. Kate remembers the day well, and how her mom had crowed to anyone who would listen that Kate was her daughter, and how proud she was. The photo is perfectly focused on Kate, who has an arrow nocked and is aiming at her target, although it’s not visible. She’s in her purple outfit, and she looks so concentrated, Kate’s afraid her own brow is about to snap from the strain.

    In the corner of the frame, a folded note has been stuck, and Kate gently pulls it free with her right hand while setting aside the frame with her left. It’s a piece of spiral notebook paper, and Kate recognizes the handwriting as her own.

    Can you pick up some more ice cream for later? Greer and Dee are coming over for movie night. We want to use the big projector, so I hope you don’t have anything fancy planned! Love you!

    The note is about six months old, if Kate remembers correctly. Or maybe eight. She’s not sure. She barely remembers writing it and putting it on her mom’s keyboard. She does remember her mom being in a meeting, and Kate running back out to do something unimportant.

    Letting out a sigh, she stares at the note, chest aching as she refolds it and sticks it back into the edge of the picture frame. The fact that her mom has kept something so inconsequential makes her wonder again how she could have missed everything else her mom was doing.

    “You probably wouldn’t have ever told me, would you?” Kate reaches into the right pocket of her jeans and pulls out the thing she found in her mom’s desk the night before. Twisting it back and forth in her fingers, she stares down at the small, plastic keycard. “You don’t always have to do the protecting, mom.”

    She nudges her mom’s mouse, but the computer screen doesn’t spring to life. Looks like someone at the company is more energy-efficient than Kate and Jack. Pressing the on-button on the side of the computer screen, Kate waits for it to boot up before sticking the keycard into the appropriate slot on the computer stand.

    Bypassing the need for a password, Kate watches as the desktop opens up with all its programs and the files her mom has saved to it. There are so many excel sheets with similar names, Kate can’t help but be reminded of herself and her own computer habits.

    Focusing her attention, Kate quickly pulls up the program she’s looking for. Her mom has way more access than Kate ever did – all she could do was search for someone’s phone number and track them with it. Her mom has access to what feels like all camera footage across the city, and Kate looks up the cameras inside the venue that houses the annual Bishop Christmas party.

    There’s a live feed, but Kate doesn’t bother with that.

    Instead, she types in the date of the party, and waits for the files to load in.

    There are time stamps, and Kate finds herself frowning when the footage doesn’t go past four in the afternoon.

    “What the hell,” she mutters, clicking to the next day for comparison and finding all the hours she could possibly want. “Well, shit.” Clicking back, she puts on her mom’s headset and opens up the video that’s marked as four pm. With a slight frown, she watches people walking around the venue. She skips forward a bit and sees her mom talking to a waiter. Just as her mom turns her face, Kate pauses the footage, and spends a couple of seconds drinking in her features.

    But this is not what she’s looking for.

    “Okay…” she says, pushing herself against the back of her mom’s chair and biting her bottom lip. “Okay, sure, I can work with this. It’s whatever. Not suspicious at all that that specific time is missing. Nope.” She leans forward and goes back out of the venue’s footage. Instead, she looks up her own address, and finds to her surprise, that there isn’t a single camera aimed at her apartment. “Goddamn it, mom.”

    With a scowl, she spends a couple of minutes watching the footage of the store next door, before exiting out of that, too. Drumming her fingers on her mom’s glass desk, she stares at her own concentrated face inside that little frame. Behind the arrow, Kate can see her open mouth, and she tries to remember that moment. Tries to remember what it was like to win that tournament. There have been so many… So many moments where she’s nocked an arrow and aimed…

    Her fingers still immediately as she remembers another.

    Arrow nocked, breathing heavy, aiming at—

    She pulls up Maya’s address and clicks through a couple of different camera angles until she finds one that fits. Heart pounding, tongue heavy, breathing growing thick, she pulls up the right date, and nearly chokes on air when she sees that none of the footage has been erased.

    “Fuck,” she mutters as she clicks on what she’s pretty sure must be the right time.

    There’s nothing at first, and when she skips to a bit later in the hour, her finger freezes on the middle button of the mouse as she sees herself and Clint walking onto the building and setting up their stuff. As he keeps talking, she watches herself walking off, and it’s the first time she sees his stupid expression when he realizes she’s gone to infiltrate the apartment herself.

    Knowing that this footage exists, Kate feels oddly uncomfortable at the magnitude of her mom’s omniscience. Of the company’s. She has half a mind to call the cops on Bishop Security, although they probably have permission from the city to have all of this.

    Skipping forward a bit, Kate’s breath hitches when she watches a masked figure clad in all black approach Clint from behind, flinching when she watches the figure clobber Clint above the head. Her eyes stay glued to the screen as the two of them fight, and even though Kate knows that she shouldn’t be, she’s rooting a bit for the masked figure.

    Because it’s Yelena.

    Of course it is.

    Not long after, Kate herself comes into view, sliding along the zipline that Clint’s bow created for her. And this time, on this rewatch, Kate realizes that Yelena yanked on the zipline to help her down. Finally, Kate pulls the postcard from the inside pocket of her overcoat and holds it in her sweaty hands as Clint pulls off Yelena’s mask.

    Her back is to the camera, but Kate knows it won’t be for long.

    She remembers it all like it was yesterday. Yelena shaking her head, and Kate lowering her arrow and her bow, just before Yelena turned and—Kate pauses the footage.

    Slowly, she zooms in as best as she can on the face.

    It’s blurry from movement, and Kate skips forward frame by frame until she finds a still that’s almost stationary. She saves the still to her mom’s hard drive, minimizes the camera footage and opens up a new program.

    Before feeding the picture into it, Kate opens it up and gazes wistfully at Yelena’s face. The last time Kate saw it, it was still a bit ashen from lack of blood. Here, it’s flushed from fighting and her mouth is turned downwards in concentration as she’s on the verge of throwing herself off the building, her widow’s bite secured behind her.

    “You are such an idiot,” Kate says to Yelena’s face, before dropping the file into the Bishop Security facial recognition software.

    Chancing another look at the hallway beyond, Kate is glad to find no security personnel coming for her. She doesn’t think the activity on her mom’s computer is monitored, but she also can’t rule it out one-hundred-percent. The company’s IT-department probably has access, but even then—who’s to say they’re even looking at their screens at any given time.

    When the program is done, Kate is disappointed to find two solitary hits.

    One of them is the rooftop Kate herself was just looking at.

    The other is a still of Yelena in front of a post office.

    Putting her face in her hands, Kate lets out an annoyed huff. Yelena is looking right into the camera, because of fucking course she is. It’s like she’s mocking Kate, or perhaps anyone else who might be looking for her.

    “Why do you have to make everything so fucking complicated.” Pulling back her face, she puts her chin on her folded hands, keeping her elbows on the edge of her mom’s desk. On the screen, Yelena’s wearing that same green jacket that Sonya took from Kate’s place and that she wore to the Christmas party, and her hair flows freely about her face. She’s beautiful, and Kate sees no indication that she’s ever been shot, despite the fact that the date shows that it was after the bullet hole incident.

    With a grumble, Kate considers her next move.

    The address of the post office might be enough to go there, but not enough to triangulate her location.

    “What would Joan Watson do?” Kate wonders aloud, leaning back in the chair as best as she can and looking at the postcard she’s put down behind the keyboard face up. She’s not ready to read the note on the back. It won’t tell her what she wants to know. “How the fuck am I supposed to find someone who doesn’t want to be found…”

    She thinks about what she knows, and then her back suddenly perfectly fits against her mom’s chair as she sits up straight.

    “Oh my God,” Kate whispers as she minimizes the facial recognition software and instead goes into the criminal database. Many of the tracksuit mafia guys have been to jail at least once – or at least they were arrested, so Kate has little trouble finding their subfolder and clicking through the different faces. When she lands on the one she’s looking for, she realizes belatedly that it’s the first time she has consciously noticed any of their names.

    Tomas Adamczyk, the file says, but Kate has come to think of him as Imagine-Dragons-girlfriend- guy.

    There’s an address listed for him, as well as several known associates and the places they like to frequent. His picture produces too many facial recognitions to count, and Kate suddenly appreciates how much effort Yelena must go to not to be noticed or seen.

    Kate is briefly tempted to look for herself, before deciding against it.

    There are some things she doesn’t need to know.

    She copies a bunch of data about Tomas onto a thumb drive from her pocket, before pulling up more guys from the tracksuit mafia and running the same search. She repeats the process a couple more times, before trying to find Kingpin himself. When she doesn’t, she’s a little bit confused, but also not really.

    She exits out of the programs and closes all the footage she’s looked at. She’s tempted to copy the fight from the roof onto her thumb drive to show to Clint but decides against it. Instead, she deletes it, hoping that no one will be able to recover it.

    Finally, there’s just the picture of Yelena on the roof that’s left, and Kate hovers over the delete button for several seconds.

    Glancing down at the postcard a final time, Kate hits print instead, and listens as her mom’s printer comes alive behind her.

    After deleting the image, Kate turns off her mom’s computer and removes the key card, before stuffing both it and the postcard into the inside pocket of her overcoat and getting to her feet. Pushing the ergonomic chair back under the desk, Kate moves to the printer, and slowly picks up the piece of paper it spits out.

    It’s not the greatest quality, but her mom’s expensive equipment makes up for it somewhat in the colors.

    Since she’s still not a psychopath, she doesn’t kiss Yelena’s forehead, but instead folds the paper neatly so that it won’t crease the picture and sticks it in the same place as she has Yelena’s neat handwriting.

    And then, because she has no desire to speak to Irina about her mom being in jail, Kate leaves. She spends the rest of the day putting up a big conspiracy wall above her couch.

    In between, she laminates the picture of Yelena, before running out to buy a frame for it. She takes Pizza Dog with her, and the two of them get hot dogs around Central Park. Later, they both collapse on the floor in front of Kate’s couch and listen to her less depressing, but much more introspective playlist, while Pizza Dog licks at Kate’s fingers and whines a little for her to pet him.

    It’s almost the end of February when Kate finally feels ready to take the next step.

    She’s interconnected the different tracksuit guys and the death scenes of the criminals that Clint is pretty sure have been killed by Yelena. They start with the deaths that the prosecution has linked to Kingpin and her mom, and then after he goes digging, he gives her several other names that they seem to have missed.

    He also digs up a location that he’s pretty sure Kingpin is actually hiding out at, and Kate hopes that Yelena hasn’t heard about that one. She doesn’t think Yelena is linked up to the SHIELD sources, but then again, who knows those sources aren’t getting their info from other people, who Yelena is connected to? It’s all a bit nerve-wracking.

    At least the postcards still keep coming every other day.

    Darleen from the front desk.

Leaning forward and putting her hands on her knees, she closes her eyes and curses quietly to herself. “She works the front desk at the same building where Bishop Security’s located.     Especially when her conspiracy wall keeps telling her that there is more to do.

    It’s nearly eleven pm by the time Kate decides to do something stupid.

The new owners must really care for it.

    Jimmying open the front lock is child’s play.

    Then again, it’s no Fenstermacher.

    She slowly creeps inside, silently closing the door behind herself and crouching down a little, like she’s a rogue and she’s playing Skyrim or some other open-world adventure. Grills and the others would be proud of her.

She pulls her arrow a little further back and he raises his hands, his beer bottle dropping onto his easy chair and spilling beer all over the material it’s made of. “Bro, what is this? I’m trying to enjoy Disney Channel marathon! All the classics!” He gestures to the television behind him with the remote, and Kate feels some sympathy for him. If she were at home, she would probably be doing the very same thing.

She would definitely cut you!” He’s in a pair of red sweatpants and a white tank top that’s covered in popcorn crumbs. “You want to watch Disney Channel instead of murdering me?”

    “Okay, bro, if you say so,” he says, sounding insincere, if a little concerned.

It’s much harder than it looks in the movies because his limbs flop all over the place, and it takes her far too long to duct tape his legs and hands to the chair. Picking it up, she puts it back in her quiver, then walks over to the window and opens it up. She allows the room to aerate for about fifteen minutes, then closes the window and takes off her gas mask.

Whatever, that’s not even the point of why I’m here.” She glares him down until he finally stops trying to look behind himself. “Like I said, I have some questions.”

She worked the front desk.”

    “Got totally blasted by the boss, bro,” Tomas says, making another explosion noise and nearly knocking himself to the ground when he wriggles again for emphasis. Kate has to grab the legs of his chair to keep him upright, for which he thanks her. “Shame about the vest, though. Nice vest. So many pockets.”

    He gives her further directions, and Kate quickly finds the room he’s talking about. It’s more tastefully decorated than she would have thought possible for a tracksuit mafia grunt and his girlfriend, but then again—Kate isn’t one to judge. Her place was redecorated by a Russian assassin.

    “Aw, bro, this is my favorite episode,” he whines, and Kate sighs.

    “What’s your passcode?” she asks, holding his phone and typing in the numbers as soon as he tells her.

    “I’m not lonely, okay? Just because I haven’t spoken to the woman I love in almost a month doesn’t mean that I don’t have other friends! And yeah, fine, maybe I’m upset that my mom is in prison and that I can’t talk to her whenever I want, or hug her, or send her stupid memes I find online, but —” She takes another deep breath and glares at him. “Just—shut up, okay?”

    “Are you—are you crying?”

    “I’m sorry, bro! It’s just so sad! My mother’s in prison, too. It’s like—we’re kindred spirits, Kate Bishop!” Tomas says, a single tear slipping from his left eye and trailing along the tattooed one on his cheek. “And I’m so sorry the boss wants to kill your mother—”

Which, while sucking really, really badly, because—I don’t know if you know this—but solitary confinement is a form of torture—” Tomas nods along, like he’s read the same literature as she has and agrees that the current prison system is a horror. “It’s keeping her out of his grasp, I guess.” She finishes her typing on his phone and slumps back into her chair. “What the hell does Darleen have to do with any of it? She worked the front desk. It’s not like she was a trained assassin or anything—wait. Was she?”

I’m sorry I barged in here like this and knocked you out and tied you up and stuff. I totally ruined your super dope Disney Channel marathon. You’re right, maybe I am a little lonely.”

    “Aw, bro,” he says, and Kate waves him off.

    “Anyway, I want you to do something for me,” she says, showing him his phone and the number she’s just typed into it. “If that tiny guy who got shot by your boss shows up again, I want you to call this number, okay? Because trust me, if you don’t, you and all your friends are going to end up dead. I can promise you that much.”

    “Who’s Elizabeth Knight?”

    “I’m not looking for him, okay? I’m looking for—” She rubs at her forehead and tries to calm herself down. Maybe she’s a little bit more shaken from seeing Darleen dead in the streets than she first realized. “Just call me. You have to promise to call me if you see him.”

    “Cool,” she says, getting to her feet and taking his phone with her. “I’ll just hook your phone back up to the charger, and then I’ll be out of your hair. I promise.” She moves quickly, because she’s starting to feel the fatigue from having to talk to Tomas for any great length of time.

    In the end, she decides not to follow irrationality, so instead she knocks him out with more gas, leaves him on the couch, and hails a taxi back to her apartment. This time, she doesn’t sit on her quiver, but this taxi driver still gives her weird looks. Kate decides he must have recently moved here, if he’s never seen a woman in a purple leather suit before.

    Pizza Dog greets her at the door when she comes in. It’s nearly two in the morning, and Kate crouches down to give him a tired hug right by the front door.

    “We’re close, buddy,” she whispers, smushing her face into his golden fur and listening to him pant in her ear. “I can feel it. We’re so close.”

    She barely sleeps that night, or the several nights that follow.

    Her days are filled with tracking the movements of the tracksuit mafia, going to class, having lunch or dinner with Greer and Dee, tracking the movements of the tracksuit mafia more intensely, watching television, cuddling and walking Pizza Dog, meeting up with Jack to fence, trying to convince the IT-department at her mom’s firm to reinstate her account, tracking the move—

But Tomas appears to have gotten it in his head that they’re friends now, and that she’s an expert when it comes to dealing with his girlfriend’s moods and whims.

    Her mom’s trial is postponed again, and Kate spends a couple of harrowing minutes talking to her the afternoon she finds out. Her mom sounds tired and worn out, and Kate sits curled up on her couch with Pizza Dog sleeping next to her and says something really dumb.

    “I’m sorry, mom.”

    There’s silence for several seconds.

    Pressing her forehead to her knees, Kate waits.

    “You have nothing to apologize for, honey.” Her mother’s voice is thick with emotion when she speaks, and goosebumps race themselves down Kate’s back. “I love you so much. More than anything else in the world, and I am so sorry for what I’ve put you through. I should have put an end to that business relationship a long time ago.”

    What she wants to say is that she’s on the verge of taking on Kingpin.

    What she wants to say is that her mom deserves better than to be locked up by herself for days on end.

    Instead, she says, “I know, mom. I promise.”

    The call that Kate has been waiting for comes sometime in the second week of March.

    She’s having a very loud and very upset conversation with the IT-department of Bishop Security, even though it’s nearly seven pm, when another call starts knocking in her ear. Pulling her phone away and looking down at the caller-ID, she nearly drops the bottle of beer she’s holding.

    “Jacob, I’ll call you back,” she tells the IT-guy, who goes to say something else, just as Kate cuts off the call to accept the one from Tomas. Almost immediately, there’s gunfire, and shouting, and Kate sets the bottle down and starts running for her bedroom. “Tomas?!”

    “Tiny dude brought a tiny friend!” Tomas says, then a very loud gunshot is heard, and Kate is pretty sure he was the one who fired.

    “Stop shooting, Tomas!” she orders him, sliding on her Hawkeye suit and wireless earbuds before scooping up her phone and connecting it to them. “Go hide in an office or something, fuck.” She almost falls down the stairs as she runs, and Pizza Dog barks from his position next to the dinner table. “Are you at the old Stark warehouse down on Neptune Ave?”

    “Contacts, Tomas! Focus! Get to an office!” she orders him again, and finally points her finger at Pizza Dog. “Stay!” Her golden retriever finally gets the message, although he looks supremely unhappy about it as she closes the door on him, her collapsible bow clipped to her waist.

    “Tomas, Tomas—” Kate says over him, her heart alternately trembling and jumping at the prospect of seeing Yelena again. Hopefully alive. “I need to call someone else but stay on the line. Okay, buddy? Can you do that for me?”

    “Bro, it’s so fucking scary,” he says, but Kate can hear that he’s moved, because the gun shots are growing a little bit fainter.

    “I know,” Kate confirms, hoping she sounds sympathetic rather than annoyed with him. “Stay on the line for me, yeah?”

    “Okay,” he says, and Kate puts him on hold to dial Clint’s number.

    Before he can so much as say a single word, Kate starts talking.

    “It’s going down right now,” she says, watching the buildings and streetlights and neon signs and advertisements rushing past her. They all blur together, leaving nothing behind but the image of Yelena. “Neptune Ave, just like we thought.”

Pulling her quiver from her back, she checks to see that she’s brought all the arrows she could possibly need. “I’ll be there in about fifteen minutes. Do you think you can hold out that long?”

She doesn’t want to tell him to step on the gas again, lest he decides she’s not worth the annoyance. “The boss is angry.” “That’s okay. If everything goes to plan, he won’t get you,” Kate reassures him, closing her eyes and straining her ears for anything else they might be able to pick up on. “You said there were two people this time? Are they both wearing masks?”

    “Yeah,” he confirms, and Kate encourages him to keep talking.

    As soon as her taxi rolls to a stop, Kate swipes her credit card as quickly as she can, barely takes time to thank the driver, then rushes from the car and towards the abandoned warehouse. She can see and hear the gun-flashes before she ever reaches the door, and when she does, she sees that it has been left half-ajar.

    “Okay, Tomas, I’m here. I’m hanging up now, okay? Just hold tight,” she says, and he confirms that he’s not moving from where he’s slunk off to. Pulling her earbuds from her ears, she puts them back into their case, then de-collapses her bow and grabs the first arrow.

    There are shouts and yells, and Kate sees a couple of bodies strewn across the floor.

    “Shit,” she whispers, moving further inside. There are several abandoned crates that obscure everything else from view. The Stark logo on them has faded, and Kate presses her back to one as she hears more gunshots.

There’s a second floor overlooking the main hangar of the warehouse, and Kate watches as someone huge steps out of the shadows.
