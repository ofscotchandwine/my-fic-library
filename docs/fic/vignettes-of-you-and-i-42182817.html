    Lena remembers it like it was yesterday.

    "We should go to the beach," Kara said. 

    "What?" Lena asked from her spot on the floor. If Lillian could see her in that moment, boyshorts and sports bra, hair as far as possible from her neck and face, she would have had a conniption.

    "To Midvale. It would be hot anyway, but the beach would at least be windy and we could take a dip." Kara commented from somewhere outside her viewfield of the ceiling.

    "You know, I burn easily."

    "Are you telling me Lena Luthor doesn't have sunscreen with SPF 150?" Kara asked, and Lena could tell there was some mocking and some challenge in her voice.

    "Just so you know, spf 30 or spf 50 works better for sun protection and it's recommended to prevent sunburn. Not that you need it."

    Kara laughed. "Does that mean you have that and we can go to the beach?"

    Lena sighed. "If I get sunburn because of it, I will make you a kale salad for dinner for an entire month."

    "High stakes, can't risk it. I will help you put on sunscreen, so we can prevent that." Kara dashed from somewhere and then stood right over Lena with a big smile. "I already packed up my stuff. Go look for your swimsuit and that very important, very treasured sunscreen. Road Trip to Midvale."

    "Wait, what?" Lena sat up, seeing as Kara had just turned around as if she was ordering her subordinates at Catco. "Kara, it's 9pm on a Thursday. We cannot go right now."

    "Yes, we can. Who's gonna fire the boss?" Kara walked up to the kitchen and started grabbing snacks. "I'll drive. I already told Eliza to leave the spare key outside so we could get in without waking her up, and I told Cat I would take the week off. Come on, babe."

    "You did a lot for something you just thought of." Lena commented, standing up.

    Kara paused for a second and then shrugged. "Just good old superspeed."

    Kara had been true to her word and drove from National City to Midvale. She told Lena that as shotgun she was in charge of the music, then complained when Lena tried to put on Danse Macabre.

    "I will fall asleep if you play classical music the entire time."

    "And I refuse to let you scream the entire Nsync discography again, let alone at this hour." Lena refuted, looking for some type of compromise on her phone. "What about Disney tunes? At least that's a little more diverse and not just Justin Timberlake's voice."

    "Fine, but I cannot believe that you fooled me into believing that they were your favorite boy band." Her girlfriend pouted once again, because of her supposed deceit.

    "I did not lie about it. But you cannot make me hear them for a week non-stop and then expect that I won't get scarred by it. I'm not 7 anymore." She said as she pressed play on Zero to Hero, from Hercules . Kara pouted again, but then started singing at the top of her lungs as if nothing.

By the time she woke up, not only had they arrived at Midvale, but Kara had carried her to the bed in the guest room, along with all of their stuff, and the sun had already peaked on the horizon. Basically, a full night of sleep.

Breakfast was an extremely comfortable affair, with Kara putting her mom up to speed about the National City heroes' latest gossip, and Lena and Eliza talking about a new xenobiologist report.

Lena had to admit that Kara was right; Midvale was cooler than National City these days, with its fresh, salted air flowing from the beach. After Eliza left for work, her beautiful kryptonian carried an umbrella, several towels, a tiny cooler, and a chair to the sand all by herself, and then called for Lena. There was some pop music that Lena had not the faintest idea who the singer was, coming from Kara's phone. Kara went into the water with her swimsuit, ("You know, I once told Winn I wouldn't use this thing on the beach. Now it's like a joke, but it feels so comfy.") and Lena pretended to read while intently looking at Kara's long strides in the ocean. After almost an hour, her girlfriend practically carried her into the water with her. To play like children, Kara let her push her underwater.

Kara was reminiscent of them all, and she ate in every single one of them. Lena only indulged in one ice cream cone at the creamery where Kara had her first job. Overall, a great vacation day.

Lena frowned but took her girlfriend's hand in hers anyway once she walked up to her. The blonde smiled at her and, as an only explanation simply said, "Bonfire."

    Darling, you're the one I want, and

"And I'd like to scream it from the rooftops. To wake up everyday with you, and deal with your pre-coffee grumpiness. And get gray hairs because of how stubborn you are. Help you with all the corporate complications and idiots that want to compare you to your brother, when you are a million times better than him. I want it all with you." Kara's face was beautiful under the moonlight, all sincerity and love. "Lena Luthor, will you marry me?"

    "I guess that would have to do."

She commented, taking the bag from Lena and walking to a spot near one of the biggest and oldest trees in the park. "Yours?"

    "And, it also helped with the plant's photosynthesis?" Her niece innocently asked.

     

    How do you exactly tell one 8-year-old that your sun was red because it was dying? That that sun couldn't help the plants synthesize anything because the planet didn't grow plants or crops naturally because your planet was also dying? That the few plants that grew on Krypton were grown in labs under lamp lights and that their leaves were rarely green with life for long, unless kept in very controlled spaces?

It's the one in my office, with the pink flowers. One of them, at least." Kara took Lena's hand and kissed it as a thank you. "The original is on Argo, all green leaves and pink flowers, way taller than I." She told her niece and nephew. "I brought one sprout to Earth… And here it bloomed as tall in a matter of months. Now I have one in my office, and Aunt Lena has another. But that first one is on the balcony at home. I think the yellow sun does to it the same that it does to me."

     

    "What do you mean?" Jackson asked.

She waited for the 4-year-old to shake his head yes before continuing. "Well this plant, just like Aunt Kara, seems to absorb solar energy, and it has never lost its leaves or flowers. No matter the season."

    "Sure, my class knows mommy works with Supergirl anyways." Esme shrugged all concern off, as any child would.

    "I don't know how they do it." She mentioned it as she sat, sprawled, next to her wife on the couch.

And Esme is so curious, it's like her brain is always connecting stuff, jumping from topic to topic very fast. And sometimes I don't even know what she's talking about."
