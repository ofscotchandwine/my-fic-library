    Chapter 1: we could’ve played for keeps this time

    Chapter Summary Rio comes back just in time. But for a child born of Death...is she fast enough? Slightly canon-divergent because MY Rio Vidal still respects her wife's wishes but will also hold her while their child is born.

    Agatha wasn't sleeping. Not even close. She would have loved a little extra rest, but no amount of runes, spells, or (safe) sleeping potions would be able to soothe the sharp sensation of her son's foot lodged firmly in her rib cage. Two more weeks, give or take. Two more weeks and he'd be in her arms.

    Almost the exact second she wondered if his other parent would be gracing them with her presence anytime soon, the door to the cottage creaked open and Rio slipped inside. She was quiet. Too quiet.

    "Long night, my love?" Agatha murmured, feeling too heavy to lift her head from the pillow, but shifting slightly, an invitation for her wife to lay down beside her. To her surprise, Rio did not take her up on it, instead sitting gently on the edge of her side of the bed and running a hand up and down her side, stopping at the exact spot where a tiny foot still rested. "Agatha?"

    "Hmm?"

    "When was the last time you felt him?"

    With no small amount of effort, Agatha pushed herself into a seated position, fighting hard to keep from wincing when the small act caused a painful tightening sensation across her back. "You're joking, right?" She breathed out a laugh, closing her eyes to soak up the moment of relief she got when whatever muscle had revolted in her back loosened up a little. "Your hand is right on him. I swear, his foot has been there for what feels like..." "A week."

    "How did you..." Agatha's back tightened slightly again, and she braced herself on Rio's arm to admit defeat and get the rest of the way out of bed. "How did you know that?" "My love..."

    Agatha froze.

    "No."

    "Agatha, we talked about this."

    "I said no."

    "I told you this might not work. After the first one, I told you this could..." Her voice trailed off and Agatha knew they were remembering the same day. She hadn't been feeling well all week and had chalked it up to winter. The sky was the kind of gray that ached with sadness and every time it looked like the ground couldn't possibly hold more snow without crumbling beneath them, more would fall. Thinking some rest would help, she had settled next to Rio, only to wake up an hour later cradled in her wife's arms, gentle kisses pressed to her forehead and one hand splayed out across her abdomen, green magic pulsing at her fingertips. She hadn't realized she was being healed until she saw the blood. That time was so early, Rio hadn't even been able to detect the soul before having to take it. Same with the next. And the next.

    Their girl was most recent. Agatha had made it to six months that time, taking every ache, pain, and bout of morning sickness with minimal complaint. She was starting to enjoy the feeling of the little flutters, and would often get scolded by Rio for not resting, but wide awake with one hand gently pressed against where she thought a foot might be and waiting patiently. As long as she was feeling all of that, everything was fine. Until it wasn't. Aurora Celeste was born sleeping on a starless summer night, named by her mother ("To send her to the stars. Pretty...right?") while her mama held her close and starred daggers at the wall, refusing to move even one finger to brush the tears from her face because that would mean even one less second she would get to spend holding her daughter.

    Their boy was supposed to be different.

    "You said this wouldn't be like last time," she said finally, and the way Rio held her gaze made her want to dissolve right there.

    "I said I hoped this wouldn't be like last time. I didn't...you know as well as I do that it's not a promise I could have made. It's...Agatha, could you at least sit down so we can talk about this?"

    Agatha had started pacing, the movement the only thing easing the tension in her back that had started to work its way all the way around her body.

    "There's nothing to talk about. He...he's fine. He's strong."

    "He's dying."

    Usually Agatha appreciated Rio's bluntness. She hated the way humans sugarcoated and danced around the truth, often causing a world of hurt that could have been avoided by having a single uncomfortable moment. It was the way her mother had treated her, telling half-truths to get her to stop asking questions until the night she found herself bound and being dragged to a stake by the women she had been told to consider her sisters. Rio never understood the human art of bullshit, never used it, and for that, Agatha was grateful. But this one time, she wished for the tiniest bit of honey and deception.

    "He can't be."

    "He is." Rio extended one arm and brushed her fingertips against Agatha's ribs like she was feeling for his foot. "I can't tell if...I think he's stuck. Sometimes that means the cord is wrapped around the baby's neck."

    "Stop it," she whispered.

    "Or maybe...it could be that..."

    "SHUT UP." She hadn't meant to yell. She really, really hadn't. But every inch of her body was crying out in pain and the more possibilities Rio posed to herself, the more it felt like she was being strangled. Her body didn't know whether it wanted to cry, scream, be sick, or just collapse, and being unable to decide she stayed standing, though every last bit of color had drained from her face.

    "I'm sorry," Rio whispered and this, she could tell, was as sincere as everything else she had just said.

    "Just go."

    "Agatha..."

    "Leave. Get out." Her voice shook on the last word, but she stared Rio down with the look, one eyebrow slightly arched in a way that suggested 'please, challenge me, I dare you.' "If something happens to you."

    "Then something happens to me. But he will be fine. You certainly think a lot of yourself, knowing when it's time to take someone. But you came back here because you live here, not because it's my time or his. Don't start acting like you're here to take us now. If you're going to take me, you know the way I'd prefer, but I don't see you doing that either, so you might as well go."

    There was something in Rio's expression that almost made Agatha wish she hadn't doubled down. She was exhausted. She was in pain. If she was completely honest with herself, there was very little that would make her happier than getting back under the covers and letting herself be held. Rio had a gift when it came to getting her to not just fall, but stay asleep. It was something in the way she would gently rub right between Agatha's shoulder blades with one hand and stroke her hair with the other. There wouldn't be a kiss exactly, but she would plant her lips at the crown of Agatha's head. It was like lulling a feral cat to sleep, eventually she would put her claws away and close her eyes. Rio only kept her sleep dreamless when she asked, but even on the rare nights she did jolt awake drenched in sweat with the echoes of her coven and her mother still ringing in her ears, Rio resumed her gentle touches until she drifted off again. She already knew she wouldn't sleep for a minute that night and Rio hadn't even left yet.

    "Please," Rio said softly, draping her cloak over one arm, "be careful. There's a midwife a village over. She's...like us. A summoning spell ought to do it."

    Agatha nodded, though she didn't know what part of going back to bed required a midwife. Rio crossed the room in a couple strides and wrapped Agatha in a hug, cautiously at first as though she wasn't sure the touch would be allowed, but held her tight. The tightening in Agatha's back came back with a vengeance, she had been standing up far too long, but she didn't pull away just yet. She would never admit it, but she needed this. On her way to the door, Rio reached into her cloak and pulled out two lemons, gently placing them on the table as she passed.

    "Te veo."

    Surprising herself immensely, Agatha did sleep that night, but the thought of calling it restful was a joke. The pains she had been feeling weren't alleviated in the slightest by being in bed, and when she did drift all the way off, she was plagued by dreams of blood, way too much blood, and a faceless midwife telling her nothing more could be done. If that was Rio's midwife, she wouldn't be summoning anyone anywhere. She'd heard of a few good ones. They wouldn't be hard to find on foot. If Rio knew she was thinking about going out like that, she would stage a mutiny in their living room, but Agatha was too worn out to care. She was pregnant, not made of glass. A little activity wasn't going to kill her. And she had to admit to herself that seeing some kind of medical professional wouldn't be the worst idea in the world. The aches in her back had been joined overnight by a fairly consistent twinging in her abdomen that almost reminded her of the day Aurora was born. But it couldn't be that. Not yet. Not when they still had two weeks.

    She decided to bring the lemons with her. Rio didn't say why she had given them to her. She'd heard of them being used to cope with labor pains by biting them, but Rio hadn't said for sure that that was why and the possibilities were endless. Maybe it was just to make tea. She just wordlessly placed them on the table and walked out. Like I told her to, Agatha reminded herself, but some bitterness still lingered.

    Agatha hadn't even made it down the path to their cottage when the pain hit, ripping through her like it was trying to separate her legs from the rest of the body.

    No. No no no no no. Not this. Not now.

    She held the gate Rio had added the summer before in a death grip waiting for it to subside, straightened herself up, and kept going, slower this time. Maybe it was a false alarm. She'd heard of that happening too, all the pain and none of the reward, no baby for weeks, sometimes a couple months. It took her a hundred more yards, another pain that doubled her over, and the unmistakable sensation of her water breaking for her to realize this was no false alarm. Her boy was coming and he was coming fast.

    She stopped on the next contraction. If she hadn't been in so much pain, she might have found it almost funny how differently each one hit her and what soothed it. Sometimes continuing to walk felt best, sometimes she just wanted to slump into the nearest surface that would hold her up and wait it out. This one had her leaning against a tree and it was there that she noticed her legs. Her water had broken, there was no denying that, but the damp fabric of her dress was just red enough. Every book she'd ever read said it should have been clear. She was bleeding from somewhere, in the middle of the woods. No Rio, no midwife. If there was a time for a summoning spell, she decided this would have to be it. She would decide later how to explain to the midwife that she was among the trees and not resting comfortably in bed where her wife had left her.

    The second the summoning spell left her lips, Agatha knew she had made a colossal mistake. A summoning spell ought to do it, Rio had said. There was only one that she knew off the top of her head, her need for analog magic was rare at most, and one word in advocamus sororem viridem was enough to clue her in. Viridem. Green. She hadn't just summoned a witch. She had summoned a green witch. More specifically, the one she had just sent away. The one who would take her son if the situation was dire enough, before he took his first breath. She had to go. She had to go now.

    Running wasn't the easiest in the state she was in, but it was the only plan she had. The trees were starting to shiver and the more the wind picked up, the closer Rio had to be. Agatha stopped only one time, pulling one of the lemons and a knife from her bag with shaking hands. She missed on the first try, only slicing off the end and thankful that she hadn't taken her finger with it. It was dangerously close. Bracing herself for how disgusting it was going to be, she bit down hard. Her goal was to stay as quiet as possible, should that buy her more time without Rio figuring out her exact location, but a contraction hit strongly enough to force a strangled scream from her throat. She wasn't dying, she was pretty sure, but if this was what the pain felt like without Rio, without green magic and numbing serums, she wasn't convinced she was going to live through this experience.

    When the contraction let up, she moved again knowing her time to get somewhere safe was running out, and realized in the kind of horror that felt like her heart had stopped that she had no idea where she was. There was a river, but she had gotten herself so far off the trail. The only thing she could see past the trees were water and even more trees. No signs of life. Her screams had probably run the wildlife off. She settled herself against the largest tree she could find, closed her eyes, and ran one hand over the spot on her side where the baby's foot had been, though it didn't seem like he was there anymore.

    "Agatha."

    Breathing through the end of another contraction, she let her eyelids flutter open. There, right where she'd told her not to be, was Rio.

    "No. I told you...I told you not to come."

    "And I told you when I married you that if you let me, I will hold you through every pain you feel for the rest of your life."

    Agatha wanted to collapse into her. She was tired. So tired. And the longer her boy was inside her, the more dangerous it would be. The greater the chances of losing him before he could enter the world.

    "I can't...you can't. It cannot be."

    "It must be." Rio twirled a flower between her fingers. "Will you let me take a look?" Agatha wrapped an arm protectively against her abdomen. "You can't take him." "I don't want to take him. I want to make sure you're safe."

    "Please let him live. Please, my love," Agatha gasped. Rio moved to her side, crouching down on the forest floor and lifting her skirt slightly.

    "You're bleeding," she said softly, and Agatha almost choked out a laugh. Rio had a habit of pointing out the obvious that, in any other situation, would have been comical. "Well spotted."

    Rio placed one cool, comforting hand on her thigh and waved the other in a slow circle. "I can offer only time."

    "How..." Agatha's breath hitched as another contraction took hold. "How much time?" Rio slid an arm around her and guided Agatha until she was comfortable and settled with her back against her. The black heart only beat when they were touching, but its gentle pulse was reassuring.

    "As much as I can get you. I promise."

    This wasn't exactly an answer, but it seemed to satisfy Agatha for the moment. "You need to push, love." It was gentle, but it wasn't a request. "Listen to your body, it'll do the rest."

    Agatha leaned back against Rio, feeling the sweat clinging to her hair soaking into Rio's cloak.

    "I've got you," Rio whispered. "You're doing great."

    Her body took over from there and in two big pushes, she was able to sit herself up and catch her son.

    Rio was in front of her almost immediately, making the fastest work of untangling him from the cord and massaging his tiny body to get his blood flow going.

    "Rio..."

    "He's okay. Give him a second."

    Agatha's eyes narrowed, but she let her wife continue working, and in just another moment, her son's newborn wails echoed across the forest.

    "Here you go, Mama," Rio said gently, settling the baby against Agatha's chest. His dark eyes opened slightly, taking her in, and he reached out with one tiny hand to cling to her hair. He was captivated by her, but it was nothing compared to how Agatha was looking at him. Her bright blue eyes were so glassy with tears that they looked almost crystal-like. "He's perfect," she whispered. "Hi, sweetheart."

    Rio sat cross-legged opposite the two of them.

    "No spell. No incantation."

    "You were made from scratch," Agatha finished, tearing her eyes away from her son for just a second to look at Rio. "You're leaving, aren't you?"

    Rio shook her head sadly.

    "Would you ever trust me again if I didn't?"

    Agatha looked down at the baby in her arms, and that was all the answer Rio needed. "We're inevitable, you know. This isn't the end of us."

    "You'll watch out for us?"

    "You're mine, Agatha Harkness. Nothing will happen to you on my watch. I swear it." If Agatha had been a little less tired, a little more observant, she might have noticed that Rio had made no such promises about their son. Nicholas, she had decided to call him. The time Rio would be able to gather for them would seem infinite. A couple seconds from everyone in the universe would add up to an eternity.

    "There's a cabin a quarter mile east." Rio's hands glowed green and Agatha felt the pain between her legs start to dissipate. "I made sure it didn't...it's safe. Take him there and settle until you figure out your next move."

    Agatha nodded.

    "Promise me, Agatha."

    "I promise."

    Rio ran one finger gently down the side of their son's face and pressed her lips to her wife's. "You know how to find me, my love. Te veo."

    Chapter 2: puzzle pieces in the dead of night

    Boston, 2006 To the untrained observer, the breeze that blew in when the door opened felt like the kind of wind that made the trees shiver and brace for rain. The witch at the bar knew better. Incoming weather didn’t smell like sandalwood and vanilla, and she pinched the bridge of her nose gently. Never too early to prepare for the inevitable headache.

    “Don’t expect me to be happy to see you.” Just the sight of Rio made the tension in Agatha’s shoulders relax.

    “It’s been a while. I missed you.”

    “I hate you.” “I missed you too.”

    Rio slid into the empty seat next to Agatha.

    “Sure, you can sit here,” she drawled, but it lacked her usual bite.

    “You okay? That was a small coven for your standards. Only took me one trip to get them out of here. If I didn’t know better, I’d say your heart wasn’t quite in it.” “At least I have a heart.”

    “Isn’t that just expensive spicy water for you? What’s the fun in that?” “Maybe I just like the taste.”

I’ll keep an eye on her.” Had the bartender been a little less focused on the drinks he was pouring, he might have noticed ice blue eyes widen with barely concealed rage and flash violet. “Agatha…”

    “You’re different today,” Rio said softly.

    “Observant. Can’t imagine why.”

    “I know you, Agatha.”

    “Lots of people know me. Want a medal?”

    She wobbled slightly on the barstool and Rio reached an arm out to steady her, pulling back when her wife’s fingertips crackled purple at her touch.

    “Okay,” Rio soothed. “Okay.”

    “You could at least pretend you know what day it is.” Agatha stared her glass down to avoid Rio’s eyes, wishing she could do anything at all to make her arms forget the feeling of being wrapped around a boy who was cold…so cold…too cold for a morning where the sun was already beating down harshly enough to leave a thin layer of sweat on her forehead. “You could act like you care.” Her voice broke and tears flooded her eyes and she made quick work of conjuring water into the glass and pretending to do one more shot. Let Rio challenge her on it being anything more than alcohol making her eyes water. Let her see how well that would go.

    “91,250.”

    “Am I supposed to know what the hell that means?”

    Rio took one of Agatha’s hands, rubbing slow circles with her thumb. To her surprise, Agatha didn’t jerk away, but almost softened into the touch.

    “250 years. It’s been 91,250 days since I had to. Agatha…I’m sorry.”

    Agatha stared down at their intertwined hands as though she was just realizing who was touching her.

    “So you know how many days it’s been since you ruined my life. Impressive.” In another world, if she were human and with another person, Rio might have felt offended. Hurt. But she knew Agatha better than she knew herself. She knew the broken eye contact, the drop in her voice, the rasp it got when she was trying not to cry. This brand of cruelty was nothing more than a shield.

Not today.” “Are you hearing this?” Agatha had turned back to the bartender. “Doesn’t it just drive you crazy when people can’t take a hint? You tell them to leave you alone how many times?” “Sounds like she’s just looking out for you.” He chuckled softly. “There are worse things in the world than letting yourself be taken care of.”

You might just learn a thing or two about marriage. Starting with avoiding it.” She nodded toward the lack of a wedding band on his ring finger. “Let me spare you some misery…what did you say your name was, hon?”

It took a force of nature to stop her wife once she was in the middle of this kind of rant, and her ashen face and slight tremble to her hands made Rio wonder if she was going to throw up. That, she could at least pin on the alcohol, but it wouldn’t get them out of there any faster. But when she looked closer at the rise and fall of Agatha’s chest, there was a hitch every couple breaths. This was two and a half centuries of feelings creeping to the surface and they needed to get out of the bar as soon as possible.

No name, no numbers on it until she slid it across the bar, and then the spell took hold. Whoever Jennifer Kale was, she would have a slightly drunk grieving witch to thank for the unexplained charges on her next statement, and Rio was too concerned about Agatha to care. I have to go. I have to. Agatha sat down gingerly on the edge of the bed running her finger over her locket until she hit the second piece of metal attached to the chain. She would deny it if ever asked, but the thought of her wedding band any farther away from her would likely elicit the same reaction as hearing the bartender’s first name. It was the only piece of her wife she could hold on to, knowing that no matter how hard she tried to speak the words “I still love you,” only venom and hatred would come out, barring her from ever hearing it in return. Then again, who was she to complain? It wasn’t like she deserved anything more.

    “Head down, my love.” Rio sat down next to her on the bed, lifting her mane of wild curls to gently rub a cool, damp washcloth on the back of her neck. It didn’t have quite the same effect as it did in their cottage in the mornings after dreaming about her mother, but the chill shocked some life back into her brain and Rio’s vanilla scent was everywhere. It was almost more intoxicating than the tequila. And she didn’t deserve it.

    “Better?”

    Agatha didn’t answer. Couldn’t answer. 91,250 days before, she had let their son slip away, lost in dreams about green torches and the ghost of a kiss on the side of her head that any other night, she would have woken up from. In 91,250 more days, the pain would be just as raw, as though it had happened just moments before. It would never get better. Nothing would ever be better again.

    “Agatha.”

    “Make me forget,” she whispered.

    “What?”

    Agatha reached behind her and started to unzip her dress, standing up to let it drop to the floor.

    “Make me forget.”

    Rio stayed where she was on the bed. “Not like this, my love.”

    Shoes were next, kicked unceremoniously across the room, one landing on the couch and the other next to the air conditioning unit.

    “I said. Make. Me. Forget.” Agatha crushed her mouth against Rio’s, all tears and teeth, surprising herself when she slid out of the kiss and into her wife’s arms. She wanted to cry out for Rio to stop holding her. Stop soothing. Stop stroking her hair. Stop making it better. Make those insincere shushing sounds, say “I’m sorry” or “it’s okay,” any of the things said to crying people that really meant “shut up.” But this was Rio, who loved her with the loyalty of a Labrador, even when she didn’t deserve it. Rio who was smoothing her hair out of her face and brushing tears away with one hand and tracing patterns on her back with the index finger of the other. Healing runes.

    “I’m not going to do that,” she finally said softly. “We’re going to stay right here. You’re going to sober up and we’re going to remember him. You need to stop…you can’t keep doing this.”

    “It’s only once a year.”

    “He wouldn’t want this for you.”

    “What if I want this for me?” Agatha winced slightly as her voice trembled and broke again and she buried her face in Rio’s shoulder.

    “Maybe you’re allowed to want it,” Rio said thoughtfully, “but we want a lot of things we can’t act on. Things that sound like a good idea. But if we don’t know for sure, better to leave it alone.”

    “He looked like you,” Agatha choked out, tightening her grip on Rio’s sweater. “I know he did, sweetheart.”

    “He looked just like you. It was unfair. Carried him for nine months and your DNA still won.”

    Rio laughed and resumed her stroking of Agatha’s hair. “He had your smile. Right down to the dimple on his chin, that was all you.”

    “I never noticed. How horrible of a mother was I that I never noticed whose smile he had?” “Scared,” Rio said firmly. “Not horrible. Never horrible. If I had thought each day with that smile could have been the last…” her voice trailed off.

    “I used to think it was going to happen right in front of me. I would dream about it every night. It took a lot of different forms, but sometimes he would start showing powers. He would blast and I would…I can’t control it. You know I can’t control it.” “I know you can't. I know.” Rio tugged Agatha’s arm gently, guiding her from the edge of the bed up to the pillows. She didn’t necessarily go willingly, sitting up completely rigid and feeling every agonizing second of this pain was far more appealing. But Rio could be very persuasive, the hands running through her hair felt better, and it was hard to turn down an offer to collapse into a pile of pillows when the majority of their run-ins ended in drywall and broken glass blasted in every direction.

    “Don’t fix the hangover,” Agatha warned. “I want to…need to…this is the only time I’ll ever be willing to face a consequence, just go with it.”

But the image of Agatha’s eyes popped into her head before she could speak, looking haunted and scared behind that plastic smile. The spell was hurting her, she was just too far gone to know that she was in pain. “I have to go away for a little bit. Just…look out for her. Whatever it is you do here, potlucks…she’d hate that, don’t do that. Give her book recommendations. Teach her about sourdough starters. No…she’ll burn the house down, don’t do that either. Make sure she’s safe. Please.”

    “Sure thing, dear. What are neighbors for?”

    Rio wouldn’t know. They’d never had a neighbor.

    “Call me Rio,” she said gently, remembering taking her husband, how worried he’d been that she would have to shoulder her grief alone. If Agnes was a fraud, the least she could do was make Rio a friend. “I work out of town a lot. But thank you…you know…for this. I’ll be back soon to check on her. I promise.”

    Rio tried to force an ounce of sympathy when the time came to take Wanda Maximoff. She even switched to her very best full skeleton face for the occasion. But doing her job that day was going through the motions. Send that witch off to wherever she deserved to spend eternity and go back to her wife, that was her mission.

    With Wanda gone, Rio walked up Agatha’s Westview driveway, dagger bouncing against her hip. She was expecting a fight, looking forward to it if she was being honest, but in the exhausted resigned way that came along with knowing this would be the final straw. Restored to her true self, Agatha would leave at the first sight of her. There was a finality. This felt like goodbye.

    “Agatha?” she called softly, knocking on the door a few times. Nothing. She raised her hand one more time and jumped back as the door swung open.

    “Westview PD, hands where I can see them.”

    Out of nothing but sheer confusion, Rio did as she was told, fighting to keep her face neutral as she took in the sight in front of her. It wasn’t Agatha. But it wasn’t the put-together Agnes from the past couple months either. Something in her eyes looked wild, like she had just finished being chased, her hair was pulled back into a messy unbrushed ponytail, and she was wearing a family reunion baseball shirt from a family Rio would place money on Agatha never even meeting. Attached to a belt loop on her jeans was what appeared to be an empty holster clumsily made from cardboard and duct tape, only missing—

    “Whoa,” Rio said, biting back a laugh. Agatha was holding the head of a garden hose right in her face.

    “This is a crime scene. Get out of here.”

    The door slammed so hard the hinges shook, and Rio turned around to a few of the neighbors watching.

    “I wish I’d seen you go up there,” one called. John, she was pretty sure. “Would’ve told you not to bother. She’s been a little weird the past couple days.”

    “You don’t say,” Rio snarled back. “Cop isn’t her normal gig?”

    “She’s been kind of agitated,” the other neighbor—Harold Proctor, Rio remembered because she had taken someone with the same name living near Agatha in Salem—chimed in. “This morning I played along and she relaxed a little. Give that a try? We just went, though. Your turn, John. You, new girl, come with me.”

    Rio followed Harold back to his front yard. He unlocked the car that was in his driveway and got into the driver’s seat, motioning for Rio to open the passenger door, opening the windows once she was seated.

    “Hey, neighbor.” She could hear John loud and clear. “Surprised to see you out here.” “Disciplinary action?” Rio whispered.

    “Yeah, we’re not really sure what that’s about,” Harold said. “She keeps saying she punched a suspect. I’ve got nothing.”

    Agatha kept rambling, softly enough that Rio had to strain to hear her. She caught bits and pieces—something about crush injuries and a dead woman, something that sounded far too much like Wanda Maximoff.

    “Gonna go help John,” Harold said. “See if we can get her inside, at least. I don’t want to call an ambulance, Sharon Davis would have my head if we did. Been like a surrogate mother for Agnes the past couple years, ever since. Well, you know.”

    He gestured at the ruin that used to be Wanda’s house. Yeah. Rio knew. “Don’t call,” she agreed. “Go help John. Give me a second to figure out what to say and I’ll help talk to her.”

    Harold got out of the car and Rio took a deep breath, popping open the glove compartment. There was a parking pass inside that she removed and clipped to her shirt, then grabbed a pair of aviator sunglasses from the center console and slid them onto her head in just the right spot to push her hair back.

    She walked up Agatha’s driveway less confidently than before, passing John on her way up. “Good, you’re playing along,” he said, taking in her props. “For the best. She just threw a smoothie at Proctor.”

    Rio eased the front door open, pausing in the entryway. No raised voices. Everyone was speaking normally. That was a good sign.

    Agatha sighed loudly. “Now get to the part I’m not gonna like.”

    Rio took that as her cue.

    “Here I am.”

    Agatha’s face fell as she stepped into the room. For a split second it looked like recognition, like some anger had finally bubbled to the surface, but she immediately resumed her rambling about Wanda.

    “She’s just here to help,” Harold said softly.

    “Eat my ass, Chief.”

    Rio fought back a laugh as Harold put his hands up.

    “She’s all yours,” he said and left, slamming the door a little too hard behind him. “What are you doing here?” Agatha rounded on Rio the second the door closed. “My job.” This wasn’t technically a lie, she could consider this part of taking Wanda. “You want to take control of my investigation?”

    “I really, really do not. If you want to be in control…you can be.”

    Any other day, a line like this would have had Agatha all over her but today she just stared, brow furrowed like she was trying to remember something that wasn’t coming back to her. “The body was moved across state lines, is that your play?”

    Rio settled herself down across from Agatha. The hatred, she could handle. The screaming, the fighting, none of that mattered because at the end of every round they would fall into each other’s arms for the night before parting ways. This was hell.

    “Is this really how you see yourself?” Agatha’s gaze drifted again, and Rio was almost shocked at how utterly, helplessly lost she seemed. “Fine, let’s talk about the case. What are your theories?”

    This seemed to bring her back a little bit and ever the perfect wife, Rio bounced questions and fake crime scene details off of her, stopping only to test the cracks of this fragile reality again. She felt almost guilty about it seeing Agatha’s reaction to suggesting she’d lived in Westview since birth. Almost.

    “Go,” Agatha said finally. “I don’t want you here.”

    Rio left the room with a soft “te veo,” but didn’t go far. She went into the kitchen where, as it turned out, the real crime scene was. Harold had gotten lucky; the counters and walls had taken the bulk of the smoothie and she set to work meticulously wiping down every surface just to give her hands something to do while she thought through her next move. She couldn’t do much without agitating Agatha and undoing what little progress she had made, so she slipped out the back door and around the side of the house to the front yard. “Rio?”

    Fuck.

    “I thought that was you!” Sharon called excitedly. “If you’re here to see Agnes, I’m not so sure that’s a great idea. She’s been…well…she hasn’t been feeling well the past couple days. Maybe you should come back another time.”

    “I can’t do that.”

    “You’re a good friend, Rio.” Friend. If Sharon only knew. “Here’s an idea! I just made pizzas for my book club and we had a few people cancel. You should take one over if you’re going to stay with her for a bit.”

    Rio tried to protest but Sharon was in and out of her house with a plate faster than she could say no. It wasn’t the worst idea in the world, she admitted to herself as she walked back up the driveway to the front door. In the height of her power, Agatha had a tendency to let everything else take a backseat if she thought she was close to something bigger. Taking proper care of herself was usually the first thing to go. There were countless nights that Rio would return to their cabin and find Agatha fast asleep at the table with her head resting on her arms, surrounded by more books than one person could even skim in one evening. The chances of her remembering to sleep or even eat in this state were minuscule at best. “What?” Everything about Agatha’s voice and the force with which she threw the front door open suggested rage, but Rio noticed the tears in her eyes in seconds. “Did you know that it is a universally acknowledged truth that a lady cop cannot be good at her job and have a healthy personal life at the same time? Hungry?”

    She was half-expecting the door to be slammed in her face, but Agatha opened it just a little bit wider, immediately resuming her laundry list of case details.

    “There was a car wreck about an hour before time of death. Eastview.”

    “Eastview? See…I thought you turned into a pumpkin that far afield.”

    “Hey, I travel. I’m worldly.”

    “Oh, yeah?” Rio pressed gently. “Where have you traveled?”

    Where hadn’t Agatha traveled? The trail of bodies wasn’t contained to the United States. Rio had collected bodies from no fewer than thirty different countries and one very memorable excursion to international waters. Unsinkable ship. Right. Tell that to Agatha Harkness. This question landed exactly how she wanted it to, another crack in Agnes O’Connor’s flimsy life story, but Agatha shook it off. She’d have to try again, something stronger. It felt a bit early to go for the kill shot, but she was running out of options.

    “Can I ask you something?”

    “Yeah.”

    “Do you remember why you hate me?”

    Memories swirled in Rio’s mind, and she tried to use the connection of their soul binding to push them into Agatha’s. A cottage in the woods. The pulse of a third life force. Trees. A river. Blood, so much blood. A boy. Six years, simultaneously too many and not enough. Something. Anything.

    “No.”

    Rio eyed the locket Agatha was turning over and over in her hand, something she was sure wasn’t there earlier. “Are you hiding evidence?”

    “No.”

    The boy interrupted at just the right time, and Rio couldn’t pretend she wasn’t laughing at her wife interrogating him in the same chair she had just occupied. She had to leave the room to cover the giggles when he started chanting in Latin. She’d already done the heavy lifting breaking that spell, but sure, let him think he was doing something. There were actually tears in her eyes by the time Agatha had duct taped him and thrown him in the hall closet. Composing herself, she walked back to the living room.

    “Case closed then?”

    “How did she die?”

    Right. Agatha had a thing for the Scarlet Witch, how could she have forgotten? “Wrong question. That witch is gone. And all the copies of the Darkhold with her, leaving you in her distorted spell. But you don’t have to stay there.”

    Agatha’s eyes met hers and for the first time in three years, their soul connection began to breathe.

    “Yes,” she encouraged. “Claw your way out. Good.”

    The Agatha in front of her cycled through so many outfits and hairstyles she started to feel dizzy, but one by one the layers peeled off.

    “You know her name,” Rio said softly, fuming that she had to mention Wanda again but holding out hope that this would have the desired effect, “so what’s yours?” “Agatha,” her wife whispered, and as they locked eyes, Rio stepped out of the room and slipped into the basement and down the steps. This would be the kind of change that would need some adjustment and she knew her wife well enough. She would be better off handling the incoming vulnerability on her own before facing another person.

    A little nip on her ankle jolted her out of her thoughts.

    “Hi, bud,” she said to the rabbit who was now eyeing her suspiciously. “Relax, I’ve been taking care of her.”

    Scratchy huffed and slammed one of his back feet onto the floor.

    “I’m sorry, do you have thumbs?”

    Another thump.

    “Okay, don't believe me. But I was, okay?”

    Scratchy hopped to the steps, fixing his eyes on Rio and glancing over to the door leading outside. Fine, she’d go. But only for a minute—Death liked to make an entrance. She waited outside for the yelling to stop before approaching the front door one last time. If Agatha was back, knocking was overrated. She didn’t mean to blast it all the way off its hinges and into a million pieces, but sue her, she was excited. This would be it, she thought as her wife picked herself up off the floor. Cornering her against the wall, she unsheathed the dagger that had been waiting oh, so patiently, pressing it into Agatha’s chest with just enough force to make it bleed. Not enough to truly hurt her, never hurt her. Just enough to have some fun.

    “I’ve missed you,” she said, and was surprised by how raw it sounded, how much her voice shook. Through their connection, she sent through even more memories. Every kiss, every laugh. Bridal carrying Agatha through the door of their cottage while she squealed and begged to be put down, afraid of being dropped despite resting in arms that would never let any harm come to her. I love you. I love you. I love you. Agatha fixed Rio in an icy stare as though taking her in for the first time, and for a moment she thought…she really thought. “I hate you.”
