“Lena?”

The name hung in the silent air of the Tower. Kara dipped her head to scan the vacant space, eyes darting to every dark corner. Sticky footprints traced behind her with each step of her rubber boots against the floor. Kara made a mental note to consider a stealth upgrade to her super suit, although it surprised her that she could hear anything over the pounding of her own heartbeat in her ears.

Her relationship with Lena, though no longer an active battle ground, hardly felt resolved and Kara wouldn’t exactly call what they had now friendship. Too much interfered for them to have any real conversations, and the back-to-back world-domination threats and quick trip to the phantom zone skipped them ahead a few steps in their recovery process. Goodness, there’s no way they could truly just move past it without ever speaking about it, could they? That was the problem, wasn’t it? Kara’s ribcage squeezed against her lungs and she resolved not to make the same mistake. For too long, Kara hadn’t prioritized her relationship with Lena, pushed it behind her own ego, refused to have a single, adult conversation because there was always some excuse.

And this time was no different. Did it count if the excuse was Lena, though?

“Lena?” she said again. “Eeek!” Kara drew her hands to her mouth to hide her full-faced grin. Two glowing eyes peered up at Kara from under one of the storage shelves. “Come here, little cutie, I’m not going to hurt you.”

Kara took a tentative step towards the hiding black cat and extended a hand as a peace offer. She bit back her smile when a little black nose nuzzled against her finger.

“Why don’t you come out of there? It’s probably filthy. Gasp! Are you looking for mice? I bet you’re hungry. I can take you home with me, get you cleaned up, pick up a few cans of gourmet cat food and, oh gosh, you could be our mascot! Would you like that? I could probably have Brainy make you a super suit or a little cape!”

As she rattled off her plans, the cat emerged from under the shelf and stood at Kara’s feet with an almost incredulous look on its face.

“Right, no cape,” Kara said, “but we have to find Lena first.” This reminder pulled her attention away from scooping the cat up and she whipped her head around. “I can hear her heartbeat, but it’s beating three times faster than it should be so I’m a little worried. You haven’t seen her, have you?”

“Kara.”

Kara jumped and hovered a few inches above the ground. She spun back to face the cat with her mouth gaping.

“Lena? Oh my gosh, you’re a cat. You’re a black cat.” She resisted the urge to pat and scratch and fiddled with her cape instead. “You’re adorable, what happened? Can I pick you up?”

Lena huffed and Kara squealed. A low, steady voice purred.

“I was practicing a few spells and, apparently, I can shapeshift.”

Kara’s teeth dug into her bottom lip and she wiggled with excitement.

“Goodness, Kara.” Lena dropped onto her butt and cocked her head. “Yes, you may carry me. But only because I refuse to walk outside and I am quite hungry.”

Kara’s arms shot towards Lena but hovered just above her fur, her hands maneuvered around Lena’s body, uncertain.

“Cat anatomy, Kara. Just pick me up.”

That was all the consent Kara needed to scoop the black fur ball up by the armpits and cradle it, Lena, she reminded herself, into the crook of her elbow. It was only with the mental note that this was Lena that stopped her from plowing her face directly into the cat’s muzzle and tickling her stomach. Although the desire appealed to Kara moreso after the reminder. What? Probably because they hadn’t been close for so long and this felt like a safe way for them to interact. Cat anatomy and all.

“Grab my spellbook, please. It’s on the counter.”

Lena’s words snapped Kara back to reality and she floated over to retrieve the book. Walking seemed a little too harsh, too jolting for the precious cargo Kara held in her arms.

“Ok, all set. Let’s get you fed,” Kara said and headed back toward the exit.

“Kara,” Lena said as they left the tower. “Did you really say the word ‘gasp’?”

“Damnit,” a resigned Lena said.

The paw drooped and hung by the thread wrapped around the claw and Kara almost missed the grumbled permission through her own silent laughter.

“Lena,” Kara gasped when she grabbed the paw, “you have pink toe beans, I can’t believe it. So cute.” And then the paw was gone. “I’m not hiding from you-”

“Lena,” Kara interrupted, “you’re under the bed.”

“I’m a cat, Kara,” Lena’s voice was soft. “And an introvert, and a social outcast, I’m not sure I could be anywhere other than under the bed. There’s a sock under here, by the way.”

“Really? Which one? No, wait, that’s not important right now. You’re not an outcast, Lena.” Kara gnawed on her bottom lip searching for her next words. “You never were.”

“You all lied to me, Kara.” The words sat heavy in the air. “Every one of you.”

“It was my secret to tell. I put that burden on everyone. It was irresponsible of me to befriend you under those circumstances.”

“You wish you didn’t.”

“What? No. I mean, yes. No, that’s not what I meant. I wanted to be your friend, but I shouldn’t have done it in a way that relied on lies for us to be friends. I should have been honest with you from the beginning instead of taking away your choice as to whether or not you wanted to be friends with an alien, let alone Supergirl.

“And then, gosh, I couldn’t even keep things separated. When I found out you had kryptonite, it felt like a knife to the back that my best friend would be keeping the one thing that could kill me, but you didn’t know it was me. It just felt so personal because Clark and I are the only beings in the galaxy that can be killed by kryptonite, and when I thought that you didn’t trust me, Supergirl, and felt it necessary to have a weapon to contain me, it was a good reminder of my alienness and the way people view us.”

Kara wrung her fingers together in her lap.

“I guess if I wasn’t so determined to keep Supergirl hidden, we could have had those conversations as Kara and Lena. Like normal friends who discuss alien amnesty and what backup plans were in place to monitor Supergirl and Superman. Instead, I relegated our conversations to the land of superficiality when I should have been talking to you about things that mattered. Because of my lies, we never had a chance, did we?”

“I’m not sure an alien detection device was an open invitation for you to be honest with me about that.”

Lena crawled out from under the bed and sat next to Kara, facing away with her head downturned. Kara wanted to run her fingers down Lena’s back, wanted to boop her nose, wanted to scoop her in her arms and nuzzle close. She settled for talking.

“But it sparked a good conversation between us. I think we both learned something new that day. We could have spent the last five years educating each other and growing together. I’m sorry. I wanted to be your friend, and I was a terrible one.”

Lena’s tail curled around her, like she was giving herself a hug.

“Can we continue this conversation later?”

“Yeah, no, I, um, sorry.” Kara deflated.

“It’s not that, Kara, it’s just.” Lena turned her head to the side then twisted it farther around to sneak at Kara. Kara glanced over and to where Lena had looked.

“Oh, right, of course. Yep, take your time.”

Kara stumbled to her feet and super sped out of the bedroom.

How ironic, Kara chuckled to herself, that Earth’s yellow sun should make her invulnerable while it could literally kill Lena.

“I’ve never done this before.”

“Sunbathed?”

“Talked about my feelings. Until you came along, I was a master at compartmentalizing.”

Kara laid in silence, unsure how to respond. She figured any reference to her and Alex talking it out on the roof as teenagers or their couch talks now might not have the intended effect of being comforting. So she stayed still. And quiet.

“She says they found a ‘weird glowing crystal’ on the floor at the tower.”

“You’re not unloveable, Kara Zor-El.”

For the second time in her life, Kara witnessed life as she knew it explode in a fiery cacophony of scrambled senses. Every untapped desire flowed through her like water through a broken dam when Lena’s lips covered hers. It wasn’t until she was tracing her mouth along Lena’s jaw towards her ear that she realized Lena now sat straddling her lap. Kara dragged her fingers down Lena’s back and when they scraped just below appropriate, Lena let out a purr. Once again, Kara found herself wrapping her arms around Lena to keep her from fleeing.

“Gosh, now I know why you stopped me from scratching too close to your tail.”

Which was the exact wrong thing to say. A perfectly sculpted eyebrow arched in a challenge. Kara’s lungs burned with her held breath as Lena placed a hand on her chest and leaned in to speak, her breath hot against Kara’s ear.

“Wait till you find out what happens when you touch my ears.” And Lena purred.
