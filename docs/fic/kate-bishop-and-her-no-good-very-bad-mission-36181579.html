    Chapter 1

    Not for the first time that night, Kate Bishop wondered if she’d maybe gotten in a little over her head on this one.

    She’d been dismissing the creeping feeling all evening. Dismissed it when she encountered like twenty more tracksuit security than she’d expected from her - admittedly brief - reconnaissance. Dismissed it when she got lost in the complex and managed to trip the alarm, sending the whole building into a loud frenzy. Even dismissed it when she’d come up against some fancy man with an electrical sword.

    Mostly dismissed it because, frankly, she’d managed to overcome all of the above challenges. Besides, the odds had been worse on Christmas Eve with Clint, and she’d survived!

    Unfortunately, Kate considered, as she eyed Wilson Fisk having to turn sideways to make his way through the door into the chamber she was in, Clint wasn’t here tonight.

    In fact, Clint had explicitly told her to not come here at all without backup. Made her promise she wasn’t going to do anything rash without his support.

    And she had promised but in her defence, her fingers had been crossed.

    So, there’s that.

    “Miss Bishop.” Fisk smiled wide at her, arms opening in a greeting that Kate was beginning to suspect wasn’t sincere. “What a wonderful surprise.”

    “It’s actually Hawkeye.” She corrected, trying to keep the slight whine out of her voice. But seriously, she has the outfit, she’s shooting a Goddamn bow and arrow, surely it’s obvious-

    Bang

    She squeaked as she just managed to dodge the bullet shot at her by an overeager henchman. A henchman who was soon followed by others, all filing their way into the chamber after Fisk, and blocking the only exit.

    Well, fuck.

    Kate decided she’d rant internally about how disrespectful it is that criminals don’t seem to accept her as the new Hawkeye later. Preferably when she’s not getting shot at, and when she can truly consider if the issue lies in the deep rooted sexism within the criminal underworld-

    Bang

    “Hooooly shit that was close.” She muttered out loud as she just about dodged another shot, this one had been sailing straight for her abdomen. Fisk was now smirking, clearly amused by the situation, and Kate wished she could find the funny side right now.

    Scanning the group in front of her, she counted 18 men she had been calling Fisk Fodder in her mind. The ones that just seemed to keep coming regardless of how many she put down, the ones that Fisk seemed to have an endless supply of despite his apparently awful treatment of them. She had 12 normal arrows left, along with 4 trick arrows. Yes, she did notice that 12 plus 4 did not equal 18, but she’s also never seen a rule she couldn’t bend, so why stop now?

    “You don’t have enough arrows, Miss Bishop.” Fisk said politely, as if he were remarking on the weather.

    She hated this man and his smug face and his awful dress sense.

    Kate scoffed, desperately trying to buy some time and concoct a plan. “That’s what you think.” She stood tall and tried to project a calm confidence, the aura of someone who definitely wasn’t panicking. She couldn’t afford to panic. Panic meant death, and if she died then who was going to feed Lucky? It just wasn’t an option.

    Bang.

    Okay she had to stop zoning out like this. This time she felt a white hot streak of pain as she just about shuffled out of the way, the bullet caressing her side just below her ribs before embedding itself in the concrete wall behind her.

    Focus, Kate.

    Fisk had started monologuing, something about her mother, which would usually piss her off but she really wanted to survive this so she tuned him out.

    The room she was in was circular, with various rusted pipes and electrical boxes on the walls and jutting out of the floor. It was clearly some kind of utility access room, because she could hear one of the cylinders in the corner rumbling like an engine, and could see steam rising from the wall near where the pipe entered. It was big enough for her to fight in on the plus side, and gave her sufficient room to keep distance while using her bow effectively.

    She’d need to take out his fodder first. Fisk was easily the biggest threat, but ideally she wouldn’t need to fight him, just get around him so she could make her way to the server room.

    Just like Clint taught her, she eyed up her opponents. The man on the far left was trying to shuffle around her and out of her sight. He had a slight limp - recently damaged left knee, if she had to guess from his odd gait - and decided a good kick should sort him out. The two guards slowly advancing on her right were bigger than her but also heavier. With any luck, she’d be faster and two normal arrows would do.

    “-and really, I’m not sure what your mother was thinking, quitting on my business?” Fisk continued. “I suppose your lack of self-preservation doesn’t just come from your father-”

    “Don’t talk about him.” Kate muttered. Her jaw ached from how hard she was clenching her teeth together. Anger roared up in her chest, hot and burning and she could see a slight tremor in her forearm from how hard she was gripping her bow. She notched an arrow - one with a red label - and drew it back, aiming at Fisk’s eye.

    Fisk was unphased. In fact, he smiled wider, and she knew she’d just done exactly as he wanted but she couldn’t help it. “Truly. Would your daddy be proud? Of a daughter who sent her own mother to prison? Of a daughter who-”

    Kate released the arrow and it sailed true. There was a quiet thwip as it scorched through the air, coming within an inch of Fisk’s right eye before he batted it to the side, sending it clattering into the wall behind him. There was a brief moment of silence, when all Kate could hear was her own thundering heartbeat in her ears and heavy breathing.

    Then-

    BANG

    This one wasn’t a gunshot. Her trick arrow had detonated behind Fisk, sending him flying a short distance and thumping to the ground hard. He immediately moved to stumble to his feet, but 7 of his henchmen weren’t so lucky. By Kate’s count, that was 11 left, plus Fisk.

    This time, the math did work out.

    Before Fisk managed to stand, she had notched two arrows in her bow and fired, sending the two guards on her right flying backwards and to the ground with arrows through their shoulders. They’d live, but the powerful sleeping drug she dipped all of her arrows in would keep them out for a good couple of hours.

    Clint had looked especially proud when she’d come up with that modification.

    9 left, plus Fi-

    Her head was ringing.

    Kate blinked. Then blinked again.

    The room was spinning? Or was she spinning?

    She couldn’t be spinning. She was on the ground, she couldn’t be spinnin-

    “I’m- on the ground?” Kate mumbled, feeling the sudden, visceral urge to throw up as her head spun harder and she squeezed her eyes shut to block it out. Her ears were ringing and her head hurt and her ribs hurt and there was a sharp pain in her leg and-

    This was bad. She squinted her eyes open again and looked up, finally finding Fisk stood over her with a small cut to his face and a scowl.

    Ah. Well that explained it.

    “Hey big fella.” Kate hissed out, hands scrambling on the dusty floor for her bow though she had no idea what she’d do with it. “Do you ever go to Hawaii or just like- collect the shirts-”

    “Enough.” Fisk snarled and stood hard on her hand. Kate cried out and she felt one of her fingers do something it wasn’t supposed to. “You’re almost as chatty as that Spider-Boy.” Though Kate was fairly convinced this was her last moment on earth, she did feel a bit of pride that she was being compared to Peter.

    “I’ve been told I’m- ah-” Kate gasped out as he pressed harder on what she was convinced was at least one broken finger. “Actually very charming.”

    He looked decidedly unimpressed and Kate swallowed as he leaned down and placed a big, meaty hand around her throat.

    She was definitely out of her depth, totally in over her head. She was ready to admit that now, and she kicked and tore at him, trying to dislodge the iron grip he had on her airways but he wouldn’t budge, and Clint was right and that was so annoying-

    She couldn’t breathe. She couldn’t breathe-

    The room went black.

    Not in the oh I’ve fallen unconscious and I’m not long for this world way, but in the someone turned off the lights way. The rumbling of the engine in the room was silent, only leaving the hissing of the pipes and the ringing in Kate’s ears.

    Fisk froze, then released his hold on Kate’s neck. She sucked in air desperately, tears stinging her eyes as her bruised throat worked to inhale.

    “Well?” Fisk demanded, and Kate couldn’t see but she could hear the way he was swinging around in confusion. “Someone go fix it.” Kate would have found the way his minions bumped into the walls and each other in the process of leaving the room funny if she was in any state to laugh right now. As it was, she tried to quietly scramble away. She bumped softly against a warm, big pipe to her right and steeled herself to crawl behind it for cover.

    The movement made her nauseous, she felt seasick and the ringing in her ears wasn’t getting any better. She lowered herself to the ground carefully and waited to hear movement from Fisk. Her finger was throbbing but she felt almost detached from the pain now, as she stayed as still as she could and listened for any threat from Fisk.

    She’d need to move soon. The second they turned the power back on, she was dead.

    Fisk was big but he was human, he couldn’t see any better than she could right now. Which meant that as long as he couldn’t find her, she was safe.

    “I can hear you breathing, little mouse.”

    Well, fuck.

    Heavy footsteps towards her position.

    Then, shouts of alarm from outside the room.

    Gunfire. A lot of gunfire.

    The footsteps stopped, but the gunfire and screaming continued. Kate could hear the henchmen still in the room muttering among themselves. She could hear the click of safety’s coming off on guns, nervous shuffling of feet as they heard the commotion outside.

    Fisk snarled. “They never work alone. Go find him.”

    The guards started to make their way out of the room but never got that far. The 6 that remained were suddenly illuminated with bright strobe lighting, dazzling them as the flashing light flew through the air and landed in the centre of the room.

    A figure appeared in the doorway. The lighting made it impossible to make out any details, made it hard to even track their movement, but they punched the throat of the nearest guard and he crumpled to the floor.

    “Clint?” Kate choked out. No, it couldn’t be Clint, he was away with his family, halfway across the world, so who-

    “Kate Bishop.” Came the reply before dazzling red electricity arced through the next goon who raised a gun. “I come to save you, and you insult me?” Kate rolled her eyes, if only to stop tears of relief from rolling down her face.

    Yelena was wearing goggles and Kate assumed it was to negate the darkness and strobe lighting. They must have worked, because she watched in flashes of movement as Yelena ran halfway up the wall and flipped off, landing on the shoulders of the next man and snapping his neck with brutal efficiency, then twisting off him and sending an elbow into the throat of the next.

    Fisk was watching with contempt and the hint of something else on his face. He watched his men get cut down with apparent ease by Yelena, who swept the legs out of the last assailant and caught his temple on her knee as he went down, knocking him out cold.

    Kate looked around and found her quiver lying nearby, arrows still secure inside, and crawled towards it with as much urgency as she could muster. She just managed to grip it as she was hauled backwards, thick hand on the back of her neck. Panicking, she gripped the 10 normal arrows and jammed them hard into his side as hard as she could.

    Still, he didn’t let go of her, instead he laughed and gripped harder, causing her to cry out as his fingers dug into her skin.

    “Let her go.” Yelena was there. She had dusted herself off and in the strobe lighting looked as threatening as Kate had ever seen her.

    Fisk dragged Kate in front of him and it was then that Kate noticed the gun in Yelena’s hands. It was still, unwavering and trained unflinchingly at Fisk as he held Kate up to cover the middle of his body.

    Grunting as Kate wriggled, when Fisk replied he almost sounded bored. “What is it, with Black Widows and Hawkeyes.” Yelena didn’t reply, body as still as a statue while Fisk continued. “What happens when I break her wings, hmm?” He grabbed Kate’s arm and twisted it slowly behind her, pushing and pushing until she couldn’t help it and she gasped with pain.

    It burned. She could feel the ligaments straining in her shoulder, her elbow and wrist almost creaking with the pressure.

    “Let her go. Now.” Yelena repeated, but her voice was darker now, accent thicker, and Kate couldn’t see behind the goggles but she was sure Yelena was looking at her.

    Suddenly, Fisk wobbled in place and he swayed. It was happening a minute later than Kate had expected, but she wasn’t about to complain. She jammed her elbow back against the stack of arrows still embedded in his side and he hissed, grip loosening enough to allow her to drop away from him. She scrambled the second she hit the floor, half crawling over to Yelena and collecting her bow from where it lay on the ground.

    The second she was free, Yelena had fired her Widows Bite directly at his face. He convulsed and toppled backwards, seemingly in slow motion until he hit the floor with a loud crack.

    They both froze, breathing heavily for a second. Then, Yelena jumped into action. She collected the strobe light from the ground and switched it off, taking out a torch and pressing it into Kate’s hand instead. Kate turned it on and found Yelena already collecting her arrows for her before yanking a gun and a few rounds of ammo from a man lying bleeding on the floor.

    “I…” Kate wasn’t sure what to say. What can you say when the Black Widow who had tried to kill your mentor not 3 months ago - before dropping off the face of the earth - turned up to save your ass just in the nick of time. “I totally had it under control, just so you know.”

    Yelena turned to look at her, goggles off now they were using torchlight, and raised a single, perfectly styled eyebrow. “I’m sure.”

    “Totally, 100% under- oh.” Kate blinked and suddenly she was on the floor again. “Huh.” That ringing in her ears was back and the nausea had returned with it, and suddenly Kate could feel the broken finger and probably fractured ribs and the slash in her leg that was oozing blood and- “Hey Yelena?”

    Yelena had rushed over to her the second she hit the floor and was busy shining a light in Kate’s eyes, one after the other. “Yes, Kate Bishop?” Her hands were surprisingly gentle on her face, Kate thought, especially compared to the last time they’d met. Last time they met, Kate had slapped Yelena. And then Yelena had punched her and Kate had thrown her and Yelena had kicked her. Now, though, Yelena was very carefully cradling her face in gloved hands.

    “I think I’m going to pass out now.” Kate sighed and then felt her body lose the last of its energy. This time, when the room went black, she knew it wasn’t the lights. Waking up was an unpleasant experience, to say the very least.

    Kate’s head was pounding, and her whole body felt like it had been hit by a truck.

    She opened her mouth to ask for water, but all that came out was, “unghhhhhhhh.” She cleared her throat, still not able to force her eyes open, then tried again. “Clint?”

    “Again, Kate Bishop, you insult me.” Came a vaguely amused but definitely offended voice from the left. Memories came flooding back, and Kate snapped her eyes open to find herself in her own bed, fingers in a splint, bandages on her face and surrounded by blankets. “I am far superior to Barton. But you have had a significant head injury, so I will let it slide.”

    “Unnnghhhh.” Kate managed again as her head pounded even harder. Still, she managed to turn her head to the side to find Yelena Belova, curled up in a chair next to the bed, watching her carefully. The sun was shining through the window behind her and it lit up Yelena’s blonde hair as if it was glowing. Kate was abruptly aware of how much of a mess she must look right now, and moved to brush her hair back from her face. “How did we get out?” Her voice was scratchy and she could only manage a whisper, but she’d take it. Yelena passed her an opened bottle of water and Kate drank from it slowly, trying not to drown herself just as she dodged death.

    “ I-” Yelena emphasised, “-carried you out of the complex.” She shrugged. “It was not hard. I had already neutralised most on the way in, and with the lights still off it was relatively easy to leave.”

    “Easy. Right.” Kate muttered, more than a little bit mortified that Yelena - Black Widow, trained assassin, Natasha Romanoff’s sister - had carried her out of the building like a child. “Uhm, was it like, fireman carry, or bridal carry…” She trailed off, catching the incredulous look on Yelena’s face. She swallowed and winced as her bruised throat protested. “Just… wondering.”

    Yelena squinted. “Well I put you over my shoulder when I had to shoot someone. And then carried you against me when I was not busy shooting someone. To support your neck, you know.” She smiled, smug and teasing. “Like you would a baby, actually.”

    Kate could feel her face burning with embarrassment. “Right.” She glanced over at the window and found the sun already high in the sky. “What time is it?”

    “Is that really what you want to ask?” Yelena replied and glanced at her watch. “It is almost 3pm. You slept for a long time, but I ensured your concussion was not too severe.” She looked at Kate again, eyes becoming piercing and face entirely serious. “It could have been severe, Kate Bishop. And we would not be having this conversation.” This time, her face burned with shame. She didn’t appreciate Yelena - of all people - talking to her about responsibility. She did have to admit that a woman who had survived what Yelena had, could maybe give her valuable advice. She avoided her gaze, and heard Yelena sigh. “I did not mean to upset you.”

    “You didn’t.” Kate whispered, then forced herself to look over at where Yelena was sat watching her. “Thank you. I didn’t say it at the time because, ha, concussion.” She wobbled her head around as a joke, but it made her so viscerally nauseous that she had to stop immediately before she vomited on a Black Widow and her nice clothes. She breathed carefully, getting control over herself again before looking back up at her. “I would have died without you there.”

    “Eh.” Yelena shrugged. “Like you said, you had it under control.” Kate raised an eyebrow, and Yelena let a little smile slip onto her face. “ Okay, so I am trying to work on my bedside manner. You would definitely have died if I had not turned up. I did not think it was polite to point out again.”

    Kate snorted, then winced. “Note to self: No laughing while injured.” She muttered under her breath but she could see Yelena’s mouth twitch in response. Kate rolled her eyes at herself and then powered through. “Since when do you care about being polite?”

    Yelena remained quiet for a moment, running her eyes up and down the length of Kate’s body under the blankets. She pursed her lips. “You have two fractured ribs, with additional bruising. You received a deep cut to the thigh which avoided a major artery by only an inch. You are concussed. You have a broken finger. I suspect you have also sprained your wrist, but I could not be sure.” She listed the injuries off, voice seemingly unaffected until she looked back up to meet Kate’s eyes. “I did not wish to worry you.”

    Kate swallowed. She had definitely been in over her head last night. “Me? Worried?” Her voice shook a little; it was just starting to hit her how badly it could have gone had Yelena not suddenly appeared- wait. “Wait, why did you turn up?” She realised her tone was a shade too accusatory, and Yelena’s eyebrows immediately shot up in response. “Not that I’m not super grateful. Obviously.”

    “Obviously.” Yelena repeated dryly, a wry smile on her face. She reached for the chocolate bar next to Kate’s bed and started unwrapping it. She took a bite and then continued, mid- chew. “Barton called me-”

    “Clint Barton?” Kate interrupted, feeling like maybe this was a symptom of the concussion.

    “Yes.” Yelena answered, voice very low and speaking slowly. “Nathaniel Barton cannot use a phone, first of all-”

    “Okay, don’t be a dick.” Kate huffed. “It could have been Laura.”

    “Well, it wasn’t.” Yelena took another large bite of the chocolate bar. “Barton called me and said he thought you were going to do something stupid.” She swallowed the chocolate and pinned Kate with a flat stare. “He was correct.” She twisted sideways in the chair and kicked her legs over the side, reclining back. “Anyway, he called me and said Yelena please help I would be forever in your debt-”

    “You were not there, Kate Bishop.” Yelena droned as she swung her legs back and forth. “Well, he explained you had called him about getting a lead on some information held by Fisk in a complex just outside of New York. He said you promised to not go, but that he did not believe you.” Kate felt her ears burning - she should probably work on her lying. “You are a very bad liar, Kate Bishop.” Yelena continued, as if reading Kate’s mind. “He knew I was… around, so he requested I go and check on you.”

    Wow. Okay.

    Kate blinked. “Okay that’s- first of all, I’m a solid liar, 6/10-”

    “Absolutely not, your lower lip trembles every time you lie and you speak much faster.” Yelena pointed out, sounding more and more amused by Kate as the conversation went on.

    “Why are you looking at my-” Kate stopped that line of inquiry as soon as it started, likely a good idea as Yelena was raising a dangerous eyebrow again. It made Kate sweat a bit. She’d hate to be interrogated by her. “Since when are you and Clint friends?”

    “We are not.” Yelena sighed then rolled her head sideways to look directly at Kate. It was Kate’s turn to raise an eyebrow, smiling a bit when Yelena narrowed her eyes and her lips twitched at the side again. “Ugh, you are annoying, I should have let Kingpin squeeze you a little harder.”

    “You don’t mean that.” Kate sang quietly. “You were my knight in shining armor-”

    “I was clearly in a black tactical suit.” Yelena said, voice still flat but Kate could see the telltale laugh lines at the side of her eyes. “You are very chatty for someone who is so injured.”

    Kate grinned, thinking back to what Fisk had said. “I am very charming.”

    “Not what I said.” Yelena said, then shoved the rest of the chocolate bar in her mouth. She balled up the wrapped as she chewed and then flicked it at Kate. It hit her square in the nose. “Barton and I have an… agreement. I am busy finding and freeing many other Widows. He babysits Fanny. It is a good arrangement.”

    Blinking, Kate tried to process what Yelena just said. “But, you tried to kill him?” She blinked again. “And who is Fanny?”

    “Ah, old news Kate Bishop.” Yelena waved her hand vaguely in the air. She caught the unconvinced look on Kate’s face and her own expression softened. “Natasha… Would have wanted us to get along.” Then, she was sniffing and the softness disappeared. “So, he looks after my dog, and I inform him of where I am every few weeks. I was in Washington DC when he called.”

    “I’m not a geography girl, but that’s not exactly in the area, Yelena.” Kate continued, but she felt warm with happiness. The fact that Clint and Yelena were getting along - close enough for Yelena to leave her dog with the Bartons - and even that Clint trusted Yelena enough to go and save Kate. It made her throat feel a bit tight, and she took an extra deep breath to suppress the emotion.

    Yelena shrugged and peeled up her shirt sleeves, folding them back carefully. “My bike is very fast.”

    “Of course you ride a motorbike.” Kate muttered and let her head fall back onto the pillows. She was ready for the concussion to go, because the thought of Yelena on a motorbike was making her feel lightheaded.

    “It is a very efficient form of transport.” Yelena smiled, before her smile turned wolfish. “And it is very sexy, correct.”

    “I didn’t say that.”

    “You thought it.” Yelena sighed, clearly delighted with how flustered she’d made Kate.

    Kate stammered, searching for words. “I didn’t think it either.” She took another breath. “In fact, it’s very not hot . They’re dangerous. Not sexy. At all.”

    “Your lip is wobbling.” Yelena didn’t even open her eyes, so Kate knew she was lying about that. “Anyway, Barton called me at 7pm, shortly after you promised you wouldn’t get involved until he returned. I arrived at 11pm and followed the trail of very unconscious tracksuit boys.” Kate wasn’t sure if she was imagining it, but there was a tone of pride in Yelena’s voice. “You know what happened from there.”

    “How did you get me back while I was unconscious?”

    “I stole one of the cars from the compound. The driver will not need it, on account of him being dead.” Yelena said, flippant. “I would have used one of your fancy knockout arrows, but he shot at you as I carried you instead of simply handing me the keys.” A dangerous smile curved her lips. “That was a mistake on his part.”

    Kate gulped. Sometimes she forgot who exactly she was talking to. Having seen her in action the night before, she realised just how easily Yelena had gone on her during their fight on Christmas Eve. Yelena was funny. Sweet sometimes. Maybe the prettiest woman Kate had ever seen.

    She was also a Black Widow. She’d held her own with Clint - an Avenger.

    She wondered if Fisk had been right about that lack of self-preservation, because Kate still couldn’t bring herself to be scared of Yelena.

    That might have something to do with the smudge of chocolate currently above her top lip, though.

    Kate inclined her head and shrugged. “I used all of my knockout arrows on Fisk. So it’s not like you had a choice.”

    Yelena nodded, then shot Kate an impressed smile. “Yes. That was good thinking, to say your brains were all rattled.”

    They sat in silence for a moment. Yelena pushed a bottle of what looked like Lucozade towards her. “You need sugar. I’ll make you some food soon.” She raised an eyebrow at Kate. “I would have made some sooner, but your fridge is tragic, Kate Bishop.” She adjusted one of her bracelets, then inclined her head out of the window. “I know there is a grocery store nearby. I did not want to leave you alone before you woke up.”

    Again, Kate ignored how that made her feel. Instead, she considered something that has been nagging at her. “How long have you been… around New York?”

    Yelena opened her mouth. Closed it. Opened it again and instead of speaking, she sighed. “Define around.”

    “I just-” Kate frowned, thinking back to all of her discussions with Clint, where he definitely hadn’t mentioned Yelena to her. “Why haven’t you said hi?”

    For the first time in the conversation, Yelena looked distinctly unsure of what to say. She fiddled with one of her rings, then sat up properly in the chair. “I thought- I thought maybe I would not be someone you wanted to see.”

    “What? Are you kidding?” Kate reached for the Lucozade and sipped it carefully. “We had fun!”

    “I-” Yelena had that stunned look on her face again. The one she had after Kate slapped her in the elevator. “I threw you off a building. And also kicked you, many times. And then tried to kill Barton. Oh, and your mother hired me.” She swallowed, squinting at Kate as if she was trying to figure out her angle.

    “I… I did not know you were serious.”

    Kate scoffed, drinking more Lucozade. She really did need the sugar. “I’m always serious about drinks.”

    Yelena was quiet for a moment before her face softened again, a small smile emerging. “You are very strange, Kate Bishop.” Yelena watched her for another few seconds before standing. She stretched and eyed the Lucozade. “Drink the rest of that. I will return soon.”

    “You’re leaving, then?” Kate asked, not ready to admit that she felt less than secure in her apartment right now.

    “I told you. You have no food, it is very sad.” Yelena said as she shrugged on a leather jacket. She caught the look on Kate’s face and paused, frowning. “They will not attack you here. Not in broad daylight, and not while I am around.”

But you will not need them.” She hesitated, then decided to explain. “Even hardened criminals like Fisk do not take a Black Widow lightly. It would not be efficient to attack you while I am around. At least, not without careful planning on their part.”

    There was the banging of a cupboard and then the sound of the fridge door opening and closing, before a quiet humming started up again.

    Kate smiled, closed her eyes, and relaxed.
