    Chapter 1

    The house was still and quiet, save for the soft hum of the refrigerator in the kitchen. Agatha was a better sleeper these days. As she woke up, she turned toward the small, round clock on the nightstand—4:57 AM. Pretty close to a whole night of rest.

    Or what counted for a full night once she subtracted the time between when she and Rio made it to their bed and when they actually fell asleep.

    Nicky was getting older, and that meant a lot of things were changing. They no longer had the luxury of staying up late, whispering about their day or indulging in the quiet after-hours moments that once defined their relationship.

    Parenthood had a way of filling what used to be quiet moments alone with school drop-offs, work deadlines, dinner prep shuffles, and bedtime routines that ran on a Sisyphian loop.

    They spent many of their nights reading just one more book to Nicky before he fell asleep, his eyes heavy as he curled between them. It was a ritual now, something small and comforting. Some nights, Rio would scoop him up and carry him back to his own bed. But more often than not, especially recently, she’d look up at Agatha and smile.

    “He’s not going to be this little for much longer. And one day he’ll be too cool for us both and we’ll miss having him here between us.”

    Agatha never disagreed. It was a gift to be able to hold her entire world in her arms at once. Last night, Agatha had put Nicky to bed with a gentle promise: “The sooner you fall asleep, the sooner your birthday comes.”

    He had giggled then finally succumbed to the pull of sleep, the excitement of the next day too much for him to keep his eyes open. It had been a perfect, peaceful moment.

    When she had returned to their bedroom, expecting to slip into bed beside Rio and catch some much-needed sleep, she found her wife still awake.

    “Wanna join?” Rio had asked, voice low and playful. “Or I can take it to the shower?”

    Agatha had eagerly replaced Rio’s hands with her own. It was frenetic and fun in a way that felt new, even after all this time. They’d exhausted each other, laughing and touching, and for a moment, the world narrowed to just the two of them.

    Rio had fallen asleep against Agatha’s chest, her cold feet tucked under her thigh.

    Agatha shifted now, stretching her limbs, and then noticed the absence beside her. Rio’s pillow was flattened, the covers rumpled. She furrowed her brow for a moment, then smiled softly to herself. Agatha had long learned not to worry when her wife slipped out of bed at strange hours; she was just impressed that she still had the energy.

    She took a moment to stretch, she was always taking a moment to stretch lately, before padding quietly down the hallway, pausing at Nicky's room. She peeked inside, and there he was, a bundle of messy hair and the soft rise and fall of his chest under the blanket.

    His hooded Pikachu pajamas poked out from under the covers; she couldn’t remember him sleeping in anything else since he unwrapped the set at Christmas.

    It was his birthday tomorrow—technically today—and they had a whole celebration planned.

    Agatha had spent the last few weeks making sure everything was just right. She’d spared no expense, not that Nicky would understand the cost, but that wasn’t the point. The point was making sure his fifth birthday felt nothing short of magical.

    The cake was a work of art, a masterpiece in the shape of a Pokéball, each layer perfectly sculpted. The bakery had truly outdone itself.

    While Agatha read to their son the night before, Rio had stayed up late decorating the house. Streamers were draped in every doorway and across the mantelpiece, a rainbow of colors hanging from every arch in the ceiling. Agatha had even picked out a few extra touches, little plastic Pokéballs tucked here and there, ready to make Nicky’s eyes light up.

    Nicky’s favorite Pokémon figurines lined the windowsill, and they had planned a little surprise scavenger hunt for him to discover the presents hidden around the house.

    But the crowning achievement was what Agatha had spent the last two weeks organizing with the precision of a military operation. Nicky’s social circle had grown, and it was a delicate balance of making sure he invited everyone he wanted without stirring up any drama. There was a whole week of conversations—subtle, gentle, asking him who his favorite friends were at school, which kids he wanted to celebrate with, and more importantly, who he didn’t.

    There was still, of course, the Pokémon problem. If they were real, Agatha would have gladly rented one; the llama encounter last year had been a huge hit. But since that wasn’t feasible, she’d gotten creative. A quick search online and she found it, an adult-sized Charmander onesie.

    It hadn’t taken much persuading at all. Agatha had presented it like a challenge: “You know, I bet Nicky would love it if you were Charmander for the party. It would make his day.”

    Rio had raised an eyebrow at the onesie, her mouth twitching at the corners as if she were about to protest before she exhaled slowly, shaking her head with a laugh. “Okay, Agatha,” she’d said. And with that, the deal was made.

    Turning to the stairs, she tiptoed down, her gaze scanning the dim living room. In the glow of the lamp that curved over the sofa sat Rio.

    Her eyes were glazed, fingers moving over the buttons of their Nintendo Switch. The quiet chirps of the game floated across the room, a comforting sound in the otherwise silent house. Agatha leaned against the doorway, amused and charmed by her wife. “My love, I don’t think he even knows what a shiny Pokémon is,” she teased gently, crossing her arms as she watched Rio.

    Rio didn’t even look up. “He will when I show him,” she mumbled, her voice soft, almost reverent. “It’s going to be the best surprise when he’s the proud owner of a blue Ponyta.” Agatha chuckled, moving into the room. She dropped a kiss to Rio’s head, the soft texture of her hair a comfort in the quiet darkness of the early morning. “Did you even sleep? You’re up way too late for this,” she said, sitting beside her, her arm brushing against Rio’s.

    Rio finally glanced up, giving Agatha an exhausted, sheepish smile. She paused the game, setting the Switch down on the counter. “I know,” she admitted, her voice a little tired. “But I want it to be perfect. I’ve been trying to breed this one for hours.”

    Agatha raised an eyebrow. "Breeding? What kind of children’s game has breeding in it?" she teased, her tone light and mischievous. She sat back against the couch, crossing her legs in front of her and watching Rio.

    “Besides, I thought after last night you might have been tired of-”

    Rio let out a soft laugh, the tension in her shoulders easing as she met Agatha's teasing gaze. “Hush. You know this isn’t like that.”

    Agatha smirked and poked her with her foot. “Sure. Go ahead and tell me about your totally normal breeding game.”

    "It’s a whole process," Rio explained, her voice shifting to a more serious, but still warm, tone. She reached for the Switch again, her fingers tracing the joysticks as she spoke. "You need two Pokémon of the same species, but with different characteristics—like, maybe one’s faster or more resilient. You pair them together, and after a while, they lay an egg."

    Agatha chuckled again, a teasing edge to her voice. "An egg?" she repeated, almost incredulously. "Aren’t most of Pokémon mammals?”

    “Babe, mammals can lay eggs, the platypus-”

    “My love, please,” Agatha moved to her knees long enough to lean forward and kiss the explanation from Rio’s lips. “I am well aware that platypuses are mammals that can lay eggs. You’ve told me this. Many times. You taught Nicky. And now I have to contend with you and your son sharing fun facts in surround sound. I feel like I’m living in a permanent nature documentary sometimes.”

    Rio laughed and pulled Agatha forward by the shirt collar to kiss her again.

    “Anyways! The egg hatches, and out comes a baby Pokémon that inherits some traits from each parent. If you're really lucky, it might be a shiny one, the rare, special ones with different coloring, like the blue Ponyta I’m trying to breed."

    Agatha couldn't help herself; she burst into laughter, shaking her head at the absurdity of it all. "You’re telling me we’re up at 5 AM because you're trying to breed a mythical horse, just so Nicky can have one with a different coat color?"

    Rio chuckled. “I promise I’m not making it up.” She rubbed her eyes and seemed to try to gauge Agatha’s interest. “You know, Pokémon games are full of these kinds of… interesting moves. Kids don’t even realize half of them are innuendos.”

    “Alright… humor me. Like what, oh Pokémon master?” Agatha chuckled, curling up next to Rio on the sofa and tucking her feet under her legs.

    Rio grinned, clearly enjoying the challenge. “Well, for starters, there’s Double Team. That’s when a Pokémon creates multiple copies of itself to confuse the opponent. You can imagine how... complicated that could get.” She winked playfully.

    Agatha’s eyes widened with mock disbelief. “Oh, so now we’re talking about clones?” she asked. “I could see the appeal.”

    “And then there’s Lick.” Rio grinned mischievously. “Straight to the point, that one.”

    Just to be contrary, Agatha leaned over and licked the side of Rio’s face.

    Rio playfully shoved Agatha away, only to just as quickly pull her back into her arms, closer than she was before. “I’m just saying, it’s all in there. Helping Hand , Submission , and don’t forget about Draining Kiss.”

    She raised her eyebrows meaningfully. “ Draining Kiss is when a Pokémon kisses its opponent to restore its own health. You can imagine how it works… in fact, I think I’ve used that one on you.”

    Agatha’s face flushed, but she couldn’t keep herself from laughing. “ Submission ?”

    “I swear it’s real!” Rio said, laughing now with her. “It’s a move where you—well, let’s just say, you subdue your opponent. It’s intense.” She paused, eyes twinkling. “You’ve never been more vulnerable than when you’re hit with Submission.”

    Agatha shook her head, her laughter dying down as she wiped a tear from her eye. “Well, we would both know. But still, I refuse to believe this. I’m going to look it up myself.”

    Still smiling, she grabbed her phone off the coffee table and began typing in “Pokémon move list.”

    Rio remained completely absorbed in her game, her eyes glued to the screen as she held her breath in anticipation. Agatha could barely hear her wife’s whispering mutterings of “Come on, come on” and “Please, please, please” over the sound of her own phone tapping as she searched. Every time Rio checked the progress of the egg, she held her breath.

    Agatha scrolled through the list of moves, her eyes widening in disbelief at the sheer absurdity of some of the names she came across. “Okay, you weren’t kidding,” she said, her voice full of mock awe. “Here’s one: Splash. A move where the Pokémon just… flops around like a fish?”

    Rio’s fingers froze on the screen, but she didn’t look up. “You think that’s bad? Try Growl.” Agatha giggled, reading out the next one she saw. “ String Shot... It’s a web of string that the Pokémon shoots at the opponent. Very... convenient.”

    Rio let out a barely audible groan, her focus still entirely on the game as she leaned forward. “Okay, but String Shot is actually decent strategically. It can immobilize your opponent.” Her voice dropped as she leaned in even closer, whispering, “This egg’s about to hatch...”

    Agatha barely heard her, too busy scanning the list for more ridiculous moves. “Here’s a good one. Harden… yep that’s exactly what it sounds like. A Pokémon uses it to toughen its body and raise its defense…” She shot Rio a teasing glance. “I’m sure you use that move all the time.”

    Rio let out a fake groan, pressing her palm to her forehead dramatically. "I can't believe this. I rolled my eyes too hard and now they're stuck, Agatha. Help me!" she cried, letting her voice crack in exaggerated distress.

    Agatha raised an eyebrow, clearly amused but also not willing to let her wife off the hook. She leaned forward, taking a moment to enjoy Rio's over-the-top performance, and flicked Rio lightly on the forehead with her fingers. "There. Fixed it," she said, deadpan.

    Rio blinked, her exaggerated distress immediately melting into a grin as she dramatically straightened herself up. "Ah! You saved me!" she said in an overly theatrical voice, sitting up and giving Agatha a playful, grateful look. "What would I do without you?"

    There was a chime from the Switch, and they both turned to look.

    “It’s happening,” Rio whispered, as if a quieter voice would tip the odds in her favor. Like this was a real creature she was trying to soothe and not just a series of, admittedly cute, pixels. “This is it.”

    Finally, the egg fully cracked open, and there it was—an adorable Ponyta, its mane shimmering slightly, but the brilliant blue Rio promised was nowhere to be found.

    Still glued to the screen as if staring harder would change the result, Rio let out a soft groan of defeat. “Fuck! Okay. It’s okay, I still have a few hours before he’ll wake up. I think I can hatch at least five more eggs by then. The odds are just—”

    Agatha glanced at the clock on the wall as Rio rambled. It was almost seven in the morning. The early hours had melted away while they’d been wrapped up in Pokémon moves, and now the practical part of her brain kicked in. She gently kissed Rio’s cheek, breaking her out of the game trance for a moment.

    “I need to make a call,” Agatha said, standing up and grabbing her phone. She stepped away from the couch, leaving Rio to her game, and dialed Billy’s number, knowing full well that her latest assistant wasn’t going to be thrilled to hear from her before seven on a weekend. Billy’s groggy voice came through the line, and Agatha didn’t waste any time. “Billy, wake up. Does your whole nerd virgin thing include extensive Pokémon knowledge? Please tell me it does.”

    Billy’s confused mumble came through the speaker. “Agatha, it’s... seven in the morning.

    Why are you calling me about Pokémon right now?”

    “No time for pleasantries, just answer the question.”

    “I mean… yeah, I was more of a Digimon guy but-”

    “I need you to get me a shiny Ponyta within the next two hours. Can I buy one? What’s the deal here?”

    Billy groaned into the phone. “Are you seriously waking me up for this? Agatha, it doesn’t work like that, you can’t just buy a shiny Pokémon.”

    “Then how do I get one?” she asked, glancing to where Rio was now lying horizontal, the Switch threatening to fall out of her hands and fall against her face. “I need this, like, now.” “Well, there is a trade system, but it’s not foolproof. You’d need to find a player who’s willing to trade a shiny Ponyta. And you have to have something they want in exchange.”

    “Yes, I’m becoming well aware of how rare they are, Billy. But I need one. Desperately.

    We’re running out of time.” She paused, letting him take it in before adding with a bit of extra weight in her voice, “I just made up my mind that I liked you enough to call you by your name and not Teen. I am counting on you to handle this immediately.”

    “Alright, alright. I’ll see what I can do.” There was a pause before he slyly added, “If I do… do you think I could leave a couple of hours early Friday? Eddie wants me to take him to-” “Yes, yes, whatever, that’s fine. I just need this to be sorted out so that my wife can get some rest before 14 five-year-olds and their boring normie parents invade my home. You really don’t want them left alone with me, do you?” Even though Billy couldn’t see her, she pouted, “Think of the children.”

    Billy laughed. “Alright, alright. You always want the impossible at the most inconvenient times, boss. But hey, that’s why I like working for you.” There was a brief silence on the other end before he added, “Give me a minute. I’ll check the trading forums. If there's a shiny Ponyta out there, I’ll find it.”

    Agatha glanced back at Rio, holding her breath as another egg neared hatching. Agatha rolled her eyes and quietly left the room, needing a moment away from Rio’s silent intensity. She walked into the kitchen and started making her coffee, her mind still whirring with thoughts of the trade and how this morning had already turned into one of the most ridiculous ones of her life.

    But if it worked, if Billy could get her that shiny Ponyta, then everything would be worth it. The next ten minutes felt like an eternity. Agatha paced the kitchen, taking careful sips of her coffee, offering a “how’s it going, love” to Rio that was met with a groan.

    Finally, her phone buzzed again.

    “Got it!” Billy’s voice came through, almost too brightly for the early hour. “I found someone willing to trade a shiny Ponyta for a region locked… never mind you don’t care, I’ve already initiated the trade.”

    Agatha felt a wave of relief wash over her, but she immediately masked it with her usual sharp edge. “Finally. Thank you. And you’ll have your time off on Friday. Take the whole day. Just make sure this goes through.”

    “Don’t worry, Agatha. It’s already done. If you can open up the game, I’ll trade it to you now.”

    "Rio, go back to bed," Agatha said, gently prying the switch from her hands. "You have got to get a few more hours of rest before the party chaos. I’ve got everything handled."

    Rio gave a sleepy but grateful grunt, rolling over and burying her face in the pillows. "Okay, alright. Wake me up when the Ponyta is officially in the bag, yeah?" she mumbled, her voice muffled by the blankets.

    Agatha chuckled softly, reaching over to brush some hair from Rio's face. "I will. Don’t worry." With that, she opened the trade window for Billy to send over the shiny Ponyta.

    When the trade was complete, she sat back and stared at the screen, where the shiny Ponyta proudly appeared in Nicky’s team, its blue mane shimmering as promised.

    With a small laugh, Agatha leaned back on the couch and started exploring the game. She watched the little Pokémon trot around and began to understand how her son and Rio would sometimes get so engrossed in the action that they forgot about the rest of the world.

    She smiled as she watched a Pidgey flutter around, and before she knew it, she was laughing at the little antics of her newly caught Pokémon, feeding them berries and naming them silly things.

    Agatha was so absorbed in the game, giggling at the antics of her newly caught Pokémon, that she didn’t immediately notice the soft padding of footsteps approaching. It wasn’t until she felt a small, familiar weight settle onto her lap that she snapped out of her trance.

    This was why she didn’t play games, she got too sucked in. The Candy Crush phase had almost killed her.

    She looked down, a smile spreading across her face as she saw Nicky, still in his hooded Pikachu pajamas, curled up against her. His tiny hand rested on her knee, his face sleepily tucked into her chest as he snuggled closer.

    “Hey, baby,” she whispered softly, kissing the top of his head. “Happy birthday, Nicky.”

    He giggled, his little body wiggling with excitement. “Thanks, Mama!” he mumbled, his voice still thick with sleep. He looked up at her, his eyes wide with curiosity. “Were you just playing Pokémon?”

    Agatha’s grin widened as she nodded, brushing a few stray strands of hair from his face. “I was. Mami’s been teaching me all about them. Looks like I might be a trainer too now.”

    Nicky’s eyes lit up, his excitement practically bubbling over as he sat up, peering at the game screen in her hands. “What are you doing, Mama?” he asked, pointing at the Pokémon that was happily trotting around the screen. “Can we catch another one?”

    Agatha chuckled, her heart warming as she gently adjusted the Switch in her hands. “I was just playing for fun,” she admitted, ready to put it down. “But I think it’s time to turn this off now so we can get ready for your big day.” She moved to close the game but was stopped by Nicky’s loud exclamation.

    “Wait! Mama, look!” His voice was full of wonder. “An egg is hatching!”

    Agatha froze, the sudden excitement in Nicky’s voice catching her off guard. She watched as Nicky eagerly pointed at the screen, his little finger jabbing at the animated egg that had just started to crack open.

    “No way, really?” Agatha laughed, her tone playful. “Are you sure you’re not making this up?”

    Nicky shook his head vigorously, eyes wide as he clutched her arm. “No! I’ve been waiting for this! I just know it’s gonna be something special!”

    Agatha smiled down at him, her fingers lingering over the controls. “Alright, alright. Let’s see what happens.”

    Together, they both leaned in closer, the quiet of the early morning filling the space between them as the game’s egg continued to crack. Nicky’s little hand was warm against her, his excitement contagious as the animation on the screen sped up. The egg gave one last shake before finally splitting open.

    They both gasped.

    Agatha couldn’t help but laugh as she pinched the bridge of her nose.

    It seemed Rio’s breeding scheme had also been effective, and their son was now the proud owner of two shiny Pokémon.

    “Nicky, pinky promise you won’t tell Mami until after I’ve had my coffee? I think she’s going to be very excited.”

    He laughed and nodded. “Okay! I’m calling him Bluey! Can we have birthday pancakes now?”

    There wasn’t anything either of them wouldn’t do for their son. So Agatha made pancakes with rainbow sprinkles. Rio came downstairs in a Charmander outfit that made their son squeal.

    And Nicky had the perfect fifth birthday.
