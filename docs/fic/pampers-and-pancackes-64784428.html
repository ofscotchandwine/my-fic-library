    Agatha froze, forehead still pressed to Rio’s, eyes wide. “No. No no no. This isn’t fucking happening.”

    Rio bit her lip, trying to stifle a giggle. “He hunts by sound now.”

    “Of course he does,” Agatha hissed.

    Another knock, this time accompanied by a slow turn of the knob. “Are you two hiding?” Like teenagers, they scrambled to manage something closer to decency.

    When the pantry door opened, she met her son’s sleepy eyes with the brightest, most innocent smile she could muster. “There you are, sweetheart! Mami and I were just… looking for a little snack.”

    Rio, to her credit, nodded gravely. “Very important mission. For the baby!” Nicky blinked at them. “Why was the door closed? And the light off?”

    Clapping her hands together, she tried to salvage the situation with an easy distraction. “Hey! Did you want to help me find a midnight snack for all of us? Since we’re all clearly awake now?”

    He nodded, yawning, and stepped closer to lean against her.

    As Agatha guided him gently out of the pantry, Rio followed, her face a picture of poorly suppressed amusement as she leaned in and whispered near her wife’s ear. “Two more pantry make-outs, huh?”

    Agatha smirked. “We’ll just have to make the next one count double.”

    Third Trimester Their bedroom was quiet, save for the white noise of the city in the background. The fan whirred low and steady, stirring the edges of the sheets where Agatha lay on her side, watching Rio drift in and out of sleep. She kept one hand curled over the soft swell of her wife’s stomach. The other was tucked beneath her cheek.

    The baby had stopped kicking nearly an hour ago, but Agatha still rested her hand there like she was keeping watch. She was a better sleeper than she used to be. Now when she stayed up late, it was usually because she chose to.

    Just to have a few minutes of quiet. To reflect. To wonder. To love even when no one else was awake to witness it.

    Rio stirred slightly. "You’re still awake."

    "Mhm."

    "You okay?"

    Agatha hesitated, then shifted closer until her forehead brushed Rio’s shoulder. "Just thinking."

    Rio’s voice was warm with sleep. "Dangerous."

    Agatha hummed in acknowledgment, but didn’t tease back like she normally would. Two months. Their daughter would be born in the heat of summer. Lilia had laughed about how fitting it was for them to raise two fire sign children. She hadn’t been in the mood to ask what she meant but now wish she’d had.

    She’d turn to religion, mystycism, anything at this point if she thought it’d soothe the budding anxiety she felt. But none of those had worked before. Only one option ever had. Quietly, she asked, "Do you ever think about what it means to raise a daughter?" Rio blinked herself into better awareness, rolling in Agatha’s arms to face her. "What do you mean?"

    Agatha exhaled slowly. "I’ve been thinking about what it would’ve been like to have a mother who told me I was enough. Who saw me and my ugliness and loved me through it. And I keep wondering what it means to be that mother to someone else. I worry about if I can be that."

    Rio reached for her hand beneath the blanket and threaded their fingers together. "You already are. With Nicky."

    "That’s different," Agatha whispered. "He came out of me with your heart in his chest. He’s always loved me so easily." Her voice caught, just slightly. "I don’t know what it means to love a girl into her… power. I never saw it done."

    Rio kissed the top of her head. "Maybe you didn’t see it. But you built it for yourself. That’s even rarer."

    Agatha laughed quietly, a short huff of air. "I built it with a lot of heartache on the way. And lots of therapy. And you. I built it with you."

    "Exactly," Rio murmured. "And she’s going to get to see that. She’ll get to know her mother as powerful and brilliant and deeply loved—not because someone handed it to her, but because she clawed her way into a life where she got to be all of that." She sighed and rubbed at her eyes. “I don’t want her to have to work that hard. I don’t want her to have to try to be loved.”

    There wasn’t a good answer to that. But Rio pulled her closer and Agatha allowed her to. "I’ve been scared too," Rio admitted. "Not about the kind of parent I’ll be," she squeezed Agatha’s hand, "because I think we’re both better at this than any thing else. But…" Agatha glanced up at her.

    "I’m scared I won’t connect with her. Not the way you and Nicky do. You two... you share a language. And he’s so used to turning to you first."

    Agatha lifted her hand and placed it over Rio’s heart. "He turns to you when the world’s too loud. When he needs quiet, or safety, or softness. That’s what you give him without even trying."

    Rio bit the inside of her cheek. "What if it’s different with her?"

    Agatha shook her head gently. "It will be. But different doesn’t mean less. It just means learning her language too."

    Rio was quiet for a moment. Then she exhaled, deep and steady, and pulled Agatha closer until her nose tucked against her neck.

    "We’ll figure it out?" she asked softly.

    Agatha kissed her shoulder. "We always do."

    There was more unspoken between them still, something tender and raw, the kind of things you don’t have to say out loud in order to know that they are real. Agatha tilted her head back just enough to look at Rio in the low light.

    "Hey. If there’s one thing I know about you, it’s that you love harder than anyone I’ve ever met. That baby’s going to feel it before they even know what to call it." Rio blinked, lashes damp, but didn’t look away. "And she’ll never wonder if she’s too much. Or not enough. We’ll make sure of it."

    A quiet knock interrupted them, followed by the door creaking open just enough for Nicky’s face to peek through.

    "Can I cuddle in the middle? I want to be burrito Nicky. I brought my blanket." he whispered. Agatha sat up, wiping away the tears that had not fallen but still clung to the corners of her eyes. "Sweetheart, did you have a bad dream?"

    Nicky shook his head, already climbing onto the bed with the confidence of a child who knew he’d always be welcomed. "No. I just wanted to be close."

    Rio reached out and helped him up between them, wrapping the blanket around his little frame and swaddling him up. Agatha kissed the top of his head as he snuggled close. For a few minutes, no one spoke. Nicky’s breathing slowed, his little fingers twined with Rio’s, his head pillowed on Agatha’s shoulder.

    "When the baby comes," he said quietly, "can we still do this?"

    Agatha kissed his forehead. "Always."

    Rio rubbed his back in slow, steady circles. "We’ll always have room." Nicky didn’t answer. But his smile was small and sleepy and full of relief. They held him between them, a soft bundle of warmth and love, and Agatha closed her eyes with a quiet exhale.

    They would have more noise. More chaos. More questions and fears.

    But they would also have this.

    This was more than enough.

    Cora Mari Vidal arrived three weeks early.

    Agatha had just settled down with her laptop when she heard Rio call from upstairs. It wasn’t a shout. It was worse—it was calm. Concerningly calm.

    “Agatha?”

    She was up the stairs in seconds. Rio stood in the bathroom doorway, one hand pressed to her belly, the other braced against the wall.

    “I think it’s time.”

    Agatha blinked. “Time for what?”

    Rio raised a single brow. “Not now.”

    “Oh my god,” Agatha whispered. Then louder, “You’re early! She’s early! You’re not due for three weeks!”

    “Tell her that,” Rio muttered, then winced. “Okay. That was definitely a contraction.” The next fifteen minutes were chaos. Agatha called Jen, fumbled through the hospital bag checklist, and then forgot where she’d put the hospital bag. Nicky watched from the stairs in wide-eyed fascination.

    “Is Mami okay?” he asked.

    “She’s fine, sweetheart. She’s just... having the baby. Early. Very early.” Rio, somehow still the calmest of the three, shouted down, “Call Alice to stay with Nicky. And unless you changed your mind about a home birth we need to go!”

    “I’m already on it!” Agatha shouted back. She pivoted and nearly ran into Nicky who was clinging near her like a shadow. “Please go grab your overnight bag. The one with the… triceratops on it.”

    “What’s happening?”

    “Your baby sister is just like her Mami and has no respect for schedules!” Agatha huffed, frantically tying her boots.

    After ushering Rio into the car, Agatha raced to the driver’s side and drove like a woman possessed.

    “You’re doing amazing,” she said, trying to keep her tone even. “It will take us fifteen- wait have you been timing? Fuck we should have been timing your contractions.” “You’re sweating more than I am,” Rio replied dryly.

    “I am not!”

    “You’re clutching the steering wheel like it’s going to run away from you.” Agatha glanced down. Her knuckles were white. “It’s fine. Everything’s fine.” “Love,” Rio said between breaths, “you are panicking harder than the person giving birth.” “I am not panicking!”

    They made it to the hospital in one piece. Cora made her entrance a mere ninety minutes later.

    Agatha cried harder than Rio.

    And the moment they placed that tiny, squalling girl on her mother’s chest, everything—the chaos, the panic, the poorly packed bag—melted away.

    Two became three. And now there were four. And everything was just beginning. After Agatha’s birthday passed quietly. And she couldn’t think of one that she’d enjoyed more. There was frosting still drying on a slightly lopsided cake that Nicky insisted on decorating himself, Rio’s homemade card was tucked between pages of her current book, and she settled into the soft weight of a newborn asleep on her chest and the sound of her son’s quiet breathing from the floor.

    She was stretched out on the couch in Rio’s favorite sweater, hair still damp from the evening’s bath, Cora nestled against her like she already knew this was her spot. Her place. Her home.

    Rio entered with two mugs of chamomile tea, nudging one into Agatha’s hand as she whispered, “Nicky passed out in the pillow fort. He said it was a special day and that he absolutely could not sleep in his bed on your birthday. He also told me to wake him up before we start Land Before Time. He told me it was the best birthday movie.” Agatha smiled. “Reasonable.”

    Rio lowered herself carefully onto the couch beside her, tucking a blanket around the three of them. “You okay?”

    Agatha glanced down at the sleeping baby, then back at her wife, her son curled in a fort of blankets nearby. The tea warmed her palms. “I think this might be the happiest I’ve ever been.”

    Rio didn’t answer right away. She just leaned in, pressed a kiss to her temple, and murmured, “Me too. But let’s see what tomorrow brings. I think there’s a lot of joy we can’t even imagine yet.”

    They sat there in the quiet, moonlight spilling across the floor. The baby shifted, sighed. Agatha sipped her tea, and felt the warmth of it settle deep in her chest. She didn’t know what tomorrow would bring. But tonight, her whole world was within reach. It was a good day. They had a good life. And her wife was right, as she so annoyingly often was. The best was yet to come.
