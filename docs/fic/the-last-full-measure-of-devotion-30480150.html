1. Part I

Author's Note:

The whole point of a clown car is to fit as many as possible, right?

When Alex found her, Lena Luthor was watching the sunset from her penthouse office balcony. If she hadn’t been worried before, that would have been enough raise her suspicion all on its own. Lena tilted her head back toward the sky, the amber sunlight catching her profile. She seemed—Alex paused before she reached the open door to the balcony—determined.

“I’ve seen that look before,” Alex said, stepping forward. “It's not reassuring.”

Lena closed her eyes for an instant, and then opened them, turning away from the multi-million dollar view of downtown National City.

“Alex,” she said, stepping past her back into the office. “I wasn’t expecting you.”

“Well, I’ve been worried. I haven’t seen you since Lex…since Kara—for three weeks. The doorman at your apartment said you’ve barely stopped by there in a month, and you haven’t answered your phone in two days.”

A watch flashed on Lena’s desk, and both women glanced at it. Alex frowned.

“Isn’t that the watch Kara gave you?”

“It is,” Lena answered, picking it up and fastening it around her wrist. “Turns out it was even more useful than she anticipated.”

“Lena, what’s going on?”

She glanced at the watch, which was flashing faster than before. “I’m glad you stopped by, actually.” Lena’s voice was controlled, but slightly higher than normal. Practiced. “It saves me from having to leave one of those overwrought video messages. I’m afraid I wouldn’t have been able to pull it off quite as well as Kara, anyway.”

“You’re freaking me out.”

“Well I’m...” The full weight of Lena’s green eyed stare settled on Alex, and for the first time, she noticed her eyes were shining with tears. “I'm scared. The isotopic signature hasn’t moved in two days.”

Alex shook her head. “We don’t even know that it’s her. Just because it matches her radiation signature on earth doesn’t mean—“

Lena held up her hand, the watch face glinting in the quickly fading sunlight. “I hacked the watch to trace it to the source. The data’s not exact, given the atmosphere and physics of the phantom zone, but it’s close enough. It’s her.”

“Okay, so it is her,” Alex conceded, failing to keep the quiver from her voice. “Maybe she found a safe spot to hide out in, or maybe not, maybe she’s… maybe she’s in trouble, but either way we can’t get there.”

“You know as well as I do that the problem was never getting there,” Lena answered, strolling past Alex and down the hall toward the elevator. “The problem was getting home.”

Alex followed her, jogging to keep up with Lena’s resolved strides. The elevator door opened, and they both stepped in. Lena pushed the button for the basement.

“Last time I checked not being able to get home is still a problem, Lena.”

“We know that the phantom zone can be entered through specific portals we create. Up until recently, we also believed that occasional spontaneous portals would open there, allowing escape to anyone lucky enough to be in the vicinity,” Lena said.

“I hope there’s a reason you’re Luthor-splaining this to me, since I was the one that filled you in on the phantom zone in the first place.”

The elevator dinged, and Lena smiled slightly as she walked purposefully toward a table in her lab. “Bad habit,” she granted. “Anyway, we both know, as scientists, that nothing in nature is random. So I wrote a code that would track the location and duration of each exit portal. It took me a week to figure it out, and the two weeks it’s been online isn’t enough for the data to be foolproof, but over the last 48 hours the algorithm managed to predict where and when the next portal would occur with 83% accuracy.”

Alex stopped walking. “Really? That’s what you’ve been building? That's-- that's amazing.”

Lena looked up from the instrument she was fiddling with on the table and smiled. A real smile, even if it didn’t quite reach her eyes.

“In approximately 29 hours, a portal will open 34 miles from where our target isotopic signature is located. That’s 34 miles too far away for her to get home, so I’m going to the phantom zone to make sure she gets to the portal.”

“What? No. No, there’s no way you’re going to the phantom zone. I was just going to wake up tomorrow with what? A video message from you that you’d decided to go on some crazy suicide mission? You need backup. You need—what am I saying, you need not to be the one doing this at all. You’re not trained for this. We can send a team, J’onn, or—or me, for God’s sake, but—“

Lena clicked a button on the watch, and Alex jumped backward. A suit rapidly expanded up Lena’s arm and across her body until she was covered in a metallic black sheen.

“What the hell is that?”

“I couldn’t very well go marching into the phantom zone improperly dressed, now could I?”

“Lena, you’re not listening to me. I know you’re hurting, but this isn’t—you can’t just unilaterally decide to take on a mission like this.”

“The isotopic signature…” she paused, swallowing hard. For the first time since the balcony, she was still. “The signature hasn’t moved in two days, Alex. You told me what the phantom zone is like, what she went through there the first time. I can’t— it kills me to think of her there, alone, exposed, going through hell again. And that’s if she’s, if she’s still…”

Alex swiped the tears from her own cheeks impatiently. “I hate it, too,” she answered, voice soft. “And look, I know how much you love her, I do. But we need to send a team. Her chances are better if we do this together.”

“I know,” Lena whispered. “But the portals vary in shape and size. The one that’s coming is only big enough for two.”

Lena picked up the device from the table, and Alex stepped back instinctively.

“That’s a phantom zone projector.”

“I need you to use it to send me there. I was going to set it up remotely, but I didn’t factor in the time this conversation would take.”

“Lena, I am not sending you to the phantom zone. If only two people can get through the next portal, then I’ll go.”

“No.” Lena stepped forward and put the projector into Alex’s hand, closing her fingers gently around the handles. “You have people here who love you. Who need you. Kelly and Nia and J’onn. Your mother. Your future child.”

Alex swallowed hard. “You have people who love you, too.”

Lena chuckled, and a tear finally escaped down her cheek. “I’m sure my shareholders won’t be too heartbroken that a different name is on their dividend check,” she said. “My deranged mother and my homicidal brother won’t miss me, nor I them. Kara—“ her voice broke, and she took a shuddering breath before regaining her composure. “Kara is the only one, Alex. And she’s gone, and if I die trying to get her back it won’t matter because this farce, this disfigured version of the world without her the last three weeks, it is not a life I am interested in living.”

Alex’s grip tightened on the handles under Lena’s hands, but she still resisted. “You said there are 29 hours left before the portal opens? Why do you have to leave right now?”
An alarm on the watch sounded loudly, and Lena stepped back, raising the projector toward her own chest as she did.

“If I open a portal in the next 30 seconds, then my trajectory should take me to within 500 yards of the isotopic signature’s position.”

“If you die, Kara will never forgive me for this.” Alex’s voice was shaking, but her hands were steady. “So Lena, you’re going to go there, and you’re going to save Kara. Not an isotopic signature, but Kara.”

Lena offered her a watery smile and clicked the watch one more time. A black helmet materialized around her head.

“For Kara,” she whispered.

“Just come home.” Alex flipped the safety off on Lena’s nod. “Both of you.”

And she pulled the trigger.

2. Part II

Summary for the Chapter:

Lena reaches the phantom zone.

Notes for the Chapter:

Apparently "within a week" translates to "within 24 hours" these days.

Nothing, not even the nightmare inducing details Alex shared with her about Kara’s first time in the phantom zone, could have prepared Lena for the desert wind and unearthly heat.

Even with her helmet, the dryness of the air sapped her mouth of moisture within two breaths. A hot gust of wind whipped a long forgotten piece of shrapnel inches from her face, and she crouched instinctively, suddenly very much aware that this particular mission could be over within seconds if she didn’t find shelter and get her bearings immediately. The burning sand threatened to melt through the fabric of her suit as she sprinted, half crouched, toward an outcropping of red sandstone.

Just as she leaned back against the rock, a howling unlike anything she’d ever heard erupted all around her. The sound was so ghoulish, so inhuman and all encompassing, that it felt like it was emitting from her own bones. Lena shrieked, covering the helmet over her ears pointlessly with her hands, and sank to the ground. This was too much for her. The heat, the deadly winds, and the calls of what she could only assume were the phantoms pushed her to the brink of madness in less than three minutes. She didn’t dare to think about what they could have done to Kara in over three weeks.

But—Kara. Kara was why was there. Kara was closer to her than she’d been in nearly a month, less than 500 yards away if her calculations were correct, and her calculations were always correct.

So Lena pushed herself to her feet. The sandstorm escalated to a fever pitch, and if she hadn’t kept her hand on the wall, she wouldn’t have known whether she was walking in circles or straight off the edge of a cliff. The sky was red, the clouds were black, and the relentless orange of the storm made navigation impossible.

Suddenly, the burning stone beneath her fingers disappeared. Frantic, Lena slapped backward and found it again. Maybe the cliff ended there. Tentatively, she turned toward to face the rock and reached out as far as she could into the abyss. Her fingers brushed the grainy continuation of the outcropping. So the void was a cave— or at the very least a fissure. Moving slowly, she took a step into the darkness. Immediately, the howls of the phantoms and the sands diminished.

“A final resting place for those who here gave their lives…”

Lena froze, her heart stopping just long enough to jump into overdrive when it started again. The voice was mumbled, more of a chant or a prayer than anything. But still—unmistakable.

“Kara?”

The mumbling stopped. And then, more loudly, the voice spoke again. “If it were really you, you’d know that the helmet isn’t necessary for human survival here.”

Lena clicked a button on her watch and light flooded from her suit.

Kara squinted against the sudden brightness. She was there, leaning heavily against the jagged back wall of what revealed itself to be a crevice shaped cave in the rock. Her hair dangled limply across her shoulders, and the left side of her face was scraped and bruised. Most concerning was the ooze of blood dripping down her side, dangerously close to her liver and her lungs and— Lena could hardly stand to think the word— heart. She swallowed hard, overcome with relief and desperation.

“You would be wearing one too,” she said thickly, “if you’d been out in that storm.”

But she pushed the button anyway, and the helmet melted away.

Before she could react, Kara shoved herself forward, covering the few feet between them so quickly all Lena could register was the iron odor of blood and the lingering scent of something so purely Kara that Lena always assumed it would be what sunshine would smell like if it could. She searched Lena’s eyes, blue bouncing back and forth between green, and then, all at once, Kara was kissing her.

Her lips were dry, cracked and peeling, but even then in her weary, broken state, everything about Kara was good. She was the kind of good Lena could never be but would always need, and she breathed in her in, closing her eyes. Lena leaned into the kiss, pulling her closer, carefully sliding her fingers around the jagged tears in Kara’s suit, too aware of the vulnerable skin underneath. Kara choked on a sob and pulled back, wrapping her arms around Lena with more strength than she ever had before. Lena hugged her back, trying to be as gentle as possible with the cracked bones she cradled.

“I know it’s not really you,” Kara whispered, her voice raspy and hoarse. “I know you’re going to try and kill me or die in front of me again, but please, just—please let me stay this way for a minute. Please.”

Lena opened her eyes, alarmed.

“Kara, I’m here,” she promised, pulling far enough away to look into Kara’s face. “It’s me.”

The tears in Kara’s eyes spilled out onto her cheeks, and she shook her head the tiniest bit. “Don’t, please,” she begged, taking a step back and clutching at her side. “I can’t do this again. Please.”

Lena, always an expert at putting her emotions into their designated boxes, found she did not have a box big enough to hold the image of a broken and pleading Kara Danvers in her bloody, tattered Supergirl suit. Her heart broke even as her resolve solidified, and she took a step forward, grasping the shoulders of the most important person she could ever hope to know. The only person she was foolish enough to believe she could bear to love, even when she knew her own shoulders could never hold the one holding the world.

“Kara, listen to me. Alex and I found a way to track you using your radioactive signature, and I am here to take you to a portal going back to Earth. I am real. I’m real, Kara, please, you’re hurt, and we have to get you home. I promise I will get you home.”

Lena couldn’t stop the tears from streaming down her face, so she didn’t even try. Kara’s eyes searched her face, her bottom lip trembling with emotion. She didn’t respond, so Lena kept talking. “Please, Kara. I’m so sorry. I’m so sorry we couldn’t get to you sooner. You’ve helped me so many times. Please let me help you now.”

All at once Kara’s expression crumpled, and she sagged forward into her. Lena held on as tightly as she dared, and for the first time since they’d met, she felt Kara allow Lena to hold the full weight of her. And so, for the first time in her life, as Kara’s blood and tears soaked through the apparently less than impervious exterior of Lena’s suit, she wondered if since the best person she had ever known trusted her enough to use her as an anchor, then maybe she could avoid turning out to be the villain after all.

“It’s really you?” Kara asked, her head still buried in Lena’s shoulder.

“It’s really me,” Lena answered immediately, “and I can’t tell you how relieved I am that I found you.”

Kara made a sound somewhere between a sob and a laugh. “So then I kissed you. The real you.”

Lena felt a smile twitch at the corners of her mouth. “Yes, you did.”

“And you…” Kara lifted her head, biting at her bottom lip, and even with the scrapes and bruises, she suddenly looked exactly like herself. “You kissed me back?”

“Yes.” Lena suddenly found herself dropping her eyes away, a ridiculous flush creeping up her neck. “I did.”

Kara wiped a tear from Lena’s cheek, and when she looked up at her, she couldn’t quite believe the expression she found on Kara’s face. It almost looked like awe—but that couldn’t be. It was probably just dehydration. Still, she grasped both of Kara’s hands in her own.

“Let’s go home,” Lena said, squeezing Kara’s fingers gently. “We can talk about everything, anything and everything, later. Once you’re safe.”

And finally, finally Kara smiled—the full radiance of her grin healing something inside Lena that was broken so long ago she had forgotten it ever existed. Lena shook her head, cupping Kara’s face in her hands and wiping away the remaining tears with her thumbs.

“We’re really going home?” Kara repeated, leaning into Lena’s touch. “We’re going home?”

“Yes,” Lena answered, returning Kara’s smile with one of her own, unable to stop it from spreading unbidden all the way across her face. “Home.”

But of course, it wasn’t as simple as that. Because, as Lena calculated before she left, they had to travel 34 miles in 28.5 hours through one of the galaxy’s least hospitable landscapes. Lena helped Kara to her feet, and she nodded toward the blood on her side.

“What happened?”

“Some of the things here aren’t a big fan of Kryptonians,” Kara answered, grimacing as she ran her fingers across the edges of the wound. “It’s not as bad as it looks.”

Lena narrowed her eyes. “I seriously doubt that.”

Kara shrugged and then winced. “I’ve had worse. I’ll be fine.”

“Are you ready to go back out there? It’s going to be a long walk.”

“I’ve never been more ready to get off of a planet. Or a zone. Whatever this place is—let’s just hope this is the last time either of us have to see it.”
“Right.” Lena re-engaged her helmet and nodded. “Here we go.”

Much to Lena’s surprise, the sky was dark and calm when she slid out of the crevice. No phantoms howled, no sand blew. Instead there was nothing but silence and stars. She let the helmet sink back into the suit.

Kara glanced up. “Time doesn’t work the same way here it does on Earth. The sun comes and goes.”

“I have to say, this is an improvement,” Lena said, checking the compass heading she had installed on her watch and moving quickly toward where she knew the portal would appear. “Even if it is creepy.”

Kara glanced around. She was managing to keep pace with Lena, but her hand stayed firmly pressed against her side, and she moved with a limp.

“We can slow down.”

“No, we can’t,” Kara answered with a small smile. “Not if we want to make it there before the portal does. I can do it.”

And, although Lena shouldn’t have been surprised by Kara’s toughness after all these years, she knew her injuries in the phantom zone hurt more than they would have on Earth. But still, she trudged on beside her through the sand, struggling up one hill after the next, accepting Lena’s hand each time she reached back to pull her forward over the crest of another dune.

They walked through the starlight until the sun rose again, scorching them mercilessly for mile after mile, even as the sand turned to lava rock and sagebrush. They talked at first, Lena catching Kara up on Alex and Kelly, Brainy and Nia, J’onn and M’gann.

“We’ve always kind of been the fourth couple, haven’t we?” Kara asked, slightly out of breath from the quick pace and the energy draining sand. “Like, it’s always like, ‘Alex and Kelly bring the drinks and Lena and Kara will bring the popcorn,’ you know, that kind of thing. Have you noticed that?”

“I tried not to read too much into it,” Lena admitted. “It seemed... coincidental? Convenient, I suppose. And then after what happened between us, I stopped—I compartmentalized that part of my life.”

“But you came back to us.”

Lena paused at the crest of a hill, offering her hand back to help. She felt Kara’s fingers slide between her own. “I came back to you,” Lena said quietly, tucking a strand of blonde hair away from Kara’s face. “I appreciate the others, of course,” she continued, walking again. “But I’ll spend the rest of my life, however long it ends up being, coming back to you.”

“What does that mean, however long it ends up being,” Kara asked, straining to keep up with Lena’s insistent pace. “It’s going to be a long, long time.”

Lena smiled. “Well I should hope so,” she said. “Someone really needs to take Brainy down a notch or two on game night.”

Kara bumped her shoulder into Lena’s, smiling through the grimace of pain the movement caused. “You know you don’t have anything to prove to me, Lena,” she said. “You got stuck with me as your game night partner a long time ago.”

As the miles passed, though, Kara’s breathing got heavier and their conversation slowed. The wind picked up again in the sage brush, and Lena instinctively wrapped an around Kara’s shoulders, trying to somehow protect her from the stinging, omnipresent sand. Lena shuttered as the unbearable howling began, but she kept pulling them forward through the storm, willing herself to keep moving, keep pushing through the desolate hellscape.

Kara made it 16 hours before she collapsed. It happened so fast Lena only half managed to catch her, and they both tumbled down to the jagged lava rocks beneath them.

“Hey,” Lena whispered, her voice cracking from lack of use. “Kara, come on. We’re so close. We’re half a mile from where the portal opens, and we can rest and wait for it as soon as we get there.”

Kara smiled, tears welling in her eyes again. “It’s still hard for me to believe you’re really here.”

“Of course I’m here,” she answered, “you thought you could just go off like a martyr and save the world without me?”

“I’m sorry.” The tears slipped from Kara’s eyes. “I’m so sorry for going without saying goodbye. For not saying everything I wanted to say… I knew I couldn’t face Lex if I really thought it would be the last time I would see you again.”

“No,” Lena shook her head, “You didn’t say goodbye then because it wasn’t goodbye then, and it isn’t now. I promised Alex I would get you home. I will not lose you here. Not when we’re this close.”

Kara coughed, and despite the nearly unbearable heat, Lena felt a chill go up her spine at the sight of the blood that appeared at the corner of Kara’s lips.

“The phantoms kept appearing as you,” Kara said, her eyes slipping closed. “Usually they tried to kill me,” she chuckled weakly, “but sometimes they didn’t.” Her eyes fluttered open again, and Lena tried to memorize all the impossible colors of blue they held. “I watched you die over and over,” Kara murmured. “I could never say goodbye. I never want to lose you again.”

“You won’t,” Lena promised, pressing her forehead to Kara’s. “You can’t lose something that’s yours.”

The extra strength she built into her suit allowed Lena to lift Kara’s unconscious body into her arms. Her muscles strained under the extra weight, legs shaking with the effort, but slowly, she plodded forward. Only half a mile left, and she wasn’t stopping now.

With nine hours to go, Lena finally managed to settle them at the base of an unstable rock pile only yards from the portal. Kara drifted in and out of consciousness, and Lena, with nothing to do but wait, planned for as many contingencies as she could muster before exhaustion overtook her.

When she woke up, the night had once again replaced the day, and Lena’s watch was flashing. She jolted in panic, unable to remember where she was until she turned and saw Kara. In the starlight, her face looked even younger, the slight brush of freckles across her nose and cheeks just visible in the semi-darkness. She watched the ruined S on her chest rise and fall with her breath, and she felt her own slow to match the rhythm.

“Kara,” she whispered, “it’s almost time. We have five minutes.”

Kara shifted and blinked slowly awake.

“We made it to the portal?”

“We made it.”

Kara tried to sit up and then sank back down, breath shallow in her chest. “It hurts.”

Lena nodded. “I know. But not for much longer.”

The watch flashed faster.

“Three minutes,” Lena said. “Let me help you up.”

Kara nodded, her face too pale, and Lena moved behind her. She held the weight as Kara sat up, and then, slowly, lifted her to her feet.

A low moaning, some metastasized variety of the sandstorm howling, echoed across the rock field. Both women’s heads snapped up toward the sound. In the distance, but growing quickly closer, a dark figure took shape on the horizon.

“It looks like we might not be the only ones waiting for this portal,” Kara said.

The alarm on the watch sounded, and Lena silenced it immediately. “One minute.”

“How long is it open once it appears?”

“Until the allotted number of beings pass through,” Lena answered, her voice tense. “Which in this case probably means not long.”

More shadowed shapes appeared behind the first, and the moaning grew louder.

“We’ll just have to get there first,” Kara said.

Lena pressed the last unused button on her watch, and a black scimitar sprung from her sleeve. She caught it.

“Don’t worry,” she said, and smiled tightly at Kara. “That’s the plan.”

All at once, the portal appeared, its proximity knocking Kara and Lena off their feet onto the unforgiving rocks. Lena caught herself with her free hand, and she felt her wrist snap under the sudden pressure. Before she could react, the shadow leapt toward her, faster than she could see, certainly faster that she could swing any weapon. The monster raked its ebony claws, so much more solid than they appeared to be, across her ribs. She screamed and dropped the sword as what felt like teeth sunk into her shoulder. The darkness reared back to strike again, but without warning, the phantom exploded into fractals of shadow. Kara stood above them, sword still plunged into the air that once held the monster’s back.

“More are coming,” Kara shouted, hauling Lena to her feet with a grunt of pain. “We have to go, now!”

Lena nodded, gripping Kara’s arm and sliding the sword from her fingers. “Jump through!” she yelled above the howls. “I’m right behind you.”

Kara turned and caught her eye, smiling wearily. “I love you,” she said. And she jumped.

The gateway closed behind her, gone as suddenly as it arrived. Lena sunk to her knees and used her last seconds of consciousness to hope Kara would forgive her for lying about the capacity of the portal. It could, after all, only take one of them home.

3. Part III

It took Kara less than a second to realize the portal closed and left Lena in the phantom zone. One instant she was there, black hair whipping in the desert wind, blood dripping down her injured shoulder and off her pale fingers—and the next, Kara was staring at a forested mountain range. She was falling; the portal somehow dumped her back on Earth 700 feet up. But she was falling alone.

She knew, even before she looked, because she couldn’t hear the one sound she depended on. For a human, that might not mean much—just because you can’t hear something doesn’t mean it isn’t making a sound. But for Kara, it meant only one thing. The sound was gone. The silence consumed her. Even when they were fighting, spitting poisonous words at each other, pushing the edges of forgivable acts, Kara listened to Lena’s heartbeat. It thrummed quietly, often at the very back of her thoughts, like a pendulum of hope. As long as Lena’s heart was beating, there was a chance.

“Anything and everything,” Lena’s words echoed in Kara’s memory. “Once you’re safe.”

But Lena had lied.

Kara crashed through the top branches of decades old conifers, startling several birds and exploding a shower of snow into the bright blue sky. Using her last remaining energy and struggling against the clawing ache of injury and grief that grinded against her every breath, she rocketed out of the trees toward National City. A sonic boom resonated in her wake, and the birds, affronted at the breach of their peace, settled uneasily back into the branches.

When Alex found her, Kara was dropping through the sunset.

They’d picked up her signal suddenly, somewhere over the Canadian Rockies, and then faster than they could track it, she was there. J’onn managed to catch her unconscious body before she hit the ground, and Alex was so overwhelmed she didn’t know whether to laugh or cry. Instead, she just held on to Kara’s hand as tightly as she could while they wheeled the gurney into the lab. Still, as relieved as she felt to see her sister—alive, she reminded herself again and again as the machines monitoring vitals beeped reassuringly—a creeping sense of dread formed a pit in her stomach. Because although Alex would have chosen Kara over Lena every time, even though it’s the type of thing no one wants to admit out loud or really even to themselves, Kara would never, never have allowed anything to happen to Lena if she could help it. Kara would have died willingly, gratefully even, Alex though to herself, blinking back a sudden barrage of tears, if it meant sparing Lena from suffering.

Which led her to the inevitable conclusion that their present situation, with Kara recuperating under the sun lamps and Lena nowhere to be found, meant two things. First, Kara didn’t get a choice. And second, Lena was suffering.

Kara woke up less than two hours after she landed in National City. She blinked under the light of the scientifically forged suns above her and then closed her eyes again, tears streaming silently past her temples and into her hair.

“Kara.”
Alex’s voice was gentle, sympathetic. And Kara knew her sister realized what she had done. But still, she felt the need to confess it. To say the words out loud and let them hang in the air.

“I—Alex,” Kara choked, barely able to breath out the words. “I left her there. I left her there, in the phantom zone, all alone. And hurt,” Kara stopped, her voice cracking. “She’s hurt. She was bleeding so much, and all I could think about was getting her back here so you could fix her, and now…” she trailed off, unable to find the strength to tell one more heartbreaking truth.

That was she was bleeding too, too much. That she was alone in the worst place in the galaxy. That Lena had lied to her—lied to her again but in a way that felt like the first time—and now she would pay with her life and Kara would pay with her soul.

“This is not your fault, Kara,” Alex whispered, gripping onto Kara’s hand with both of her own. “Lena chose to save you. She understood what that might cost.”

Kara opened her eyes and sat up, suddenly furious. The lingering burn she felt every time she filled her lungs decreased with every passing minute, but she welcomed it while it lasted.

“I didn’t ask to be saved,” Kara said, her voice rising. “She thinks this is what I wanted? Some sick sacrificial act so that I could live and then what-- what is the point of that? What is the point of me having all these abilities, all this power, if I can’t even help the people I love the most?”

Alex, who had let go of Kara’s hand when she sat up, dropped her eyes. “Believe me, I understand how you’re feeling,” she said, her voice low. “The anger? I get it. I used to feel that way every time you threw yourself into one more impossible fight. You were always so willing to die in order to save, even if the people you were saving were strangers, and I hated it. I hated how powerless it made me feel.”

“But you are powerless, Alex.” Kara stood up as she said it, ready to go charge into the phantom zone right then if she had to, but her face immediately turned pale, and she lowered herself back to the bed with shaking hands. Alex rushed forward to help her, and as her sister’s strong hands wrapped around Kara’s arms, her eyes filled with tears.

“I’m sorry,” Kara whispered. “I didn’t mean it that way. You’re the most powerful person I know. I just hate that there’s nothing I can do. And I hate that she lied to me.”

“Hey,” Alex said, and waited until Kara met her eyes before gave a small smile. “It’s okay. But Kara—and, keep in mind, I’m saying this as someone who has not exactly been Lena’s biggest fan in the past— she lied to you for the exact same reason you lied to her all those years. She was trying to protect you.”

Kara bit her lip, shaking her head. “If she dies in there, I’ll never be able to forgive myself.”

Alex sat back in her chair, suddenly looking pleased with herself. “Well, good thing we’re not going to let her then, isn’t it?”

“What are you talking about?”

“This isn’t the same as what happened to you,” Alex explained. “We didn’t know where you were. Thanks to Lena’s technology, though, we know exactly where she is. And as of about an hour and a half ago, we have someone who can get us in and back out again.”

“Pretty sure that’s my cue.”

The Danvers sisters both looked toward the voice, Kara unsteadily rising to her feet. She looked around Alex and couldn’t stop the grin once she found the owner of the voice.

“Cisco,” Alex said, striding forward to shake his hand. “Thank you again so much for coming. And for, well, being willing to make an exception with your powers.”

Cisco stuck his hands in his pockets and rocked back on his heels, shrugging. “I have to admit, I was a little surprised when Barry told me I was going to be saving a Luthor,” he said, chuckling. “But then I heard which one, and it all kind of clicked.”

He peeked around Alex to grin at Kara, and she moved to hug him, still smiling. “It’s good to see you,” she said. “Thank you for doing this. Last time I talked to Barry he said you still hadn’t gotten your powers back after… after everything that happened.”

“Yeah.” He sighed. “I wasn’t sure I wanted them back. But at some point, I think I just had to accept the fact that power or not, I want to be the guy who can help people. And I can help more people if I can vibe, so, what can I say. Logic is my kryptonite.”

Kara laughed, and squeezed Alex’s shoulder gratefully. “I’m just glad you’re both here. The phantom zone seems to be my kryptonite lately.”

“Well technically, I think actual kryptonite is probably—“

“Yeah, yeah, you know what I mean.” Kara’s tone was playful, but the happiness of the unexpected reunion dwindled as the seriousness of the situation set in again for everyone. She looked at Cisco.

“You can vibe us in and out of there?”

He glanced at Alex, who nodded. “I can vibe you in, let you grab her, and then vibe both of you out. But you’ll have to be fast. Like, Barry fast.”

“Oh my God,” Alex said.
“What? That was a good one.”

Kara ignored them both. “How fast exactly?”

“Well, I’ve never attempted this exact type of maneuver before,” Cisco said, moving to the computer against the wall and firing up a program. “But I’m guessing you’ll have like ten seconds.”

Alex frowned. “That doesn’t sound very scientific.”

Cisco drummed his fingers across the keys. “Not peer reviewed, no. But me reviewed, so I mean, you could do worse.”

“Let’s go,” Kara said, moving next to Cisco and placing a hand across his shoulder. “I’m ready.”

“Kara, you can barely walk,” Alex objected. “You need at least another hour under the sun lamps.”

“Alex, she’s hurt.” Kara’s voice was soft but determined. “I’m not leaving here there for one more second longer than I have to.”

Cisco raised his eyebrows for the go ahead, and Alex wrapped Kara in a hug before stepping back.

“You better not leave me again,” Alex said. “I need you here.”

Kara nodded, smiling as confidently as she could muster. “I’ll see you in ten seconds.”

She had never understood how exactly Cisco’s vibing worked, but whatever he did took them to the right place. Even before she opened her eyes she felt the oppressive heat.

“You have ten seconds,” Cisco shouted above the howling and the wind. “Pick her up, but make sure you are both touching me before I take us back.”

Kara forced herself to open her eyes once more to the desolate landscape. The phantoms were gone. Hopefully, she thought, they had lost interest in the area after the portal disappeared. Kara steeled herself before she dared glance down at the place where she knew Lena would be. Out of all the terrifying things she’d faced, the thought that she would look and see Lena dead threatened to overwhelm her.

“Kara,” Cisco shouted. “Get her, now!”

Lena was on the ground. She was on her side, knees curled up toward her chest, sword limp in her unmoving fingers. Her hair, tangled by the wind, blew away from her face long enough for Kara to see that her eyes were closed. Her lip was cut, and her face looked pale—too pale and too still.

Kara dropped to her knees, not caring that the rocks bit into her suddenly vulnerable skin, and she shifted Lena into her arms. She lifted her carefully, trying not to think about the warm drench of blood soaking through to her own skin or the gurgle of breath fought for and lost. And then Lena’s green eyes fluttered open and she couldn’t think about anything at all. She lost herself in them faster than any portal could ever take them, and then, once again, they were closed. Ignoring the heat constricting her throat, she placed Lena’s hand on Cisco’s arm as gently as she could, and then, pulling the three of them into a sort of falling hug, she hurtled through the void.

When Alex found them, they were covered in blood.

“Kara!” She shouted, dropping to help lift the three of them. “What the hell happened?”

“It’s not mine,” Kara answered, not bothering to hide the panic in her voice. “It’s Lena’s—Lena’s blood. I don’t… I don’t know what to do.”

Alex nodded at Cisco, who helped them lift Lena onto the waiting gurney.

“That’s okay,” Alex said, grabbing Kara’s hand and looking her in the eye. “I know what to do. Okay? Stay here. Sit under the lamps. Let me do this.”

And then they wheeled Lena away, and Cisco followed them, and Kara stared at the empty space suddenly filled only with echoes and blood stains.

Seven minutes passed before Kara forced herself to sit down under the sun lamp. She was numb, and long memorized and mostly forgotten words rattled through her head. The phrases came in snippets and pieces until one caught and stuck, burrowing circles through Kara’s exhausted thoughts.

“…increased devotion to that cause for which they gave the last full measure of devotion.”

She couldn’t get her brain to stop repeating the final four words like a prayer, an elegy that belonged to others, long ago, and now unbearably seemed to belong to Lena. Kara murmured the refrain, hating the way the syllables tumbled from her lips. All she could was wait, but the ghosts haunting her now scared her far more than the phantoms. Flashes of red sky and Lena, falling under shadowed claws and teeth. Howls of ever nearing monsters, and Lena’s cry of pain, her shaking hands covered in her own blood, her final smile at Kara’s final words to her.

And still, the words echoed in her head. Hours passed until finally, finally, Alex returned.

Kara sat up immediately, unable to stop the strangled cry of relief when she saw a weary smile rise on Alex’s tired face.

“She’s going to be okay.”

Alex grunted in surprise at the strength of Kara’s answering hug, and by the time the sisters pulled away from each other, both of them were laughing.

“You saved her,” Kara said, crying through her smile. “Thank you.”

“Runs in the family,” she answered, shrugging playfully. “How are you feeling?”

Kara shook her head. “I don’t even know how to answer that. Better now though. Can I see her?”

“She’s already been asking for you,” Alex said.

With a final grateful hug, Kara sped out of the room and stopped abruptly at the open door of the recovery bay. Lena looked smaller somehow in her hospital bed. The beginning of a nasty bruise disappeared beneath her shoulder into the gown, and Kara was suddenly afraid to take another step. This version of Lena seemed too fragile, too vulnerable for Kara’s clumsy love.

But then she looked up and saw her, and whatever hesitation Kara felt drained away the second the unmistakable relief and fear flooded through Lena’s expression.

“I’m so sorry.” Lena’s voice was hoarse, and she winced as she tried to sit up. “I know I lied to you, and I know we’re still rebuilding what we lost. Please understand, I knew you wouldn’t ever let me stay there while you took the portal, and I couldn’t… I needed to be the one to protect you, for once. I hated lying to you, Kara, but I’m so glad you’re okay, and even if you hate me, I can’t be sorry for that.”

Kara sighed and shook her head, lowering herself slowly onto the side of Lena’s bed. “I don’t hate you,” she said, not daring to look away from Lena, in case she disappeared again. “Even last year, when I wanted to, I couldn’t do it. You were trying to protect me, and Alex reminded me it would be a little hypocritical of me not to get that.”

Lena’s eyes flooded with tears, and she tilted her back against the pillows. “Thank God,” she said, chuckling a little. “I really don’t think I could have taken that.”

“But Lena,” Kara said, her voice quiet and fierce, “never do anything like that again, please. I just, I can’t lose you. I can’t.”

“You won’t lose me,” Lena said, taking Kara’s hand from where it rested on her lap. “But I can’t promise I wouldn’t do the same thing again, if I needed to. If it meant saving you.”
Kara closed her eyes, feeling more tears slip through. “How can we keep doing this to each other, Lena?” she asked. “It’s tearing me apart.”

“I think we start by telling the truth,” Lena answered quietly, and Kara opened her eyes. “And then we just keep forgiving each other.”

“The truth, huh?”

Lena shrugged and grimaced. “I know we have a way to go before all the trust is whole again. But it’s a path I want to walk.”

Kara blew a strand of hair from her face and shifted down onto the too small bed so they were lying next to each other. “I kissed you in the phantom zone,” Kara said.

“You didn’t know it was really me,” Lena answered, hating the fact that she knew Kara could hear her suddenly increased heartrate. “I understand if you didn’t mean it that way.”

Kara was silent for a moment, and when she spoke again, her voice was soft. “Would you understand if I did?”

Lena slid her fingers through Kara’s, smiling and tired and so, so happy. “I kissed you in the phantom zone, too,” Lena answered. “And I knew exactly who you were.”

Kara propped herself up on her elbow, looking down at Lena with the same expression she had before. “So that’s a yes?”

Lena moved forward and caught Kara’s lips with her own, soft and quick, and never enough. But she pulled back anyway. “One more impossible hope you’ve gotten me to believe in, Supergirl,” she whispered, sinking back down to the pillow and pulling Kara next to her. “And you left before I got the chance to tell you before, but I love you, too, for the record.”

Kara wrapped her arm around Lena as tightly as she dared without hurting her, and she smiled into her hair, sighing. “This has been a really stressful day,” she whispered, sending them both into surprised, exhausted laughter. “But so worth it. We’re home.”
