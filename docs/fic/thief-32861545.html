thief

 

Beyond all hope, I prayed those timeless

days we spent might be made twice as long.

 

 

-Sappho.

 

 

The day she turned 25 years old, Kara, just like every other traveler before her, was allowed into the great Argo library and was encouraged to choose a planet of her choosing to explore and study across their time. 

 

The notion every kryptonian lives by- to explore, to learn, to study and to observe. To gather information of every society across time and space as safe keeping for future generations to come. 

 

Krypton, the library of the universe. 

 

It wasn’t always. But it all began because centuries ago a man, filled by curiosity and the desperate need to understand time, cracked the enigma of time travel. And so, the counsel sent the first group of five travelers into five different planets along with a time machine and the clear instructions to collect information and then return to Krypton. 

 

The beginning of the species, the creation of their languages, their evolution, the wars in between, what they eat, how they sleep, the people of relevance, their mistakes, the beginning and the end of civilizations. In every continent, in every country, in every decade and every year.

 

The travelers came back successful- with new information and new allies to talk about, new planets they’ve heard about that might be of interest and a new and profound passion. And so, five travelers became ten. Ten became forty. Forty became a hundred. And centuries later, once Krypton was already known around the galaxy as the star of knowledge, their entire culture and society revolved around the gathering and safekeeping of every planet in the universe.

 

Artifacts were updated. Technology advanced faster on Krypton than on any other planet. As did their bodies once the codex was created and kryptonians were made stronger, faster, almost ageless. Guilds were created, one to recopilate the knowledge, one to protect it, one to find new planets to go to and one that trained and prepared the travelers.

 

On their 25th birthday, each traveler walks into the library and is left alone until they pick a planet of their choosing- leaving the room with an empty crystal which has the coordinates of the planet, the same crystal that will be filled with new knowledge.

 

When Kara steps inside the library, she doesn’t pay attention to the descriptions of the planets on the table, nor the pictures hanging from the walls. “They want you to choose those,” her aunt Astra had told her, “even though they know what we’ll find there. The Astros Guild is becoming extremely boring.”

Instead, she walks past all of it and rounds corner after corner of the tall bookshelves. She spends hours wandering around and reading the spines of every book that catches her attention. But it wasn’t an extravagant book that she ended up choosing. It wasn’t the description of incredible purple oceans or mountains made of pink snow that she ended up choosing. 

 

She was walking around the section between dragons and proven myths when she tripped on her own feet. And when she crashed into one of the bookshelves, one lonely page fell from the top of it. It slowly flew down to the floor until it fell on top of her lap. The words on top of the yellowish paper were in a handwriting she had only seen in pictures, pictures preserved and saved so as to not forget the words of the first explorers. 

 

Traveler Seg-El had not written much, just a set of coordinates and a small hypothesis: “Planet Earth. Believed to be of slow technological progress although they may have an abundance of cultures.”

It was enough to catch her interest and prompt her to carve the coordinates into her empty crystal. And even if she got more than one odd look from the people around her, she was smiling as her pod was launched on a journey to Earth.

 

There were three clear rules that every traveler had to follow as soon as they arrived at the destination. 

 

One, find clothes to fit in. 

 

Kara found out pretty quickly that a pair of pants and a simple shirt was decent enough clothes on any age on earth. She was thankful, the garments comfortable and casual- save for the texture, it almost seemed like the uniforms of the Military Guild. 

 

Two, find a place to collect the information. 

 

She found a house in London, bought it after the first owners died in 1835. It was spacious and what humans considered “fancy”. The windows reminded her of the ones on Krypton and the room filled with bookshelves was big enough to hold all the journals she might have throughout time. It had three rooms and an extensive kitchen, as well as two bathrooms- all things she knows will never be used as long as the house belonged to her.

 

And three, which was more of a rule and not a step; never, under any circumstance, change the course of history. 

 

Although that was easy to remember. Time has already passed. Lives had already been set. Kara is just a mere observer, a watcher and a student of the ghosts around her. And it was very clear that they were all ghosts, too. Because of her powers- powers that came upon kryptonians after they connected themselves to time, after they became part of time itself. Powers that always showed her the death of the person she just met.

 

Sometimes it was just a glimpse of it. Sometimes it was a whisper in her ear. Sometimes just a vague idea that runs quickly through her mind. She’d learned to acknowledge what they mean.

 

Cancer, age 77.

 

Automotive accident, 53.

 

Homicide, 31.

 

Days spent living amongst ghosts that are alive and dead at the same time- their stories marked by time itself. And she, with no power to ever stop it. For she can only travel. Jump through time, randomly most of the time, and collect the information she came to this planet for. 

 

Her “jump-suit”, as they called it, was nothing more than what looked like a black vest. It had two mechanical circles, one which was used to set the time and place she was going to and one which started the jump. Nothing could go wrong- kryptonian technology has always been enos before everyone else.

 

But, somehow, with her luck, after a couple of hundreds of years, her jump-suit did not work, leaving her stranded in 1891. 

 

Although she’s sure it had more to do with the council not being excited about her decision than the fact that Kryptonian technology can actually fail . Whether it was actual sabotage or just a careless mistake on her jump-suit’s check-up, she’ll probably never know. 

 

She’s thankful that, no matter how advanced a society is, if they’re creating something related to time- clocks, watches, time machines- it’ll have wheels and gears. And there’s no better element for wheels than gold, which humans in this time have an obscene amount of. 

 

The clocksmith’s shop looks entirely too much like Ollivanders- the wand shop from the Harry Potter books she read in 1997 and the one she saw in the movie in 2001. Wooden tables and boxes piled up on the cupboards, clocks hanging from the wall and watches left in exposition on top of the tables.

 

The man marvels away at the broken piece of her jump-suit, whispering to himself “I have never seen pieces like this before,” in awe. All while whispers in Kara’s head mention that he’ll die, at the age of 56, a couple of years from now, when he trips on the bathtub and hits his head against the floor.

 

He puts the piece back on the table and slides it towards Kara, shaking his head, “I make clocks, watches.”

 

“Well, it belongs to something very similar to a watch,” she explains, “A timepiece. Intricate, precise and I need you to recreate it for me exactly.”

 

“This is gonna take weeks to tool. And a considerable amount of money,” the man sighs, expecting her to back up at the mention of money.

 

“The price is not a concern,” she hurries to say, “only its perfection. I have every faith in your ability-”

 

She doesn’t get to sugarcoat the man more, a trick she had learned long ago- praised and confident workers tend to work faster and better- because someone, a woman, walks into the shop. Kara hears the subtle crack of a gun’s safety lock being taken out before she hears the woman speak. 

 

“I want your bits of gold,” she says and when Kara softly turns around to see her face, she notices that the woman is wearing a white plague mask, effectively covering her face and taking away the attention from everything else. “The pieces that Morgan Edge brings you. The ones that he steals. I saw him drop some off this morning, so let's have it.”

 

“Are you sure you want to steal from Morgan Edge?” The man asks, even though his hands are already reaching from the pieces the woman is asking for. The burglar just steps forward, the gun pointed at the clockmaker’s forehead.

 

“I'll be having the pieces or your head.”

 

The man lays the objects on the table, a beautiful diamond necklace and a golden bracelet with an elegant engraving along with a couple of rings. As soon as he does that, the woman comes forward, taking everything in her gloved hands, “I kindly ask you not to take-” Kara tries, unsure if she’d take her piece too, but as soon as she puts everything else in her pocket, the woman grabs the broken piece of her suit and runs off the store, “-that one.”

 

Kara runs after her, quick on her feet but slow enough to not bring attention to herself, but the woman seems to be faster and knows the city way more than the blonde. Taking shortcuts and going through shops that leave Kara disorientated until she sees her again and the chase begins again. 

 

The woman led her to a hospital, or what people called a hospital in the 19th century. Nuns came and went, moving between the small beds as they tried to help anyone they could. Adults groaning and children crying as doctors- or at least people willing enough to help- tried to ease their minds. 

 

Kara is no stranger to death because neither is humanity. She’d seen plenty of it in the years she’d spent travelling earth on its many ages. There’s always deaths, plagues, diseases, pandemics that take lives left and right. But it’s always sadder to see it, when it happens out of a simple thing. Fevers killing millions when a simple pill would help them. People bleeding out when a surgery would fix it in a matter of hours. 

 

And there’s some beauty in it, she can’t really lie about it. The beauty of evolution and development that always happens right after humans went through a dark age. They’re resilient- they always come on top and if they don’t, they find a way to make it better. 

 

She guesses, hope is resilient.

 

“More water, ma’am?” a small whisper takes her away from her thought, “Ma’am? More water, please.”

 

A child, no older than twelve, begs from the small cot to Kara’s right. It's nothing more than a fever, Kara guesses by the way he looks, but let a fever go on longer than a couple of weeks at this time, and it’s as deadly as anything else. A flash of light shows up in her eyes as she presses a cold glass against his lips- the boy, two days from now, eyes closed and no movement on his chest. “The pain,” she says, resting a hand on his forehead, “it’ll go away soon.”

“You're misplaced,” a voice says behind her, the clicking sound of the gun’s safety ringing in her ears again. Kara chuckles.

 

“You have no idea.”

 

“You have no fear of a pistol at your back,” the woman says as Kara turns around slowly. The woman has dark black hair and bright green eyes. A sharp jaw and lips that stand out even as she purses them in worry.

 

“In my experience, they're inconvenient from any direction.” Kara expects the telltale of the woman’s death to show as soon as she pushes Kara into one of the columns of the hospital, but it doesn’t come. Odd. She shakes her head slightly as the brunette presses her gun against her chest, “You stole something from me, thief.”

 

“Better live without it."

 

“I'm far better with it,” she answers quickly.

 

“But is it worth your life?” she asks, a tint of an accent on the end of her words.

 

“I assure you, it has far greater value to me than to you,” she says and the woman takes a step back, her gun still pointing at Kara,“I have no interest in what you stole from the watchmaker, aside from curiosity. Save for one piece-” she takes the end of the woman’s gun, spins them around, taking her by surprise as she reverses their positions, “-Round. Golden in color. If you please.”

 

The woman sighs, her green eyes bore into Kara’s and for a fleeting moment, she wonders if the woman can read her mind, read the desperate need of why Kara needs the piece back, because, after a moment, she takes it out of her pocket and gives it to Kara.

 

“Thank you,” she smiles.

 

Her words are only answered with the roll of eyes and the soft push on her shoulder as the woman walks past her. Kara trails behind her, even as the brunette ignores her- too busy changing her black coat for the white yellowish apron all nurses are wearing. She watches curiously as Kara picks up the mask.

 

Kara nods, her eyes still focused on the disguise, an object that will become a symbol of death centuries from now. “Why the mask?”

 

“If you're promising death, best to look the part.”

 

Kara looks around, “Must be dreadful to wake up on the table and wonder whether it's a demon or a doctor tending to you."

 

“It's even worse if they don't wake up at all,” she says, one of her eyebrows moving up, “don’t you think so?"

 

“So you're a doctor,” she tilts her head, “why would a healer play the thief?”

 

“The poor are expected to just die here,on the streets, penniless,” she says, both anger and sadness covering her voice, “Medicines, well, they cost money.”

 

Kara nods in agreement. It has not been the first time she met someone who takes justice into their own hands and it certainly won’t be the last. Still, she feels like she has to keep talking- it’s odd. Too odd. That she still doesn’t know what is the fate that will come to the doctor.

 

“It's a real shame about the mask,” Kara says, catching Lena’s attention away from the remedies in front of her, “you have a face Lazarus himself would rise to see.”

 

“Of the two of us,” she says, resting her palms on the table as she leans closer to Kara, “I suspect that your mask is older, more finely crafted. And no one understands themselves as well as someone who has perfected their charm.”

 

Her words are sharp and not that far-fetched, Kara has been using the mask of a human for so long, sometimes it feels like she’s becoming one. She can travel anywhere, anywhen, but she can’t go back home. At least, not yet.

 

“Right,” she nods, “Good day, ma'am.”

She leaves the hospital, much more quickly than she should have. She doesn’t look back at the woman and she certainly misses it the way green eyes followed her figure until she disappeared from the front door.

 

The clockmaker gives her instructions to come back in a week and so, she spends the next seven days inside her house. Finally uses the kitchen that hadn’t been used since it’s former owners, almost burns it down too, and reorganizes the information she had collected so far. But after five days of compilation and uploading books and movies and stories into her crystal, the activity became boring and tedious.

 

She walks around town on the morning of day seven. And it’s only when she sees a kid cough and his mother rubbing his back, that Kara notices she hasn’t stopped thinking about the doctor. 

 

Always present in the back of her mind. There’s a question, a wonder, a curiosity that the blonde cannot disregard, no matter how much she tries.

 

A question, on what future awaits the woman. Why Kara, in all her experience and power, can’t see what it is.

 

A wonder, on why a woman that carries herself in prose and utilizes sophisticated words is living out of helping others in second hand clothes as she has to become a thief just to even be able to help.

 

A curiosity, on the woman’s sharp words and quick answers and the passion she saw behind her eyes. 

 

The kid on the other side of the square stops coughing. Runs away to where his friends are without a fuss. And Kara turns around and walks into the clocksmith’s shop. It’s a shame that when she picks up the replica of the broken piece of her jump-suit, she already knows it’s not good enough.


“It needs to be stronger. If the pieces seize together while the mechanism is initiating-” she applies pressure on the corner of the piece and it breaks in half, the man looks at her with a sad and disappointed look. She hands him the broken parts, “Keep trying.”

 

She turns around, a bitter feeling in her mouth at having to stay even longer in this time with no friends and no activities that catch her attention like the ones in other decades. 

 

“Are you staying in the city for a while?” the clockmaker asks, “London's not a terrible place. I'm sure a wealthy gentlewoman like you could find ways to pass her time.”

 

She wishes she would have gotten stuck in 1946, that way, she’d have Alex and the Danvers- the family she helped smuggle out of Germany during World War II. She visited them from time to time and Alex always loved to joke she never stayed with them for long and she’d forget her face and Eliza told her she’d teach her how to cook if she stayed for at least a week.

 

If she’d gotten stuck in 3068, that way, she’d be able to spend some time with Querl, the scientist that never seems to run out of new ideas for inventions and with whom Kara had had the most interesting conversations she’d ever had in her entire time on Earth.

 

Or had she gotten stuck in 2023, she’d be able to enjoy Nia’s company. The woman loved to claim that Kara is still not caught up enough on pop culture every time she saw her and that she’d be her “Yoda”- Kara understands it’s a Star Wars reference, a franchise humans love through history, but still hasn’t seen any of it. 

 

She thinks of the only thing, person, that has caught her attention. “I’m sure I will. Have a good day, sir.”

 

Her feet walk on their own and deal her to the hospital. But it’s not until a nurse is in front of her asking what she’s doing here that she realizes it. 

 

“I'm looking for the, uh… The woman?” stumbles out of her mouth, “The doctor?”

 

“Why are you here?” a voice asks, making Kara’s eyes move from the nurse to her. The doctor is being carried by two other women, who seem to be holding her up. The already light color of the woman’s face now looked akin to the white-greyish ash that comes out of a fire.

 

“I realized I neglected to introduce myself the other day. I’m-”

 

Her words die in her throat when the brunette’s eyes, which were filled with a fire of certainty and confidence the last time she saw her, go into the back of her head and she falls into the floor. The two women holding her up not strong enough to support her dead weight. 

 

She moves in autopilot as she kneels down in front of her and checks her pulse. She notices the red and swollen skin on the side of her forearm and instantly knows that she’ll need antibiotics- none that this hospital will have, at least not for another century.

 

“What are you doing?” One of the old nuns asks when she sees Kara’s arms move to the back of her legs and her back, holding the woman up in a bridal carry.

 

“She won’t survive here,” she answers desperately, “I can help her. What’s her name?”

 

“Lena,” distrust covers the woman’s face and voice as she answers, “Where will you take her?”

 

“A hospital outside of town,” she lies quickly, knowing it’ll ease the women’s worries, “she’ll be back in a couple of days, I promise.”

She runs back to her house as quickly as she can. Lena weights basically nothing in her arms but she still maintains a human enough pace to not attract the attention of anyone around them. Or, more like, not attract even more attention- someone carrying an unconscious woman in their arms is not a common occurrence. 

 

It’s not until hours later, when Lena is sleeping peacefully on one of the beds of her guestrooms that Kara realizes that she, foolishly and recklessly, changed the course of history. There’s no proof anywhere, or anywhen, that this woman was supposed to live past the infection that overcame her- no matter how strong the pull and curiosity that Kara had for her was. 

 

Lena remains in a state between sleep and groggy consciousness for two days, although, even tired and sick, she fights Kara every time she tries to help her drink down the pills that saved her life.

 

She walks into Lena’s room on day three to find her sitting up on the bed, confused eyes looking around- a flick of fear shows up on them when the brunette notices Kara.

 

“Stay calm. You are quite safe, Lena” she says, raising her hands in the air in the world wide sign to prove she means no harm.

 

“Where am I?” Her words are guarded and her brows furrow into a frown.

 

“My house,” Kara explains, “I told the nurses I'd take you to a proper hospital.”

 

Lena snorts, “This is no hospital.”

 

“A hospital, by my definition, is a place where you have the least chance of dying,” Kara lets a small smirk take over her lips as she walks closer to the bed table, hands the woman on the bed a glass of water. “Here.”

“How am I here?” 

 

The blonde understood her actual question perfectly, how am I alive?

 

Dumb luck that I save human medicine in all the houses I travel to , she almost answers but do not expose yourself has always been the one unspoken rule of her kind.

 

“There will come a time, one imagines, when even the simplest grocer will stock medicines that could have saved a millennia of corpses,” Kara blushes slightly at the exasperated and yet amused look on Lena’s face. “You- You had a staph infection. A simple infection. Nothing more.”

 

“How long have I been here?”

 

“Couple of days. I had to give you a sedative. You were lucid briefly. You put up a hell of a fight.”

 

Lena tenses instantly at her last words, which just confuses Kara. “And did you have your way with me?”

 

“No, that's not part of the cure,” Kara says, understanding where her fear came from, “I find you far more interesting conscious. May I?”

 

She points to the side of Lena’s bed. She looks at her for a couple of seconds before nodding lightly and chuckles when sits down. 

 

“If you're so learned in medicine,” Lena starts, sitting up straighter on the bed, “why don't you help me in the hospital? Be of some use to others?”

 

Ah, the question. The one everyone asks her when they see how much she knows in pretty much everything. Why don’t you help in the cure for cancer research? Why don’t you help me win the lottery if you already have the winning number? Why don’t you write an accurate book of that culture if you actually lived with them?

 

But time is an angry being, unlike the misconception that it’s delicate. It’s not delicate, it shapes itself, it turns, it goes back and forward. No kryptonian has ever dared to anger the beast and Kara certainly won’t be the first one. 

 

“Unfortunately, my travels keep me moving too much to be able to help.”

 

Lena’s eyes find hers as she nods. No one says anything for a couple of minutes and Kara takes in the moment to appreciate the fact that Lena has color on her cheeks again, her eyes have that little spark on them and, even though her hair is completely dishevelled, she looks extremely beautiful.

 

And although she has connected with humans in all her time here, there’s a fleeting feeling inside her chest she’d never felt before when Lena smiles shyly and says a quiet thank you , breaking the silence between them. 

 

“I- uh- I made you some soup,” Kara says, standing up, “I admit I’m not very good in the kitchen but it should be edible.”

 

And if the feeling shows up in her chest again when Lena laughs at her words- and when she holds onto the side of Kara’s arms as she walks down the stairs and when she makes a funny face when she tries Kara’s soup and when she decides to teach her how to make a proper soup- well, then, she completely ignores it.

 

 

The clocksmith fails again. The piece cracking under Kara’s fingers once more. So, she turns to the only matter that doesn’t bore her or tire her while she’s in this time- Lena.

 

She hears the woman’s screams, louder than the groanings of a man, before she sees her. 

 

“You’ll die if you won’t let me get it out! Stay still! Hold him down!”

 

Lena is standing beside one of the beds, her hands wrapped around the grip of a sword. A sword which blade disappears on the side of the man’s abdomen, he’s screaming and complaining as three other nurses try to hold him down.

 

Kara clears her throat, distracting the man for a single moment- one which Lena uses to finally pull the sword out of him in a harsh movement. He screams again and the nurses and Lena start to work on stopping his bleeding and seeing his wound efficiently and quickly, like a well oiled machine.

 

The blonde stands quietly to the side as the women work and when they finish up and Lena is cleaning off the blood off her hands on a bowl, she walks closer. Lena just raises an eyebrow as a hello and Kara sends a small smile her way.

 

“My journey's been delayed,” she says, “I thought you might like to dine with me tonight.”

 

Lena laughs at first, as if Kara had said the funniest joke she’d heard in the entire day. And when she looks at her again and finds the blonde expectantly waiting for an answer, she frowns and scoofs. “No.”

 

“I thought you'd say that,” Kara nods before whistling. At her command, five women walk through the door, Lena looks between them and Kara confused.

 

“And who are they?”

 

“These are five highly-qualified nurses I've hired to give you the night off,” Kara sends a cheeky smile her way, “Since I knew you wouldn't abandon your duties unless you were properly compensated for here.”

 

“God, I hate the way you speak,” Lena lets out a disbelieving laugh and rolls her eyes, “fine.”

 

She makes Kara wait for her, leads her to a very small and uncomfortable chair against the wall. She watches as Lena walks from one side of the room to the other, giving her helping hands to anyone who asks. Lena is focused and quick as she changes bandages and checks people’s temperature. 

 

After an hour of blue eyes following the woman around, Lena walks over to Kara, hands just washed of all blood and dirt. She’s wearing the clothes she had been using under her apron, and Kara had expected nothing else so she takes out her arm and smiles when Lena’s own tangles in it.

 

The restaurant isn’t too far away so they walk instead of taking Kara’s carriage, she’d never been a fan of small spaces- she prefers the spacious cars from 2463, really. But Lena doesn’t seem to mind either and Kara babbles away, both from her own inexplicable nervousness and the need to really talk to someone after years of simple conversations.

 

As soon as they walk inside the restaurant, Lena scoofs again. As they walk to their table, people look the brunette up and down, her second hand clothes standing out in a room full of people in their finest attire. 

 

“Did you bring me here to embarrass me?” She asks Kara, as she discreetly takes out her arm from around Kara’s. The blonde hurries past Lena and gently pulls out her chair before the doctor sits down.

 

“Not you. Them. You should have more pride in your simple doctor's apron than they have in their hats and petticoats. And who cares what they think? I think you look beautiful,” Kara says, a smile on her face and so much honesty in her eyes that it makes Lena turn away, a small blush covering her face. Too much, maybe, Kara realizes. “Have you eaten here before?”

 

“Of course not,” Lena scoffs at her attempt to change the subject, “Why?”

 

“You pretend ignorance. But it’s obvious from your demeanor that you’re not from the area that you serve. The past is merely a shadow that the present cannot hide,” Kara’s words are calm and collected, she looks Lena up and down, noting her posture, the way she already took out her napkin, things not everyone on this time would know. “It stalks us.”

She wanted to point out Lena’s clear past and, somehow, she inadvertently called out her own. Her own past, full of deaths and deceivings. A past which turns on itself on twists and bends, all in the name of her world. The things she’s done- for her world. Because the council didn’t tell them how hard getting all the information actually is, how much danger they’ll have to put themselves through. How many lives they’d have to see being taken away and how many they’d take themselves. 

 

And it’s very heavy, to carry the history of an entire world on their shoulders. It’s dark and ever present- like a shadow. And only after years of loneliness and darkness, Kara has finally understood why everytime someone returns from a world, their eyes are always dimmer.


She finds Lena’s eyes watching her curiously and Kara clears her throat, reaching forward for the menu. “Shall we- shall we order? The food here is very good.”

 

“Is that meant to impress me?” Lena asks abruptly.

 

“What, the menu?”

 

“No, no,” she chuckles and raises her eyebrows in mockery, “Your insight. How about the truth?”

 

“Okay, fine,” she sighs, “the food here is terrible.”

 

“About why I'm here,” the brunette corrects, “You know, since I was a little girl, I've known two types of people.”

She’s not sure what she’s hearing at first. This man’s death? Unlikely. She’d never met him. But she’s never one to ignore her own premonitions and the tellings of time so, she pays attention.

"I'm glad you knew, my beloved thief."
