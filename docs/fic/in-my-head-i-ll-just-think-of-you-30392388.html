Lena’s not sure how long she’s been sitting there, staring off into space, fingers wrapped loosely around a half-empty glass of scotch, drumming a little beat against the side of it. Absently, she muses that Kara would probably think of it as half-full; the thought brings the smallest of smiles to her face, and then she remembers.

And then there’s no more smile.

The bottle is there resting on the desk too, drained to its last dregs swimming around the bottom. There’s another one in the kitchen somewhere, she’s sure of it, but she can’t bring herself to get up and look.

Just out of the corner of her eye, she can see some old photo of them propped up innocently on the mantle, and its presence is enough to make her heart feel like it’s being wrenched out of her chest. When they’d moved over to Earth Prime, Lena’s apartment had been chock full of photos of the two of them, pinned to her fridge and hanging on her wall and even one in a frame on her bedside; it had felt like a sick joke, to have to go around dismantling pieces of a life she hadn’t even led, to have to stare into their faces in photos she hadn’t been there to take, only weeks after she’d had to do exactly the same thing in her old apartment.

She’d only assumed that Kara had done the same, that the last thing she would have wanted would have been to come home every day and face a veritable shrine of their friendship after Lena had spent the past several months finding new and creative ways to press the self-destruct button on their friendship.

But no, apparently not. Apparently Kara didn’t have any problem with leaving the photos up, or leaving Lena’s favourite blanket draped over her couch, or keeping a little drawing she apparently left on the fridge of a raccoon and a fox making friends.

It’s disconcerting, being in Kara’s apartment; in some ways, it’s like walking through a graveyard of their friendship, and being all too aware of the fact that this -- something that feels so pure and good -- is what she threw away and now may never really get back. In others, it’s like waking up one day only to find that all of your furniture is stuck to the ceiling instead, and everyone else is acting as if it was always that way. She can’t get over the fact that these memories aren’t really hers - in their real world, her blanket was navy blue, not purple, and Kara was the one that left refrigerator drawings, never Lena.

It’s like trying to stuff yourself into clothes that should fit but don’t, or like looking at yourself in the mirror one morning and realising something isn’t quite right.

The whole apartment is full of physical evidence pointing to a life that neither she or Kara ever led, but one that’s so achingly familiar it sometimes feels like she could somehow conjure up the memories if she stared at the pictures for long enough.

She hadn’t wanted to stay here, not really ever again but especially not without Kara there, but Alex had insisted, something about sticking together and strength in numbers, and Lena hadn’t had it in her to fight it. She regrets giving in so easily now, though, tired to the bone but completely aware she won’t be able to get even a second of sleep curled up on Kara’s couch.

All she wants -- no, needs -- is to go back to her apartment and sink into bed, and do absolutely everything in her power not to think about Kara for even a few minutes. She’s desperate at this point, has been for months - fuck, she tried to mind control a whole population just so that she would be able to feel anything other than the weight of their broken friendship crushing her at all times of the day, all she needs is to not be plagued with thoughts of her best friend for one fucking second.

It’s worse now, though, now that they’re starting to move on with it all, now that they’re in the process of forgiving each other. Now she can’t just convince herself that what she’s feeling is anger and betrayal and all of the other ugly emotions she’d pinned her histrionics on. Now, with Kara gone and no clue when she’ll be able to get her back, she’s forced to admit that it’s something much more akin to heartbreak: knowing you want something you can’t have, and hating yourself for wanting it in the first place.

At least before -- when she’d hidden behind her anger as a way to avoid confronting the fact that she’d only felt so betrayed because she couldn’t compute the idea of Kara keeping something so big from her when Lena had always willingly lay every card she had on the table without Kara ever having to ask -- she’d always known that Kara was there. That she was waiting, ready to welcome her back with open arms and a smile on her face. That assurance had taken a hit for a while there, after their confrontation in her apartment, but Lena had never had any real doubt that Kara would accept her apology should she ever choose to offer it, even if it would take a little more work.

But now…

Now Kara’s just gone. Stuck in a place so horrible Lena can’t even picture it, all on her own, with nobody there to help her or comfort her or assure her that they’re going to get her out. There’s nothing Lena can do, no way she can help her, and the thought of her all alone in there feels like a knife is being slowly twisted into Lena’s heart.

She’d told Alex earlier that she gets it now, how Kara had been protecting her by keeping her in the dark, and while there are so many ways she knows this is true, it’s glaringly obvious to her now that above all else, Kara had been doing her a kindness. She’d worried about Supergirl before, mostly in the abstract, but now, knowing the pain that comes from having to worry about the Girl of fucking Steel?

She doesn’t think she’d have been able to handle this for the past three years.

It’s like, most of the time she shouldn’t have to think about it, doesn’t have to think about it, because Supergirl is that one constant, untouchable thing, but all of a sudden she’s been faced with the reality that there is something that can take even her down, and.

That’s just incomprehensible to her. How is Lena meant to protect Kara against something, some one, that can go head to head with the most powerful person on Earth and come out victorious? How could she ever be enough for that?

She’s pulled from her thoughts by the sound of footsteps approaching, and then the familiar weight of someone sinking down onto the couch next to her. She blinks a few times and then turns to look at her new companion, slightly afraid it’s going to be Alex, and then breathes a sigh of relief when she finds it’s only Kelly, looking comfortable in a pair of sweats and holding two steaming mugs in her hand.

“Hey,” she says, holding one of the mugs out to Lena to take, and the look on her face is so kind that Lena has to actively stop herself from bursting into tears. “How are you doing?”

Lena takes a long look down at the glass in her hand, almost tempted for a second to put a brave face on and say she’s fine, before exhaling a shaky breath. “I’ve, uh… I’ve been better, that’s for sure.”

Kelly nudges the mug in Lena’s direction again, so she takes the hint and puts her glass on the coffee table, accepting it with a small smile.

“Yeah, I can imagine.” With one hand freed up, Kelly leans over and rests a comforting hand on Lena’s knee and starts rubbing soothing little circles. “I can’t even begin to imagine what it must feel like, to lose Kara again so soon after getting her back.”

Lena doesn’t say anything, can’t say anything, because, well. She’s right. It is unimaginable.

“Thank you,” Kelly pushes on. “For speaking to Alex. Convincing her to tell me about Kara.”

Lena gives a little half-shrug, as feeble as she feels. “It was no big deal.”

Kelly frowns. “Don’t do that. Don’t downplay your good deeds. Alex was upset, and she needed someone to be there for her, and you were that person. That’s not nothing, Lena.”

Lena can’t help but scoff. Alex never would have even been in that situation, never would have been upset to begin with, if it hadn’t been for Lena. If she hadn’t taken Kara’s lies so personally, hadn’t poured all that time into Non Nocere, then Lex would never have been able to abuse it, they never would have been here, Kara would be alright, and --

“Hey, Lena. I’m serious. You’re a good person.”

She rolls her eyes. “You have to think that, you’re a therapist.”

Kelly’s laughter is always so warm, like the first splash of sunshine after a rainy weekend, and Lena can’t help but find herself affected by it. It’s an involuntary reaction, like it’s completely out of her hands; she can’t help but be drawn towards her, constantly compelled to be basking in her warmth and kindness no matter what time of day.

“That’s not true, and you know it,” she says with a teasing smile. “In fact, if anything that should make me more qualified to say whether or not you’re a good person, shouldn’t it?”

Lena tips her head back against the sofa, eyes fluttering closed. “I’m a Luthor, Kelly. I’m just like them. It’s in my DNA. No point trying to deny the truth.”

Kelly snorts. “Well, that’s not dramatic at all.”

“I’m being serious.”

“I know you are, that’s the scary part.” Kelly sighs and takes a long drink of her coffee before placing it down gently beside Lena’s glass on the coffee table, moving her hand from where it’s resting on Lena’s knee to covering her fist. “Listen to me, Lena. You’re a good person. You’ve done some bad things, yes, but they don’t have to define you unless you let them.”

“But I —”

“But you what? You threw in the towel. You admitted defeat. You stopped fighting, and you apologised, and you’re already doing everything in your power to make things right. Does that sound like something a bad person would do? Something your brother, or your mother, would do?”

Kelly’s voice is so insistent, so sure of itself, that Lena almost finds herself convinced of what she’s saying for a second. Maybe Kelly’s right, maybe she is good, maybe she isn’t destined to turn out like Lex after all --

But then an image of Kara in the phantom zone flashes in her head, curled up on the floor completely alone, reliving the worst moments of her life with no one there to help her, with no hope of ever being rescued, all because of Lena and her family, and. 

She just. Can’t.

Lena takes a deep breath in, counts to five in her head, and then opens her eyes, gaze fixed to the ceiling. “Kelly, I appreciate what you’re trying to do here, really, but. I’d really rather be alone, if that’s okay.”

Her eyes are swimming with tears, and she wants to blink them away, wants to sit up and turn around and be grown enough to just look Kelly in the fucking face, but she can’t. Can’t bring herself to show her weakness like that, to see the look of pity on her friend’s face that’s just automatically there whenever someone interacts with her these days.

All Kelly wants to do is be there for her, and she can’t even let herself have that. Doesn’t deserve it.

(Somewhere deep inside, the lid to one of her boxes pops off and screams in a tiny little voice that she doesn’t want it from anyone but Kara, but she pushes that down as quickly as it rises.

If she doesn’t deserve Kelly’s comfort now, she’s sure more than ever that she’ll never deserve Kara’s ever again).

Kelly, lovely as ever, just squeezes at Lena’s hand, like she’d expected the brush off from the minute she sat down, like nothing Lena could do or say would ever phase her. She supposes Kelly had a point earlier, it kind of is her job to be prepared for just about anything she could come out with -- she probably wouldn’t be surprised at anything Lena could come out with.

“Look, Lena, I don’t want to push you. If you’re not ready to talk, we don’t have to. We can just sit here and drink our coffee, maybe watch a little TV, talk about whatever you want. But I am here for you. And so are Alex, and Nia, and Brainy and J’onn. We’re your family, okay? I know that things have been a little rough lately, and I’m not gonna sit here and tell you that you’re gonna just be able to go back to normal like nothing happened, but everyone is willing to work on getting back there again.” She squeezes again, harder this time, like driving home her next point is the most important thing in the world. “We are not giving up on you, okay? No matter how much you want to try and push us away.”

Lena’s breaths are coming in stops and starts now, bottom lip quivering with the sheer force of trying to hold back her tears, trying not to let herself break, and she feels so fucking pathetic, like Kelly has somehow managed to cut her chest open and bare all her deepest darkest fears to the world, without even thinking about it.

It feels dangerous, and so fucking scary, but on the other hand -- 

Safe, too?

Like she knows she’s protected. Like maybe if she starts letting her guard down again, just a little bit, like she’s been so afraid of ever since she found out Kara had lied, the world might not explode like she’d been convinced it would.

“I -- thank you, Kelly. It… it means a lot. Truly.” She can literally feel Kelly’s grin burning into the side of her head, and it gives Lena the smallest of smiles too. “Maybe we could, um… we could talk about some of it tomorrow? But for tonight, if we could just watch a movie and just. Pretend none of this is happening, pretend everything -- every one , is alright, that would be, um. Really great.”

She finally looks to the side, just in time to catch Kelly’s softest look being thrown her way, and something in her heart swells with pride, much akin to the Grinch at Christmas, just to be able to call these people her friends.

Her family.

Here she is, literally having tried to blow up everyone and everything they care about -- the one person they all care most about, including herself -- and they’re all still there, just waiting for her to come back.

Come home.

And it’s not like she’s stupid, she knows it’s not going to be that easy to regain their trust. She’d betrayed them, all of them, for months; burnt every bridge they had to the ground and didn’t once consider the consequences of what she was doing, too blinded by her all-consuming rage to ever consider what would come of their relationships should the dust ever settle, convinced she’d never let her guard down enough to let that happen.

But now it all feels so much more manageable than it did before, even after her hug with Alex and her two-person pity party with Brainy, and her… everything, with Kara.

Now, it feels like less of a distant hope, a dream she’ll never be able to fulfil, and more like something that is actually within her reach: getting her family back.

“Of course we can, sweetie. As long as you’re okay with watching a trashy teen movie from the early 2000s, we can do anything you want.”

“Oh, absolutely. A woman after my own heart.” Lena gets up and starts rifling through Kara’s DVD cabinet on autopilot, eyes immediately falling to her frankly impressive collection of romcoms and chick flicks that apparently made the switch to Earth Prime. She quirks a smile as a thought comes into her head, and she says, “You know, maybe I dated the wrong Olsen.”

Kelly barks out a laugh, like hearing Lena make any sort of joke was the last thing she’d expected to come out of this conversation. “You know, I think both Alex and Kara would probably burst into flames if they heard you say that, so I’d maybe keep that one between us if you’re looking to mend bridges.”

Lena just grins as she starts mentally weighing up Bring It On vs Bend It Like Beckham.

“Actually, that reminds me, one last thing before we put the topic to bed for the night.”

“What’s that?” she asks, vaguely distracted.

“Yours and Kara’s whole fight makes a lot more sense now that I know about the whole Supergirl thing.”

Lena lets out a small laugh just as she slides Bring It On back onto the shelf and pops the case of Bend It Like Beckham. “Oh God, yeah, I can imagine that must have been confusing. What did they tell you we were fighting about?”

“Well, Kara didn’t really like to talk about it, and Alex always just skated around it when I asked. Honestly, I kind of just assumed that the two of you were exes or something.”

Lena very visibly freezes in her spot as soon as Kelly says it, looking somewhat like a very embarrassed, very red still life. Her mind kind of just. Shuts down, like an old computer going all blue screen of death, because, um.

Fuck?

“Oh,” she says eventually, voice far too high to be natural, and forces out an even worse giggle. “God, no, that would be-- Um. Wow. Imagine! Me and Kara, God, I. Exes. God.”

Mercifully, Kelly doesn’t comment on her newly found bumbling ineptitude, and instead takes pity and says, yeah, crazy, before leaning forward on the sofa and peering over Lena’s shoulder. “Ah, good choice,” she says, nodding at the disc Lena’s white-knuckling. “I’m gonna go make another coffee, do you want one?”

Lena blinks, and then blinks again, and then again a few more times, before she finally snaps out of it, shaking her head probably a little too aggressively. “Ah, no, I’m, um, I’m good. Haven’t really touched my first one yet.”

Kelly takes her mug anyway, disappearing into the kitchen with the promise of refilling it anyway, because, in her words, nobody deserves to have to drink cold coffee, no matter how much teenagers these days are trying to convince everyone it’s good.

By the time she comes back, Lena is tucked under her blanket on the couch, marginally more put-together and awaiting Kelly’s return with her finger poised over the play button. They watch in relative silence, save for Kelly’s occasional commentary or Lena’s quiet laughter, and it’s nothing like the experience of watching a movie with Kara, but. She thinks that’s okay. Thinks it’s what she needs right now. That if she thinks about having Kara here with her instead, who would be curled up with her head in Lena’s lap right now rather than at the opposite end of the sofa, she might burst into tears all over again.

When the movie’s over, Kelly stands up with a yawn, pulls Lena into a warm hug, and retreats back into the spare bedroom where Alex is waiting with a promise to force Alex into making breakfast in the morning. Lena stares at the sofa she’d just been sitting on for a long, long time, deliberating over what to do now -- so tired she can barely keep her eyes open, but unable to face the thought of another night sleeping there -- before eventually trudging silently to Kara’s bedroom with her blanket wrapped around her shoulders.

She sleeps alone in Kara’s bed, curled up in a ball on the left side because she knows the right was always Kara’s, wearing a hoodie and sweatpants taken from her drawer, and for the first time in weeks, sleeps the whole way through the night.
