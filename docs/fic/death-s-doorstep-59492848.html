    Agatha poured salt onto the grass, and scowled.

    Rituals required too many damn ingredients.

    Rosemary. Thyme. A small animal bone. Three tears of a virgin. And now—salt. Not just any salt, though. Kosher rock salt. Organic, if possible. The spellbook had been very specific.

    So here she was, one maddening trip to the grocery store later, sprinkling an overpriced container of mineral on the dewy autumn grass in her backyard. No—she caught herself— Agnes’s backyard.

    She refused to call it her own. Taking ownership over this sad plot of land meant acknowledging that the last five years of her life had actually happened. Recognizing the humiliation of living as a slave to Wanda Maximoff’s suburban purgatory.

    No. Agatha laughed. Not happening.

    She dragged a long branch through the dirt, digging the spell’s outline into the ground.

    One triangle. One rectangle. Three spheres. It was the Homestead Hex. A spell designed to take you to shelter—a hex to find a place to call home. Not just any place, though. The Homestead Hex found you a place that would welcome you with open arms.

    A home that was craving you just as much as you were craving it. That usually meant lonesome, isolated places. Think the hotel from The Shining. Or a cabin in the woods overrun with ivy and mold. Fixer-uppers. Agatha was aiming for something a little more flashy than that. Maybe a billionaire’s abandoned compound, stock full of food supplies for an apocalypse that never happened.

    Of course, she was never that lucky—so she would tolerate settling for a cabin if need be. The most important thing, above all, is just that she was left alone.

    She threw the stick aside, and shook the last bit of salt into the raw wound she’d carved in the earth. In response, the rune came alight. A blazing sea of purple in the otherwise placid yard.

    I did it.

    Agatha sighed, exhausted, and fell to her knees in the middle of the circle. The last slivers of her magic flooded through her body, starting from her hands and then running up her veins into her heart. Her eyes turned a vivid yellow. Mist began to bubble from the hex, enveloping her body, cloaking her like a warm embrace.

    Hope flitted through her. Finally, she would be free.

    All that was left were the words.

    “By hearth’s embrace and weathered wall, I seek the home that heeds my call, A lover lost, yet bound to me, A place that whispers, “You’ll Never Leave.”

    The mist unwound.

    Agatha breathed heavily as the spell dissipated, her vision bleary as her new surroundings revealed themselves. Or rather, a lack of surroundings. Everything was as dark as night, completely unseeable, and as cold as a grave. The wet autumn humidity of Westview was no more, replaced with the temperature of a cellar.

    And—more worryingly—it smelled like death.

    As in: bone, blood, decaying skin, mildew, moss, a touch of tupperware plastic.

    “Of course. Just my luck,” she muttered to herself.

    Best case, she’d landed in a graveyard that badly needed some upkeep. Medium case, this place belonged to a serial killer who had done a seriously good job of evading the police. Worst case… She refused to consider the worst case.

    Please be a graveyard.

    She stepped forward experimentally, expecting the worst. But the ground didn’t give out beneath her. No lava stung her toes. From the gentle crunch, she reasoned she was in some sort of forest. She took another step, then another, her hands extended in front of her like a football player so as to not run into anything large or pointy.

    Eventually, her hands hit something. Wood, by the touch of it. She frowned.

    So a corpse-infested cabin in the woods it is, then.

    Still better than Westview, she was about to say, before a door promptly swung open, and clocked her straight in the jaw.

    “What the hell? ” Agatha groaned.

    She stumbled backward, hands flying upward reflexively. Magic bubbled from her fingertips, but there wasn’t enough of it left to put on any real show—purple fizzled in her palms as she caught sight of the first two lights she’d seen since arriving.

    No—not lights. Eyes.

    Green eyes, as vivid as the greenest grass on Earth.

    They were the only thing swimming in the overwhelming darkness. Agatha’s stomach dropped. Those eyes. They couldn’t be. Wouldn’t be. “Ugh,” an agitated voice said. “Another one? For the last time, this is not hell. This is my vacation home—purgatory. Emphasis on the vacation. I’m not taking house calls right now. If there’s a soul surplus, you can contact the Grim Reaper. Now, get lost.”

    Agatha’s throat went completely dry.

    Emotion swelled in her chest, and she found her lungs unable to produce a single breath. She couldn’t move, couldn’t think .

    No. No. No.

    The unseeable figure groaned.

    “Why are you still standing there? Leave!”

    This wasn’t a dream.

    This was her very real, very angry ex-girlfriend.

    “Is this some kind of trick? You’ve sent some sort of body double down into the depths of hell just to play with my emotions? Is that it, Harkness?” The booming tone sent shivers down Agatha’s spine for the first time. Not because it was scary—but because it sounded so hurt.

    She gritted her teeth. Not that she cared. Rio had hurt her. Not the other way around.

    “Put. Me. Down. You. Idiot,” Agatha choked out. “Fuck—I’m not an illusion. I’m real.”

    Rio’s pupils, which had blown fully black, quieted slowly back into green. Her grip on Agatha’s throat weakened as the realization hit her. “Wait,” Rio said softly. “If that’s true…”

    She set Agatha gently back down on the dirt.

    “Agatha Marie Harkness. Are you finally dead?”

    Agatha groaned, throwing Rio’s hand away from her. The other woman let her this time.

    “Of course not,” Agatha said. “I’m just lost.”

    “Lost on your way to hell?”

    “Lost on my way to a homestead,” she grunted. “Not that it’s any of your business, but I used the Homestead Hex, and instead of sending me somewhere normal, it sent me here. An obvious mistake.     Agatha tried brushing by Rio into the door to her cabin, but Rio stopped her, grabbing her wrist. The look in her eyes wasn’t hurt anymore. Nor agitated.

    It was just… something else. Unreadable.

    “If that’s the case, I have some bad news for you then, darling.” Her voice was half-mocking, half-serious. Agatha bristled at darling . “Don’t call me that.”

    “Don’t tell me what to call you.”

    The two stared at each other for a long beat, a hundred years of sizzling tension darting between eyes. When Rio refused to look away, Agatha just scoffed.

    “Spit it out, then. What’s the news?”

    Rio’s tongue swiped across her blood-red upper lip.

    “You can’t just leave.”

    Agatha stiffened, her jaw clenching.

    “Why not? You gonna try and stop me?”

    “No,” Rio said plainly. She bit down on a lip, and her eyes raked up and down Agatha’s face. It made Agatha’s throat clench. “The spell’s lyrics. A place that whispers ‘You’ll Never Leave’? The Homestead Hex is a blessing and curse, Agatha. You must be getting truly sloppy if you didn’t remember that. The home has to be… done with you, for you to leave it. It has to be ready for you to go.”

    Rio’s throat bobbed ever-so subtly.

    “And you, also, have to be done with it.”

    The emotion in Rio’s breath pushed the air of Agatha’s lungs. They were no longer just talking about a house.

    But Agatha refused to acknowledge that fact. Especially when Rio was looking at her like that. Like she hung the goddamn sun and stars. Even after a hundred years.

    She felt a small, buried part of her soften.

    And then reharden just as quickly.

    Agatha scoffed. “I’m sure this corpse-cabin doesn’t need me that badly.” At that, Rio shrugged, and turned away toward her laundry basket like nothing had ever happened. The tension fizzled like a needle dropping to the ground.

    “Sure. Maybe you’re right,” Death said, no longer looking at Agatha while she picked up the clothes from the ground. “But in case you aren’t, I have a guest bedroom up in the attic. Let me know if you want me to wash you a new duvet.”

    Before Agatha could protest, the woman had vanished into the darkness.
