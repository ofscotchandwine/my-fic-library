    Who Wander in the Mire, Love and Joy Come to You

    Lena had not decorated her penthouse for the holiday season in years. Nowhere ever felt comfortable or permanent, her apartments had been somewhere to sleep, somewhere to hide from her family’s name, from the press. A liminal space for when she wasn’t buried in projects. Slowly over the last few years it had started to turn into somewhere to live. A place to retreat and learn to enjoy life. It was Kara who had brought Lena her first holiday decoration, a wreath for her door. Just days before that horrible fight Kara had with Reign. Lena had been somewhat stunned at the time, but, even then, she couldn’t resist Kara’s pull. Kara seemed to decorate her apartment for every holiday, but it always felt to Lena that she was going through the motions. Now, after Kara had gotten back from the Phantom Zone, Lena couldn’t help but wonder, if she had been doing just that, trying to fit in.

    “Do you truly celebrate?” The curiosity was clear in Lena’s voice, as Kara fully stepped into her apartment, a wreath in hand, it had quietly become a tradition between them.

    Kara wandered over to the kitchen island where a little container with multiple bowls was filled with different snack options. It was something that had appeared in Lena’s place after game nights had resumed post well… everything. Lena had stopped stocking snacks while Kara was trapped in the Phantom Zone, often forgetting to eat during that time, and then they had all been so busy. But with game nights back in full swing and Esme now in everyone’s lives Lena felt it was even more essential to have snacks easily accessible. Kara always took her time picking what she wanted.

    “Hmm, no,” Kara murmured around the chestnuts she had just tossed into her mouth. Lena had gently placed the elaborate wreath on her dining room table as she moved toward her spare room looking for her door hanger. Kara turned toward Lena, after finishing her chestnuts, “But we celebrated solstice, the maximum or minimum declination the sun reaches and the festivities that tended to coincide with them. Rao is our sun god, as you know, so I do my own observance at those times of year here on earth.”

    “You’ll have to elaborate for me some time, if you are comfortable.” Lena replied warmly as she headed back toward the dining table, picking up the wreath as she went.

    When she had opened the door, Kara floated into action.

    “Here, let me.” Kara offered from her position floating behind the door, her hand held out for the hanger and wreath. There was a glow about Kara that still could freeze Lena in place. Somehow, despite her life experiences, Kara had a genuine welcoming spirit that blazed from every part of her, but especially her eyes.

    Not breaking the eye contract, Lena grinned and handed the items to Kara. Before they knew it Lena’s first decoration of the season was on her door.

    “It looks wonderful.” Kara’s voice held a gentle reverence that pulled Lena further into her orbit.

    “Wanna help me pull out my other decorations?”  Lena whispered, not wanting to break the spell.

    Kara turned to look at Lena and grinned freely down at her before floating over toward her office. 

     

    A few days later the team got news of a creature causing serious damage in Glacier National Park. The damage was too significant for it to be bears or wolves.

    “Should we go check it out?” Alex was rubbing her chin in front of the monitors, various articles splashed across them about the unusual destruction and attacks on property.

    “I do not think the entire team is needed at this time.” Brainy piped in from his work station. He had been tinkering with improving containers and boxes for Dreamer’s home garden, his Christmas gift to her, and he was stressed about it.

    “How do y---” Alex started, but Kara cut her off, “I agree Brainy. I can handle this one.” Kara’s tone was firm. 

    Alex tried again, but this time Kara elbowed her a little in the ribs and she finally got the hint. Alex let out a small huff in reply.

    “I’ll head out in a little while. I want to take some equipment with me. I think I know what it might be.”  Kara elaborated as she walked backward toward the storage room.

    Alex went back to scanning for other odd happenings and potential disasters.

    Lena had been in another corner of the lab still working on both new upgrades to Kara’s suit and on an entirely new, different, suit with extra ability to absorb the sun’s rays. She had kept working on improving designs while Kara had been trapped in the Phantom Zone, even if only to distract herself. It had given her something to do on those sleepless nights and, looking back, she knew it had helped her cling to hope. She had hesitated to show them to Kara, but the worries were unnecessary – Kara was thrilled. Apparently, she had been wanting some changes for a while. Lena had preferred working on Kara’s suit rather than exploring more of her witch abilities. The idea of her abilities still left her feeling off kilter. Kara had been gently nudging her to try to explore them again. Lena wasn’t sure she was ready. 

    She looked up from her screen after the exchange and quietly wandered over to the storage room.

    “Where did it…” Kara was mumbling to herself while she packed a hiking bag with equipment and a few snacks. 

    Lena found herself leaning against the door frame, watching Kara work at the slightly faster pace she favored when she wasn’t hiding her Kryptonian abilities. It was nice seeing Kara so comfortable again. It had been intense after her return, no time to collect themselves at first. There was a regal grace to her that had slipped by Lena when she hadn’t known all of Kara.  

    “I know you’re there.”  Kara murmured; the smile clear in her voice while quickly tetris-ing everything perfectly into the bag.

    Lena scratched a bit behind her ear, the smile in Kara’s voice pinging deep into her heart. “I could go with you.” 

    Kara zipped up the bag and caught Lena’s eyes over her shoulder noting her fluttering heart and slightly flushing cheeks. “You have a gala to attend and a holiday party at work this week.”

    “So do you.” 

    “You love those events! You actually look forward to them.”  Kara insisted, turning around fully and instinctively moving into Lena’s space. Lena had shifted a step closer to her as well. The pull between them had been undeniable this last year and they always seemed on the edge of the precipice of deeper connection.

    Kara caught her eyes and looked so genuine that Lena practically melted under her caring gaze. 

    Lena reached out to touch the shelving as a reason to break the eye lock. Hoping her slightly shaking hand went unnoticed. 

    “Maybe I’m not ready for you to be so far away yet… Not without any backup.”

    “What about when I went to help with the tornados in the south a month ago? Or the large trainwreck in Switzerland? I went alone.”

    Kara had gently moved fully into Lena’s space with every sentence, as if they were cirrus clouds colliding in a spring breeze. Individual strands of Lena’s hair were falling from her braid near her forehead and Kara’s eyes focused on them. The perfume of Lena’s shampoo filled her lungs.  Kara fought the urge to move closer, of slipping her arms around Lena’s waist, and cinching their bodies together. 

    “This is different. Those were earth disasters, not...” Lena had leaned into Kara’s warmth when she got closer like a key wanting to interlock, but there was still a barrier between them, something gentle and solid.

    “Plus, you have parties this coming week, too, and you are hosting one at your place again. You love these holiday events as much as I do.” Lena uncharacteristically scraped her foot across the ground, her eyes following the movement.

    Kara didn’t move back out of Lena’s space, instead she closed her eyes for a moment while listening and then opened them to look at Lena’s still turned head, as her eyes cataloged the items on the storage shelf for the tenth time. A swift smile formed and vanished from Kara’s face, a history of knowledge in her eyes.  

    “Take some time to get your things and a coat, uwedhing krep w rraop.” Kara whispered into the breath of space between them before superspeeding to grab the hiking bag and leaving. Lena let out a long breath, the heat from Kara still lingering in the air around her.

    She took a few moments to shut down her work area, got her coat, a scarf, and knit hat. She also made sure she had a few other practical items like her wallet and her phone.

    When she went to the balcony about thirty minutes later, she couldn’t help but admire the full-length cape hanging from Kara’s suit. Lena had redesigned it with the old suit’s neck design, at Kara’s request, and made it full length on a whim, Kara loved the adjustment. It fluttered so regally around Kara in the breeze that was always swirling around buildings this tall. Kara was watching her city and Lena had to admit it looked good on her – it fit.

    “Ready?” Kara smiled as she turned her head at Lena’s approach.

    Lena just nodded purposely stepping into Kara’s space so she could be more easily picked up.

    “El Mayarah.” Kara whispered to Lena while she gently took off into the air.

Kara remained floating, as she swung over the various areas of ruination. Snow always blanketed this part of the world this time of year, but there were oddly exposed patches of brown grass and broken or bent tree trunks almost looking like blood in the landscape.

    “It’s almost like dulled claw marks.” Kara commented floating over a large area that almost looked like it had been torn and rolled over at the same time. Kara used her x-ray abilities searching for any anomalies, she took her time and did it thoroughly and only after pulling her powers back in did she notice Lena’s gentle, warm breath on the side of her neck. The cloud of her breath curling up into the sky in Kara’s peripheral vision. It hit Kara that she was holding Lena, that the shape of her body fit against her perfectly, that the perfume of her shampoo, lilacs, and underneath her natural scent bergamot and amber was stronger in this pristine environment, close to Kara’s perpetually sun cell heated body.

    “Did you find any clues?” Lena’s curiosity was clear in her quiet voice. Kara suppressed a jump in her movements gently turning to meet Lena’s eyes.

    Kara shook her head and then lifted them above the treeline. The vast, but unpopulated area opened up before them.  Mountains. Snow. The forest.  Kara loved wild areas like this one. 

    The destruction was more than Kara expected and she took a breath and focused her vision scanning for more wreckage as they had found no trace of the creature park rangers had reported.

    “There seems to be more damage further north.” Kara whispered while she followed a surprisingly large trail of damage: rocks smashed, trees snapped, the few buildings torn asunder…and then she heard whimpering, it was painful to listen to.

    “What?” Lena’s voice was whisper-soft next to Kara’s ear.

    “I think I found them, hang on.” Kara flew them north following the path of destruction until they neared Kintla Lake. When she reached the open space at the lake’s edge, she lowered Lena to her feet and took off the hiking bag she had slipped on under her cape. Kara let her powers loose and —there— Kara was gone in a whoosh so strong that Lena’s scarf fluttered where it was tightly woven around her neck.

    In the woods, past the furthest part of the lake to the east, Kara found them, a mother and a youngling. The mother was dead. The youngling was old enough to know what had happened and was desperate for its mother.

    Kara was floating just behind them when she heard a gun cocking, she twirled so fast that her cape wrapped around her like a blanket and she zoomed right into the hunter, meeting their eyes with her glowing ones. She gripped their gun and caught the bullet in her hand, then she popped all the remaining bullets out of the gun and twisted it into a pretzel for good measure.

    The hunter stood frozen much like the prey they hunted from the safe position behind a weapon of metal and iron.

    “Leave.” Kara murmured, her voice so steely that no breath cloud formed in the air when she spoke – her freeze breath was creeping up out of her throat and her breath matched the deep cold in the air.

    The hunter could not suppress a shudder before turning and running through the snow back toward wherever their camp was.

    The creature had curled up next to its mother and was still moaning.

    Kara began to float over to the creatures. The mother looked like a zuurt from her home world, but that seemed crazy. When Kara got closer to the animals, she settled back on the ground, not wanting to scare the youngling even more. She had figured the weird happenings were alien related, but she had not been expecting…

    “It’s… you’re not alone…” Kara began knowing it was terrible. This baby had lost its mother.

    Kara kept a low stream of words shakily flowing as she got closer to the creature. 

    The baby eventually turned to her, tense, but still looked into her eyes knowingly. 

    Kara sat in the snow and slowly reached her hand out to rest on the mother’s head. Something had clearly taken her apart and it wasn’t the hunter, as Kara had originally thought.

    It appeared something or someone had ripped into her. Tears fell from the corner of Kara’s eyes, while she gently ran her fingers over the mother’s head. 

    A few moments later Kara felt a weight in her lap and then heard a mournful cry fill the air that she couldn't help but match with her own lament for this child, and the child she had been, too soon separated from its mother.

    Eventually their cries petered out and Kara slowly rested her hand on its head. The creature did not leap away but did try to fit its too big body even more into her lap. Kara paused and then wrapped her arms around it.

    After some time passed, “Alright kid, I think we need to have some funeral rites.”

    Hoping the creature understood, Kara slowly got out from under it and stood up. 

    She floated over to a knoll about twenty feet away and started gently blasting her heat vision to soften the soil and then she started to dig.

    Eventually, the creature came over and started to help.

    When the hole was dug, Kara floated back over to the mother.  

    “I am so sorry.”  Kara gently whispered slowly crouching down to get a hold on the large animal and then she lifted. She felt breath on her side when she fully straightened up holding on to the mother. 

    This time she walked over to the grave and then reverently placed the body in the hole.

    Kara straightened up, arm across her glyph, and then initiated the funeral rites of her people, as the mother was definitely a zuurt from her zhgehv, looking at her offspring, Kara suspected it was a mixture of zurrt and an Earth animal. Four legged, shaped almost like a cross between a bear and dinosaur. Part way through the rites, the youngling cried gently and leaned forward to snuggle its mother one more time. 

    We will remember you in every dawn. And await the night we join you in the sky. Rao’s will be done.

    They slowly covered the mother and Kara rested her hand on top of the mound before slowly standing back up.

    “Well, do you want to come with me?” Kara looked down at the creature, expecting it to understand her though knowing it could not. Kara had started to walk back toward the lake, the snow crunching under her feet. The creature stayed on the mound for a few more moments before slowly starting to follow Kara.  

    Kara had started to gently fly toward the lake and floated above the water’s edge waiting for the creature, giving it time. She stared at the wild and gorgeous landscape tilting her face toward the winter sun stretching out her limbs, her cape swirling around her. She heard an elk herd in the distance, the patter of mountain goats, and the swift running of wolves legs. Kara could smell a snow storm rolling in from the East. 

    Eventually the creature bleat-woofed up at her, floating over the lake, Kara was out of its reach. 

    “Alright,” Kara smiled down at the creature dancing in circles under her, “but I am going to have to carry you. My friend is waiting on the other side of this lake.”

    Kara landed and the creature came right up to her wanting attention. A chuckle left Kara’s chest and then she gathered it into her arms as best she could. She floated and the creature bleated a bit but mostly just snuggled more tightly to Kara’s body. Kara took that as a sign to fly back to Lena. Not worth waiting for the creature to get scared.

     

    Kara flew slightly slower than usual, but she also felt the tug of wanting to get back to Lena. She zoomed in her vision and saw Lena had found a log to sit on with the large bag Kara had packed. Lena seemed to have found the blanket and thermos of tea Kara had slipped into the bag while she had waited for Lena to get ready before they left. Kara suppressed a smile at the sight of it - Lena sipping tea, her pony tail poking out from under her knit hat, relaxed as if she was sitting at home. She heard her humming and the steady beat of her heart. It was comforting after what had just occurred. Kara ignored how relieved she felt shaking her head at it being Lena’s presence.   

    Kara made sure to land a few feet away to give him space to get upright.  After lifting the creature up into her arms, Kara was sure he was a him.

    “Did anything bad happen,” Lena stood up, rushing anxiously to check on Kara. The hand not holding the thermos top ran up Kara’s arm and over to rest on her glyph. Kara had dirt on her suit and a few smudges on her cheeks from digging the mother’s grave. Lena’s fingers involuntarily dug into Kara’s glyph and Lena felt a surge of magic flow through her body, and suddenly all the dirt fell from the suit. A swirl of purple disappearing in the air. Lena pulled her hand back for a moment before gently placing it back on Kara’s glyph. Kara was so wound up, she didn’t notice the purple surge of magic. 

    “It was… his mother was killed, and I couldn’t leave him. Great Rao! You won’t believe his mother was a creature from Krypton and I have no idea how he and his mother got out here…perhaps it was…”  Kara lost steam and watched as the creature came closer to her side for comfort, she had instinctively folded closer to Lena seeking the same. Their breath mingling in the frosty air, Lena running her hand soothingly up and down Kara’s arm, resting her forehead against Kara’s. Lena caught Kara’s eyes, the open pain in their depths causing something to catch in Lena’s chest.  

    “Of course, you couldn’t.” Lena replied, gently running a thumb over one of Kara’s cheeks before placing her hand in Kara’s. Lena noticed Kara’s cheek was too clean for it to just be from her thumb brushing over it. Kara was now looking down at the creature, but her hand pressed firmly around Lena’s smaller one.

    “I don’t think I can fly both of you.”

    “Well, where could we go? I’m not sure we should take him right back to National City. Look at how thick his coat is.”

    Kara met her eyes again, “Hmmm, well, we could road trip up North.”  She scratched behind the creature’s ears. “We’ll have to give you a name as well. Can’t keep thinking of you as ‘the creature’ in my head.”

    “Why North?”  Lena was not opposed, just curious. She kept her body close to Kara, their hands still entwined. 

    “Well, the Northern Lights are breathtaking this time of year and I know some folks in Athabasca where I can leave my truck until I return. I’ll have to pick it up from Whiskey Gap and get it to 89 first. Think you can wait with…” 

    Lena was just staring at Kara, mouth agape, as the words came tumbling out of her mouth.

    “What?”  Kara grinned, her eyes sparkling with mischief, “I’ve helped a lot of folks and I’ve always been thrifty with money.”

    “I just was not expecting you to be so prepared for a road trip.” Lena waved her arm at the scenery around them and then at Kara’s cape.

    “Lena,” Kara’s face took on a serious expression, “One must always be prepared.” A gentle smile broke across the corner of her face. “Plus, I love this part of the world, it reminds me of the Jewel Mountains. Can you stay with Khir, yes, I think Khir is a good name.” Kara had been patting his side, her gaze trained on Lena.

    Lena just nodded and then Kara moved to slip the hiking bag back on and went off like a shot.  Khir cried for a little while before moving closer to Lena.

     

    It seemed like  moments later, though it had been closer to an hour, that Lena and Khir were in a large truck while Kara started to drive them. Kara had changed and her face and hands were clean. There were blankets in the truck cab and other supplies and Kara got Khir settled and then took some time draping and straightening a blanket over Lena’s legs. Lena could see the dark cloud behind her eyes, but she didn’t press, she just ran her hand over Kara’s arm and up to smooth out the crinkle between her eyebrows, before Kara gently stepped back and shut the passenger door. 

    Kara was driving now with her fingers playing in the wind as she had her window half open despite the heater being on full blast. Lena found she couldn’t look away from her; her freshly scrubbed cheeks, her sharp jawline, her fathomless, dancing blue eyes safely gazing at the road ahead. There wasn’t a hurry to the driving, the radio was quietly playing holiday tunes, and Lena felt warm, almost cozy, in a way Kara had only ever been able to make her feel. Lena shifted her eyes back and forth between Kara and the gorgeous scenery of this rugged part of the world. A gentle snow started to fall part way through the drive. Eventually, Kara started to quietly sing along with the holiday music. Lena slowly started to work the braid out of her hair. Somehow Lena had never felt more alive, yet, all was calm and peaceful.

She had always preferred it and it was better than going to Calgary with Khir in tow. She still worried this would not be up to Lena’s standards.

    “It’s so festive.” Lena gasped, the joy clear in her voice, as she took in the cabins ensconced in the tall trees and the creek running behind a row of them.

    “Why, I haven’t seen you in ages, where have you been Beth?” The woman at the desk’s joy was infectious as she grinned at Kara.

    “Oh, here and there you know.”  Kara smiled in return, “Need a room for the night, two beds if you please.”

    “Ah, I see you have company.”  Rosie smiled at Lena. “I’ll put you in number three and if you could help Nathan chop some firewood, I’ll bring you an extra cherry pie.” 

    Kara’s eyes lit up at the mention of pie, “Of course Rosie, once I get us settled in the cabin, I’ll find Nathan.”  Kara saluted Rosie, while holding the door open for Lena.

    Lena waved to Rosie as she exited. Once the door closed she couldn’t hide the smirk on her face, “Beth” was all she said. The weight of the fake name lying between them. 

    Kara brushed her hand along the pine needles on the trees above them nervously. 

    “Well, can’t go around being Kara everywhere, it wouldn’t make sense.”  Some snow started to sprinkle on them and Kara lowered her hand from the trees and started to ring her fingers together. “I should have been honest with you from the start.” 

    Lena scoffed quietly, “I just was thinking there has to be better cover names than Beth...”And just like that they were both laughing, as they climbed back into the truck. 

     

    Kara showed Lena into the cabin and went back out to help with chopping the firewood, leaving Lena to take in the large room complete with beds, bookcases, and a fireplace. Khir paced around the room, but kept moaning periodically, wanting Kara no doubt. Lena couldn’t blame him.

    Lena busied herself with taking some items out of the bag and cleaning the thermos in the bathroom sink. She could hear the faint sound of Kara’s laughter bouncing off the snow and then the distinct sound of wood being chopped.  Lena didn’t notice the smile that spread across her face. And much faster than it should have happened, Kara was back.

    Khir sprang to the door immediately, “Hey.” Kara smiled as she patted his head, several large bowls in her other hand. “I brought you some salad and greens.” Kara put one of the bowls down in front of the fireplace and suddenly their new companion was occupied.

    Kara sped back outside and came back in with a tray. It was laden with cookies, a mountain of marshmallows, and held two giant mugs of hot chocolate with whipped cream on top and cinnamon sticks for good measure, “Hot chocolate.”  Kara’s grin was infectious while she sped the tray over and placed it on the table in front of the fireplace. “I ordered us some dinner, got a little of everything, and Rosie is bringing two cherry pies and an apple pie.” Kara had one hand on the mantelpiece and Lena found she couldn’t look away. Kara was in jeans, a Star Wars t-shirt, and had a plaid button up on to complete her rugged ensemble. That was unusual, but it was not what had captured Lena’s attention. 

    Kara was, currently, eating one of the cinnamon sticks with such joy that she had started to float just a tiny bit. Kara was free, she was herself with no barriers, no thought to hiding in front of Lena.  Kara had always been so open and intimate with her even before Lena learned of her Kryptonian identity, but now, in this cozy cabin, it hit Lena that she was truly seeing all of Kara. In a way, she suspected no one else ever had. She had never felt anything from Kara, but a welcoming warmth, a ‘come reside’ in me warmth, but in this moment, it was a blazing fire that burned the darkness out of her soul. Lena had to take a few quiet breaths, conscious of Kara’s heightened senses, not wanting to break the delightful gift that was before her and within her. “Thank you.” Lena smiled from the one of the chairs in front of the fireplace, finally taking a slow sip of her drink. Kara just grinned so large it lit up her eyes and she grabbed the second cinnamon stick from her mug. A moment later she pulled her phone out and then Christmas carols filled the cabin.

    “Let me get this going.” Kara bent down, her plaid shirt slopping around her, as she got busy preparing a fire. She had to work around Khir who was still nose deep in his greens. Lena soaked Kara in, this relaxed Kara was intoxicating. Lena had to fight to hide her smile.

I think his mother may have been on Fort Rozz and made it to Earth.”

    “Really? That’s astonishing.”

    “We never did fully know what was on there.” Kara’s jaw clenched. “As to what to do with him, well, they tend to like colder climates. It might be best to have him live at the Fortress of Solitude.”

    “Kelex, is gonna love that.” Lena snickered gently behind her hand.

    A chuckle left Kara’s lips as she straightened up in the chair next to Lena’s.  

    “Did you find out what happened to his mother?”

    “Mmm,” Kara finished a sip of water. When she spoke her voice was cold, “It was illegal loggers. I think they tried to capture her and she got caught in one of their machines or they may have tortured her and she eventually got away.”  Kara’s hand had fisted up on the arm of her chair.
