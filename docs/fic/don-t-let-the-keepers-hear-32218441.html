Lena Luthor wasn’t too fond of people.

 

She preferred the cool, unfeeling efficiency of machines and lab equipment over the wishy washy attitudes of those around her. Call it emotional trauma, but it was a tool she wielded with surgical precision. It’s how she was, quite frankly, still alive. Well, that and her firearms training and security teams.

 

And perhaps a superhero she tolerated.

 

Lena Luthor was no stranger to the inhuman and extra-terrestrial citizens of Earth, and she honestly did her best to keep a diplomatic distance when applicable. It was more for those individual’s safety rather than her own. She personally was completely accepting of most everyone she crossed paths with. However, her family history horrendously tarnished any attempt at personal friendship before it could even get off the ground. So she stuck to polite exchanges and fair opportunities for work and trade. She saw no benefit to loudly flaunt the charities and community programs L-Corp donated to and supported in an attempt to take some of the heat off of the company. Those numbers always showed up in their quarterly reports anyways, and to even try to have a simple report on those donations resulted in an unreasonable and undeserving amount of ire. It was safer to be the silent donor.

 

All of that compounded on how she lived in National City.

 

She hadn’t come here to make friends, she came here to put something good back into the world. She came here to try to repair the damage her brother inflicted. She came here to show the world that a Luthor and a Super could coexist in the same city and not resort to attempted homicide.

 

If only Supergirl’s overly-earnest and sunny deposition hadn’t unfortunately rubbed her the wrong way after nearly falling out of the sky in an almost-helicopter-crash. Their first meeting hadn’t gone as what she would consider, smoothly . Most of her interactions within the first few months of moving to National City hadn’t gone well in general, from Supergirl to the people working at the newly rebranded L-Corp, even to Clark Kent’s cousin trying to play nice when Lena knew she was just looking for her big break into journaling. The woman had of course protested, saying that she was just trying to be kind and offer friendship. They all said that. She had of course kept a wary eye on her like she did with most journalists who tried to get too buddy-buddy with her. Probably another vein of the afore-mentioned emotional trauma.

 

No.

 

No new friendships. No girlfriends. No boyfriends. No flings or rendezvous.

 

Just Lena and her technology.

 

And… yes, okay. Her friends Jack and Samantha. Whom she tried to pencil in at least twice a month, but really she was lucky to even get one half hour visit around her duties to L-Corp. There was just too much to do and she was annoyingly limited to earth’s physics and her body's human shortcomings. She did what she could and probably more than was healthy, but she had her worth to prove and that was easily going to be a long and arduous task for the rest of her life. A task that was becoming increasingly more difficult as she watched another one of her lab employees be escorted from the lobby in cuffs. She huffed and stepped a little further inside the building, staying as far away from the cameras outside as she could. The October chill still wafted in and wrapped around her bare ankles.

 

“Miss Luthor? A moment of your time?”

 

The deep baritone of an FBI agent caught her attention as he sidled up to her. She knew he wasn’t actually FBI, the ear pieces were different and the texture of his windbreaker was far too techy and advanced. Lena crossed her arms in mock boredom and stared down her nose at him. If he wanted to play dress up, fine. But she wouldn’t play along. It was past time for games.

 

“Yes?”

 

The man was tall and broad chested, his head and face clean shaven. The bright lights in the lobby shined harshly on his dark skin highlighting the strong square line of his jaw. He tucked a notepad into his pocket and linked his hands behind his back in a military parade rest.

 

“Unfortunately Lars had none of the data chips on his person nor in his home. We’re afraid he’s either stashed them or made the sale.”

 

“Weren’t you supposed to stop said sale?” Lena bit out, clenching her teeth tight enough her dentist would flinch.

 

A muscle jumped in the agent’s jaw. “It was our priority, yes. We’re still keeping our eyes and ears on potential buyers. If we can locate them perhaps we can intercept those packages before they change hands again.”

 

“I would be happier if they hadn’t left the premises at all .” She ground out after eye-rolling her way through his answer.

 

The agent pressed his lips together in an attempt to keep from giving his own frustrated retort and exhaled through his nose. “I understand you’re upset, Miss Luthor-“

 

“I’ve been in this city for three years . I have done what I can to keep my own house in order but they still keep slipping in under the radar. Not once has your department successfully helped me stop these vermin. I’m more than a little upset and now I’m missing some very delicate technology. I am quite literally a moment away from taking matters into my own hands.”

 

“That would not be wise.” He chastised.

 

If Lena could have boiled over she would have. Hot heavy rage thundered in her veins and she very nearly saw red. She just barely caught herself in time from making a scene and struggled to get the roiling anger under control.

 

“Then I would suggest you don’t underestimate me.” She snarled. “I want my data chips back. I’ll do it myself if I have to.”

 

Lena spun on her heel and stalked away back towards the elevator bank before he could continue talking to her as if she were a child. She had better things to do-like chew out her HR team again for somehow letting another worm into her company. Her legal team would also need to be contacted. Perhaps a fourth body double hired. This was beginning to get out of hand and Lena would kill to have a quarterly review not plagued by some sort of mess. Perhaps it was time she did what Luthors do best. She jabbed the call button with her thumb and glanced back towards the lobby as the hum of voices picked up for a moment.

 

The familiar form of Supergirl strode into the lobby in a swirl of red fabric and skin tight blue. Lena wondered how she managed to make her blonde curls bounce so obnoxiously. Her social reserves were already scraping the bottom of the barrel at a wonderful 9:32 a.m. and she didn’t have any niceties to spare for the Girl of Steel if she were to survive the rest of the work day. The elevator mercifully dinged its arrival and she strode inside just as the Super turned to look for her. If Supergirl wanted to speak to her, she would have to call for an appointment just like everyone else. She had a business to run and a mess to clean up and those took precedent over half-baked excuses of incompetence. Much to her displeasure, the thump of heavy boots approaching from the lobby had her jabbing the close door button with a vicious force.

 

“Miss Luthor-”

The elevator door paused and slid back open, revealing the sleek and powerful form of Supergirl. Her expression was polite but her blue eyes were guarded.

 

“Miss Luthor, if I could have a moment.”

 

Lena huffed through her nose and pressed the button again, having no patience to spare. “Time is money, Supergirl. Please call my assistant to arrange an appointment.”

 

Supergirl reached out and bumped the door guard again, preventing the door from closing. “It won’t take but a moment.”

 

“I will call building security. Excuse me.” She jabbed the button again, letting the ice of her frustration seep into her tone.

Her polite expression dropped into a glower that was usually reserved just for Lena and the door slid shut behind her with a quiet ding . If Lena had been any other person she might have thought about being afraid while trapped in a small space with an upset over-powered alien. Apparently they hadn’t. The elevator car rolled into motion and Lena matched the Super’s stare second for second.

 

“Miss Luthor, I know we don’t see eye to eye-”

 

Lena broke the stare-down and rolled her eyes for the upteenth time that morning. She could hear Jack’s teasing voice in the back of her head saying roll your eyes hard enough and they’ll pop out .

 

“-but I think that this is a situation where we need to play nice .” Supergirl rushed through, her voice raising a little in volume.

A mean curl to the corner of her lips sent a muscle jumping in Supergirl’s jaw. “Don’t think I don’t know he’s not really working for the FBI. Is he your babysitter? Perhaps someone from the DEO? Your handler? Keeper?”

 

Supergirl slowly moved her hands to her hips, the material of her body suit creaking under her grip. Her expression was stony, but Lena had her pinned.

 

“He’s not my keeper.”

 

“ Ah . So they do have you trapped to keep an eye on you. Someone always watching you? Watching you eat? Watching you sleep? Typical government goons.”

 

Supergirl reached out in a blur and hit the stop button on the panel with her fist hard enough Lena heard an ominous crack . She couldn’t stop her heart from leaping into her throat, but she managed to keep herself a motionless statue of disinterest. Yet another expense, but she would be sending the billing invoice directly to the DEO. The car lurched to a halt and a quiet alarm started pinging. A crackle echoed through the speaker in the top right and the deep soothing voice of her head of security filtered through.

 

“ Everything alright, Miss Luthor ?”

 

“I have it under control.” She called out calmly, watching Supergirl’s eyes dart around the inside of the elevator. “Please override the stop.”

 

“ Right away. Do you need personnel ?”

 

The alarm ceased and the car rolled into motion again, carrying them up to the top floor. Lena gave a frustrated Supergirl an icy look.

 

“If you would be so kind. We have an unauthorized guest.”

 

“ Yes ma’am .”

 

Supergirl’s cheeks flushed and she pressed her lips together in a thin line. “That won’t be necessary-”

 

“Oh I think it is , Supergirl. Until you can respect my wishes and stop damaging my property I think extra measures need to be put into place. I have been playing nice. Too nice. It’s time you and your agents met me in the middle.” Lena bit out, cool and dangerously low. “If you would like to make an appointment, you may call my assistant. Until then, I don’t want to hear of you setting foot inside this building. Do you understand?”

 

Lena hadn’t seen Supergirl’s expression so angry in a while. Good. Maybe she had actually listened this time. She could feel the uncomfortable tension rippling through the tiny space and she wondered idly if it could be cut with Supergirl’s heat vision.

 

The elevator car rolled to a gentle stop and the doors slid open with another quiet ding . Just on the other side of the door were three security officers in black. The elevator stalled again with the soft chiming alarm, most likely from the control room.

 

“Ma’am?” One of the men called, his hand resting on the firearm at his hip. They all knew it wouldn’t do any good against a bulletproof alien, but it was a small comfort that he would try to do his job.

 

Supergirl barely cocked her head to the side and listened to the three men behind her in the hall, and then what Lena could only assume were suspicious whispers of the employees on the top floor. The Super pursed her lips together in frustration, knowing that doing anything else at this point would reflect poorly on her public image. She threw Lena another glowering look and graciously stepped aside to let her pass.

 

“Have a good day, Miss Luthor.” She spoke woodenly, the muscle jumping in her jaw again.

 

Lena took the opening and strode briskly from the car, throwing a quiet thank you gentlemen to her security team. She didn’t look back when she heard the three of them step into the car nor when the doors slid closed, taking another trip down to return Supergirl to the lobby of L-Corp. Now the real trick was to try to figure out how to focus for the rest of the day. She clicked down the hall, trying to ignore the few stragglers of her employees trying to be sneaky just ahead of her as if they hadn’t been trying to snoop after the security personnel that had run through the top floor. Word would get out soon enough of Supergirl’s pushy behavior whether she wanted it to or not. Feeding into it would only perpetuate their weird feud. Lena was here to work, not to get roped into some annoying high-school drama CW television show. If the tabloids wanted to write about cat fights or other hair-brained theories she was happy to let them run their mouths until her legal team decided they were being a little too obnoxiously close to defamation of character and harassment.

 

Lena Luthor was no stranger to deals and handshakes under the table, it was just business after all. But one thing she didn’t like was a secret government agency attempting to put her on a leash when she’d been nothing but a helpful and law-abiding citizen. She wasn’t stupid, she knew it was because of her lineage. Supergirl may try to play the superior moral standing in regards to business, but even she was bound by the same laws of the land as she was. It had been a touchy subject for the Girl of Steel to have her own words thrown back into her face with a stack of legal documents, but Lena did what needed to be done to keep L-Corp as independent as possible. She had been rudely made aware that if a simple gentle smile and wide blue eyes didn’t work, then other more underhanded measures for the good of humanity were employed. The photographer James Olsen had been permanently barred from all L-Corp properties for helping fabricate a story about her creating Kryptonite. Of course the so-called footage leak had been courtesy of her mother, but the man had taken a single forty-three second video and justified it as an excuse to call for her arrest. Thankfully simple digital forensics had easily torn the evidence to pieces and she had been released after a single, uncomfortable night in holding.

 

No, she and Supergirl din’t see eye to eye. It would have been a great partnership had egos and secretly-funded government agencies hadn’t stepped on her toes.

 

It was frankly a bit of a pipe dream now, but maybe one day when the Super wasn’t choked by the DEO- if she could get out from under them…

“Yes, Jess?”

 

“ Supergirl is requesting an appointment with you as soon as you’re available. ”

 

Lena knew Jess heard her frustrated sigh, she hadn’t even bothered to stifle it. “Please tell me I’m full for the next six months.”

 

“ Unfortunately you had an hour open up next month, the first Tuesday. Or shall I deny her request? ”

 

Lena set her stylus down before she threw it.

 

“How likely do you think it is that I’ll just be able to ignore this and it’ll go away.” She griped.

 

“ I suppose that’s a yes for next month then .” Jess commiserated with gentle professionalism.

 

“I don’t particularly want TMZ pushing that stupid catfight again, so yes. What time?”

 

“ Four O’clock .”

 

“Oh good. It’ll be five o’clock in the midwest, that’s close enough to a socially acceptable time to drink.”

“ Yes, Miss Luthor .”

 

“Very good.”

 

The intercom clicked off and Lena buried herself in her work for the rest of the day.

 

 

“Jess? Please make sure Supergirl checks in at the Lobby. Just like everyone else.”

 

“ Of course, Miss Luthor. ”

 

Lena sighed in annoyance as soon as her assistant ended the page.

 

She has enjoyed her weeks without outside interruption so much that she had completely forgotten that she had forced Supergirl and her handlers to play by her rules. She had forgotten all the way up until yesterday morning when Jess gave her the weekly schedule spreadsheet for her reminders.

 

At least she had been able to choke down a meal replacement shake around one, having been far too annoyed with her upcoming meeting to eat a proper meal. Her computer dinged with a new slew of emails and she distracted herself with them over the next ten minutes until Jess was paging her again.

 

“ Miss Luthor, your four o’clock is here .”

 

Lena bit back her sigh and carefully closed out windows with sensitive information before locking her computer altogether. She had already put away all of the unreleased data or unfinished research in the special lead-lined safe in the corner. She had hoped to forego such an extreme measure, but the kryptonite debacle had shot that chance in the foot.

 

“Send her in.”

Lena had been so braced for the vibrant suit and obnoxious hair-commercial curls that her sleek black tactical suit and large fishtail braid threw her for a loop. Her jawline and cheekbones were that much sharper with her haired pulled back, and her face was carefully neutral. She had a black folio tucked safely under her elbow.

 

“Good afternoon, Miss Luthor.”

“If you would, Miss Luthor.”

Lena pursed her lips. “I can behave when I need to.”
