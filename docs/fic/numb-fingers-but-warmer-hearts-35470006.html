    Chapter 1

    The walk was Clint's idea, but Kate is more than happy to spend Christmas Eve with the Hawkeye. He's heading back home to his family later anyway, so she'll take all the time she can get. Besides, letting Lucky loose in the woods is a lot safer than having him run across a road. Again.

    "So… What's on your Christmas list?" Kate asks, shoving her hands in her pockets. It is easily below freezing and she is really regretting not bringing a pair of gloves. Clint gives her a sideways glance. "I'm way too old for a Christmas list." Kate rolls her eyes. "I'm starting to see why people say never meet your heroes. You're boring."

    He frowns, opening his mouth to respond, but he's interrupted by his ringtone. He pulls his phone out of his pocket, checking the screen before stepping away. "I need to take this, I'll just be a minute. Go on without me, I'll catch you up. And don't lose Lucky!" "I would never," Kate replies, offended, before looking around and realising she has no idea where he's got to. As instructed, she keeps walking, keeping an eye out for a flash of blonde fur. Lucky has been enjoying the freedom of the forest, where he can run around without them worrying about him being hit by a car. Besides, he’s smart; he always comes back, because he knows Kate is a reliable source of pizza.

    Everything is going fine until the trees start to thin, and Kate realises exactly what he is about to do.

    “Lucky, no!” she yells, but he just wags his tail at her as he skitters out onto the frozen lake. “Come back here! Bad dog, come here! Please Lucky, get off the ice!”

    Lucky sits in the middle of the frozen lake, his tongue lolling out of his mouth as he stares at Kate. She is seriously starting to wonder whether this dog has a death wish. “If you come here, I’ll give you an entire pizza,” she tries to bargain. “I’ll let you sleep on my bed. I, uh… I’ll let you lick Clint’s face! Come on, Lucky. Be a good boy, come to Kate.” He doesn’t budge. He almost looks proud of himself.

    “I hate you,” Kate mutters, stepping towards the edge of the frozen lake and eyeing it warily. “I should have let you get run over by that car.” She throws her hands up. “Okay, that was a step too far, I’m sorry. I love you. Please come here now?”

    When Lucky still doesn’t move, she takes a deep breath and tentatively pokes the ice with her foot. When it seems to be solid, she cautiously steps out, holding her breath. “Okay, I’m coming to get you,” she says, her eyes glued to the ice. “Don’t go anywhere.” Kate manages three more steps before a resounding crack echoes around the lake. She looks up to see Lucky almost within grabbing distance, his head tilted adorably to one side. “Shit,” she mumbles, and the ground underneath her crumbles.

    For a moment, she doesn’t feel anything. Then, the cold hits her and she gasps reflexively, choking on the freezing water. It is all consuming, chilling every single cell in her body, and she wonders whether this is how she is going to die.

    No, she thinks, and she forces herself to pay focus. You didn’t win the Open Water Swimming Championships in 2016 to drown here.

    With a few powerful kicks, she fights her way to the surface, blinking water out of her eyes. “CLINT!” she screams, desperately hoping he is close enough to hear her. “CLINT! HELP! PLEASE HELP ME!”

    Kate scrabbles at the edge of the ice, but trying to get any kind of grip is almost impossible with her trembling fingers. Water is still pouring down her throat and freezing her from the inside as she desperately tries to gulp down air, and it occurs to her that she might not survive this. It is taking all her strength to keep herself mostly afloat, and the ice under her nails tells her that she isn’t getting out of this situation by herself.

    “Clint,” she mumbles, her head half underwater. A part of her knows she needs to concentrate, to keep swimming until help arrives, but she feels so heavy, and each kick is less and less effective at keeping her floating than the last. Slowly, she sinks below the surface, one hand extended up towards the surface, the water slipping through her fingers. For just a moment, Kate can think clearly. She stares up at the hole she fell through, glad that the last thing she will ever see is the vivid white of the snowy sky above her, and then her eyes slide closed and there is nothing but darkness.

    Then, there’s a hand holding hers, and she is being yanked out of the water and onto the ice in one surprising show of strength. Kate is pretty sure she can hear someone talking, but the words float by her as she lies limply on the ice.

    There’s warmth on her face - hands holding her cheeks and air being breathed back into her lungs - and water dribbles down her chin. The hands move to her chest, pressing down hard in short, sharp bursts, and she comes to with a gasp right as she feels her ribs crack under the pressure.

    “Oh thank God,” Clint says. “Kate, can you hear me?”

    All she can do is nod weakly, still coughing water up out of her lungs. Clint helps her roll onto her side just as she starts retching, gasping for air as the water spills back out onto the ice. After a minute or two, her breathing has steadied a little bit, Clint’s hand rubbing comforting circles onto her back as she manages to breathe again.

    “Is Lucky okay?” Kate asks between gasps.

    “Of course you ask about the dog first,” Clint sighs, helping Kate to sit up slowly. “He’s fine. It’s you we should be worried about. We need to get you to a hospital.”

    Normally, Kate might have protested that, but she’s sure she stopped breathing for a minute there and her ribs feel pretty broken. “Alright.”

    “Come on, let’s get you to the car quickly,” Clint instructs, eyeing her. “You aren’t shivering, and that isn’t a good sign.”

    “Oh, yeah,” Kate replies, staring blankly down at herself. “I should be cold, shouldn’t I?” Apparently that was not the answer Clint was looking for, because he quickly stands up and scoops her up off the ice, carefully heading straight for land. The last thing they want is for them both to end up in the water, because Kate doesn’t think she could survive another dip. They reach dry land without any further difficulties, and then Clint starts walking even faster. Lucky is right at his heels as they hurry towards the car - Kate has always had a sneaking suspicion that he likes Clint more than her, and this looks to be proof - but even with Clint’s speed walking, relative safety is still ten minutes away.

    “Tell me a story,” Clint demands, adjusting his grip on Kate so he doesn’t drop her. “What were you doing out on the ice?”

    “Lucky went on it first,” Kate mumbles in protest, turning her head towards the warmth of Clint’s chest. His jumper is slowly getting soaked through thanks to her soaking clothes, but he doesn’t complain.

    “Do you always follow dogs into dangerous situations?”

    “Only this one.”

    They are silent for a few minutes. Clint’s arms must be aching from carrying her all this way, and she can feel him shivering underneath her, but he carries her anyway. The woods are a blur around her, just a series of blurry greens and browns and whites melting into one. “Clint?” Kate asks, just as they emerge from the woods.

    “What is it?” he answers, fumbling for the car key in his pocket while still carrying Kate. Kate blinks up at him a few times, her mind fuzzy. “Where are we?”

    Clint looks down at her with a concerned frown, biting the inside of his cheek. “We went on a walk in the woods, remember?”

    “Did we?” Kate replies vacantly, staring up at the grey sky with unblinking eyes. “Come on, we need to get you warmed up,” he says, his voice tight. He finally gets the keys out and manages to open the door with his foot, gently lying her across the back seat before rushing around to the front and turning the heat up to full blast.

    Kate’s eyes start to slip closed again, her cheek pressed against the leather of the seat, but Clint comes back around and gently shakes her. “Wakey wakey, come on. Don’t go to sleep just yet. We need to get you out of those wet clothes.”

    “M’kay,” Kate mumbles, helping to wriggle out of her sodden clothes and into one of his old jackets. Clint wraps her in the old towel they had brought in case Lucky had got muddy, and with one final glance to check she is as warm as he can get her, he climbs into the front seat and starts to drive.

    “Kate, I need you to hang on for me,” he tells her, trying to be comforting. “The hospital is close, you’re going to be absolutely fine.”

    “‘S cold,” she replies, starting to shiver again. Lucky, who had seemed to be very much enjoying being allowed to sit in the passenger seat, climbs over to the back, cuddling with Kate to help her warm up.

    “You’re a good boy,” Clint says, leaning back to stroke his head without taking his eyes off the road. “You look after Kate for me until we get there, okay buddy?”

    Lucky wags his tail, the soft fur brushing against Kate’s leg, and she starts to smile as she falls back into unconsciousness.

    The next time Kate opens her eyes, it is to an empty hospital room. She feels like she has been dragged through a hedge backwards - or, more accurately, been dumped in an icy lake in the middle of December - and she has no idea where she is, or how she got here. The last thing she remembers is the inky blackness of the water as she sank below the ice, choking for air and only being able to swallow water. Her heart rate starts to quicken, the monitor by the side of her bed beeping faster and faster, and she starts to shake, descending into a coughing fit as she struggles for air.

    “Hey, hey, Kate,” a familiar voice says, dragging her back to reality. “It’s okay, you’re alright now. Just breathe, you’re alright.”

    Kate manages to take a few deep breaths, slowly calming herself down, as Clint rubs circles on her back.

    “You okay?” he asks, searching her face. “You scared me shitless, you know that?” Kate swallows, blinking tears out of her eyes. “I managed to scare an Avenger?” Clint laughs without meaning to, dragging a hand down his face. “Jesus Christ. You have given me so many grey hairs.”

    “That’s nothing to do with me,” Kate replies, managing a weak smile. “You’re just old.” Clint clutches his chest, mock offended. “I’ll let you get away with that, but only because I broke your ribs.”

    “I thought they hurt a bit more than usual,” she winces. “But I’m pretty sure you saved my life, so thank you.”

    He gives her a sad smile. “You’re welcome. Just don’t chase that dog into any more dangerous situations, okay?”

    Kate frowns. “Where is Lucky, anyway? Is he alright?”

    Clint rolls his eyes. “He’s sitting in the car. I went to check on him a few minutes ago, he’s fine.”

    “Good. I’m starting to think he might not actually be all that lucky, though.” Clint laughs. “I think you might be right.”

    They are quiet for a moment, Kate leaning sideways into Clint as he continues to rub her back.

    “Some Christmas, huh?” Clint comments.

    “Are you going back to your family now?” Kate asks. She isn’t sure how long she has been asleep, but he must be close to missing his flight if he is still here with her. “I am,” he says, turning to face her. “But Laura suggested… well, if you wanted to, you’re welcome to come home with us for Christmas. If you aren’t doing something with your mom, of course.”

    Kate starts to smile - a full, proper smile, back to her usual self. “I would love to spend Christmas with Hawkeye and his family. Especially since he has saved my life twice now.” Clint smiles back, a mischievous glint in his eye. “Well, then, it’s a good thing I already arranged for the Avenger’s old quinjet to pick us up from the hospital roof then, isn’t it?” Kate’s mouth drops open. “You’re kidding.”

    “Happy Christmas, kid,” he answers, helping her up, and she can’t help but think yes, it is.

She hesitates for a moment, staring at her reflection, before shaking her head and swinging the door open.

    Standing at the bottom of the stairs is Clint, a worried look on his face. “You okay?” She smiles. “Yeah, I feel better after that nap.”

    He raises his eyebrows. “Nap? You’ve been asleep for six hours. That’s more than I usually get at night.”

    Kate sticks her tongue out at him. “Leave me alone, I’m ill.”

    “No shit,” Clint responds. “Laura thinks you might have a mild case of pneumonia. You’ll be alright, we should probably just keep an eye on you for a few days.”

    Kate nods, running a hand through her hair in a futile attempt to try and clean it up a bit. “I told you that being a superhero is rough,” he jokes, smiling up at her. “Anyway, you think you’re up for some Christmas dinner? It’s nearly ready.”

    Kate looks down at her pyjamas before shrugging and heading down the stairs. “That sounds nice.”

    Laura is in the kitchen, already dishing up. The kids are all sitting at the table, laughing and talking over each other. Kate doesn’t miss the way Clint pauses in the doorway, watching them all with a fond smile on his face.

    She nudges him, grinning. “All things considered… I think this Christmas has been pretty good.”

    He frowns. “I had to save your ass from the Tracksuit Mafia and then a frozen lake while also trying to not get killed by Maya or my dead friend’s younger sister. What about that screams ‘Best Christmas Ever’?”

    Kate waggles her eyebrows. “I used your cool coin flipping trick to blow up a bald guy and I made a new best friend. What about that doesn’t scream ‘Best Christmas Ever’?” “We’ve talked about this,” Clint sighs, directing her towards the table. “I’m your partner, not your best friend.”

    “I was talking about Lucky, actually,” Kate smirks, patting him on the head. “You’re a good boy, aren’t you?”

    Clint rolls his eyes, but he’s smiling. “Come on then, let’s get something to eat, Hawkeye.” “Thank you, Clint,” Kate grins back, and they both know it’s for more than the food.

    THE END

    “I think you will prove to be a much better Hawkeye than Barton, Kate Bishop,” Yelena declares, once Kate has finished her story.

    “Really?” Kate responds, her eyebrows shooting up. “I mean, he’s kinda the original, so-” “Original just means older,” she interrupts, holding the little pink umbrella out of the way so she can take a sip from her drink. “You are the newer, younger version. Besides, anyone who is friends with me is clearly better than Barton.”

    Kate beams, stirring the ice in her glass with her straw. “If I’m the new Hawkeye… doesn’t that make you the new Black Widow?”

    Yelena pauses, putting her drink down on the table, and for a moment Kate thinks she has said the wrong thing. “I suppose you are right. You know, I am glad we got drinks together, Kate Bishop.”

    “Me too,” she replies, flagging down the bartender so they can have another round. At the rate they are going, Kate is going to struggle to get home without throwing up, but Yelena barely seems affected. “You don’t have to say my full name every time, though.” Yelena shrugs. “It’s like free entertainment. You’re paying for all of this, right?” Kate rolls her eyes. “Sure.”

    They lapse into silence for a moment, watching as the bartender makes their drinks. Yelena is drinking some kind of tropical cocktail, while Kate’s is fruity and sugary enough to give her a sugar rush, with a puff of candy floss on top as a bonus.

    “I have a crazy idea,” Kate announces, the straw gurgling as she reaches the bottom of her drink.

    Yelena raises an eyebrow, amused. “What’s that?”

    “We should start our own superhero group. We could call ourselves the Young Avengers!” Yelena hesitates, thinking it over for a few seconds. “Well… most of the original Avengers are dead. The world is rather lacking in superheroes right now.”

    “And we’re totally badass!” Kate exclaims. “We could fight aliens, and everything, Yelena.” She laughs, and Kate is too drunk to tell whether she is laughing with her, or at her. “Who would we even recruit?”

    “Get me a napkin,” Kate demands. “I have a few more ideas.”
