    Chapter 1

    Daisy slammed her fists against the console in frustration, the dull thud reverberating through the Zephyr’s bridge. The latest loop had ended in yet another failure. It was the same song and dance every time: a plan, a glimmer of hope, a crash, and then the reset.

    “This is ridiculous, Coulson! We’ve tried everything! Every time we get close, it’s just reset, reset, reset! I’m so done with this.”

    Coulson leaned back, arms crossed, his expression calm but weary. “I know it’s frustrating, Daisy. Trust me, I know. But giving up isn’t going to—”

    “Giving up?” Daisy interrupted, spinning on her heel to face him. Her voice rose, sharp and biting. “You think this is me giving up? I’m the one who has to wake up in that damn pod every time, start from zero, and pretend like it’s not driving me insane! You get to remember, Coulson. You get to piece it together loop after loop. Me? I just get to die, over and over, and forget it all!”

    “I’m done,” Daisy muttered under her breath, turning sharply on her heel.

    Coulson looked up from where he was reviewing notes from the previous loops. “Daisy—”

    he started, his voice cautious.

    “Nope,” she snapped, holding up a hand to cut him off. “Not this time, Coulson. I need a break from this... all of it.”

    Without another word, she stormed out of the room, leaving Coulson frowning in her wake.

    As she strode down the hallway, her boots clanging against the metal floor, she passed Sousa, who raised an eyebrow but said nothing, sensing her mood.

    Out in the hallway, Mack and May, who had been mid-conversation, both stopped and turned as they heard Daisy’s raised voice from inside the bridge. Moments later, she stormed past them without so much as a glance, her boots hitting the floor with a determined rhythm.

    Mack frowned, his gaze following her retreating figure. “What’s up with her?”

    “Daisy,” May called after her, her voice steady but tinged with concern. “What’s going on?”

    Daisy didn’t respond, her shoulders stiff as she rounded the corner toward the galley.

    Daisy Johnson was slouched in the common area, her arms draped lazily over the back of the couch. She was so utterly, completely done.

    “What’s the point?” she muttered to herself, kicking her boots up onto the coffee table. She’d lost count of how many loops they’d gone through. The time storm kept rewinding everything back to the same moment, and no matter what they tried, nothing seemed to stick. For all she knew, this was loop #247. Or 2,247.

    “Hey, Daisy, have you seen my datapad?” Simmons’ voice floated in from the lab.

    Daisy smirked, knowing exactly where it was: wedged awkwardly behind a console panel after one of Deke’s ill-fated attempts to “optimize” the Zephyr. But why bother answering? In a few minutes, the loop would reset, and Simmons would lose it again.

    “Nope,” Daisy called back, popping a piece of gum into her mouth. “Try the fridge.”

    A moment later, Simmons appeared in the doorway, looking both perplexed and mildly exasperated. “Why would it be in the fridge?”

    “Stranger things have happened. We’re stuck in a time loop, remember?” Daisy said, waving her hand dismissively. She grabbed the half-empty bottle of whiskey from the table and poured herself a generous glass.

    Jemma’s eyes widened. “It’s ten in the morning!”

    “Is it? Or is it ten at night? Or ten years ago? Time’s fake now, Jemma.” Daisy clinked her glass against an imaginary one in the air. “Cheers.”

    “Daisy?” Jemma’s soft, concerned voice broke the silence.

    Jemma’s brow furrowed. “What do you mean, ‘time loop’? Daisy, you’ve been acting strangely for hours.”

    She steped further into the room and crouching slightly to meet Daisy’s eye level. “What’s going on with you? You’ve been tense lately, but this—this is different. You just walked out on Coulson.”

    Daisy sighed, tipping her head back against the couch. “You wouldn’t understand,” she said, her voice softer now. “You can’t understand. And honestly? It doesn’t even matter. None of it does.”

    “Of course it matters!” Jemma exclaimed, her tone insistent. “It matters because you matter, Daisy. I don’t know what’s happening here, but we’re a team. Whatever you’re carrying, you don’t have to do it alone.”

    Something in Daisy’s chest twisted at Jemma’s words, at the raw sincerity in her voice. She turned her head to look at Simmons, who was staring back at her with wide, concerned eyes.

    By now, she’d perfected the art of chaos within the loops. One time, she’d hotwired the Zephyr’s controls just to see what would happen (answer: they spun in circles until Coulson yelled at her). Another time, she’d convinced Sousa to join her in a karaoke rendition of “I Want It That Way”, which had gone surprisingly well.

    But today? Today felt like a “kiss Jemma Simmons” kind of day.

    The alcohol buzzing through her veins emboldened her, made her feel reckless. The idea popped into her head like a spark, unbidden and impossible to ignore. What were consequences anyway?

    Before she could talk herself out of it, Daisy smirked faintly and set her glass down. “You really want to know what’s wrong?”

    Jemma nodded without hesitation. “Of course.”

    Daisy leaned forward, closing the small distance between them in a single motion. Jemma’s eyes widened slightly, but she didn’t move as Daisy leaned in, hesitating for only a fraction of a second before pressing her lips to hers.

    It was brief, soft, and wholly impulsive.

    When Daisy pulled back, Jemma’s eyes were wide with shock. “What—” she began, but before she could finish the sentence, the familiar jolt of the time loop reset hit them both.

    She woke up in her stasis pod, gasping for air as the lid slid open. Around her, the Zephyr was exactly as it had been at the start of every loop. Jemma was gone, back to the lab, none the wiser about what had just happened.
