1. Lena Meets Miah

Author's Note:

This is my first attempt at a story, so apologies for mistakes.

Lena Luthor was busy at work when her desk phone beeped once. She considered ignoring it, knowing Jess would give her the message later, but she thought a distraction would prove useful.  She swiftly picked up the phone and brought the receiver to her ear. She smirked when she heard her assistant’s clipped, no nonsense tone, “The school group is here.  Did you want to make an appearance?”

Lena never really considered herself as nurturing or good with children, but she was an excellent businesswoman.  She knew that someone would take her picture and it would be good for her image to be seen hosting a school group at L-Corp.  She closed her eyes and took slow breaths through her nose.  On the third exhale she opened her eyes and responded to her assistant, “Yes, let’s go meet up with them.”

On their descent in the elevator, Lena wrung her fingers together finding some calming in the process of knotting and unknotting the digits.  Once the elevator dinged, she relaxed her hands and features and followed Jess out and down the lab corridor.  As they walked down the hall the clicking of her heels was joined by the sounds of children whispering excitedly.  When they rounded the corner she saw a group of 15 or so children dressed in blazers in varying degrees of tidiness, a few teachers/chaperons and a pair of her scientists all huddled around a large lab table.  It looks like they were just in time for the demonstration. 

The first scientist, Dr. Danvers, looked at the group with a smile and started, “Okay what is the first thing that we need to do?”

The small children looked around at each other and their teachers for a few moments before Dr. Danvers spoke again, “Okay, let’s work this out together.  What is the first thing you do before you cross the street?”

The kids practically buzzed with energy as they shouted out together, “Look both ways before you cross the street!”

Lena smirked at the near identical delivery.  Clearly this was a well-rehearsed phrase.  Dr. Danvers, expecting this response, continued on, “Right!  Now, what is the first thing you do when you get into a car?”

All the kids shouted, “Buckle up!”

“You guys are on a roll!” Dr. Danvers congratulated them before moving on, “Okay, here is a tricky one, what do you do when you hear a fire alarm?”

There was less cohesion with this and only a few phrases could be heard through the din, but most of the children seemed to have a good idea of proper procedure. 

Dr. Danvers chuckled at this, “Sounds like you are all very prepared.  So what do all of these things have in common?”

There was a bit of nervous chatter and then a little boy raised his hand.  Lena could have sworn that Dr. Danvers face went just a bit softer when she looked at him. Then she responded, “Yes, Miah, what do you think they have in common?”  Lena quirked an eyebrow at this.  It seemed that her esteemed scientist knew the child; if the affectionate pet name was any indication.  For some reason, Lena found herself captivated.  So she studied the boy.  He had a head full of unruly blonde curls, big blue eyes and dimples you could probably see from space.  He was adorable and Lena found herself getting closer so she could hear his response more clearly. 

Miah, as he was called earlier, gave Dr. Danvers a lopsided grin that made the dimple on the left check more pronounced (if that was even possible), and answered, “Safety.  Those things keep us from getting hurt.”

Dr. Danvers lit up at his response and smiled widely (Lena found herself joining in), “Correct! And that means that the first thing we need to do here today,” she looked around and then spotted Lena for the first time.  Dr. Danvers straightened a bit and nodded her chin motioning Lena to join in. 

No one was more surprised than Lena when Lena finished for Dr. Danvers by saying, “The first thing we need to do today is get you outfitted in scientist safety gear.”

As she finished speaking, the whole room had turned to give her all of their attention.  She didn’t let her nerves fray and she continued as though this was all a well-rehearsed routine.  She made a show of looking around and then let her eyes settle on the little boy who had spoken moments before, she gave him a genuine smile and approached him slowly.  When she got within arm’s length she spoke again, “You were the one who got the question correct.”

Even though she did not phase the statement as a question the little boy still gave her an answering nod. Lena smiled again and then held out her hand for him to take while she said, “My name is Lena Luthor, what is your name?”

The boy quickly glanced and Dr. Danvers, further confirming that there was some sort of relationship between the two. If Dr. Danvers was shocked by her boss’s behavior she hid it well. She gave a quick nod to the boy and he took Lena’s hand in his own and said, “I’m Jeremiah Matthews.”  He paused for a moment and then he quickly tacked on, “Oh, it’s nice to meet you!” Lena’s smile grew larger.  She appreciated that this boy’s parents had obviously been working with him on being polite.  She shook his hand and then cocked her head slightly to the side and said, “Do you think you can help me with something?”

Jeremiah nodded, dimples deepening as he smiled brightly.  Lena continued to hold his hand and led him over to another assistant waiting with a cart of supplies.  She let go of his hand when they arrived and felt a brief pang at the loss of warmth.  She took a lab coat with a small L-Corp symbol on the pocket and helped him put it on over his blazer. Then she took a pair of goggles and placed them on his head.  She briefly tousled his curls and said softly, “There you are, you are all ready to be a scientist.”

Then she gave him a wink and addressed the rest of his class.  “So the lab coat will protect Jeremiah’s clothes and the goggles will protect his eyes.  Do you think he needs anything else?”

A girl in the front shot her hand up and she shouted “gloves” before Lena could call on her.  Lena chuckled at her enthusiasm and said, “Right you are.  Let’s get Jeremiah some gloves.  And then, let’s get everyone ready.  I know that Dr. Danvers has so much for you to learn about.”

As the children rushed forward to get their lab coats, goggles and gloves, Lena helped direct Jeremiah away from the chaos and over to where Dr. Danvers was waiting at the lab table.  She greeted Lena with a polite, “Ms. Luthor, I’m glad you could join us.”  Lena was barely able to nod her greeting before Jeremiah attached himself to Dr. Danvers legs. Dr. Danvers smiled down at the boy and then she looked back up at Lena, “And you have already met my nephew, Jeremiah.”

The boy detached himself at this point and looked back up at Lena with bright eyes that even the goggles could not dull.  He spoke sweetly, “Are you going to stay with us, Ms. Luthor?”

He spoke so hopefully that Lena felt compelled to give in to his demand. She returned with, “I can stay for a little while.”

“Yay,” Jeremiah responded while practically vibrating in his shoes with excitement.  He seemed to have taken quite the liking to the young CEO.

While they waited for the rest of the children to be fitted, Lena engaged with the child, “So, how old are you Jeremiah?”

“I’m almost five and a half.”

“Oh wow, so a whole hand.”

“Yes,” he nodded proudly.  Lena had to grin a bit at that.

“What is your favorite thing to learn about at school?”

“I love science.  Just like my mom, and Auntie Alex.”  Lena smiled a prodded a bit further.

“I love science too.  So I know your Auntie Alex,” she said while briefly smirking at her employee before continuing, “works here at L-Corp. What does your mom do?”

“She’s a professor.  She teaches about space.”

Dr. Danvers interrupted briefly with a hint of pride, “My sister.  She’s an astrophysicist.”

Lena was impressed.  The Danvers family seemed to be quite accomplished.  She wanted to ask a few follow up questions but she also found herself enraptured with the little boy in front of her. So she continued on her conversation with him.  “Wow,” she said, “And what does your dad do?”

A small crinkle appeared between his brows and he responded, “Auntie Alex says he doesn’t do much.”

Lena let out a chuckle while Dr. Danvers whispered, “Miah!”

2. Lena Chats with Alex

Summary for the Chapter:

Lena and Alex have a conversation about Lena's big project. This chapter is really just a necessary evil...gotta have some background.

Lena absentmindedly wiped the lipstick from her glass as she peered at the tablet in front of her.  Before her was a set of detailed schematics and pages of data.  She took a breath to calm the excitement she felt boiling in her.  She was going to rebuild the space station.  This was going to be the biggest L-Corp venture.  While she wanted to schedule a press release and unveil her plans she knew she needed to bide her time.  It had been 17 years since the explosion of the Zorel International Space Station and people were still wary.  She couldn’t blame them.  The explosion was so vast that debris were still falling to earth.  There were no survivors. To top it all off the entire incident was shrouded in mystery and government red tape. 

Lena had done her best to obtain all of the information about the explosion that she could.  She was a Luthor after all and had quite a few favors owed.  But even with all her sway there were still large sections that were missing, and many questions unanswered.  Some of the files she was able to obtain alluded to human subjects data, but she was never able to get very far when she tried to obtain more information.  Even with her name and connections, that was apparently off limits.  It made her brow twitch in annoyance when she thought of the missing pieces but she took another swig of her coffee and moved on.  She pulled up the file on the head scientists; John and Alura Zorel.

The Zorels were responsible for almost all of the advancements made in space travel.  Lena felt herself drawn to the image of the two staring up at her.  John seemed tall and sturdy, and while his attire screamed “mousy scientist,” his blue eyes were bright and sharp behind his thin frames.  Alura was much more elegantly put together.  Lena was instantly draw to the dimpled smile.  A small smile crossed her face when she thought of the little boy from earlier in the day.  Jeremiah nestled himself into her thoughts and she found herself enjoying the reprieve.  She chuckled at herself and then went back to her study of the couple before her.

The Zorels were both brilliant scientists.  John was a physicist with a medical degree and Alura was an engineer and she ‘dabbled’ in chemistry.  They lived on the space station for 14 years and during that time they pioneered limited space travel, fuel efficiency, alternative energy, and were testing possible cancer treatments.  The zero gravity environment was proving useful in the testing of different chemical components on human cells.  They were basically a powerhouse of innovation and the whole scientific community was still mourning the loss.  With no children, their legacy died with them.  Lena looked at them and wondered what happened that day.  She looked again at John and thought back to the human subjects data.  What could he have done that would have led to such important data being destroyed.

As she peered at the image before her she was interrupted by a rap at her door.  She quickly closed her file and told Jess to enter.

“Dr. Danvers is here for your 4 o’clock meeting.”

“Send her in Jess, thank you.”  Lena stood from her desk so she could greet the woman as she entered.  When Alex entered, Lena reached out to shake her hand and said, “Good to see you again, Dr. Danvers.”

The scientist smirked, “Likewise.  And you know you can call me Alex.  We have known each other for a while and you did make my nephews whole week this morning.”

Lena gave her a genuine grin and responded, “He is absolutely adorable. And you should also call me Lena.”

Alex nodded her acceptance and then Lena gestured to the couch so the two of them could sit.  Once they were seated Lena planned to dive right in, but something compelled her to try a different direction.  If all went well, her and Alex would be working together quite frequently and it wouldn’t hurt to have an amicable acquaintance. 

 “So your nephew is quite precocious.  Does he remind you of your sister when she was his age?” She delivered with a small grin.

“Oh,” Alex, paused in an uncharacteristic show of discomfort, she scrunched her forehead and continued, “Well I kind of wish I knew,” She finished softly.

Lena quickly interjected trying to ease the other woman’s sudden discomfort, “My apologies, I didn’t realize she was older.”  She shook her head fondly and then looked sincerely at Alex, “You just seem like the ‘big sister’ type.”

Alex smiled and then chuckled, “No, you are right.  I am older.  And she will definitely get a laugh when I tell her what you said.” Her gaze unexpectedly softened, “My sister was adopted.  I didn’t meet her until she was 12.”

“Oh,” Lena whispered.  She felt an unexpected connection to the unmet Danvers sister.  She continued, “I was adopted too.”

Alex smiled in response then cocked her head slightly, “Small world.”

They chatted for a little while longer about inconsequential things and then Lena steered the conversation back to her original purpose.  “I called you in today because I want to talk to you about the Zorel Space Station.” 

Lena noted that Alex was perfectly stoic in response.  This absence of a response struck Lena as odd.  Most people had something to say when she discussed the most horrific accident of their generation.  In fact, she often avoided discussing it because it usually prompted people to share their memories of what they were doing on that day and inevitably took entirely too long.  But Alex gave away nothing.  No memories, no sympathetic head tilt, no shock.  She waited a moment to allow the scientist to respond.  When she realized that Alex was not going to interject, a response she filed away for later evaluation, she continued, “While the advances in fuel and alternative energy are well documented it is not well known that the Zorel Space Station was also making considerable advances in cancer research.”

She paused.  Lena was expecting some reaction.  The only indication that the scientist was even listening was a slight whitening of her knuckles as she clenched her fists in her lap.  Lena continued to look at her expectantly and Alex eventually unclenched her knuckles and spoke in a measured tone, “That is interesting.  What sort of data do you have?”

If Lena didn’t know any better she would think that the doctor was suspicious.  She allowed her eyebrow to raise slightly at the tone and decided she would be open with her employee, “I have some of the preliminary data about how chemicals and compounds react in a zero gravity environment.  The data is quite promising and if L-Corp is to move forward with our plans for a new space station, this is one of the components we would like to move forward.”  Lena took a moment to allow her employee to process what she said.  For some reason she had upset the woman and she did not know why, but she wanted to smooth it over, so she said softly, “Alex,” the doctor’s head jerked up at the softened tone.  When their eyes met Lena continued, “I would like your help with this.”

“No.”

Lena leaned back in shock. She raised both eyebrows, “No?”

“No, I won’t help you with this.”

“Alex, I’m not asking you to go into space.  I’m just asking you to think of the possible tests you would like to run on different biological processes.  Surely, as a scientist, you can see the potential for advancement?”

“I see a dangerous line.” Lena frowned at the cryptic response.  She was completed flabbergasted by the researcher.  Most scientists she knew would be chomping at the bit for this opportunity.  Then Lena was instantly thinking about those sharp blue eyes and the destroyed file...human subjects.  Was there a dangerous line?  Lena took a different approach.

“Alex, I don’t mean to upset you.  I am only thinking of all of the possible good we could do.  But let’s take a step back.  If you were to join the team,” she held up her hand to stop the woman’s interruption, “Just hear me out?”  When Alex nodded, she continued, “If you were to head up the bio team planning, it would be prudent to think about guidelines and safeguards?  I mean this is new territory, there are technically no laws in space.  We would have to be at the forefront of innovation and legality. I am not as well-versed in biological research as you are.  I would defer to your expertise in this area.”

Alex sighed and closed her eyes. When she opened them again her brown eyes were sharp and bore down on Lena, “Let me ask you a question?”  Lena almost looked away at the intensity, but she held her ground and nodded and gestured to go ahead.  If possible, the intensity of Alex’s gaze increased and she said, “Do the lives of many outweigh the lives of a few?”

Lena had a feeling that how she answered this question would not only affect her venture, but it would also have unknown ripples.  So she paused and truly thought about it.  Did she believe that it was okay to sacrifice a life if it could save more?  Where was her line?  She looked at Alex and said, “No.  It isn’t ethical to experiment with human life.  That kind of power over others is dangerous.”

Alex seemed to deflate and then Lena felt a twinge of guilt.  She knew she needed to be completely honest, so she sighed and said, “I know that there were human subjects.”

Alex practically snarled in response.  She started to rise from the couch and Lena reached out to touch her gently on the arm, imploring her to stay.  “There is nothing in my files that specifies what was done. There are only a few references to human subjects.  Please sit back down,” she tilted her head in a soothing gesture as she finished.

Alex sat stiffly on the couch and Lena pulled her hand away and continued, “I am not saying that I want to experiment with human subjects, I just wanted to be upfront with you about what I know.  Please listen.  I have seen children die from diseases that I know that we have the ability to cure if only we had the facilities to do the research.  Doing research in a zero-gravity environment has the potential to open all sorts of doors.  BUT, and please believe me when I say this, I do not believe that the ‘ends justify the means’.  I know that I need to make sure that I have checks on myself and everyone involved, because power corrupts.  I wanted you to head the team because you are a brilliant biologist.  But I now know that you also aren’t easily swayed by the temptation to play god.  Think about my offer.  You would get to decide what we do and how we do it.  We are going to rebuild the station, I would rather do that with you than someone else.”

Alex straightened imperceptibly and spoked in a resigned tone, “How long can I have before you need a decision?”

Lena looked at her for a moment and responded, “Let’s meet in a week.”

Alex stood and placed her hands in her pocket, “I’ll set it up with Jess on my way out,” and then she left the office without looking back.

Lena slumped back into the couch and let out a long breath.  That did not go at all like she planned.  She hadn’t intended on sharing all that she knew, but she felt it was imperative to gain the older woman’s trust.  She was also relatively sure that Alex Danvers knew about the human subjects before Lena told her.  Lena had a hunch that Alex not only knew that human subjects existed but she suspected that Alex knew what happened to them as well.  So, this begged the question; who was Alex Danvers?

Lena pinched her nose and called to ask Jess to make another coffee run.  She was going to need some caffeine for this.  She pushed herself off of the back of the couch and grabbed the tablet from the table in front of her.  She opened the employee database and pulled up the personnel file on Alex.

Alex was an accomplished scientist.  She had a Ph.D. in biology and had completed two post-docs before starting work at L-Corp.  Her mother, Eliza, was a pediatrician who was well known for her work in adolescent trauma.  Her father was deceased but had worked for the FBI.  She felt a pang of sympathy for Alex; she was well aware of the struggle of losing a father too soon.  Lena paused as she looked at the name, Jeremiah, and once again thought of the little boy she met that morning.  She wondered if he was named for the older man. While she was thinking of that she looked in the file for information on Alex’s adopted sister and was surprised that it was missing.

Lena was momentarily frustrated.  She ran a huge corporation but she liked to keep her ducks in a row.  That included having extensive files on all of her employees.  The fact that she had to go looking for additional information was irksome.  She pulled up her search tab and realized she didn’t even have a first name.  She frowned and then entered, ‘Matthews astrophysicist.’  She huffed pettily when she saw the hundreds of unrelated results.

Jess knocked and entered in the middle of her huff and brought the coffee over to the couch.  She looked over at the search result and smirked down at Lena before saying, “I never took you for a cyber-stalker.”

Lena narrowed her eyes, but took a sip of the coffee instead of rising to the bait. Jess took pity on her then and prompted, “Is there anything I can help you look for?”

Lena took another sip before responding, “I was looking for information on Dr. Danvers sister.”

“Oh you mean Kara?”

Lena startled at the familiarity of the response. “You know her?”

“Everybody does.  She comes here occasionally to get lunch with Alex.  She is literally the nicest person I have ever met.”

Lena was gobsmaked.  How was she this out of touch?  She pressed on, “You said her name was Kara?” Lena tried to ignore the way her stomach flipped when she said the name.

Jess, unaware of Lena’s strange response continued, “Yes, Kara.  Is there a reason you are interested?  You do know that she is married,” she added in a wry jest.

Lena practically chocked on her coffee.  When she regained her composure she said, “I just wanted to fill in the blanks on Dr. Danvers personnel file.”

Jess chuckled and then said, “She works at UCNC.  That should help with your stalking,” and then she turned and left the office with a smirk on her face.

Lena could pretend to be annoyed but she was very fond of her assistant.  And from an outside perspective, it would seem as though Jess was riling up the Luthor CEO, but in all actuality that bit of playful teasing was just what Lena needed to settle down.  She took a final swig of coffee and resumed her search.

Just a few clicks later and she had found her, Kara Z. Danvers, astrophysicist at UCNC.  Lena’s heart nearly jumped into her throat when she saw the picture on the page.  Jeremiah certainly favored his mother.  Bright blue eyes, blonde hair that fell in gentle curls, and a single dimple on her left cheek.  And where Jeremiah was adorable, his mother was breathtaking.  She had an impressive resume to boot.  Lena briefly wondered if she could sway her to join L-corp.  An astrophysicist, especially one this accomplished, would be perfect for the space station endeavor.  If nothing else, maybe she would be willing to consult on some of the logistics. Maybe Alex could arrange a meeting?  Then Lena felt a bit of nerves, for some reason she felt as though Alex Danvers would not want her sister anywhere near a space station venture.

3. Lena meets Kara

Summary for the Chapter:

Lena meets Kara and gets the Davers' family treatment.

Lena worked furiously for the rest of the week and well into the weekend.  There was no rest for CEO.  She would have worked the rest of Sunday but an angry Jess stormed into her office just after one in the afternoon.

“I was out and about and imagine my surprise when I notice that you are at work, on a Sunday.”

Lena chuckled, “Just finishing up, not to worry Jess.”

“Okay, then I will wait and walk out with you.”

Lena frowned, she knew that Jess had called her bluff and she would not be getting any more work done, at the office at least.  She sighed and began to pack up her things.  Quicker than a flash, Jess was taking her things.  “Nope, you are not taking work home.  You are going to go for a walk in the park, grab some take out and then watch stupid TV until you go to bed at a decent hour.”

Lena raised her brow and said with fake sternness, “You do know that I am your boss, right?”

Jess, without missing a beat, said, “Not today you’re not. Now let’s go.”

Lena just chuckled, she could take a half day off.  It was a nice day and a walk in the park sounded nice.  “Join me for a bit?”

Jess gave a half-smile, “I’ve got some time.”

The two strolled for a while and chatted about various things.  Jess furiously avoided all work related topics and made sure Lena did the same.  After a while they parted ways and Lena decided to walk the south side of the park before heading home.  When she rounded the corner she saw a familiar face.

Jeremiah Matthews was running full speed toward her. “Ms. Luthor!  Do you remember me?” He huffed out when he stopped in front of her, checks red and hair windblown.

Before Lena could respond, a woman ran up after him yelling, “Miah, you can’t just run off like that!”  Lena found herself disappointed when the woman following after the boy was not Kara.  She was annoyed at herself for wanting to see someone she had never met.  She chalked it up to professional curiosity and didn’t allow the thought to linger.  When the woman looked up she startled when she realized that Jeremiah had found some company, “Oh, sorry about him.  He’s faster than he looks.”

Lena chuckled, “It’s no trouble at all, Jeremiah and I,” she said looking down at the five year old with a fond smile, “are actually good friends.”

Jeremiah beamed in response and bounced in place, “Auntie Maggie, this is Ms. Luthor!”  Lena recognized the name, this was Dr. Danvers’ wife.  She stuck out her hand, and said, “Lena, it’s nice to meet you,” she trailed off waiting on the other to introduce herself.

Maggie took her hand and finished the introduction, “Maggie.  It is nice to meet you Lena.  Alex, my wife, speaks highly of you. And this one,” she gestured down at the boy, “Thinks you just about hung the moon.”

A small blush rose on her cheeks at the praise and she smiled down at Jeremiah and said, “The feeling is mutual.”

Before they could continue Lena saw Alex cresting the hill holding a small chubby child in her arms.  She heard her yell out, “Hey Mags, Miah, come on we are going to go get some pizza!”

Maggie gestured for Alex to come closer and then Alex realized that Lena was with the pair.  Alex stiffened briefly then relaxed and spoke, “Hi Lena, I didn’t realize these two had company.” She gave her an easy smile while she adjusted the small girl on her hip.

Lena was glad that after their last meeting that Alex could still be cordial.  It gave her hope that they would work together on the project after all.  She smiled at Alex and responded, “Jess made me leave and gave me strict orders not to work for the rest of the day.” They both chuckled thinking of the tenacity and audacity of the assistant. Then Lena turned her attention to the child in Alex’s arms, “And who is this?” she said giving a smile and small wave to the little girl.

The girl hid her face in Alex’s neck and Alex rubbed her back soothingly before answering, “This is our niece, Emilia.” She looked up at Lena and continued, “Unlike her brother, she is a bit shy.”

Maggie interjected, “She takes a while to warm up,” she tickled the little girl in the belly to coax her out of Alex’s neck. The tickle did the trick and the little girl gave a small smile and looked at Lena bashfully for a second before hiding back in the safety of her aunt.

Lena could see the resemblance between the siblings; the girl differed in that her skin was fair and she had dark brown hair. It was short, but she could see that it was already starting to curl at the ends.  They both had the same startling blue eyes.  Eyes that, she was a bit ashamed to know, came from their mother.

Lena asked after studying the child for a moment, “How old is she?”

“Nine months,” Alex responded without having to think about it.  She obviously had a close relationship with both children.

Lena smiled, “She’s adorable,” and then she realized that they probably had places to be so she continued, “It was nice running into you,” then she turned and addressed Maggie, “And it was nice meeting you Maggie.” She ran her fingers through Jeremiah’s curls and marveled at the softness.  She leaned down on her knee so she could speak to him eye level, “And you mister, next time you come to visit your aunt, you’ll have to come up and see me.”

She rose to her feet and was turning to leave when Alex stopped her, “Are you a fan of pizza?”

Lena was startled by the invitation. She was going to decline, but when Maggie saw the hesitation she leaned toward her and said, “You wouldn’t want to let,” she pointed down to a pouting Jeremiah and finished with, “this little guy down. Would you?” Lena realized at that moment that she was in trouble, there was no force on earth that could make her say no to that face.

“I’m not opposed to it.”

Lena passed Emi over to Kara and leaned down so she could Miah into a bigger hug.  “Thank you, sweetheart.”  Of all the accolades she had been given, the one’s coming from her son meant the most.  After a few moments of simply basking in the love of her family Lena addressed the whole group, “So, pizza?”

…

Later that evening, when the kids were tucked into bed with promises of sweet dreams Lena met Kara back in their bedroom. 

“Thank you for what you said today,” Kara said with a small smile.

Lena came closer and took Kara’s face in her hands, “No amount of words could ever express how much you mean to me.  I love you so much.”

Kara smiled serenely back, “I love you too.” She took a brief moment to seemingly collect her thoughts before she began again, “When I first came here, I had lost my whole world.  It took meeting you to realize that it wasn’t my world.  You,” she reached out and tucked a stray lock behind Lena’s ear, “You are my world Lena.  You are my everything.”  With that Kara brought their lips together in the sweetest kiss.  It was the merging of the remembrance of the past that brought them to this place and a promise for the future and all of the wonderful memories they had yet to make.

When they pulled away Lena found herself hoping that reincarnation was real, because getting to spend just one lifetime with this woman would never be enough.
