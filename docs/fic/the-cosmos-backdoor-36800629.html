Chapter 1

She wakes up to the sound of a ticking clock. 

 

Her limbs feel heavy, the pain is numbing and yet strong, it makes her wince when she tries and fails to move. She vaguely hears the buzzing and whirring of machines around her but they sound different to the ones at a hospital, there’s no beeping from her heartbeat or even the horns and ticks of an MRI machine at work. 

 

The comfortable chair under her almost lulls her back to sleep before she hears the sound of three heavy pairs of steps and then. A voice. She knows that voice and yet it feels too far away for her to pinpoint who it belongs to. 

 

Fluorescent lights blind her momentarily before her eyes adjust to the room around her. It’s in that moment she notices that the lead she feels on her arms isn’t just the tiredness and the pain but two heavy and thick shackles around her wrists, shackles that don’t bulge no matter how much she pulls at them.

 

“You will hurt yourself,” the voice says behind her before appearing to Lena’s right. She recognises the voice now.

 

“Kara?”

 

The blonde is wearing a long sleeved plain grey linen shirt. The house of El emblem stitched on the fabric above her heart in golden over the pocket, matching in color with a belt that separates the shirt from fitted black pants. It’s an odd sight. She doesn’t think she’s ever seen Kara in kryptonian clothes beside her suit. And yet. She’s so glad to be seeing the woman face to face to even care about the unusualness of it all.

 

“Kara! Oh my God, thank God you’re alright!” 

 

But Kara doesn’t reach closer to her when Lena tries to sit up, she doesn’t wrap her arms around her like Lena had expected her to when they finally rescued Kara. If anything, the woman looks alarmed at her reaction, eye’s going slightly round before she steps away from her. 

 

“How do you know my name?” she asks. But surprised, bordering on scared, tablet pressed against her chest, covering herself from any danger, Kara isn’t the most surprising thing out of the whole situation. It’s the articulate and harsh words leaving her mouth, it takes her a minute to realize what Kara had just asked, for her brain to understand that Kara is speaking in kryptonese and translate it in her mind. 

 

But why would Kara ask that? Why wouldn’t she know her name?

 

“Can you understand me?” Someone else asks. She tears her worried eyes away from Kara and finds two men and a woman in front of her. 

 

They’re all wearing clothes similar to what she remembers Alura, Kara’s mother, had worn when she was giving her the Harun-El. The woman in the middle with shades of light blue and dark green, the man on the right with green and the man on the left with orange. All carrying symbols she doesn’t recognize nor understand. She nods her head anyway, suddenly thankful a manic brother and a big crush on a pretty alien had led her to learn kryptonese. 

 

“I am Councillor Mayra,” she begins, then points to her right, “these are General Lek-Var and Dax-Ur, head of the interplanetary division of the science guild. Can you tell us how you got here?”

The woman’s voice is calm and her posture is relaxed- had Lena haven’t gone through the ordeal of being questioned by authorities a million times thanks to her brother, she would have relaxed too. But she can see how Dax-Ur is casually examining the screens above her, probably with her entire medical record, instead of listening to her words. She can see how Lek-Var’s hand is placed subtly above the gun on his side pocket. 

 

“Where-” the foreign language curls uncomfortably around her tongue and it leaves with uncertainty instead of the confidence Lena would have loved for the words to have, “Where am I?”

 

“You are on Krypton in the southern constellation Corvus.”

 

“Krypton? But Krypton-” she shakes her head. 

 

This doesn’t make any sense. Krypton is gone. Had been long for a long time. She has the memory of a man in red and blue flying around her city. The memory of a best friend she’s in love with. The face that’s standing to her right with an unreadable look in her eyes as she checks something on a tablet with Dax-Ur. 

 

“You fell out of the sky,” Kara says, kind as ever, sensing her distress. “Do you know why?”

 

Her mind feels fuzzy, every recent memory blurry around its corners. Until she remembers Lex again, Phantom Zone projector in his hands and a look of triumph as he walked around the fortress, and the fear shines a light on everything else, clearing her mind with adrenaline and desperation once again. The memories only become brighter in color the longer she looks at Kara and her so similar yet so different than usual clothes.

 

“I was- I was trying to find my friend. She was stuck in the Phantom Zone.”

 

All four kryptonians around her raise her eyebrows in surprise. A tense silence fills the room before the general scoofs, “You must be crazy then. Whoever is sent to the Phantom Zone is lost forever.”

 

Councillor Mayra raises her hand, stopping him from whatever he was going to say next. Lena watches from the corner of her eyes how Kara and the scientist quickly start to check the screens again, the blonde even disappearing behind her. 

 

“And did you?” the woman asks, “find your friend."

 

Lena frowns. “I’m not sure.”

 

Had they found Kara? Or had one of their plans gone terribly wrong again? It certainly wouldn’t have been the first. Finding Kara had turned out to be a much more difficult task than they had thought, but Alex’s and Lena’s inability to give up on it had made sure the ship had always stayed on course. 

 

But Lena cannot tell if their last attempt had worked. Or even what was the last attempt. She knows, more than anything right now, that she needs to go back to her friends. Find Kara. 

 

“You’re a long way from home,” Dax-Ur says, sliding his fingers along the tablet he’s holding until the images show up on a bigger screen on Lena’s left. The other people in the room look automatically, she has to maneuver with her restraints to turn to the side. She recognizes the planet instantly, “You’re from Earth. Located in the Orion Arm of the Milky Way, about a third of the way out from the center of the Galaxy.”

 

“I’ve also found Phantom Zone energy on her,” Kara chirps in, “her story appears to be partially honest.”

 

Lena wishes she could see her face right now, so she could understand why Kara doesn’t seem to recognize her. And then. “Hold on,” she frowns, “Earth is two-thirds from the center of the Galaxy. I’d know, one of my astrophysics projects was about it.”

 

She explains further when the people in the room look at her with uncertainty and a low chuckle leaves the general once again. But Lena starts to understand why the councillor is the one in charge, she remains calm and collected, her brown eyes give out nothing but patience as she talks to Kara and Dax-Ur. “Had there been any planetary movements or explosions that could have caused this?”

 

They both check as soon as the words are out of her mouth and as they type away Lena almost wishes something had happened. But Kara’s sure no, ma’am and Dax-Ur’s shake of his head have Lena’s heart beat in fear and Mayra’s eyes fill with curiosity.

 

“What’s your name?”

 

“Lena Luthor,” she answers, confused.

 

“General,” the woman turns to him, nodding her head at Lena and moving her hand from behind her back only to lace them together in front of her, “please open Miss Luthor’s handcuffs. It looks like we have a visitor from another universe.”

 

The man doesn’t move, he only looks at the woman as if she’d gone mad. “You got all that from an astronomy fact? For all you know she might have been a terrible student.” 

 

The Councillor’s face hardens, Lena sees the way her jaw clenches but she quickly relaxes it. Lena’s impressed, she knows the feeling of a man below her questioning her decisions but she always lets them see her anger, see what they had done wrong. Mayra only stares him down. “That was an order, general. Open her handcuffs and bring her into the tower. Kara Zor-El, with me.”

Kara exits the room just as the sliding doors are about to close. She doesn’t look back to Lena.

 

 

General Lek-Var holds her arm tightly as he roughly leads her down hallways and turns corners and corners of the building. All white walls with immaculately clean white floors in which she can see her own reflection, all illuminated by small thin windows on the highest part of the wall, showing just the tiniest bit of a reddish sky. The lack of big windows surprised Lena until she saw a big one, just from the corner of her eye, before Lek-Var carelessly pulled her arm until they’re turning another corner- no sights of Krypton for her it is. 

 

They walk until Lena’s feet are aching, she’d woken up without shoes and they hadn’t bothered to give them back to her as they walked on the cold floors. She never once complains, she knows that any words leaving her will be carefully taken into account, any complaints kept in the back of people’s minds. But still, once two, engraved with a symbol she doesn’t know, silver doors open, she sighs subtly. 

 

She walks into “The Tower” as Mayra had called it. It’s basically like any observatory she has been to on earth, she knows. And yet the technology seems, and probably is, a million years more advanced. The telescope is the biggest she’d ever seen, leaving the newest models of NASA to shame and her fingers itching to try it on. 

 

“Miss Luthor,” Mayra catches her attention, a little mirt in her eyes as Lena quickly lowers her head, finally taking her eyes away from the marvels around her. She points to the two men beside her, “These are the El brothers. Zor-El and Jor-El. They’re both part of the leading team of the science guild.”

 

Her heart beats faster at the names. She’s quick enough to connect the dots for herself. Zor-El. El. This used to be Kara’s family. This is Kara’s family, given by the blonde that’s still watching her curiously as she stands straight a couple of feet beside them. 

 

Lena can see the similarities between the brothers. Broad shoulders. Cuadrangular chin. Baby blue eyes. But while Zor-El’s hair was black, Jor-El’s was a light brown. While the first brother watched her curiously, look so similar to the one on his daughter's face, Jor-El was watching her with childlike excitement, a small yet impossible to wipe away smile on his face. 

 

And Lena knows, she should probably be excited to be with the brightest minds she’ll ever be in a room with. Two adult kryptonians, heads of the science guild and with the knowledge of an advanced planet. And yet. All she can focus on is how much of Kara she can see in them. 

 

She sees Kara in the glimmer of Jor-El’s eyes. Finds Kara in the angle of Zor-El’s jaw. Sees the perfect eyebrows in them both. It only makes her miss the woman more.

 

“I am terribly excited to meet someone from another universe,” Jor-El says, walking closer to her and quickly extending his hand, as if he’s been holding himself back from doing so since she walked in.

 

She smiles back shily. His grip is strong and his eagerness reminds her of Jack, brings her back to a moment in which the man had introduced himself to her as he praised her latest invention. 

 

Jor-El quickly fires question after question about how did she get here, what was she working on when it happened, if she had been studying the Phantom Zone for long. 

 

“Not really,” she’s only able to answer the last one, “My friend got sent there a couple of months ago.”

 

“How did they get sent there?” Zor-El asks, coming closer to Lena but keeping his hands behind his back, letting the S proudly stand out. 

 

“A Phantom Zone projector,” she explains, wondering if her vague answers will be enough to calm curious kryptonians, she doesn’t think your planet is dead in my universe will get her many good points, “She’s got… enemies and one of them sent her there. Me and some friends were trying to find her, when....”

 

“When you ended up here,” Zor-El concludes. “Do you have any idea how?”

 

“I truly don’t,” she says, cursing her own memory. She tries to sound hopeful and confident when she says, “But it’s likely that I was in the Phantom Zone when it happened. There should be some soft of distortion or trail left behind.”

 

“We’re afraid that’s not true.” Jor-El supports his words by projecting a live feed of the Phantom Zone on one of the big screens. She sees, clear as day, the emptiness, the nothingness of it all, just Fort Rozz floating in the middle, everyone in the room watches it silently, all of them aware of what, who, is in there. “There had been no anomalies or disruptions in the Phantom Zone, ever. Everything is normal.”

“This means, unfortunately,” Zor-El adds, “that whatever brought you here, might have done so permanently.”

 

Permanently. Permanently. Permanently.

 

It’s such a decisive word. She wishes this could be a weird dream. Maybe her suit broke while she was in space and the lack of oxygen is making her hallucinate. Yes, it must be that. 

 

She can’t stay here. Surrounded by people she doesn’t know, no matter how amazing their technology seems to be. She has to go back. She needs to find Kara. She can’t leave Kara there , all alone. Especially when it was her fault. It was all her fault. Lex was her fault. She has to bring Kara back. 

 

But the kryptonians seem to have a different idea. Councillor Mayra walks closer to her when Lena says nothing and her face gets paler and paler. Lena doesn’t want to cry. She won’t cry. But her heart breaks a little at the thought she might never see her friends again. Never see Kara again. 

 

“We can help you get to Earth,” Mayra speaks, voice soft and kind, as if she knows it’s what Lena needs right now. “You’ll live in your original habitat with your people.” 

 

“But it is not my people,” she breathes out, “I am in a different universe. Could I stay-” 

 

“Krypton is a civilization in which everyone must serve a purpose and must do their part,” General Lek-Var spits out, as if the idea of Lena saying here disgusts him.

 

“I can do that,” she challenges, “I’m a scientist.”

 

“We cannot allow a mere foreign to enter a guild simply because they feel like it,” Jor-El says, although Lena can hear some disappointment in his words. She understands, the thirst of knowledge about planets far away and how they could help hers had been the main incentive on why Lena had allowed more and more aliens to be employed by L-corp every year, “it’d be unfair to everyone else.”

 

“I need an assistant in my research” Kara interrupts, stepping away from the far away table and getting closer to everyone else. She directs herself to the Councillor next, “I can school her, teach her our ways.”

 

The woman carefully observes Kara before her eyes move to the scientists, she says nothing but both of them seem to understand her question and nod their heads- Zor-El slower than his brother, as if he’s still not sure of his answer. Their actions are enough confirmation for the general, who scoffs and storms out of the room, letting the heavy doors close loudly against each other. 

 

“Very well. If you’re useful enough to miss Zor-El, we will let you stay here, maybe even let you enter a guild one day,” Lena smiles, relief in her heart, which makes the woman add, “But remember, any misconduct and we can quickly send you back to Earth, or worse.”

 

Her eyes trail the prison still being projected on the screen. Lena understands the message. She nods, whispers a thank you as she and the brothers leave the same way the general did.

Kara stays behind, she turns off the projector, the image of the Phantom Zone disappearing like dust in the wind. Lena uses the moment to watch her. The woman looks the same way her Kara does, except… she doesn’t. 

 

Her hair is shorter, straighter and a darker shade of blonde, too. There’s no glasses sitting in the ridge of her nose or red and blues covering her body. Her posture is straighter too, her hands resting behind her back as she walks around, so similar to Zor-El, and the bright smile that was always covering her Kara’s face is now more of a curious raise of eyebrows and observant eyes. 

 

“Thank you so, so much,” she says, when the woman stops in front of her.

 

“Don’t thank me just yet,” and Lena curses that her voice sounds the same and yet the string and combinations of words seem so far away from what her Kara would say. “I have only done that because you knew my name. If you did then we must know each other, in your universe.”

 

“We did.”

 

“We were… friends then?”

She’s not sure how to explain a story of friends to best friends to betrayal to another betrayal to enemies to acquaintances to forgiving each other and becoming somewhat friends to being lost in space. She’s not sure this Kara would even believe her or want to hear it. 

 

But there’s always one word when she thinks of Kara, when she’s not thinking along the lines of ‘love of her life’ and ‘soulmates’. 

 

“Best friends,” she says, only getting a small smile out of Kara.

 

Her feet still ache as Kara leads her out of the building, but at least now they’re covered in shoes that feel like she’s walking on clouds, after Kara apologized for Lek-Var’s insensitivity and brought them to her.

 

Kara leads her out of the building, guards stopping them on every entrance to question them but whose worries were all easily dismissed by Kara’s short but clever words. By the time they reach the street and walk into the kryptonian version of a high speed train, Lena is studying the council's brand new gift for her- a strong and heavy wristband. 

 

“It’s a monitor,” Kara says, sitting on the seat beside her. The train is filled with windows on every side and the roof, giving the perfect way to let Lena take into the beauty of Argo City in full movement, at the perfect time of the day. If she weren’t so preoccupied with the bracelet they have locked into her wrist. “They use it for the military guild soldiers sometimes. It both tracks you as well as checks your heart rate, blood pressure, things like that.”

 

“I think the council mostly did it for that first part and not my well being.”

 

Unlike the Kara she knows, this one doesn’t go into a speech about the goodness in their hearts or the duties they might uphold to defend them. Kara chuckles. “Perhaps.”

 

The red sun makes the city look like it’s in a constant sundown, the tall marvelous buildings made mostly of what looks like glass and white metal reflect the train as they pass beside, below and above them- all to reach a destination Lena knows nothing of. She wants to ask about it all, why there’s a garden on top of every building, why there’s a square every three blocks, why some buildings seem to have no entrance doors, why there’s no cars nor something that can tell one building apart from the other except one big number on their front doors. 

 

But Kara’s done enough for her today. And this version of her doesn’t look like one to answer the very and many questions of a new foreigner, not when those questions are probably simple and well known things. 

 

They continue on the rest of the trip in silence. In fact, most people seem to travel in silence, only sharing one word or another when they recognize someone and sometimes loudly questioning who Lena is, with her odd clothes that do not belong to Krypton. Kara always calmly explains she’s a new consultant and they all seem to calm down at it.

 

When they step out of the train, Lena thinks it’s actually starting to be sundown now. They walk into a building that looks just like any other building but the number 10 on its front door. Once inside, up and up and left and right down many corridors, they walk past a door with the house of El emblem engraved on it.

 

The apartment is so awfully not-Kara that it makes her want to cry. Every piece of furniture is either white or gray with small details in gold and every other artifact in the room only seems to be there out of complete necessity. 

 

There’s no coziness to this. No crooked table or mismatching chairs, or fluffy couch pillows, only an armchair that looks more decorative than comfortable. There’s no art hanging from the walls, only a screen with news on it but no one speaking on them, just a list one can expand on if they want. Latest robes. H'Raka’s disease. Second eclipse of the year is coming soon. 

 

There’s no pictures hanging anywhere and Lena can only describe it with one word: lonely.

 

It reminds her more of her own apartment on Earth than Kara’s. 

 

Kara comes back from the room she had disappeared into, holding a stack of clothes on her arms, she guides Lena into a door, two down from the one she’d walked into before. She lets her know the middle door is the bathroom and she can grab any food from the kitchen. 

 

“I’ll see you tomorrow, bright and early, lab partner.”

 

 

“Good day, Miss Luthor. Breakfast has been served. Report for duty.”

 

The robotic voice wakes her up with a start, her hands reaching out for something to defend herself with but only finding the empty bedside table. She wonders if Kara’s is empty, too, or if her room holds all her personal objects. 

 

The robot, lanky golden limbs and faceless face, house of El sigil shining on its chest, floats a couple of meters away from her, repeating its message until Lena lets out an exasperated okay . Its light twinkles momentarily, giving Lena the impression that its blinking, making her feel seen and uncomfortable under its gaze. She looks away. 

 

The clothes Kara had given to her are extended on the feet of the bed, black pants and white shirt, similar to what the blonde had been using yesterday, only with the lack of house emblem on her pocket. 

 

“Do you mind?” She looks back at the robot, feeling its light twinkle as she starts to get undressed. It's quick to turn around and leave the room, a whoosh filling the room as the door closes. 

 

None of her possessions are with her. The boots she was using that night. The green and white striped t-shirt she was wearing. Her jeans. She sighs, clicks the kryptonian belt into place. 

 

“Hello.” Kara doesn’t look up from her tablet, she hums it back distractedly as Lena sits down in front of her. It’s midday already, the kryptonian let her sleep longer than Lena ever remembers sleeping, all the way through the morning and well into lunch time. She was more tired than she thought, to not have woken up at all.

 

The robot quickly sets a plate in front of her, filled with cubes of different shapes and colors on the right and one slice of a sticky white rectangle on the left. 

 

“The red ones are twellian, a fruit native to Krypton,” Kara explains when she sees her moving her food around with her fork. “The yellow ones are imported from Daxam, the purple ones from Thoron and the green ones are artificially created on a branch of the science guild.”

 

“And the sticky brick?”

 

Kara chuckles, “Artificial as well, it possesses all proteins and nutrients to fill your morning quota. It tastes better than it looks.”

 

It does. It tastes like the soup Kelly had once brought her when she had come down with the flu and its contexture feels like ice cream once it's in her mouth. Before she knows it, her plate is empty and the robot is taking it away. She watches it curiously.

 

“That’s Kelex,” Kara explains, “a robot caretaker. It's here to help in every way it can. Food, clothes, information."

 

She nods. It’s weird to think about. Her Kara with a servant, basically. Kara, who always refuses for her friends to help her clean up after game night and stacks the plates together for the barista when they’re out having dinner and helps the delivery guy when her own orders are too big. But this Kara is completely comfortable as Kelex cleans the kitchen. 

 

“What do we have to do today?” 

 

Kara finally looks away from her tablet, sets it face down on the table and joins her hands. She’s wearing a black t-shirt today, perfectly fitted for her body and ending on her neck by a small round line of silver. Lena feels like she’s in a boardroom. With Kara. It’s unsettling. 

 

“Ten years ago Van-Zee created a material called Grahu, made out of oil and materials that were found by scientists miming the planet’s core.”

 

“So, plastic,” Lena says, versed on it.

 

“What?” Kara frowns.

 

“Nevermind, continue.”

 

“The material was easy to make and easy to mold so we started to use it on many things. But its longevity was less than we thought and waste started to accumulate. We’re not a… wasteful society,” Kara’s jaw clenches a little, “so, I was tasked in finding a way to recycle this.”

Kara turns her table back up, projects graphics and statistics in the air to show to Lena. She spends hours explaining the process of creation to Lena, how they’ve used some of the waste. Kara lets her know she’s got an audience with the council and the leaders of the science guild in a week, putting them in a slight deadline- one which Kara doesn’t feel too hopeful about given this is her fifth month on the project and she still hasn’t made any progress. 

 

It’s all fairly similar to earth’s plastic, with a few differences in the process here and the chemical structure there. And if humans had seen all its waste as the risk it was decades before an island of waste was created on the ocean. 

 

Before she knows it, Kelex is placing plates, a jar and glasses in front of them. Lena hadn’t even noticed him turning on the lights when it turned dark. But sure enough, the sky is painted by Krypton’s many moons, Agoron, Wegthor and Koron- Their dispositions vaguely reminds Lena of Orion’s belt, just bigger, more up close. Longing for home fills her heart. 

 

“We used to have another moon, too,” Kara speaks, breaking the silence. “It spun out of orbit a couple of years ago, almost eight now. It was weird at first, seeing the sky so different.”

 

She thinks about the little girl who had to change her entire landscape without a say, without an explanation. She wonders if that girl missed the big moons and red sky and the infinity of stars. She wonders if she misses Earth’s lonely moon now, trapped in the Phantom Zone. 

 

“Can you see any of these from the Phantom Zone?” Lena asks, “The moons, I mean.”

 

Kara eyes her carefully, curiously, “No. You shouldn’t be able to see anything there, maybe a really bright sun, with a lot of luck.”

 

She nods, having known the answer but still holding a little hope for her Kara to have something to make her company. But she’s all alone. 

 

The next day they’re on the train again, Kelex wakes her up earlier than the day before and points to her clothes before leaving. The outfit looks fairly the same as the day before, empty white shirt, gray pants and, to Lena’s surprise, big combat boots that are comfortable yet heavy once she puts them on. She struggles with them at first, but Kara walks around as if her own weight nothing. 

 

She understands the need of the heavy, combat-like boots once the Science Guild elevator opens into sublevel 12 and mountains, and she means mountains , of garbage fill the room. The room is nothing more than a dumpster but it’s chaos. Scraps of machines and pieces of furniture and containers for things Lena doesn’t know of. The only organized part of it is the circular structure in the middle of it all, holding consoles and keyboards- Lena follows Kara through a ramp, one in which is half covered by the waste and puts the need of her boots to good use, until they get to it. 

 

“Is there any way to organize this?”

 

“What do you mean?” Kara asks, fingers instantly opening the data on how much is here, how much is expected after this week.

 

“You know, arrange them into types of plast- Grahu,” She says, eyes observing the trash and wondering why she doesn’t smell anything, only Kara’s strong fragrance. “Could be easier to check which ones are recyclable and which ones aren’t.”

“It’s possible some aren’t?” Kara’s eyebrows turn down into a frown. The crinkle almost, almost, makes her think it’s her Kara.

 

“If this is anything like on Earth.”

 

It takes hours for the “people above”, as Kara called the higher levels of the Science Guild that monitor the projects and their needs, to approve the use of five Kelex units. It takes them even longer to program them into recognizing the different molecules inside each Grahu to separate them but, by the end of the day, when her feet are starting to ache and Kara’s perfect posture is starting to falter, they’ve separated the chaos into nine big piles. Organized chaos.

 

The weight of her impending briefing starts to weigh heavy on Kara two days before the date. They spend morning, day and sometimes night in the dumpster- Lena is suddenly grateful for the invisible barrier she’d found out about on her second day, containing the Grahu, and its smell, behind it. And in moments they weren’t at the dumpster, they were talking about it. Their conversations were continuous and wide but never moving past it.

 

Kara hadn’t mentioned anything personal, no name of friends, no hobbies or sports she takes part in or even a mere interest beside science. Dams are only one component of a comprehensive hydroelectric complex, but they are one of the most noticeable. A hydroelectric dam's objective is to provide a location where water's potential and kinetic energy may be converted to electrical energy using a turbine and generator.”

She feels like she’s in college again. Blood rushing through her veins, her heart thumping in her ears. Palms sweaty as she explains a concept to a room full of professionals. “Do you think the recycled Grahu could be used?” Kara says, directing herself more to Lena than the little group of people that were discussing the idea. “On foundations and as bricks.”

 

“I guess,” Lena hums, thoughtful. “They’d be solid enough once they dry up.”

 

“We could mold them into bricks,” Kara says excitedly. She looks at Lena’s unsure face and adds, “Using them for constructions is the best idea we’ve had so far.”

 

“We still don’t know if it’s 100% safe.” And they don’t. The recycled Grahu doesn’t seem to be destroyed with anything, not on any of their simulations and tries to do so. It stays firm and solid under anything so using them for housing and structures seemed like a logical choice.

“It’s science, it’ll never be 100% but the bricks will be strong,” she says, charming smile trying to pursue Lena her way. She sighs, because a pretty smile and a good argument is all Kara ever needs. “And it’d recycle every bit of Grahu that we have saved up in that enormous unit storage.”

It’s a plan. A good plan. Even if it was produced in a couple of hours and all thanks to Lena. But Alura, a council woman all of her own, thinks it’s a good idea. And Dax-Ur, head of the interplanetary division, does too. And so do the twenty or so people adding on their thoughts around them. 

 

“If there’s no other ideas,” The councilwoman on the podium says, after Lena was encouraged by Kara to explain it to everyone else, “Miss Zor-El’s and Miss Luthor’s project it is. Let’s get to work.”

 

 

“Are you worried?” Lena asks as they go up the elevator to the penthouse belonging to Alura and Zor-El. Kara had been quiet and thoughtful ever since the earthquakes had started.

 

“About us having dinner with my family?” She asks, turning to the woman to give her a comforting smile. “Of course not. They already like you.”

 

Lena smiles, raises to her tiptoes to place a kiss on Kara’s lips- the woman’s excitement since she had received a gram from her aunt Astra had been palpable, it was almost enough to drown out Lena’s nervousness and anxiety. Almost. 

 

“I meant about the dam."

 

Kara’s eyes darken again, crinkle presenting itself once again. “I just don’t like the idea of you going alone.”

 

“I’ll be fine,” she says. Sighs as the elevators open, leaving way to the extensive and perfectly polished… everything in the house of El. Shining floors and windows that take up the whole wall, showing the perfect landscape of Argo city, watching everything from above. Unlike Kara’s apartment, the house is filled with more color, reds and blues in between of white and golden- and, unlike most of the many rooms she’s been in, the Els have the luxury of having art hanging from their walls, a subtle yet noticeable touch that shows their higher status. 

 

“You’re still going closer to the epicenter of it all,” Kara says, a pout Lena wishes she could kiss away on her lips, one she doesn’t dare touch in case someone finally finds them. “There’s still shakes and avalanches there. No mentioning the possibility of the flood getting faster and stronger.”

 

“It’s going to be fine,” she squeezes Kara’s arm, green eyes pouring into blue ones with all the conviction she can muster. “Someone has to go and supervise the construction, who better than the one that gave the idea?”

 

“I know,” Kara sighs. Her eyes drop to Lena’s wrist, her fingers play with the ends of Lena’s clothes, a soft and green-shaded robe that was given to her by the science guild. “I just wish I could go with you.”

 

“You know you can’t,” she whispers, kindly, letting her hands wander at the lack of anyone’s presence around them, cautiousness be damned when Kara looks sad. The woman had been suggested to not go anywhere that would put stress on her leg, pressuring her to walk as little as possible- a suggestion that had become mandatory once Alura heard of it, leaving her unable to go to the dam’s construction and left her in charge of the Grahu’s transformation into bricks. 

 

“I know,” she sighs. Her head falls on top of Lena’s shoulder, breathing her in for just a second in silence. “But I’ll miss you.”

 

Lena chuckles, the sound traveling all the way into Kara’s heart, taking place and forming roots to never leave, instead growing and growing into an everlasting love. 

 

“You’ll survive without me for a couple of weeks.”

 

“It sounds terrible, though,” Kara adds.

 

She should care more about where they were standing, how easily anyone could have walked in. But Kara looks so charming with her red fitted shirt and so utterly adorable with her big pout and so unbelievably earnest with her bright blue eyes. Catching her lips between her own is almost an automatic response- Kara’s appreciative hum, a natural response now, too.

 

“I’ll miss you, too,” she whispers when pulls back, noses touching each other, eyes still closed. 

 

“And I have missed my niece very much,” a voice full of mirth says. The woman stands tall, the elegant black bodysuit clings to her body and her short cape, Military Guild red, hangs over it like a sweater. She smiles curiously at them both before Kara runs to her, exclaiming aunt Astra and wrapping her in her arms.

 

“It’s been a long time, little one,” she says, dropping a kiss on Kara’s temple. 

 

Kara looks small, content in her aunt’s arms, delighted as she surrounds herself with her love. Her light eyes find Lena- she doesn’t cower under her gaze and it seems to be the right thing to do when Astra smiles, pulling away from Kara.

 

“General Astra,” she introduces. “You must be the human.”

 

“That’s me,” she nods, “Lena Luthor.”

 

“Can’t say I haven’t heard anything about you,” she chuckles. “You’re quite the talk of Argo city, and my niece, of course.”

 

“Aunt!” Kara exclaims, blushing. 

 

Astra chuckles again and doesn’t waste any more time with pleasantries, instead leads Kara and Lena to the dining room, where Alura and Zor-El are waiting. 

 

The dinner isn’t anything out of this world, even if Lena is, in fact, in another world. Astra answers Kara’s many questions about her travels, Alura comments on their cultures and Zor-El inquires about their technology. Family drama. After all, Astra and Alura seem to be different sides of the same coin. If only for the side they choose in every discussion. “It’s completely natural for species to dissipate,” she waves off. “If the H’Rakas were going extinct we should have let it be so.”

 

Lena was going to mention how dissipate wasn’t really the right word. How dissipate contains a gradual disappearance. How words are the easiest way to turn a big issue into nothingness and a detail into the main problem.

 

“ Let it be so is just your favorite thing, isn’t it, sister?” Astra snaps, her plate and everyone’s long forgotten, the good atmosphere giving way to tension. “Because letting it be so has not changed Jor-El’s findings yet. No matter how much you’d like that.”

 

“Astra, please,” Zor-El says, all curiosity and warmness left from his voice. “Do not.”

 

“ Do not what?” She raises her voice, “Talk about a very serious issue?”

 

“We’ve all seen the data, aunt,” Kara says, her voice tenser than Lena has ever heard, colder around her aunt now even though she had only shown love and appreciation so far. “It is nothing to be concerned about. Father has said as much.”

 

She knows she’s not privy to most of the information on Krypton, she probably knows as much as Kal-El, the curious and clueless fourteen year old- if she doesn’t know less. But she’s confused as words are thrown around, as Astra seems to get angrier at her niece’s words and even more so when the room falls into a tense silence after she speaks.

 

“What data?” Her heart bangs against her sternum, the fear she’s too scared to think too much about coming right back into the forefront of her mind. No one answers. “Kara. What data?”

 

Kara’s shoulders sag, a breath leaving her lips and all gentleness coming back to her voice when she turns to Lena. “Nothing relevant,” she says, “just some anomalies my uncle found that have already gone back to normal.”

 

“I don’t think the partial destabilization of Krypton’s core is nothing relevant ,” Astra says, raising her hands in the hair, begging the others to feel her urgency. Her eyes find Lena’s and they light up for a second, when she seems to notice that Lena has not written her off as insane. When she seems to notice the fear in Lena’s eyes. 

 

This is her home now. She cannot go back to where she once came from. And now, her new home, is replaying the steps another version of it once did? It’s too much. Too much. And yet. It seems like such a tiny thing when she realizes Kara hadn’t told her about this. Not when discussing secret projects and dream projects and fears and wishes under the light of a rising Rao. 

 

She doesn’t find Kara’s eyes. She’s not sure what to tell the woman, yet. She’s not sure if she’s mad or sad or just plain disappointed. She’s just, mostly, confused and scared. 

 

“When did Jor-El find this out?” she asks Astra. 

 

“Jor-El was conducting research in the desolate northern region of Zith weeks ago,” she explains, eager to get someone on her side. “He insists that the planet's core is undergoing a mounting chain reaction that will destroy it.”

 

“Krypton is just undergoing a slight polar shift,” Jor-El dismisses her, drinking from his cup as if they’re not discussing a possible end of the world. He’s calm. Relaxed. Lena wonders if he was like that when her universe’s Krypton was dying, too.

 

But Lena knows the kind of dismissal this is. The one so many of her life saving projects received, only because they’d put a serious issue right on the spotlight. Only because it would have forced certain people, people that didn’t care, to accept the damage they did.

 

“What happened to Jor-El’s project?” She looks for confirmation. 

 

“It was annulled for lack of evidence,” Astra rolls her eyes. She receives confirmation. 

 

“That is a good thing,” Kara tells her, her hand finding her thigh under the table, trying to give Lena comfort and only making her worry more. “Lack of evidence means his theory is nowhere near right.”

 

“But shouldn’t it be worth looking into?” She asks, snapping more than she wanted to. 

 

This is her home. This is her home, now. How is it dying again? How many more will she have to lose? Her mother, Ireland. Lex, Metropolis. Kara, National City. Earth. And Krypton, too?

 

“I do not understand why you keep making such a fuss out of it,” Alura exclaims at her sister, raising from the table, all composure leaving her, “if Jor-El’s project was seen unfit by the council then it should be out of our minds already.”

 

“ You are the council!” Astra fights back, mirroring her sister’s pose, “ You deemed it unfit and might have put all of our lives in danger! Kara’s life!"

 

“You think I do not care about my own daughter’s safety? Of all the things you-”

 

“Okay, that’s enough!” Kara yells, standing up, too, becoming the barrier between the two women, stopping whatever was coming out of their mouths. “Stop talking like I’m not here! I am not a child!”

 

“Yes, we know that, little one but-”

 

“You are literally just calling me little!” Kara exclaims at her aunt’s words. Then sighs, willing herself to calm down and Lena watches, eyes unable to move from Kara and how much strength she has, how easily she can stand up for herself. The gift of a woman that has never been made to hide herself, that has not been second guessed or laughed at by mean and shitty highschoolers. The calmness of a woman that has not seen her whole world die and so, does not think this one will, believing blindly to her government's worlds.

Maybe as a way to calm the woman. “But you must trust that our council, my mother , are doing what’s best. Do you honestly believe creating paranoia and chaos will do any good? All for a simple polar shift that has already been reversed?”

 

“You’re right,” Astra sighs. Lena can see she’s, in fact, not convinced at all, and maybe everyone in the room does too but lets it slide as well. She’s more sure of the fact when Astra’s eyes find Lena’s momentarily, just a small glimpse, maybe just enough to see the worry still etched on Lena’s features. “You’re right, little one. You’ve really become such a tenacious and intelligent woman, haven’t you?”

 

Kara smiles, either because of the compliment or because the tension has left the room- even if Alura is still sending daggers to her sister and Astra’s shoulders have not lowered in the slightest, ready for a fight, ready for the argument to start again. And Kara must have known that.

 

“Who is also very tired,” she says. Lies, more like. Lena knows for a fact Kara is not tired at all after being put on rest because of her injury and having spent all day complaining about how bored she was in the compartments of their apartment. “We should head back.”

 

Astra nods, takes the dismissal for what it is. “Yes. I have some friends to catch up with, too.” 

 

Kara places her hand on Lena’s back when she stands up, it’s a subtle touch, nowhere near indecent and yet Lena is surprised at it all the same, especially when Alura’s eyes visibly drop to it but Kara does nothing to move away. 

 

But that it’s not what stays on her mind as they ride down the elevator and travel all the way back to their apartment. It’s Astra’s hug- or, more like, what she had whispered to her and had hidden behind the action. If you have more questions, ask Ona.

 

“Are you alright?” Kara asks, as soon as they walk through the door, “You’ve been quiet.”

 

“Why didn’t you tell me?” she asks back, quietly.

 

Kara frowns, crosses her arms. “I wasn’t aware I should tell you everything.”

 

“Of course you don’t,” she says, “It’s just-”

 

She can’t say it. She really can’t. She can’t look into Kara’s eyes, those eyes that aren’t plagued by anything, no pain, no burdens, no dead planet on her shoulders, and tell her that Krypton is following the recipe that has led it to destruction once before. She can’t say it without mentioning why she thinks it’s all happening- can’t say it without the possibility of losing Kara. 

 

“Nothing,” she sighs instead. She walks over to Kara, uncrosses her arms with gentle fingers and watches as she melts under her touch- crinkle and worrying disappearing from her face. “I just- would have liked to help. We’re a team, aren’t we?”

 

Kara smiles, “That we are, my love.”

 

She cradles her face in her hands, long and strong fingers trace her cheekbones and all fears and questions and worries leave Lena’s body with every bush. This is not her universe, she has to remind herself. It’s not the same. It will never be the same. She has Kara now, in a way she never had before.

 

“This is home, now,” she whispers, winning a giant smile at her admission, “I don’t want anything to happen to it.”

 

“And nothing will,” Kara promises. Gentle. Reassuring. Her voice sounds like the echo of a promise she once heard. “As long as we’re together, everything will be alright.”

 

 

She doesn’t get to talk to Ona. Either because she was too nervous to do so, because she’d not sure she’d like the answer, and because most of the time Kara has been with her- she doesn’t think she’d like it, to see Lena digging more and more on the issue when they’ve decided to put it to rest. 

 

Before she notices Kara is wishing her good luck and saying goodbye in the nicest of ways in the confines of their bedroom. Before she notices she’s flying all the way to the other side of the continent without Kara, the safety blanket she has had since she arrived on Krypton. But there are familiar faces, Seg is there with her, followed by a kind technician, Tai-Un, who seems very excited to work with Lena. 

 

As soon as the skimmer- what Lena had learned are called the kryptonian fancy airplanes- touches the ground, they feel the ground shaking. He hands them all a disc that he instructs them to up on their chests and press, letting a subtle yet strong and heavy suit slowly cover their bodies. Commander Preus walks them through the starts of the construction- the removed soil to level up the ground, the progression of the water and the estimated time for it to reach where they are.

 

Kryptonians work quickly, efficiently. And before she knows it the foundation is done and the bricks, all made with Grahu and by Kara’s team, are arriving with masks and additional gear with them. She spends most of her days overseeing the construction and responding to questions and talking to the workers and trying to convince Tai that ball rooms are not needed inside of dams. The earthquakes usually wake her up, or wake them all up really. They’ve started calling them matutine quakes because they all happened in the mornings and usually lasted through breakfast. They’ve gotten smaller and shorter with time but by the end of month number one, they were all used to them to even let them stop their progress.

She falls. 

 

And falls.

 

And falls.

Dar-Essas growing from every corner Kara could see, taller and brighter than she ever remembers. So tall that they hover over the tables and chairs, giving anyone a chance to enjoy the day out without getting sunburned. 

 

Sunburned. Sun. Rao .

 

She turns until she sees him. Rao. High and bright in the sky. Rao. It wasn’t her tiredness or pain or a side effect of it all that her vision was different- it was him. It was Rao. It’s not an accident that she feels it’s all tinted in red, that it all feels dimmed- calmer. It was him. It was Rao.

The light that was replaced by a brighter, yellow, one- one that holds together her skin but not her soul. Not like Rao does. 

 

She kneels, unaware of her surroundings and the place she’s in, long enough for the square to clear up. She kneels long enough for a Military unit to show up and jank her away from the floor, not giving her a fighting chance. 

 

And when she says her name, the kryptonese leaving her lips feels so robotic, so out of touch, that even she feels it wrong- the man in front of her only scoffs, locks the door of her cell. 

 

“Lady Zor-El is currently at a council meeting.”

 

“You know me?” she asks, unable to move from the bench thanks to one thick silver handcuff. “Do you know Lena?”

 

The guard stops. The red Military Guild sigil shines on his back. He doesn’t turn back but still, he asks. “How do you know Lady Luthor?”

 

“I’m her friend. I’m here to take her back home,” she answers, gulps, suddenly unsure. Suddenly worried they’re not talking about the same person- but she has no time to focus on how he used Lady before Lena’s family name, how that means status and respect. She has no time to wonder, to worry nor to ask something else because the man walks out the room and leaves her alone- only a small glimmer of Rao lighting up the room through a small high window.

 

 

“You're not my daughter,” Alura’s voice is as loud as the gate locking itself behind her, making Kara flinch and sit up from the curl up position she’s been in for hours. She doesn’t know how long but definitely enough for Rao to stop reflecting himself through the small window and rise once again.

 

She clears her throat, “No, I’m not.”

 

Alura stands tall on the other side of the room, two guards watch from outside, quietly. She’s wearing red- clothes similar to the ones Kara vaguely remembers from before . Clothes she used on special occasions. She’s almost tempted to ask what the occasion is.

 

“But you’re my… something,” she notes. Not unguarded, but curious. 

 

“I am Alura's daughter,” she says, sitting up straighter, even when the words do not fill her soul like they used to- before she found out everything her mother did, everything she ruined and everything she knowingly destroyed. “Just… not you.”

 

Alura nods and Kara sees something akin to disappointment in her eyes and before Kara can ask herself why, Alura speaks. “You really are here for Lena.”

 

Kara clenches her jaw. It’s understandable. That if Lena was here that she’d form connections, that people would like her and not want her to leave. Kara knows, Kara understands. She’s the first person to be a victim to Lena Luthor’s charm. But the thought still tastes like bile- it tastes like jealousy that someone else has become Lena’s family and fear she might not want to return to Kara at all.

She holds her cards close to her chest, literally, figuratively. “Who says I didn't?”

 

The woman smirks, almost laughs- Kara wonders if she’s seeing the “stubborn and hot headed El character” that she always complained about to her father when Kara was younger. Alura sits beside her on the bench.

 

“Why are you here?”

 

Kara frowns, “You said it already, I’m here for Lena.”

 

“And why did you come here for Lena?” She points to Kara’s dirty suit and bruised lip, “I assume it wasn’t an easy task.”

 

“I- She’s my-” she frowns. She doesn’t know what Lena is to her. She was her friend. Her best friend. She was her enemy. She’s both sunshine and the clouds. She’s her savior, her landline. She’s the woman that trapped her in a kryptonite cage and stole Myriad but she’s also the woman that saved her from the Phantom Zone- from hell itself. “She’s Lena,” she manages to say, no words enough. Never enough.

 

Alura chuckles, “I feel like I’ve heard that a lot, lately. My daughter seems to never stop talking about her.”

 

The monster creeping inside of her is the same green as Lena’s eyes and she hates it’s there. Is she even allowed to feel this? Does she even know why she’s feeling it? Does she get to feel possessive, protective, over Lena when she’s not anything to her? When she had been the one to take the final step into breaking their relationship? The one that had given up on her? 

 

Because if Lena had destroyed the bridge between them, if Lena had been the one to take a hammer and break it in two, Kara was the one to set it aflame and turned it to dust with a few direct and final words- and now she’s the one praying part of the structure is still standing.

 

She doesn’t have Lena now. She doesn’t have much of anything, if she’s honest. 

 

“We talk?” it’s what she wonders, instead. It’s hard to imagine. Kara talking freely in front of Alura, sharing her excitement and her worries- especially when those days in Argo had been so stilled and so robotic.

 

“Yes.” Alura frowns. “Do we- do you and your mother not?”

 

“We don’t… see each other much.”

 

“Why not?”

 

Because she sent her away. Because she left her alone. Because she left a thirteen year old be haunted by memories of a dead planet and her mother’s own mistakes. Because she never looked for her, even when she was alive. Because thinking her own mother is dead is easier than being in front of her and not being able to scream at her and cry in her arms.

 

“It’s complicated,” she says, clears her throat when it closes up on its own.

 

“Tell me.” Kara thinks she’s about to grab her hand, giving her some comfort. But Alura only reaches forward to uncuff her. “Lena told us about Krypton’s destruction, in your universe. I imagine it was hard to be a family on a new planet.”

 

Kara lets out something between a laugh and a scoff, “Oh, we weren’t a family at all. You sent me away. To save me. I thought you were dead for the longest time.”

 

“But I’m not?”

 

“You’re living in the remains of Argo City, flying through space. It’s hard to reach it and much harder to reach you when you never want to talk about anything serious with me.” She lets out the words more feelings behind them than she intended. But someone is listening. Someone is listening and she feels like she’s confessing to Rao himself while she’s under his gentle rays. “I’m sorry, I shouldn't have dumped this on you,” She says when Alura remains silent, “You just have her face and- Rao… ”

 

“He’s always calming, is he not?” Alura smiles at Kara’s nod and long exhale. 

 

“I had forgotten how much. We always used to kneel on the balcony at home. I used to love praying beside you, feeling your warm arm pressed against mine. Did we- you and her, do that, too?”

 

It’s maybe a little childish that she asks. It doesn’t really mean anything, now. Not when she and her own mother haven’t done it in ages. Not when she’s asking about another version of themselves.

 

“We did. We do.” 

 

And yet. The answer brings her some comfort anyway. Because somewhere, somewhere, that little girl that loved her mom’s warmth stayed beside it until she wasn’t little anymore- unlike Kara, who was ripped away from it and thrown into the cold cold cold darkness.

 

“That’s nice.”

 

She looks away, blinks away the tears and only finds Alura’s eyes again when she calls out her name.

 

“Care to join me?” She’s kneeling on the floor, her face towards Rao and Kara smiles, then follows. Alura’s warmth still feels as nice as she remembers it, even if it’s not the same woman- it comforts her anyway. Whatever she gets from this journey, at least she got this. Just for herself. 

 

“I know I'm not the same woman,” Alura says, “but I know that if your mother is alive then she also wants to talk to you, truly talk to you. And if you’re anything like my Kara, you sometimes need to get angry to be firm, to be fully honest. Let yourself be angry with her once, she’ll not hold it against you- I never do.”

 

 

After her many questions about Lena’s whereabouts go unanswered, even as Alura leads her out of her cell, Kara starts to think her kindness was more the need to know if she was a threat than genuine kindness towards her daughter’s lookalike. It makes the anger flare up inside of her.

 

Alura offers her a shower but, out of fear of someone taking away her suit and her and Lena’s chances to go back home, she refuses- only regretting her choice when the dirt starts to stick uncomfortably on her skin and the small wind coming from the open window makes her shiver over and over. 

 

She’d also, in an attempt to get Kara to stop asking about Lena, made someone deliver a plate of fresh food in front of her. It looks delicious. It smells delicious. It reaches Kara’s nose and lights up a part in her brain that hasn’t been used in so long- that she never thought would light up again. 

 

The cooked iron-beetle, a squishy and big insect, similar looking to a centipede with a turtle-like shell around it, is turned upside down- ready for Kara to dig in with the spoon and eat its flesh along with the purplish fruit Kara doesn’t even remember the name of.

 

It works. Momentarily. It works when Kara looks, baffled and curious, at the food she remembers her aunt loving, once. The food she remembers her aunt complaining it was gone, once. The food she never thought she’d see nor smell again, even when she always thought it looked disgusting when she was a kid.

 

But the food becomes unimportant when her non-super hearing picks up on someone shouting her name. Someone who says it in an accent that’s somewhere between Irish and American. Someone that says it almost with fear and with desesperacion. Someone that could only be Lena.

 

She doesn’t have time to appreciate the way kryptonian clothes look on Lena, how they hug her body in the perfect places and how the house of El’s shade of blue looks so breathtaking on her. She only has time to whisper her name, so thankful she’s okay, so relieved she’s in front of her, before Lena’s body is crashing into hers.

 

“Lena,” she says. Once. Twice. Over and over again. Her arms wrap themselves around her waist, they bring Lena closer than Kara has ever allowed them to, with much more strength she could have ever felt comfortable using yet knowing all her might would not hurt Lena now. 

 

And even if when she breathes in, all traces of Lena’s expensive perfumes are gone from her hair and clothes- it’s still Lena. It’s Lena. There’s nothing to ever complain about when she’s got Lena in her arms. 

 

“I thought you were gone,” Lena says, face still buried in the crook of Kara’s neck and her hand holding onto her red cape as if it’s a landlife. 

 

“I thought you were gone,”  Kara says, “I’m so glad you’re okay, Lena.”

 

Lena pulls away and smiles at her. Smiles. Smiles. That gorgeous smile Kara never thought she’d see directed at her again. 

 

The smile she never thought she’d see again when Lena didn’t answer when she told her she was Supergirl.

 

The smile she never thought she’d see again when Lena told her she always knew- that she had played Kara like Kara had played her.

 

The smile she never thought she’d see again when Lena walked into her portal, out of the fortress of solitude- out of Kara’s life. 

 

The smile she never thought she’d see again when all her plans to get Lena back were futile. When every single universe Mxy showed her ended up in chaos.

 

The smile she never thought she’d see again when she flew away from Lena’s apartment after losing all the fight inside of her and calling her a villain. 

 

The smile she never thought she’d see again when Lex blasted her into the Phantom Zone and the smile she never thought she’d again when Lena was pulled into the black hole’s orbit only to disappear.

 

But the smile is here. It’s directed at her. As warm and vibrant as Kara remembers it. It makes her own reflect in kind- happy, relieved, in love.

 

Someone clears her throat and Lena steps further away from Kara when a gentle hand wraps around her waist, pulling her into them instead.

 

The woman’s suit is perfectly tailored, the shade of blue matching with Lena’s own dress. The house of El sigil stands out in a shining shade of gold- and now, her own suit, always so powerful, always so impressive, lacks all its feeling as it’s dirty and ragged. 

 

It takes her longer than it should have to realize she’s standing in front of her own face. Kara Zor-El in all her glory- the way she never got to be. 

 

“You’re… me,” she says, for lack of better words. 

 

“I would like to think I’m my own person,” she answers. And even their voices don’t sound the same. Manners of speaking. Use of words. Punctuation. Pronunciation. It’s similar but it’s not the same. 

 

“You know what I mean,” she scoffs, crossing her arms.

 

“Do I?” she asks, annoying Kara even more. Is she this annoying? She doesn’t think she’s this annoying.

The protectiveness of Lena. But at the end of the day, every artist adds their own touches, and these two Kara, the longer you pay attention, are not the same.

 

Whatever Kara was going to answer is stopped by Lena. Who places her hand on the arm of the woman beside her and chuckles. “Behave,” she threatens, no real bite on her tone.

“You’re married?” she burst out. “How long have you been here?”

 

Lena sighs, letting her hand fall and the sleeve of her dress cover the bracelet. Kara wants to take her hand in hers, check her wrist and make sure she truly saw a wedding bracelet and pray to find an empty wrist- but by Lena’s small and cautious smile, she knows she assumed correctly.

 

“It’s been almost a year, for me,” she says. 

 

“Wha- but- but it’s only been three months.”

 

Kara, the other Kara, looks curiously at her. “Time passing differently makes sense,” she hums, stepping away from her and Lena and grabbing the closest tablet, typing and reading something Kara cannot seem to care about right now.

 

“You didn’t answer my other question,” she says, looking directly into Lena’s eyes.

 

“Just engaged,” Lena clears up as if it makes it  any better. There’s a small smile on her face, it’s an excited one and Kara hates it, hates it, hates it- even though she shouldn’t.

 

It would be funny, if it wasn’t absolutely depressing. 

 

How is it possible to have seen the person she loves fly away into the unknown, twice? And when she finds them again, they’re bound to someone else, twice ?

 

It’s like destiny and the universe keep laughing in her face. It’s like cupid keeps throwing an arrow in her direction, close enough for her to catch a small wind of what she’s supposed to feel like, before it lands on someone else- and they get the love she wanted.

Kara sighs.

She’s on Kara’s and Lena’s balcony. But not hers. Not herself. Another Kara. The other Kara. 

 

The one that calls the apartment theirs and kisses Lena’s cheek goodbye when she’s called away by one of her projects.

All in the name of not drawing attention. And now, watching her life- looking at the simple food and the science papers from work on the table- she hates it.

Kara sighs, “I’m fine. This is just… a lot.”

 

“Well, the landscape is certainly not National City,” she tries to lighten the mood, again- all to no avail. All because Kara’s mind is too far away to even want to hear a joke, much less when she knows they’re just empty- just Lena wanting to pretend it’s everything alright, like when she’d mentioned Kaznia in the middle of a mission.

 

But how can she joke around, pretend all is fine, when Lena had been thrown into an entire new world- alone, all alone, just like Kara was once- because of her?

 

“Were you okay? Here on your own?” she asks, dreading the answer. Knowing Lena won’t tell her about the hardships just to ease her mind.  

 

“I wasn’t on my own,” she says, and somehow, that’s worse. Because it means there’s no kinship between them, because it means Kara doesn’t know what Lena is feeling. She feels like she hasn’t known her way around Lena’s heart for a long time now. “I had Kara. It shouldn’t surprise me that you’d come to my rescue- even in another reality.”

She hears the apartment’s door open, watches the other Kara walk in with two bags under her arm. Watches the way Lena smiles back at her when she picks up a can of something and excitedly shows it to Lena. It’s utterly domestic. It’s Lena being the softest and most domestic she’s ever seen. And it’s not with her.

But now it’s directed to someone else. And even when that someone else has the same face as her, she feels the green monster inside of her grow and grow and grow. She feels lightheaded. She feels thirsty and full and with a small headache on the back of her mind- she feels like what Alex describes as a hangover. And she hates it.

It’s diamond shaped, a smaller size than her crest- ready to be placed on top of it and be her and Lena’s way back home. “Brainy says it contains the power of a small sun, basically,” she explains. “It should be enough to create a rip in space and send us back to the same black hole that brought us here… Should be enough to go back home.”

 

“You’re going back?” The other Kara blurts out, having made her way to the balcony as Lena inspected the object. Her eyes are round, filled with confusion and a lingering fear. Kara cannot say she doesn’t understand the feeling but the anger creeps inside her anyway. “Sorry,” she breathes out, finding Lena’s eyes, “Can we talk?”

 

“Of course she’s coming back,” Kara interrupts. How dare she keep Lena from going back home?

 

The woman looks at Kara, jaw clenched and a new burning determination mixed with anger in her eyes. “I think that should be Lena's choice, don’t you?”

 

The words only make her angrier. Kara stands taller, thankful that she’s clean now, making her more imponent. She walks closer to her, presses her finger into the woman’s chest, right on the house of El sigil. “Yes. Which is why you need to leave her alone."

It wouldn’t have been the first time Kara has had to deal with another version of herself. But never with one so articulated, one so full of confidence and one that holds the heart of the woman she loves. She cannot punch her way through anything on Krypton and it makes her realize that she’d lost that part of her too- she’d lost the kryptonian side of her that solved arguments with debates and replaced it with americans brute force. 

 

“Stop.” Lena stands between them, placing her hands on two strong arms- making the women relax instantly. Lena looks over to her fiance, all pain in her eyes and says, in a quiet small voice, “I think maybe I should. You know what me being here is causing.”

 

The other Kara shakes her head, refusing to even entertain the thought of what Lena is suggesting, and all Kara can do is watch from the side as the blonde takes Lena’s face between her hands.

 

“We don’t know if it’s you,” she says. Breathless. Desperate. Sad. “Maybe Krypton was always meant to explode. Maybe you being here is exactly what’s needed, because you have the answers, because we’re a perfect team.”

She wants to pull them away, wants to take Lena in her arms and fly away, not give her a chance to be convinced. But watching another version of herself with the woman she loves  is like watching into the mirror of Erised- and the sight in front of her is as captivating as the sight of her parents was to Harry.

 

“Kara- '' Lena's voice breaks, she breathes in and out. “Come on, you know me being here was a mistake”

 

“But it led me to you ,” she chokes out, her fingers shaking even she holds onto Lena’s face. 

 

And it takes a second for Kara to see it. To see the way Lena’s eyes change and her walls crumble. It takes a second to see Lena has changed her mind. It takes a second for Kara’s heart to break when she realizes Lena will not be coming home with her- because her eyes are not looking at the other Kara with pity. The pitiful eyes are directed at her. 

 

The trumbing of her heart feels like a bomb about to go off. Faster and faster and faster. So fast it aches. So fast it makes her whole body feel dumb, as if she’s there but not really there. As if her own body is rejecting the idea of Lena not coming back with her. 

 

The ground shakes and she watches her mirror image fall to the floor along with her. She vaguely hears Lena call out her name before it all turns black- and she hates, hates, hates, that she knows, she didn’t direct it at her when she screamed “Kara” .

A little too late for Lena to be happy, as usual.

“You have to,” Lena says, stepping closer to her and placing the object on Kara’s shaking hands, “There’s no one else here.”

 

“We don’t even know if that’s safe-”

 

“-Kara, please-”

 

“Lena-”

You have to send me away,” she repeats, breathless as tears run down her face. “You’re the only one that can. "I'll like you. I'll love every version of you, in any reality," Lena says.

 

Kara mouthing I love you is the last thing she sees before black surrounds her and something pulls at her entire body. Her stomach drops like in the start of every roller coaster but it keeps going and going and going and it never seems to end. Sparks of green. Sparks of yellow. Sparks of blue. The ticking of the clock picks up and up and up and up until it overtakes Lena’s mind. 

 

Until it ends. And her knees land against the floor painfully. The ticking stops.

She can pretend her day is ending the same way it started, lying beside Kara- but it’s not the same Kara. Not anymore.

 

 

Everything in her body aches. 

 

There’s a throbbing pain in her head. It costs her twice as much as it used to to close her fingers. She keeps coughing and going out of breath. Her skin burns every time she tries to go out into the sun. And her heart aches, although that might be the only one that has a psychological explanation. 

 

The pressure from the black holes. The different gravity pulls between Krypton and Earth. Earth’s polluted air. The once again yellow sun. Brainy had given her a run down of every symptom she has and every new one that might possibly show up in the future. He’d taken charge of her care and had extended offerings of help as her body got back its strength and she could move on her own, no matter how many times Lena had refused them. 

 

Brainy had been the reminder that she does not hate everything about the world she’s back in but an angry and watchful look from Alex from across the room would remind her just how much she was not welcome in the Tower’s medbay. So, the first day she could walk on her own, she left the Tower and pointedly ignored Kara’s room on her way out. 

 

She doesn’t go back to the tower for months. She doesn’t step or drive anywhere close to Catco or Noonas. She doesn’t want to see Kara, no matter how much her heart is asking her to. 

 

It’s not fair. To either of them. 

 

It’s not fair to Kara that she’d be beside her and think and miss someone else. It’s not fair to Kara that Lena wants to fix their relationship when she has no ground to stand on, because even if it feels and was ages ago, her and Kara’s misdeeds are still a solid wall in between them. Kara’s lies. Lena’s reactions. Saving each other from hell and a parallel universe are not the band aids to a broken relationship, no matter how much Lena wants them to be. 

 

It’s not fair to Kara that Lena would want to be friends again after Kara saw how she really feels about her and is probably confused, annoyed, disgusted and everything in between. 

 

It’s not fair to her, either. Because can she really see Kara? When she’ll be thinking about her ex-fiance? Is it fair to her to fix their relationship when it’ll never be the kind of relationship she wants?

 

So, she stays away. She doesn’t focus her mind on things she cannot fix. She focuses on things she can. She turns Luthor Corp to L-Corp again and changes every policy that isn’t perfectly green- in three months, L-Corp becomes the leading company on green energy in and out. 

 

She can’t fix her broken heart. She can’t fix her relationship with Kara. But she can try and fix Earth, make sure it never shakes and breaks the same way Krypton did.

 

She terminates Lex’s leftovers projects, puts old ones on pause and creates new ones, ones that are meant to help. Her desk is back to being full of paperwork like it was on the first days she took over. And her fingers ache because she has had to correct and sign every contract and certification in front of her- instead of aching because she’s the one holding the tools. The one creating instead of approving. 

 

Expecting another pile of papers to be dropped in front of her, she doesn’t look up when she hears the door open. But Jess doesn’t walk closer to her and tells her to go home. Because it’s not Jess at all. 

 

“Kara.”

 

It’s a strange feeling, to miss the sound of her own voice when she says Kara’s name. She went from saying it every morning, every day, every afternoon and every night to not saying it at all- a whiplash to her own vocal cords. 

 

Kara looks nervously at her, a small smile on her lips that’s probably trying to be reassuring. She’s holding a small brown bag in her hands. “I- uh- I brought you some scones, from Ireland,” she says. 

 

Kara had taken her to a bakery once, the only bakery on Argo. She had brought one of everything so Lena could pick her favorite one and they could serve them at their wedding. The sight of Kara- even if not the same one, even when this one is wearing glasses and a white and yellow dress instead of Science Guild clothes- brings tears to her eyes. 

 

She doesn’t want to think about her wedding, or the lack of it. The engagement bracelet no longer hangs from her wrist, knowing it’ll never turn into a marriage bracelet- it's safely guarded by the wood of one of Lena's drawers.

 

“Why?” she ends up asking, clearing her throat to cover up a sob. Kara looks at her confused. 

 

“Brainy says you’ve been working a lot,” she explains, biting her lip, “I just- I thought scones might cheer you up.”

 

Lena sighs, wishing she could stand up to get a glass of scotch. She kind of always needs one when Kara is around- either to calm her heart down or to drown her sorrows. But the little table to the left feels too far away and she knows even attempting to get it would tire her down enough to have to lay down for hours. “I doubt anything can cheer me up,” she confesses, after a long moment. 

 

“Look, I know this is hard but-”

 

“Don’t. You don’t know what’s going on in my mind,” she interrupts and looks away. She doesn’t need Kara and her never ending hope. She doesn’t need a speech about how it’ll all be okay when she has no idea if it will be. She doesn’t need to look at the perfect replica of the woman she left behind. “You have no idea what I lost.”

 

“Actually,” Kara answers, a little bite in her words. “I think I understand it perfectly."

 

She hadn’t really thought of it. Even though it was always right in front of her. She never thought about Kara and her feelings. The desperation and the longing and the nostalgia of having seen her old world again. Of losing Krypton, twice. One to save herself, one by choice.

 

“Right,” she whispers. “I’m sorry."

 

She doesn’t even know what for. For being insensitive? For forgetting about Kara because she was too focused on her own pain? For making her walk away from Krypton again? For almost marrying another version of her?

 

“That’s fine,” she answers, her words sharp and cold as she places the bag on her desk. “Enjoy your scones, Lena.”

 

Kara walks away. Lena doesn’t go after her.

 

 

Kara doesn’t reach out after that. But the messages from Nia and Kelly from day to day are probably prompted by her anyway. It’s not like they had really been her friends before . The only messages she sort of trusts are Brainy and he has been the only one that had understood that her many excuses meant she is only willing to talk about work. 

 

“Can’t go to lunch today, L-Corp is launching a new project.”

 

“I have a conference with the UN tomorrow, raincheck?” 

 

“I’m meeting the Mayor to discuss sun powered subways, maybe another day?”

 

They weren’t dishonest excuses, at first. Nia, Kelly and Brainy had taken them all in stride and had continued to invite her over anyway. But by month number two, it felt like the women were doing it just to absolve themselves of any guilt- just to be able to say they tried. Lena Luthor doesn’t need pity, even if sometimes she would like to have lunch with someone. 

 

But who needs the fake friends that pretended she didn’t exist when she was fighting Kara? Who needs the friends that only try out of guilt, right? Right? But Lena’s justified, she is. Who needs the friends that had welcomed her into the tower just because they needed her to save Kara?

 

After all, Nia had finished the tower’s tour with a big sigh and a “then once Kara is back, we can forget this all ever happened” and Kelly had visited her in the medbay only to mention how much work had been to get Lena back. Because being around Lena was always gonna be temporary. Because saving Lena was work, one none of them wanted to do. 

 

She stops answering the messages at all when she comes to the realization. Nia and Kelly stop trying, eventually. Lena doesn’t know if she’s sad or relieved about it.

 

She only meets up with Brainy, from time to time. Both because he likes to help her in certain projects, a relaxing pastime for the two of them, and because he’s the only one that understands. He mentions how Nia still doesn’t fully trust him, her eyes always plagued with what he did. He complains about how much Alex second guesses him, wondering if he’s having second intentions- no matter how much he reassures her he doesn’t.

 

So, Brainy is the only one she trusts. Because Brainy doesn’t push her to talk about Krypton, but gives her a kryptonian sphere as a present, the kryptonian version of a rubik's cube that she'd seen Kara play and fumble around with in bed- she tears up at the sight of it.

 

Because Brainy discusses Lena’s new project with a childlike excitement in his eyes that reflect the woman’s and he’s not Kara, he’ll never be Kara, but he’s the only one that manages to refute her ideas and help her work through her failed attempts. Because she loves Brainy’s company and he loves to work with her- so much so he ends up splitting his time between the Tower and L-corp.

 

And it should attest to how much she likes her comrade when she forces herself to walk into the Tower after the man convinces her the technology the superfriends possess is the best way to check if their new invention, the small cubicles that can turn plastic back into their core components, work properly. 

 

It’s also the only place that won’t create a three square blast if it fails and the only place Supergirl will be close enough to save them. Not that Brainy mentions that- even if it’s implied.

 

She hopes doesn’t have to run into her. Run into Kara. Not after Kara had tried to be kind and Lena had made her run from the hills with her own sharp words. Not when her own dreams are still plagued with those blue eyes but she doesn’t know to which blonde they belong to- the one she misses to death? Or the one she has in front of her?

 

But, of course, she’s a little too soon, this time, and finds Kara and Alex still on the tower when she walks in. 

 

Alex stares her down, watches carefully as Lena steps out of the elevator. If she didn’t know before, she knows now, that whatever truce Alex and her had formed to get Kara back from the Phantom Zone is long over. 

 

Fake. False. That’s all Alex seems to be with her. Friends when she needs Lena to do something, enemies when she goes out of line. Perhaps it's Alex's military nature why she never fully liked Lena. The nature that considers everything past a certain limit a possible threat and Lena, who has gone above and beyond for certain things, especially Kara, never fit into a non-threat check box.

“Brainy told us you guys were close to a breakthrough,” she says, her hand running up and down her neck in a nervous tick, “sounds… fun.”

 

“Yeah, thanks for letting us use the space,” she says. Silence. Awkward, uncomfortable, terrible silence. It had never been uncomfortable with Kara, the silence. She points to the lab before her starts to eat her nails, “I should- I should go find Brainy.”

“What?”

 

“I mean, it’s obvious,” she scoffs at Lena’s shock. “You won't talk to anyone. You probably miss that girlfriend of yours.”

 

Lena clenches her jaw. She keeps her eyes on Alex’s, cold and firm, and they both ignore Kara glently calling out her sister’s name so it wouldn’t scalate. 

 

“You know I can't go back,” Lena snaps. Brainy and Cisco had explained to everyone the new dangers of interdimensional travels. Lena had come to the realization on her own way before that meeting. “I wouldn’t try it.”

 

“Wouldn’t you?” she pushes. “You seemed to like it there, much more than here.”

 

Alex’s movement is subtle. It’s barely there. But Lena is too focused on her, on not backing down from an argument to not notice the way Alex’s eyes look at Kara momentarily before going back to Lena’s.

 

“What’s that supposed to mean?” she asks, forcefully, fully aware of what Alex wanted to say- fully aware what it means and that she wanted Lena to snap again. 

 

“I just think you care about that other Kara than you do about this one.”

 

“That’s fucking ridiculous,” she does snap. Alex gets what she wants. Lena’s hand tights itself on a fist, even if she knows she can’t do anything about it. 

 

“Is it? You were about to marry her, weren't you? Or was that another plan to break her heart too? Wasn’t enough to break her heart here, had to do it in another universe too?” Alex asks, question after question, all filled with venom. All in the name of Kara, because both Alex and Lena know Kara would never ask Lena this- even if she thinks it.

 

“Shut up-”

 

“What are you up to?” she interrupts, ignoring Lena’s uneven breaths. “You’re still a Luthor, no matter how much you want to change your own mind. Are you still lying?”

 

She half expects Kara to jump in, to tell Alex to stop and make her back off. To defend Lena like she’d done so before many times. But Kara stays quiet and avoids Lena’s eyes when she tries to find them. She guesses she was too used to her knight in shining armor, because when it doesn’t show up, it all feels entirely too dark. 

 

“I’m not lying about anything,” she lets out through clenched teeth and a rapidly beating heart. “I haven’t lied since- I helped you get Kara back-”

 

“-Yeah, out of guilt.”

 

Lena snorts. They’re one to talk. She wills her own heart to calm down, she wills herself to not make the situation worse. 

 

“Same reason you saved me, then,” she answers and Alex doesn’t respond. She finally adverts her eyes. “My project is none of your business,” she ends up saying, finally walking towards the lab. She isn’t stopped this time. “I’m sure you’ll be able to read about it soon enough.”

 

 

Two months after the words “You’re still a Luthor” plague her every nightmare, she calls Kara. Sometimes the words are coming from Alex, sometimes coming from Kara herself, but always right before the ground starts to shake and Lena wakes up in her bed, alone, to the yellow and red of an explosion.

 

She had tried, honestly, to go to Kara. She’d tried to knock on her apartment door but she had quickly told her driver to turn around as soon as she saw the Hope street sign. She’d tried to go to Catco, a bag of potstickers on her hand and all, before she’d seen the elevator and turn around- hands shaking and bag on the ground. 

 

Normally, she wouldn’t even want to make contact with Kara and naturally, she probably will be able to find the information on her own. But part of her heart misses Kara, her laugh, her smiles, their lunches and movie nights, and she wants the excuse to work- at least to hear her voice again. And, she has to admit, that somehow, when her mind wanders around and it finds itself yearning, she’s thinking of ways to get this Kara back and not ways to return to the other one.

“Are you okay?” is Kara’s instant response. Lena grimaces, hating the fact that Kara think she’d only call her if she’s in danger. She sighs, she is calling because she needs help- Kara isn’t too far off.

 

“I’m alright,” she reassures her. “I- Uh- I was wondering if you’ve read Nia’s article?” 

 

Lena had been a little disappointed when Nia had walked through the door as Catco’s representative. The woman had done a wonderful job, she’d shed more light and beautiful words into her and Brainy’s Wall-E’s, as Nia had jokingly called them- a nickname the public had quickly started using too.

“I was wondering if you… knew Aquaman?” she asks, uncertain. 

 

“What? What does he have to do with your invention?” she chuckles. “You miss Argo and are now trying to see every other wondrous world? Wanna find Atlantis?” She tries to laugh, but the joke falls short- because there’s a hint of fear that it might be true in Kara’s words.

 

Lena clenches her jaw. “I can find him on my own. Thank you, Kara,” she snaps. 

 

“No. Lena, wait-”

 

She hangs up the phone. 

 

She doesn’t need Kara doubting her actions just like her sister. She can find Aquaman on her own. She can directly ask him for help, instead of doing it through Supergirl.

She spends a couple hundred of dollars to get old DEO files on him, another few on updating a facial recognition software for it to be actually useful and stop matching Aquaman’s face to an A-list movie actor.

 

Finding his name was much easier to find him, too, it turns out. And, in the end, it was his good deeds that made her find him- like every good hero, it was the need to help that exposed him the most.

He’s sitting by the bar, inside a cold pub in the coldest town Lena has set foot on- she doesn't know how he can be drinking a cold beer. He looks ridiculously big on top of the tiny stool.

 

She can feel the stares on her as soon as she opens the door- a town this small, any foreign is meant to gather attention. But she’s sure they’re wondering why someone like her, with a coat twice her size and still shivering, is there. 

 

Arthur looks her up and down as she sits beside him and asks for a scotch, anything that would warm her. Then, he pointedly ignores her. Even when she clears her throat, getting the attention of two men sitting a couple of feet away, but not his. 

 

“I wanted to speak to you,” she says, managing to get him to look at her. He tilts his head and raises his eyebrows, expecting her to continue, “alone.”

She’s seen pictures of Aquaman, especially once she had set herself on finding him, but she never thought he’d be so… similar to what she saw. He’s not like Kara. He doesn’t make himself bigger once he’s in the suit, he’s already big. He doesn’t take on a slightly different personality. He doesn’t even seem to care how easily it’d be for someone to realize who he is- she guesses that’s part of the point. 

 

The more yourself you are, the less people would think that the guy that drinks cold beer on a bar that smells like rotten fish is the same one that has teamed up with the rest of the Justice League multiple times.

 

“So?” he asks when Lena spends a minute too long staring at his tattoos- the bubbling curiosity to ask what they meant on the tip of her tongue. Does he just like them and don’t mean anything? Are they related to Atlantis? Do they represent his status there?

 

“Right. Uh-” she clears her throat. She goes through her bag, grabs the pictures in it and prays she won’t get instantly thrown out of the bar right into the freezing sea. “I know who you are.”

 

She places the picture of Aquaman on the table first, follows it with a picture of him- side by side, it’s even more obvious. But that’s not what Lena cares about. She takes out picture after picture of the tsunami that affected every shore on Earth, the one that scientists could not explain and the one that had sent every conspirator down a rabbit whole. The same tsunami that had made every little piece of garbage and waste land on the beaches. 

 

Arthur looks at her curiously, “I don’t think I heard your name,” he says instead of giving Lena a good threat and making her run for the hills. 

 

“I’m Lena Luthor.”

 

“L-corp,” he chuckles. She’d heard that type of laugh enough times in her life by now, it’s the laugh men use when they are confused- confused she’s in front of them because they think she doesn’t matter. The laugh that tells her that they either hate her because they used to agree with her brother and she helped put him in prison, or because her brother had ruined their lives in some way. “-Oh I don’t give a shit about your brother,” he interrupts, laughing a little. And Lena is surprised to find there’s truly no hate in his eyes. “I’m just very curious why the woman restoring the seaflora on two different coasts is looking for me.”

Continues when he sees that Lena isn’t backing down, “That took an insane amount of power to do and it made people whine about dirt on their beaches for years.”

“Why?” he asks, after a while.
