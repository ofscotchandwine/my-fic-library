New Year's Eve, 2009

Kara breathed in the cool Midvale air, enjoying the evening chill with her hot cocoa in hand. She was wrapped in a warm blanket that she didn’t need, gazing out into the darkening fields from the back porch of the Danvers home.

She had just finished her first semester at National City University. Alex was visiting from med school at Stanford, the two sisters happy to spend some quality time with Eliza over the holidays. Midnight was just a few hours away, and the family didn’t feel up to partying - they opted for a quiet night at home, with glasses of champagne to ring in the new year.

Kara glanced to her right, eyes landing on Kenny’s old telescope, which was set up in the corner of the porch. Eliza has been taking good care of it, Kara thought warmly.

She rose to her feet, setting her mug down before shuffling over to the telescope, her hand drifting lightly over the outer casing. I used to look at stars more, she thought. National City was always so bright, even at night - drowning out the Milky Way above. 

Kara glanced at the eyepiece and removed the caps, swiveling the telescope slightly to a region of the sky that she knew all too well. As she turned a dial to focus the telescope, a warm red light crispened in her view. Rao, she thought, her heart twinging as she examined the red star from two thousand lightyears away.

Krypton was long gone, but she cherished seeing the soft light that used to cascade across her planet. I miss you, she thought solemnly, mulling over how her life had changed. So much has happened since then…

Five years. She had spent just over five years on Earth. Adjusting to her new strange powers, adjusting to an alien culture, even finding a sense of home. She still felt adrift in certain ways - without a sense of purpose. The expectation to join the Science Guild had defined her childhood, but there was no longer a Science Guild to join.

She thought of her baby cousin, of the ease he had in his life. His powers posed complications, sure. But he was as human as anyone else on the planet, and had found purpose in both his reporting and when he took the cape. But years ago, the FBI had demanded she not follow that path. Rao, Kara thought, remembering the face of the agent that had visited, she looked so much like my mother.

Kara sent a small prayer to the heavens - gem i :divi, in your light - before lifting her head from the telescope, replacing the caps. It was the new year, after all. A time of beginnings and fresh starts. Though Kara would always look back, she had to look forward too. There had simply been no other option in the past five years of her life on Earth.

She was pulled from her thoughts as she heard the door behind her open - knowing it was Eliza before she turned, sensing the familiar heartbeat.

But she frowned. Eliza’s heartbeat was unusually fast - and Alex’s heart, somewhere in the living room, was also racing. More than that… there was a third heartbeat inside.

“My name is Hank Henshaw,” the man said, holding up his badge. “Of the FBI. You’ve spoken to one of my agents before.”

Kara’s eyes narrowed on the man. “Okay,” she said, “I haven’t been using my powers-”

“I’m not here for that,” Hank replied.

A crinkle appeared on Kara’s brow, as she glanced to Eliza and Alex - who she noticed were looking back at her. Not with confusion, but with uncertainty - and a bit of sadness. What’s going on?, Kara thought, realizing they already knew. “Is it Kal-”

“Your cousin is fine,” Hank said. “But there is news you should hear.”

Hank shuffled into his bag, removing a small metal sphere - no larger than an orange. Kara’s eyes widened at the silver lining, etched with familiar symbols. “A translight gateway,” Kara said, reaching out her hand to hold it. “Where did you get this?”

“It fell into our atmosphere a few days ago,” Hank said.

“What does it do?” Alex asked.

“It can transport radio waves faster than the speed of light. Krypton used to send these out for long-distance communication,” Kara said, examining the satellite closely. “The charge on this one is dead, it’ll take a few weeks-”

“We were able to make contact with an alien civilization before the satellite died,” Hank said.

Kara glanced up. “What are you talking about?”

“They are on an asteroid, about five lightyears from Earth, orbiting the red dwarf star Proxima Centauri.”

“There are no civilizations around Proxima Centauri-”

“The inhabitants were familiar with multiple Earth languages,” Hank said, cutting her off. “I was able to speak to a man claiming to be Jul-Us of Argo City.”

Kara felt as though her heart had stopped. He spoke to Jul-Us? That's impossible. “What are you saying?” she asked.

“Argo City survived,” Hank said. “Unfortunately your father was lost in the destruction of Krypton. But he was able to construct a large dome to spare most of the residents, including your mother.”

Kara’s eyes drifted back down to the unpowered satellite in her hand, as her heart raced in her chest. My people are alive?, she thought. My mother, my city-

“Much of Krypton’s technology was lost in the blast,” Hank continued. “They have spent decades rebuilding-”

“I want to see my mother,” Kara said, glancing up at Hank. “I- I need to go back-”

“And you can,” Hank said. “I have received an exemption from the FBI to give you back your pod. Argo does not have the capability for interstellar travel, albeit they may be able to rebuild faster if they have your pod as a template.”

I have to go back, Kara’s thoughts raced, I have to go back, I can go back, I can see my mother-

But somewhere in the fog, Hank’s words cut through. If Argo didn’t have the capability for interstellar travel - if Argo needed Kara’s pod as a template - then getting back to Earth wouldn’t be an easy task. The city would need to take apart her pod to study it, to replicate it. With the destruction of Argo’s factories, new pods would need to be built by hand…

She couldn’t know how long it would take for her to return to Earth.

Kara glanced up again - not at Hank, but at Eliza and Alex - both who eyed her with painfully understanding eyes. What if I can’t see them for years?, she thought, feeling her heart pound in her chest as the confusion swirled in her mind. What if I never see them again? 

The walls around her felt somehow narrower, the room feeling slanted and strange. She was dimly aware that her now-sweaty hands had dropped the satellite on the floor, and that there were tears streaming down her face.

She couldn’t breathe, couldn’t think, couldn’t process Eliza calling her name. Kara backed away towards the porch door, fighting the squeezing feeling in her chest, I can’t do this, I can’t do this-

Without another word, she rushed outside, and disappeared into the night.

It was odd, in a way, how the powers that had once made her feel so alien in her own body were her only outlet in that moment. Taking to the skies for the first time in years, Kara soared east in the black night, chasing the darkness on the horizon until it became a subtle shade of red.

She couldn’t breathe. She couldn’t breathe. What if this had been years ago?, she wondered, thinking back to the last time the FBI visited. Why did it have to be now?

Because if this were years ago, in the aftermath of Kenny’s death, the decision would’ve been easy. Alex hadn’t been her sister yet. The Danvers house hadn’t yet become her home. The blue and green planet she had landed on wouldn’t fill her heart with warmth. They were strange aliens under a strange yellow sun.

But now they’re family, Kara thought, now Earth is my home, too.

Kara could feel the agony rise in her chest. She ached to see familiar spiraling towers, to listen to the traditional wind chimes, to hear kryptonese spoken casually in the streets. But most importantly of all, I want to hug my mother again.

She knew her decision was made, and yet she hated herself for making it. But her people - her mother - were alive. And more than that, they needed her pod, would benefit from the technology it held. Who knows how long before we can rebuild one, Kara thought, who knows how long it would take before I could visit Earth again…

But to help her homeland rebuild? To bring back a piece of lost technology, to perhaps bring some human technology along… it would be rudimentary by kryptonian standards, but if she could bridge the gap, help reconstruct a society that had almost died? Is this what destiny has in store for me?

She thought of her mother, drifting alone on a rock in space - aware that somewhere out there, her daughter lived. She thought of Eliza and Alex at home, at the separation this would bring, knowing that any communication she had with them for years to come would be little better than Morse code.

There was a bittersweet recognition in her heart, how lucky she was to call two places home - to have two families to love. There are worse problems than this, she thought to herself, brushing away at her tears.

The answer was clear. It had been clear the moment the words left Hank’s mouth. She just needed to admit it to herself.

I want to go back.

Dawn had broken in Midvale by the time Kara landed on the porch. She had the surreal realization that it was the new year, and she was still in her pajamas - that her mug of hot chocolate still sat near Kenny’s telescope, long cold in the aftermath.

She could hear Eliza inside, apparently having stayed up all night - she was waking Alex. “She’s back,” Eliza whispered through the walls, followed by the sound of Alex rushing to throw her blankets off.

Kara swallowed harshly.

Walking inside, she saw them both coming down the stairs. It was Alex that ran to her, throwing her arms around her. “I’m sorry,” Kara choked, fighting the tears that were starting to overwhelm her.

“We understand,” Alex said, and Kara melted as Eliza threw her arms around her daughters.

“I love you both,” Kara said.

“We love you too,” Eliza said.

“You better write often,” Alex said, “Every day.”

“I will,” Kara promised.

The Danvers family watched as the FBI loaded small box after small box into Kara’s pod, shoving what they could behind Kara’s seat, lining the sides, anywhere they could find.

“You think it’ll help?” Alex wondered out loud.

Kara shrugged. “It’s like if you were transported to the Middle Ages. Maybe you know how a car engine works, but that doesn’t mean you can manufacture one.”

“It’d be difficult,” Eliza said, “With so few resources, building up that infrastructure again will take decades.”

“This’ll help,” Kara said softly. 

The final boxes were loaded, and Kara turned to the other two women, giving them hugs and promises to return. The FBI still had the satellite, which would be recharged in weeks. With an agreement from Hank, the three women would be able to use it for communications until Kara someday returned.

Kara stepped over to her pod, lowering herself slowly into the seat. Part of her hated being in the cramped space - even worse this time, with the loaded boxes of technology. Her heart ached, not for the first time, for the world she was leaving behind.

Alex stepped forward, handing Kara a small wooden box. Kara couldn’t take many personal effects on this trip - her clothes, her books, her art, everything that had defined the last five years of her life would stay in Midvale. But this box, to sit in her lap, contained what it could - trinkets and photos, reminders of her life as a human.

Kara glanced up at Alex, as Eliza wrapped a comforting arm around Alex’s shoulder. We’ll be okay, Kara told herself.

She’d be back. It was only a matter of time. 

Kara’s heart pounded as the shield closed above her, knowing that soon she would be hurtling into the dark. But as Eliza and Alex waved goodbye, as the engines rumbled to life, Kara thought of her mother waiting for her on the other side - and the society she could help restore.

It’s time to go home.

Five Years Later

Dust and darkness and crumbling walls. Rao, are you there?

It had been years since anyone besides Kara had stepped into the temple - even by the time Kara had arrived back on Argo, the temple had long gathered dust. Most of the doors were blocked by the debris from Krypton’s destruction. Old hymnals lay on the floor, art hung crooked on the wall, broken wind chimes flanked the altar.

She could remember the place from her childhood. She could remember the candles burning in the nights as they celebrated the new year. She could remember the spring equinox, when all the doors would be propped open, allowing the breeze to cause the wind chimes to sound. But there was no breeze within the protective shield of the asteroid. Were we spared for a reason?

“Kara,” came a voice behind her.

She felt a shudder from Lena’s body, realizing that her heartbeat had ticked upwards, causing Kara to pull her closer. It was as Lena’s face pressed into Kara’s shoulder that Kara realized there were tears. “Lena?”

“I thought I would lose you,” Lena murmured.

“I’m here now,” Kara said, pulling her closer still and nuzzling into her hair, “I’m here.”

A soft sob emanated from Lena’s chest, causing Kara to pull back slightly, leaning down to kiss away the tears on Lena’s cheek, caressing and crooning until Lena’s breath slowed again. She lost a lot, too, Kara thought, feeling a guilt in her chest, we’ve all lost so much in recent days.

Lena looked up in the dark, eyes wide and beautiful, hands drifting up to cup Kara’s face. But we’re going to get through this, Kara thought, together.

Lena pulled Kara in, and their lips met.

Lena woke to the smell of coffee.

She could hear small shuffling sounds, quiet in the bright light of the morning. She stretched out onto the bed, breathing deep with the sleep she had gotten, relieved and renewed.

She could hear Kara setting something down, walking across the room to the kitchen, no doubt springing into action at hearing Lena’s heartbeat. Lena smiled as the blonde entered the bedroom, carrying a mug of coffee. “Good morning,” Kara murmured, passing her the mug. 

“Good morning,” Lena said, gratefully taking the coffee.

They sat in peaceful silence, as Lena sipped at her drink, as Kara looked out the window to the city side streets. Lena could hear the distant chirping of birds, the odd passing of cars. “Do you want to open the window?” Lena asked, “Let the breeze in?”

“Soon,” Kara said, turning towards Lena.

Lena looked curiously at Kara, whose expression was… a tinge sad. Perhaps far too forlorn. Oh, Lena thought, preparing herself for what was coming.

“I’m sorry about Lex,” Kara said, “I’m- I’m so sorry.”

Lena leaned over, placing her hand on top of Kara’s. “It wasn’t your fault.”

“You’re hurting,” Kara said, “I never meant to hurt you.”

“You didn’t hurt me, Kara,” Lena said, “He made those choices for himself.”

Kara bit anxiously at her lip. “I don’t think you’re…”

“Processing?” Lena said wryly, “There’s been a lot going on.”

Kara sighed, turning towards Lena, taking her hand and raising it to her lips. “Is there anything I can do to help?” Kara asked.

Lena smiled softly. “You already are.”

Kara smiled back.

The two rose from the bed, making their way into the living room. Lena found what Kara had been doing that morning - there were paints and brushes sitting in the corner, along with a fresh canvas, on a brand new easel. Kara had already painted the base layer, a swirl of burning red disappearing into a soft blue.

“I didn’t know you painted,” Lena said, taking a seat on the couch. 

“I haven’t before,” Kara murmured, as she hovered up to open a window, before floating back down, “I wasn’t allowed to on Krypton.”

“You bought this at 6am?” Lena asked.

“It’s 9am in Boston,” Kara replied as she took a seat.

Lena curled up next to Kara, feeling the relief of being wrapped in Kara’s arms again. They sat, enjoying the soft ringing of the chimes, and it occurred to Lena that this was a first for them. There were no experiments to run, no alien disappearances to investigate, no world to save.

For just one moment, they were able to slow down.

Kara sighed, dropping her head into Lena’s shoulder. “I don’t want to be rage anymore,” she said quietly.

“What do you want to be?”

Kara mulled in silence for a moment. “I’m not ready to be her yet.”

“A lot has happened,” Lena said, “You don’t need to rush. I think we both just need to step away from our lives for a while.”

Kara pulled Lena in tighter, sighing in thought. Lena turned her head up, noticing the pensive expression on Kara’s face. She tilted her head, asking Kara a silent question.

“I think I want to go back to Argo for a while,” Kara said, “If you’re up for it.”

Lena’s eyes widened, and she slowly smiled.

It didn’t take long for Lena to shut down LuthorCorp. She formed a new foundation, one that would mostly be able to run in her absence - supporting medical research and humanitarian work. Kara mused that perhaps, someday, the Luthor name would be defined by her.

They both talked about the vague future in which they’d return to Earth - rearching cancer or viral illness or microplastic poisoning, keeping people from dying of deadly disease. Perhaps, someday, they’d be ready.

But for the time being, Kara packed up her art supplies and clothes, and Lena subleased her condo. They finally landed on Argo.

“My daughter,” Alura said, pulling Kara into her arms with a small sob, “Rao, I’m glad you are here.”

“Me too,” Kara said shakily.

Alura pulled Lena in as well. “Thank you for saving her,” she said, squeezing tightly, “Kal tells me it was your idea to use the harun-el.”

Lena smiled shyly.

“Come,” Alura said, finally loosening her grip and gesturing behind her. “Lois is preparing dinner. She is introducing us to an ancient Earth method.”

“Ancient Earth method?” Lena asked.

“Named after Bar Bee-Que.”

Kara and Lena grinned.

Life on Argo was, finally, calm.

There were occasional tensions, moments when Kara would meet someone she had hurt in her red lantern haze, or a child of someone who had died in the blast. While most on Argo didn’t blame her - the community instead wrestled with how kryptonians could create such a device in the first place - it took a while for Kara to process the panic that it would bring.

She took time in solitude, often painting stunning sights - of Earth, of the stars, of the debris of Krypton’s destruction, of Argo and reflections of its past. The asteroid was still far from the Krypton of the past, without its connection to its religion, bathed in artificial white under the protective dome. Still, it made Kara smile when Lena figured out how to create a breeze in the structure, bringing back a more natural feeling to the atmosphere.

The pair slowly took up experiments again. They optimized harun-el usage, revived lost agriculture, stabilized old damaged buildings - including the sanctuary to Rao. It meant something to Kara, and sometimes she even found other kryptonians in the sanctuary again.

Lois became pregnant, extending her and Kal’s stay - it was safer to not be under the yellow sun. Kal still stumbled through learning kryptonese, his accent never quite right. “You sound like you learned kryptonese from a book,” Kara would joke.

Alex and Eliza later visited for a couple of weeks, taking in the sights that Kara had frequently described in childhood. It made Kara marvel at the strange blend of her family, at the wonderful shape it made.

It was at dinner one evening - with Kal and Lois watching, with Alex and Eliza visiting, with Alura informed in advance of certain Earth customs - that Kara kneeled in front of Lena and pulled out a ring and bracelet. Lena, of course, said yes.

One day - as Kara held her fiancee’s hand, strolling through the outdoor marketplace to the quiet sanctuary at the end - she heard an ancient sound. She glanced up, finding clay bells, swaying in the breeze.

Three years passed when the rumors started.

A figure who appeared as a plane was about to crash. The figure appeared again after an earthquake, and then again after a tsunami. As alien distrust from years ago started to calm, people could only speak of the blonde woman in the cape as someone they were grateful to see. Perhaps there was some familiarity from the almighty S she wore on her chest. 

Names like Supergirl and Superwoman were passed around, but it was Lois Lane who spoke to the new kryptonian first. The mysterious figure was Superman’s cousin, the reporter said, but this hero preferred a different moniker: Shahrrehth.

The woman wouldn’t reveal what it meant - neither would Superman, as he reemerged on Earth a few months later. It simply wasn’t his answer to give.

All the woman would say is that it was the virtue that had saved her. And now, perhaps, it might save others.
