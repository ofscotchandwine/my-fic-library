1. Chapter 1

Author's Note:

I've watched entirely too much Stanley Tucci in Italy and this is what happened...

It was starting to get dark, dark and cold. Okay, so maybe storming off into the woods by herself, in a different country no less, wasn’t the brightest idea she’d ever had. Kara sighed. She hoped that if she could get to a little clearing in the trees she could maybe get her bearings. All she needed was to be able to see that church tower, the main landmark of the town she was staying in.

So that was the plan for now: get to the clearing, see the church, head in that direction. And with a little luck she’d be back in the little vacation rental before midnight. Maybe she’d make it in time before Mike even noticed that she was gone.

Mike. Ugh. Just thinking about him made her pick up her speed, stomping harder against the soft forest path under her feet. He was such an idiot!

At first, he didn't want to come to Italy at all.

“It’s a little cliche, no?” he had said. “A little too ‘Eat, Pray, Love’. Isn’t that something for when you hit your midlife crisis?”

It had taken Kara months to convince him, to explain that she simply wanted to see the country, to maybe find a connection to parts of her family she only recently learned about.

“I know you’re not adopted, so maybe you don’t understand why I need to do this. I didn’t even know that my birth mother’s family is from Piedmont. And I know it’s unlikely that I’ll find anyone there who’s still alive, who’s related to me, but I still need to see it.”

After months of explaining and begging, Mike had finally relented. “Okay, but only if I get to rent a scooter and eat all the pizza I want.”

And rent a scooter he did. In fact, he rented it and drove away with the woman from the scooter rental business, presumably just to “get a lesson”, but when Kara walked down a picturesque small alley of the town hours later on her way to find some gelato, she found Mike and the woman leaning against the old stone wall of the church getting an entirely different kind of lesson.

That’s when Kara stormed off, walking and walking, until the town was far behind her and all she could see was trees, golden and red leaves covering the forest floor on a beautiful fall afternoon.

And now here she was, lost in that very same patch of forest, and it was getting so dark that for a moment, Kara wondered if she would even be able to see the church tower now once she got to the clearing. She knew she had no choice but to try, so she kept walking, even though she struggled to keep going as the strain of the day caught up with her. Her feet ached, her legs were sore. Apparently slip-on Vans weren’t a great choice for a prolonged hike. She was also only wearing a thin cotton sweatshirt and a pair of jeans, no jacket. That had been perfectly sufficient in the sun-drenched town earlier that day. Now, not so much.

It was then that she heard a rustling noise to her left. She stopped to listen more closely. At first she thought it was maybe just the wind moving through the fallen leaves. But when the noise didn’t stop and appeared to be coming closer and closer, Kara froze.

She knew from her research about Piedmont that this area had a few wild animals, mainly boar, and that they could be quite aggressive. She looked around, trying to make a plan for what she would do if she indeed came face to face with one. She knew she wouldn’t be able to outrun it, but maybe she could climb a tree? One of the trees behind her seemed sturdy enough that it should hold her weight and it had a few long-hanging branches that would allow her to climb up if needed.

The rustling noise came closer and closer and Kara walked backwards toward said tree, just in case. Her heart was thumping wildly, her hands felt sweaty despite the cold evening air. She listened and trained her eyes in the direction of the noise, straining to see in the fading light. Her mind flashed back to images of boar she had seen on the internet, but she had no real concept of how big they might be. Pigs could get very big, she knew that, so she had no doubt that she would face off with some large monster in a few seconds.

Staring straight ahead, her eyes slowly adjusting to the dim conditions, she told herself she could handle whatever was about to happen. She always did. She was a survivor. And then, with the rustling louder than before, a dog came running around the bend toward her.

Kara let out a big breath and pressed a shaking hand against her chest in relief.

“Hi buddy,” she said as the dog, a friendly little Border Collie, barked a little and greeted her with some enthusiastic tail wagging, “you have no idea how glad I am to see you.”

She bent down and scratched the dog behind the ear, willing the adrenaline in her system to dissipate. She partially succeeded until —

“Who the hell are you? And what are you doing in my part of the woods?”

Kara jumped to her feet, startling the dog, and turned toward the angry voice. Based on the tone, she expected some gruff old Italian woman and she tried her best to remember the few Italian words she had learned for this trip, but when she spotted a young woman who was all sharp jaw and proud posture eying her suspiciously, all words died on her tongue.

“Um,” she stammered, her hand still scratching the dog who was sitting next to her. “I’m lost.”

The woman raised an eyebrow and scoffed. “Ah. Let me guess. American tourist out for a walk and you couldn’t read the signs for how to get back to town?”

Kara felt some annoyance bubble up in her stomach. She’d done her best, with a lot of research and preparation, to not come across as the typical American tourist. She wasn’t just out for some leisurely walk after eating too much pasta. She’d had a life-changing afternoon and needed to clear her head. This woman knew nothing about her or why she was here.

“American, yes, but I can read the signs, thank you very much. Love being judged by someone with some posh English accent who clearly isn’t from here either!”

As soon as the words left her mouth, Kara regretted them. She knew this was the adrenaline speaking, and maybe also her overall frustration with her day, with her trip, with Mike, with her life. This stranger didn’t deserve to have her head bitten off like that.

“I’m sorry,” Kara mumbled, “that was rude. I just, this has been a supremely shitty day and all I want is to get back to town so I don’t have to sleep out here in the woods.”

The woman tilted her head and regarded Kara for a moment, before she turned on her heel and let out a loud whistle, prompting the dog to follow her. Kara stood frozen in her spot, unsure what to make of the interaction, but a few seconds later the woman looked back over her shoulder.

“Well, if you want to make it back into town, don’t just stand there. I’ll walk you back to the road.”

The words were still a little gruff, but there was also some amusement in her tone now, and despite feeling a little dazed by the entire encounter, Kara quickly shuffled along the path to follow the woman and her dog.

They walked in silence for the most part, even though Kara was dying to ask the woman who she was and what she was doing here. But Kara wasn’t stupid. Her work as a reporter had taught her to read people, to know when to press and ask questions, and to know when to stay quiet and observe. This woman’s body language practically screamed “leave me alone”. She didn’t look at Kara and instead focussed on the path ahead, taking fast sharp steps that made it clear she wasn’t someone who lingers. Occasionally, she seemed to be shaking her head a little and Kara assumed that had something to do with her current predicament. But the woman didn’t say anything, kept her body turned away from Kara as much as possible on the narrow path, and clearly simply wanted to get this all over with as fast as possible.

Walking alongside her, Kara took a moment to really look at the woman. She was dressed for the weather, more so than Kara was, in a greenish parka and a thick long scarf that was wrapped around her neck. There were strands of long dark hair peeking out under a wool hat that looked homemade, along with a matching pair of fingerless gloves. She also had some sort of bag slung around her shoulder, which made Kara wonder what was in it and why she was carrying it with her on a walk in the woods.

Eventually, the path widened and the trees disappeared. Kara could now clearly see the church and the road leading into town, and she felt so relieved she almost wanted to cry. As if sensing the shift in Kara, the woman stopped and faced her, eyes scanning Kara curiously.

“I assume you can find your way from here?”

Kara nodded, feeling a little too emotional to speak.

“Are you going to be okay?” the woman asked, her voice much softer now than anything she’d said to Kara so far.

Kara nodded again. “Yeah,’ she said, her voice a little croaky, “thank you. Sorry, I’m not normally like this,” she waved at her own face and the tears she could feel spilling down her cheeks already. “I just, I’ve had the worst day, so it’s all a little much, but I can find my way back now. Thank you so much.”

The woman studied Kara some more before she said:

“It’s still quite a long walk into town. I live just up the hill from here. Why don’t you come for a glass of wine, and to warm up by the fire for a bit. And I’ll drive you into town after that.”

Kara was completely surprised by the offer and she knew it was written all over her face. She hesitated.

“I promise I’m not an axe murderer,” the woman said, her tone both serious and amused at the same time. “But I also understand if you just want to get back into town. I could still drive you. I just have to get my car.”

“No,” Kara stammered, “I mean, yes. Yes to the wine, and a fire sounds heavenly right about now, to be honest. But, I don’t want to inconvenience you more than I already have.”

“You’re not. You just look like you really need a drink.”

Kara laughed a little. “Yeah.”

“Alright. Come on, then. It’s just up the hill.”

They followed the narrow country road for another 15 minutes before Kara spotted a small, rustic stone house nestled into the hill. Even in the dark, it looked like it was out of some Tourism Italy brochure. It was old, clearly, but well maintained and looked cozy and inviting, with a small garden at the side and a beaten down old car sitting in the small driveway.

The woman led them up a narrow little stone path towards the door when she stopped abruptly.

“What?” Kara asked, slightly alarmed at the sudden change. “Something wrong?”

“No, no,” she said, “I just realized I never even introduced myself. I’m so sorry! I guess I’m a little rusty when it comes to proper social etiquette.”

“Oh. Well. I assume you weren’t really expecting a run-in with a stranger in the woods.”

The woman chuckled a little. “That too, but still. I apologize. I’m Lena. And this,” she nodded over at the dog sitting beside her now, “this is Patrick.”

She held out her hand, fingerless gloves and all, and Kara took it.

“Nice to meet you both. I’m Kara.”

“Well, Kara, let’s go inside and get you that drink.”

With that, Lena unlocked her door and pushed on inside, switching on the lights as she went. Inside, the house looked simple but comfortable. The small kitchen and dining area were open to the living area, which mostly consisted of a couch and more books than Kara had seen in a long time. There was a hallway toward the back which Kara presumed led toward a bedroom of sorts.

Lena pulled out a chair at the kitchen table and motioned for Kara to sit.

“I’ll get the fire going. Why don’t you sit down? I assume you’ve done more walking today than you’re typically used to.”

Kara laughed a little. While she was definitely athletic and fit, her workout regime at home usually involved weight training at the gym and some spin classes for cardio. She was definitely not much of a hiker. As soon as she sat down, she could feel the exhaustion hitting her like a ton of bricks.

“Yeah, that was a little much,” Kara admitted.

A few minutes later, Kara could hear the telltale sounds of a fire crackling in the little wood stove in the corner of the room. Lena went into the kitchen to grab a bottle of wine and a couple of glasses and brought them over to the table. She poured them both a healthy amount before she scooted back into the kitchen to grab a plate, two big pieces of cheese, some bread and a knife, which she set on the table between them.

“You’re probably hungry after all that,” Lena said as she pushed the plate closer to Kara.

“Thank you,” Kara said a little sheepishly. She hadn’t expected food, or anything really, but she was grateful. The last meal she’d had earlier that day was a small breakfast, wanting to save her appetite for a nice dinner with Mike.

Mike. Merely thinking of him threatened to open the floodgates again, and Kara struggled to swallow down a piece of bread as she tried to compose herself.

Lena got up and went back to the kitchen, only to return a minute later with two shot glasses and a bottle of something that looked homemade.

“Here,” she poured the pale gold liquid into one of the glasses and held it out to Kara. “Trust me, it’ll help.”

Kara nodded and took a swig. It was strong, and slightly herbal tasting. Kara grimaced a little at the unexpected flavour and burn, but as soon as she felt it warm up her throat and stomach, she smiled.

“Thank you. Again.”

“It’s no problem,” Lena said as she took a good sip of her drink as well.

They sat in silence for a moment, and Kara wasn’t sure if she should make small talk, or explain why she was here and what led to her rather unfortunate adventure in the woods.

Once again, Lena seemed to sense Kara’s struggle and leaned back, paying some attention to Patrick, who was angling for food scraps or pets or both.

“Sometimes the best thing to do with a shitty day is to remember that it will pass. Patrick here taught me that. We should be a little more like dogs sometimes.”

Kara hummed.

“Well, maybe without the rolling in poop or dead things part,” Lena added with a small laugh.

Snorting, Kara held out her glass. “I’ll drink to that.”

After that, the conversation between them felt less awkward. They didn’t talk much. Kara mostly complimented Lena on her place and how homey it felt, and Lena chided Kara for being entirely too trusting of strangers who invite her to their home without even knowing their name. For the first time in a long time, Kara felt herself relax, which also meant that she felt increasingly tired.

Lena must have noticed because not much later, she brought over a glass of water and said: “I should drive you back into town. I’m sure whoever you’re with is worried by now.”

Kara huffed. “Unlikely. But yes, I should probably get back.”

It didn’t take long once they both folded themselves into Lena’s tiny car. The drive was short and quiet, neither of them saying much. Kara told Lena to just drop her off right at the church, that her vacation rental was just around the corner from there.

Before she got out of the car, Kara turned to face Lena.

“Thank you so much for everything. For getting me out of the woods, for the drinks and food. I feel more human again, thanks to you.”

Lena smiled and looked down for a moment before making eye contact again.

“It’s no problem. I’m glad I could help. I hope the rest of your stay here is better.”

Kara sighed. “Yeah.” Then, as she opened the door and got out of the car, she added: “You know, you’ve ruined me now. I might follow any stranger to their house from now on, hoping to find a friendly smile, a cute dog and some great wine.”

Lena laughed. “Bye, Kara. Take care.”

Kara waved as she drove off and then squared her shoulders to mentally prepare herself for the final few steps back toward her vacation rental and Mike.

2. Chapter 2

Alex picked up on the second ring.

“Please tell me about all of the incredible food you’ve been eating. I’ve been stuck at work with nothing but stale crackers to keep me company for hours. I need some inspiration.”

Kara let out a noise that was halfway between a laugh and a sob, and her sister’s tone immediately switched from tired to fully alert.

“Kara, what’s wrong?”

“Mike,” Kara managed between gulped breaths as she attempted to keep her tears at bay.

“Ah. What has he done now?”

It was no secret that Alex was never a big fan of Mike. She tried to play nice to make Kara happy, but on sister nights, when it was just the two of them, Alex had let her know multiple times that she didn’t particularly care for his brand of “manchild behaviour”, as she called it. At the same time, Kara knew that no matter what, Alex was always in her corner, with reluctant support for her relationship or a shoulder to cry on, like now.

“I caught him making out with some woman from a scooter rental in town. And I just got so mad that I stormed off and went for this very long walk, and then I got lost in the forest and it was getting dark and I thought there was a boar, but it was just a dog and this woman, and she took me to her place and we had wine and it was really nice, even though I didn’t even know her name when I agreed to go with her, and then she drove me back to the apartment here. It’s midnight, so I thought he’d be here, waiting for me, and you know, maybe a bit worried that I was gone for so long but he’s not even here. He never even left a note.”

“Whoa,” Alex said, “slow down. You lost me somewhere around the part with the boar.”

Kara sighed. She didn’t really feel like repeating herself. “He made out with some girl from town. And now he’s not here, even though it’s super late. That’s pretty much the gist of it.”

There was a moment of silence on the line and she could hear Alex take a long deep breath.

“Oh Kara. I am really sorry. I wish I could say I’m surprised. I’m not, but that doesn’t change how shitty this is for you right now.”

“Yeah,” Kara choked out, fighting back a fresh round of tears.

“What do you want to do now? Do you want to come home? I can pick you up at the airport whenever, just say the word. We can do an emergency sister night and throw darts at his photo together.”

At that, Kara chuckled a little. It was tempting to simply pack her bags and leave, no looking back. At the same time, Kara didn’t come to Italy just for some random vacation. She came here with her own purpose, to find out more about where her birth family was from, and she wasn’t about to let Mike’s inability to keep his hands to himself throw her off her game.

“I appreciate the offer. I really do. But I think I need to stay. I came here for a reason and I need to see this through.”

“Alright. I get that. But what about Mike?”

Kara hesitated for a moment, but then said: “I don’t care what he does. I’m going to pack up his stuff today and leave it outside the door. He can go be with scooter rental lady or do whatever he wants. I don’t care.”

“Good plan,” Alex said, and Kara could hear something that sounded like sisterly pride in her voice. “But now tell me about this woman you met who saved you from the boar.”

Kara laughed, wiping the last remaining tears off her cheeks. Her sister always had a talent for steering a conversation back to more entertaining matters, especially during difficult times.

“There was no boar! I thought there might have been because I could hear a noise, and it was getting dark and I was out alone in the forest. But it was just her dog.”

“Okay, but she still saved you?”

Kara laughed again. “I guess she did. I was a little lost, but she found me and walked me back to the road. And then we had a drink and some bread and cheese at her house. Alex, you should see her house! It’s this cute little old stone house on a hill. Kind of like you see in all the travel brochures. But real, and more remote, I guess.”

“Well,” Alex said, her tone suddenly more serious, “I’m glad she helped you out, but Kara, you know you shouldn’t just follow a stranger into their house, even if they lure you with a cute dog and promise you food.”

“It wasn’t like that,” Kara protested, even though it was absolutely like that. “She just wanted to help, and she is nice.”

“Right. How did you two even communicate? I mean, I know you learned some Italian, but I’m not sure the phrases you know would help in a ‘I’m lost in the woods and my boyfriend is a dick’ situation.”

“Oh, she speaks English. With some posh accent that sounds vaguely British, I think.”

“Kara,” Alex sighed, “do you know anything about this woman? Who she is, why she is there?”

Kara could feel her jaw clench at the hidden accusation in her sister’s words. “I don’t. I know her name and that she was nice to me in a moment when I needed help. It’s not like it matters, I likely won’t see her again.”

“Fair enough. Just, be a bit more careful, okay? I know you’re going through a lot right now, with trying to find out more about your birth family, and now with Mike being an ass. Just, look after yourself? Please?”

“I will, I promise. And on that note, I should, you know, deal with this Mike being an ass situation and pack up his stuff.” Kara yawned, the last few words coming out garbled as a result.

“Yeah. And maybe also get some sleep? It's, what, close to one in the morning there now?”

“Yes, mom!” Kara rolled her eyes.

“Hey! You know I love you. I just worry about you, that’s all.” Alex sounded as tired as Kara felt now.

“I know, I know. I love you, too.”

“Promise you’ll call soon to keep me updated? And let me know if you need anything. Anything at all. Or if you decide to come back early after all. Okay?”

“I promise. Love you!”

They hung up and Kara stood in the middle of the apartment, looking around at various items, both hers and Mike’s, on the bed, the night table, the dining table. It was a mess, frankly, and she thought a good cleaning while packing up Mike’s stuff would help her clear her mind. By around two o’clock, she had managed to pack all of Mike’s belongings into his oversized duffel bag, wiped down all tables, cleaned the bathroom and swept the floors. When she finally collapsed on her bed after brushing her teeth and slipping into her pyjamas, the last thing on her mind before she drifted off to sleep was the image of Lena, sitting at her table and laughing as she told Kara to be more like a dog.

Kara was woken up a few hours later by a loud thump right next to her face. She shot up, eyes scanning the room when she spotted Mike hovering near the side of the bed.

“Babe, why did you pack up all my stuff?” He was rummaging through his duffel bag. “And it’s all just stuffed into the bag. My shirts will all be wrinkly now.”

Kara scoffed. “Oh I’m so sorry that your precious football t-shirts might have a wrinkle or two now. Maybe scooter lady can iron them for you?”

Mike rolled his eyes. “Come on, Kara. That wasn’t anything serious.”

“Oh and you stayed the night with her to, what, get more scooter lessons?”

At least Mike had the decency to look a little embarrassed at that. “We just had a little harmless fun. It’s not like you and I are exclusive.”

“We’re not?” Kara said, her voice loud and hard now. “That’s news to me.”

Mike frowned. “I thought you knew? I mean, you knew about Imra at home. So I thought you, you know, were cool with it?”

And that was the moment when Kara lost it. She had known about Imra, yes, but she figured that the fact that they fought about it for weeks after she found out was a dead giveaway that Kara was, in fact, not cool with it at all.

“Get out,” she spat, getting up from where she was sitting on the bed so she could face him properly.

“What? Come on, Kara. Don’t be like this.”

“I said, get out,” her voice now more a growl than anything else.

“But where would I even go?”

Kara walked over and grabbed the duffel bag from him, opened the door and tossed it into the hallway outside.

“I don’t care. Go to scooter lady. Fly home. I don’t care. Just go.”

“Babe —”

“No!” Kara yelled. “We’re done. Get. Out. Now.”

She wasn’t sure whether it was the finality of her words, or her tone, or the fact that she was holding the door open and now everyone else in the building could hear them, but something about her last sentence hit home. Mike shuffled towards the door, stepped into the hallway and just as he was about to say something else, Kara slammed the door shut in his face.

“You’re going to regret this,” he huffed from the hallway. “I’m sure you’ll come crawling back to me as soon as we’re both back in National City.”

Kara didn’t respond. She waited and listened for a few minutes, wanting to ensure that Mike wouldn’t just stay put in the hallway. When she heard his heavy footsteps on the stairs and then nothing for a while, she allowed herself to sit back down on the bed and run a shaky hand across her face. She picked up her phone to send a quick text to Alex.

KD:

But first I need coffee

Despite the time difference, Alex’s reply was immediate.

AD:
Good!
And don’t let him weasel his way back into your heart, okay?
You deserve so much better than him!

Kara nodded to herself, then got ready for the day. Her first priority was coffee and food, but she also thought about going for another walk, a slightly less impromptu version this time. She got ready with this in mind, and instead of choosing a lighter outfit, she went with heavier shoes, a proper jacket and a little backpack for some water and snacks. Just before she left the apartment, she also grabbed the little paper map of the area and her eyes immediately caught on the patch of forest surrounding the town as well as two smaller roads leading into the hills.

A walk to explore, she thought to herself. After all, she did come to this town to get to know the area better, to maybe feel some sort of connection with her birth family. That one of the roads would lead her right back to Lena’s house was a mere coincidence at this point.

“She’s probably not even home,” Kara mumbled to herself just as she stepped out into the street. But that didn’t deter her from her plan.

She wandered down to the little cafe in the town square and ordered a coffee and an egg dish with some shaved white truffle on top. At the first bite, Kara realized just how hungry she was. Aside from a few pieces of bread and cheese with Lena, she hadn’t eaten anything most of the previous day. She’d never tried truffles before either and as soon as she did, she immediately understood why they were such a delicacy around here and all over the world. Kara knew they were expensive and she wondered briefly just how much an average sized specimen would sell for, but she was soon distracted by a few delicious looking pastries the waiter brought to her table. She packed a couple of them into her backpack for later but couldn’t resist to bite into something that looked like a Danish but was filled with sugar-glazed chestnuts instead of fruit. Remembering that she was in public, Kara held back the delighted moan that was sitting just at the back of her throat.

When she was done with breakfast, she grabbed a couple of bottles of water and set out on her hike. It didn’t take long to leave the little town behind her, and to follow the undulating road into the hills. She saw several smaller paths that led into some fields and then into the woods, but she stuck to the road for the time being, not wanting to trespass on what looked like private property in most places.
