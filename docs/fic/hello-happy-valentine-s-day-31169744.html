Valentine's Day. Ugh.

It's not that Lena hated the day and all its eccentric tradition of giving roses and boxes of chocolates. In fact she actually anticipated the day, looked forward to greeting her girlfriend Veronica when they wake up from a heated night.

Looked forward to the domesticity of cooking together before Veronica heads out first, giving a small peck on Lena's forehead as a temporary parting gift.

Looked forward to the real gift that Veronica might give her when they head to that restaurant Lena loved that serves the most amazing stuffed salmon rolls she'd ever tasted.

And definitely looked forward to the almost sleepless night after dinner, filled with heated kisses and touching bodies that made her weak until the next day.

But that was 5 years ago. Back when the brunette had been the happiest she'd ever been. back when her older brother Lex was still alive and slowly beating the tumor in his lungs. Back when she was young and in love with her ex-girlfriend. Her ex-girlfriend, who Lena spent an unbelievable amount of affection and devotion to, only to find her back in their shared apartment with her hands clinging to another woman's thighs and lips working their way feverishly against each other. Lena wouldn't be able to get that image away for months, always managing to tear up whenever it flashed in her mind.

She also idly remembered Veronica asking for her forgiveness, even so far as kneeling in front of her and trying hard to put the blame on the other woman for making the first move. She didn’t even try to remember what the other woman’s name was because she stopped listening after the first few minutes and walked silently out of their shared apartment.

So here she was, 5 years in the future, in a new city that she moved into because Metropolis is just filled with so many memories that keep her from actually moving on with her life without slowly turning into an alcoholic.

Today was Valentine's Day. The brunette sighed silently in her admittedly very lifeless apartment, with its drab white and grey walls, similarly colored furniture, and extreme lack of any personal trinkets or paraphernalia that would typically give a space it's signature homey vibe.

Meow.

Lena glanced at her open bedroom door from her place in the couch. A few minutes after she heard the sound, a black and grey furred munchkin making its way toward her. The woman smiled a little, glad to have at least a companion for her to get through this forsaken holiday. The cat, which she named Kale from her absolute favorite and Lex's most despised food, was adopted due to Lena's incredibly inept decision making skills when drunk. A few days after Lex's burial, Lena went home straight from the funeral and cried her eyes out, replacing the lost fluids by emptying two large bottles of scotch that she'd typically consume after a tiring day at work. In her defense, she didn't really know what she was capable of doing when she becomes absolutely shitfaced because, well, she is Irish. And the Irish are if not notorious for there incredible ability to suck in alcohol and still manage to live without alcohol poisoning.

She woke up that day, temples throbbing painfully, eyes way too sensitive to the sunlight streaming through the floor to ceiling windows, and mouth filled to the brim with imaginary cotton and dry as a desert. She spent way too much time that day trying to get up, to no avail, until she laid down for a few more minutes and promptly fell asleep for another two hours. When she woke up, well, her head feel like shit but she found herself capable of standing up. She made her way to the kitchen and drank two full glasses of water before slumping down in the kitchen aisle in agony. She took the phone out of her pocket and was surprise at a message from a person she once recalled to be nice to her when she asked about adopting a puppy as a gift for her friend Sam's daughter Ruby for her 13th birthday. Somehow she'd forgotten that the woman gave her her number, stating that she was doing it in case she has any more questions she'd asked about pets and adopting.

And in her drunken state, she did just that. Apparently. there was a message from the woman, Alice. Lena hadn't remembered her name at all, but thankfully the woman named her number after putting it there.

Hey Lena! Just asking what time you'd come over to work on the adoption papers?

The dark-haired woman stared dumbly at the words. Adoption? What adoption? What? Lena groaned in the table, putting her head between her folded arms. Somehow in her drunken state, she'd asked Alice if she could adopt a pet. Before Lena even managed to work out the mechanics of how her drunken self had managed to get a hold of the woman and know that this was the woman from the pet shelter just across town, she figured that she couldn't possibly tell the woman that hey sorry I was blackout drunk because my brother just died and I guess I was looking for a way to cope and I don't know how I managed to call you but again it was all just a big misunderstanding!

Lena even wondered how she'd managed to tell the woman that. She saw that she hadn't sent any messages at all, so that meant that she had called Alice herself. And how the woman somehow did not doubt that she was drunk was another mystery she was not prepared to find the answer to.

Instead, she drove to the pet shelter, greeted the woman, and prepared to tell her everything despite the possible burning humiliation she was prepared to face. But Alice greeted her so sincerely she lost her footing, and after a few minutes she was nodding and saying "A cat would be nice, I guess," and she was being dragged to the back lined with cages, meows and purrs ringing in the air around her.

The fact she saw a cute and fat black and grey furred munchkin made sure her explanation died completely in her throat.

Kale dragged his furry face in the waiting arms of Lena, weaving his way in her dangling legs from the couch. She reached down to pat his furry body as he made his way around and around her legs, leaving visible furs in her black leggings. She sighed as she fetched the lint roller from the table on the side of the couch, and dragged it to her legs to gather the visible threads of pet hair.

The cat weaved its way around her legs after she did that. Lena stared at the grey cat affrontedly. The cat stared back after getting up from the coffee table near her open laptop. Almost daring her to move him away.

"You know that you're not allowed in the coffee table, Kale," Lena said at the cat in a defeated manner. The cat meowed back in reply. She sighed, figuring out that she trained the cat enough to behave, but apparently not enough to not be an asshole.

"You're lucky you're cute." The cat purred, stretched along the table, and plopped its head down near the laptop's side. She sighed again, this time dragging her right hand to pet the cat's sleeping figure on the table. She smiled despite herself, thankful that no matter how terribly lonely this day might be, she still had a cat that she could be lonely with, no matter how much of an asshole it could be.

She continued to work through her e-mails, alternating between typing and dragging her fingers through her cat's soft fur.

Despite it currently being her most disliked holiday today, Lena has a tradition to uphold. And girlfriend or no girlfriend, this was the one constant that made her feel that this day might not be as lonesome as it really is.

Lena will bake a coconut cake.

Her ex-girlfriend Veronica hated coconut cake. In fact the woman hated a lot of things that wasn't worth upward of a thousand dollars. With her, something's only valuable if you'd spend a lot of money on it.

Plus she hated coconuts. And fruit in general. Lena was pretty sure that Veronica's diet only consisted of golden filigreed shrimps surrounded by way too many caviar to be palatable. Once they were together for a few months and she saw Lena baking her traditional cake, she pulled away stared at it with an unreadable expression.

"No way I am eating that," Veronica said, still glancing at the actual coconuts that Lena bought from the grocery store. Lena looked at her oddly, but smiled nonetheless. She might be allergic, she thought.

"Are you allergic to coconuts?" Lena asked. The older woman only gave a half-hearted shrug and said no. Lena looked even more confused. So she just. . . hates it then?

"I just don't like to eat cake that much." Lena looked at the ingredients strewn about in the table in her kitchen. The brunette didn't say anything else, knowing full well by now that Veronica tended to be.. . abrasive whenever she doesn't get her way. She learned that the hard way when Lena wanted to go to a nearer place to her office instead than the one downtown that Veronica insisted on simply because she liked the atmosphere more.

Needless to say, that particular Valentine's was not the greatest one for Lena.

She made her way to the aisle, picking up ingredients left and right to start making the one thing that remained fixed despite everything else being unstable. She weaved her way around the store. She got cake flour, cream of tartar, cans of coconut milk, and even managed to find some actual coconuts that the stall owner thankfully cracked open for her so that she wouldn't have to stress about doing it herself.

She did that once. The first Valentine's day that prompted this yearly tradition started when Lex ordered her to go to the store and ask for coconuts. She managed to find two and bring them home. Then Lex, ever the big and caring brother, taught her how to operate the oven, whip the ingredients together, and how to crack open a coconut. It was the most tedious chore she'd done. She probably hit the thing with a hammer for about a hundred times and it still wouldn't give. When it did, it became a matter then of shredding the coconut meat. Lena's arms were extremely sore that day, but once she presented a slice to her then college girlfriend and received a rather explicit noise of approval, she found that the pain in her arms were slowly being replaced with a warm fluttering in her stomach.

Later that night, her adoptive mother Lillian asked her what they'd done with the kitchen and why are there empty coconut husks in the kitchen? Lena said that Lex made her buy some coconut for the cake she'd be making.

"Why didn't you just buy already shredded ones?" Lillian asked with confusion. Lena stared at her for a few long minutes, causing Lillian to ask if she's okay. She wordlessly went to the sofa in the living room where Lex was currently lounging, and promptly hit him in the face with a pillow. She attempted to murder him when his laugh became louder, but Lillian forced the two away before any blood is spilled.

She sighed longingly at the memory, still standing near the packages of sugar. She picked one up absentmindedly, placed it in the cart she was dragging along and made her way to the register.

"So you could have called someone and then meet them on the street and you'd never know, then," Lena said. She phrased it almost as a question and a statement at the same time, and the blonde agreed.

"Pretty much. That's my only rule, to be honest. It's not that I don't want to meet them. It's just that I don't really want to force anything on the other person, you know? Just because I called doesn't mean they have to keep conversing with me or something." Lena hummed in agreement. She was about to ask another question but surprisingly Kara found herself asking the woman one instead.

"So, um, how about you? I'm not bothering you, am I? I could hang up now if you're busy or something I just-" the woman rambled and Lena cut in almost immediately when the woman said that she should hang up.

"No, no I'm not doing anything at all. In fact I just finished baking a cake to celebrate being alone on the day of love, you know, Self-care and all that." She laughed at the sarcastic response, but was surprised by the blonde's next response?

"What flavor?" the woman asked simply.

"Uhh. . . coconut? It's a coconut cake," Lena replied. She didn't realize she was dreading the woman who she's managed to feel close to about her cake flavor choices. Then again, the fact that she's the only one who likes that flavor always made her the receiving end of many weird looks about her favorite flavor.

Instead Kara said "That's my favorite," and went silent again. Lena was surprised, nonetheless. She didn't know if the woman was saying the truth or just being respectful because she is just a random stranger from the phonebook, but she couldn't help but feel the need to validate what Kara had said.

"Really? Are you saying that out of respect because I know lots of people who are weirded out by that flavor," Lena said almost indignantly.

"No! No, I mean yeah I really like that flavor. At first I didn't because the coconut has this weird consistency that just fails to match with the cake, but then I went to this fancy pastry shop when I was in Metropolis that served coconut cake and we ordered it and, well, let's just say my opinions changed rather quickly." Kara laughed as she finished, and Lena felt her heart beating faster at the woman's explanation. The fact that she knows that restaurant does not help at all.

"Wait wait is the restaurant named M'gann's Pastry Factory and Co.?" Lena asked, barely managing to keep the incredulous tone on her voice. This is one hell of a coincidence, she thought. She heard the woman exhaled so sharply she winced at the sudden sound in her ear.

"That's it! You know it too?" Kara exclaimed, excitement clear in her voice. Lena laughed at the woman's enthusiastic voice and said "Of course! I was trying to replicate their recipe based on taste alone but God I haven't found a way to do that yet so I just settled on buying them for now." She laughed again at the situation. She was expecting this day to be terrible, accompanied by shitty reruns, meows of Kale ringing around in the apartment, and a comfortably dressed Lena lounging about eating coconut cake.

She never once thought that this random phone call would be one of the highlights of this day. Hell, probably even the whole month. She heard Kara laugh again and said something about wanting to return there someday, but the fluttering in her stomach is effectively distracting her from hearing anything. And it might be the absurdity of the situation, or the fact that she is now very fascinated by who this woman is. Hell, she even asked if she might be as pretty as her voice sounds. And when Lena Luthor is curious about something, she will make sure that her curiosity is satisfied.

Even toward the possibility that this person might be dangerous and playing with her.

Now or never, Lena, she thought nervously. She cleared her throat before she starts talking.

"So if, say, I go to the Oval park near the NC Theatre at around 8:00, would you go too?" The end of the other line remained quiet after a few minutes. Lena thought the woman had already hanged up, scared by her sudden forwardness, but then she heard the Kara clear her throat.

"Wh-what? Why would you tell me that?" the woman replied confusedly. Suddenly the fluttering in Lena's stomach is replaced by a large weight dropping instead, causing her to grip tightly at the phone in her hand to stabilize herself. She could have been reading the signs wrong. It was clear from the start that Kara had only intended to say her piece and disappear forever, but now that there was an entire conversation that made Kara into a person she might actually see and hear and touch and not some random Valentine's Day greeter, Lena found it hard to believe that she'd forget the woman anytime soon.

"I don't know. To be honest I feel like we're connecting somehow, but I guess I must have been reading too much into it. Forget about it I. . . I didn't mean to," Lena stuttered out, disappointment in her voice evident. Kara also seemed to be grasping at whatever appropriate reply she could find.

"Well yeah I think we definitely connected there, but Miss Luthor I don't want to force you into anything, okay? It's my only rule and if I-" she heard the woman said but Lena quickly cut her off.

"I know what rule is that, Kara. You've already explained it to me. I don't know what I was thinking when I said that, but it would be nice if you could just forget about it. Sorry but I need to go I have something to attend to. It was nice talking to you, Kara. Have a Happy Valentine's Day," the brunette said with little enthusiasm. She heard a few more squeak and the tiny voice of Kara saying "Wait!" before she replaced the receiver on the wall mount and made her way to the table and sat, staring at the now cool coconut cake.

She sighed. She thought that something good would come out of it, and she'd been a fool again to think that being optimistic about something as unstable as a random phone call to establish a meaningful relationship with someone. As expected, her mind drifted again to Kara's velvety voice. her awkward laughs and those genuine all-belly laughs that she'd heard just a few minutes earlier. Lena expected that she wouldn't be able to easily forget the woman anytime soon, and she was right.

She was replaying their entire interaction for about fifteen minutes until she was broken out of her trance by the grounding presence of her cat meowing at her from the floor. She sighed again and bent down to scratch the cat's ears, earning her a thankful purr from the feline.

"Oh, Kale. Am I stupid for getting my hopes up from some random caller?"

Mrroow. Lena thought it probably said yes, along with the unimpressed stare accompanied with it.

She sighed again, this time letting the silence of the apartment hang longer. She glanced at the digital clock near the couch and noticed it was only a few minutes till eight. She instantly remembered her sudden meet-up offer, and felt stupid for ever thinking Kara would agree.

Despite that, however, she suddenly found herself craving to go outside. She remembered vaguely mentioning the NC Theatre, and she figured that now would be a perfect opportunity to go to the one place that she has little to no experience going to. It's not that Lena despised watching movies along with hundreds of other people, but her tedious job as a CEO and, well, having a large flatscreen and a monthly subscription to Netflix despite not getting to use it more often, just seems a more practical way to get her fix of pop culture that she so severely lacked.

According to Sam and her daughter Ruby, that is.

It's with this ignorance that she didn't think about whether bringing her own food was acceptable or not, choosing to slice a large piece of the cake and putting it in a portable air-lock container before grabbing her keys, making sure that Kale was locked inside her room with enough food and water, and heading out to the cold February night to walk the three blocks to the theater.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Lights glowed through the soft darkness of the night. All around her, couples milled around, enjoying each other’s company. Some were positioned near food stands, sharing a simple meal while talking about everything and nothing. Some were walking down the sidewalk to unknown destinations, fingers intertwined and hands swinging beside them. The air was cold, not too frigid. Couples immediately gravitated towards each other to share the warmth of their loved ones.

Lena could only stare and feel the all too familiar ache she felt after breaking up with Veronica.

She made her way downtown, making her way through the throng of people going against her to their respective destinations. A long gust of wind blew, making the brunette clutch tighter at her sides to absorb any potential warmth her long burgundy coat might offer. It wasn’t a lot, but she was still thankful she hasn’t started shivering yet.

The theatre loomed ahead, marked by the sound of people lined up to get tickets for the next blockbuster movie titles placed atop the red and gold embellished ticket booth. She stopped a few meters away, squinting at the line of people waiting.

It was a long line. At the sight, Lena sighed. She knew this was a possibility, it was Valentine's after all, but she will admit that she had actually been looking forward to doing enjoying the night in a dark room with hundreds of couples around her. Okay that might not be a fun experience at all, Lena thought. She sighed again and prepared to turn back home to instead go back to her original plan of eating half of the cake she baked accompanied by some wine, her cat, and the drone of whatever movie she knows she won’t really watch completely.

As she turned around, her peripherals caught a movement on the sidewalk to her right. She glanced at the direction, noting how people were looking at someone, someone running quickly and trying to avoid the opposite crowd with little success. Lena did not catch any indication of who the person might be, but almost as if a lightning strike in her chest her heart started going into overdrive.

Her breathing suddenly became labored, forcing visible stream of fog with every exhale. She glanced at the wristwatch on her hand, and her eyebrows shot up instantaneously.

It was eight in the evening. It’s eight it’s eight it’s eight, Lena’s thoughts echoed.

Could it be her? Should she check? Maybe it was someone late for a date in the park. Maybe it was a thief putting some distance from the unlucky couple he’d manage to burgle. No matter what reasons she throws around, she couldn't help but cross the street, barely managing to glance left and right for any incoming vehicle. When she reached the other side, she made her way through the wrought iron gates that were severely rusted.

The park near the theatre, eloquently named Oval park, is a rather large elliptical park that has stood in the city for as long as the NC Theatre has managed to be operational despite the vintage architecture. The outer ridges of the park were lined with trees and shrubs, with the occasional food stand off to the side. In the center of the park is a more subdued grassy area that serves as an empty space for the occasional strolls, frisbee games, family picnics and running dogs during morning and afternoons. Four cemented paths were laid out in a cross, spanning around to encircle the park and spanning some more in four directions that serve as individual exits and entrance. It was a simple and elegant design for a park this size, and Lena found herself wanting to wander the place during early mornings.

Lena made her way towards the western entrance, circling her way to the left. All around here the murmurs of conversation flooded her ears and filled the night with the same mindless white noise that her TV used to provide for her. She spared a glance towards the middle of the park, seeing couples and other people roaming around. The side of the central circle is orbited by wooden benches, currently occupied by more couples and the rare presence of a singe, evidently lonely person gulfing down a hotdog absentmindedly.

She continued to move to the left, nearing the northern entrance and spun around at her solitary spot in the cemented path. The traffic in the path were not heavy, so she mostly didn’t notice the odd looks being tossed at her direction. She didn’t even care how her erratic breathing seems to be a sign of distress for them, and instead started to head back where she came from when she was stopped in her tracks.

A blonde woman was starting to go near her spot in the northern gate. She squinted at the woman due to the distance between them. A few feet more, the brunette found herself breathless for a completely different reason.

Simply put, the blonde was beautiful. She was too goddamn attractive and Lena knows she’s staring and her mother’s soft voice scolding her it’s not appropriate to stare at a person Lena and she is well aware but she can’t find herself to pull away even for a second. The woman was wearing a blue striped button down shirt that's buttoned up to cover her long neck. Her jaw was tense in concentration as she turns around, surveying her surroundings with what Lena could tell was probably exasperation. She was wearing dark blue jeans that hugged her figure just right, as if it was tailored fit to her exact shape. On top of her blue long-sleeved shirt was a black leather jacket that was definitely too big for her, the left sleeve slipping down to cover her left arm, a direct contrast from her perfectly put outfit underneath.

Lena was astonished. And she kept gaping at the woman until she noticed finally that the woman was staring right back at her, mouth hanging unabashedly. The brunette would have laughed at the sight, but then she felt her mouth hanging open in an almost exact same manner, so instead she forced herself to close it and slowly, almost in a daze, at the blonde woman.

Surprisingly the first one to recover was the blonde right in front of her. She started bumbling some words, as if to test if she can talk clearly, and Lena had to suppress a gasp at the familiarity of it.

It was her. It was definitely Kara, and Lena found herself suddenly thankful for not running back to her apartment earlier.

“Lena?” the blonde blurted out. This time Lena couldn’t help but inhale sharply, cold air filling her lungs.

“Yes. Kara?” Lena replied, indicating at the blonde, to which the other woman simply nodded.

Lena tried to clear her throat to speak more clearly, the surprise slowly wearing off now that she has a confirmation, but nerves still evident because God Kara was looking at her like that and Lena couldn’t help but melt under the blonde’s incredibly cerulean gaze.

“I didn’t imagine you’d be blonde,” Lena said simply, smiling a little at how the blonde ducked her hear at the thought of this raven-haired beauty imagining her from just one phone call.

“Well I didn’t imagine you’d look like a runway model, but now that I think about it your voice should’ve been a dead giveaway,” Kara retorted back, fidgeting with the black glasses perched on her nose.

This time, it was her time to duck her bashfully, feeling the warm on her cheeks despite the cold air surrounding them.

“I thought you wouldn't come,” Kara blurted out almost dejectedly. Lena only gave an awkward chuckle before answering. “Yes, well truth be told I wasn’t really supposed to come here. I figured I’d go to the theater instead, but then I think I saw you rush in here, and I can’t help but know if. . .if you’d actually come.” She saw that Kara made her way closer to her. Once she did, Lena looked up, and she really shouldn’t have because those blue orbs were so much bigger and brighter under the streetlights that she looks ethereal.

“Well, I. . . you were right. I felt we were connecting there and I just- I just panicked because there’s another reason why I don’t want to meet the people I call and it’s because I’m really bad at interacting with people I don’t know well, and I’m sorry that I might have troubled you by turning you down,” the blonde gushed back. She took a deep breath once she finished, and Lena was looking at her with so much understanding that she couldn’t help but stare back at those verdant eyes of hers.

“That’s okay, Kara. Like you said, I wouldn't want to force you to do something you’re not comfortable with, no matter how much I enjoyed that small talk we had.” The brunette threw a grateful smile towards the blonde, and Kara returned it all too eagerly.

“Yeah, I- I enjoyed that too,” she said, voice barely above a whisper now that they were so close and everything just seems to disappear around them.

Lena stood still for a few more minutes before abruptly opening her purse. Kara looked back with confusion until the other woman pulled out a small Tupperware. Lena offered it to the blonde, and Kara looked inside it to see a pale white cake with shredded coconuts sprinkling the frosted surface. Kara couldn’t help but laugh a bit too loudly, causing Lena to flash an amused grin at her reaction.

“I heard it was your favorite,” Lena quipped. Kara laughed again and shook her head.

“Oh yeah, who told you that?” Kara retorted back, playing along if only to see Lena shoot her another one of her dimpled grins.

“Well there was someone who called me randomly earlier. She said you liked coconut cake, and frankly I’m glad because there are way too many people in my life weirded out by it.” Lena explained with an amused expression. Kara laughed again at the absurdity of the situation she found herself in. Lena wordlessly gestured for them to seat in one of the unoccupied bench closest to them and both sat, placing the container between them.

Lena then paused, as if suddenly remembering something. Kara shot her a worried glance.

“You okay?” Kara asked. Lena turned to look at her and said “I forgot to bring a spoon.” The blonde couldn't help but chuckle and said that she’d be right back before running of to the nearest food cart she could find. Once she returned, she triumphantly held two plastic spoons to Lena who smiled at her again and thanked her.

“You know, I honestly didn’t know that my night would end like this,” Kara said simply, savoring the flavor of the cake and raining Lena with praises that she couldn’t help but feel the warmth of her blush creep to the tips of her ears.

“What, sharing a cake with a random person on Valentine's Day?” Lena asked. Kara gave an affirmative hum in response.

“Yeah, that. I’m not complaining, though. This has got to be the best Valentine's I’ve ever had,” Kara replied, looking Lena straight in the eye, just so the other woman could feel the amount of appreciation that she can’t fully express.

“Me too,” Lena said plainly and found that, for the first time in years, she believes it. This was the best Valentine’s Day she’d ever had, and she found herself hoping for a few more.
