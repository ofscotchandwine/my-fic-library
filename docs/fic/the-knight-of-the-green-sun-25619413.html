“Mom brought it up a few weeks ago. And now it makes sense why she keeps tabs on these people.” Alex paused. “I don’t know why the Luthors did what they did, if they did it, but they did one hell of a job covering it up.”

“Alex…” Hot tears welled at the corner of her eyes.

It couldn’t be.

Lena wouldn’t hurt her.

Her sister, always tall and strong and dependable, pulled her in for a hug. She patted her back and stroked her hair, as if urging her to stand straighter but never forget that she wasn’t alone.

“Do you think Eliza is awake?” Asked Kara.

“I don’t know, but we can try calling.”

The sisters came apart, allowing Kara to reach for her phone on the desk. Her hands didn’t quite work right in the panic of the moment, so she passed it over to Alex.

“Don’t worry, I’m not going snoop around your gallery.”

Kara blinked at her. Was this her attempt at easing the tension? “I wasn’t even thinking about that.”

“Sure.” With a few swipes of her thumb, she dialed Eliza. Three tones rang for agonizing seconds.

Kara swore she heard Alex’s heart jump when Eliza finally picked up. The sound was set to speakerphone, so her voice reached both sisters, “hello? Kara?”

“Hey, mom, were you asleep?”

“We were about to go to bed-“

“We?” Both squeaked in a mix of dread and shock.

Eliza laughed, however, as though she had planned to pull their leg all along. “I was talking to Clark, sweetie. Kara’s cousin?”

Kara’s brow furrowed. Why is Clark with Eliza?

“Speaking of which. Alex, what are you doing with Kara’s phone?”

“Oh, she’s right here.” Her eyes flickered towards Kara. “Maybe you should explain.”

Oh, god. Explain all over again, from the beginning, and get the exact same spiel about trusting a Luthor for the nth time. Kara dreaded it, but she tried nevertheless.

Eliza listened intently. Alex moved to sit at her bed, but Kara preferred to stand and pace nervously. “And she posted this job offer in a group chat?” Eliza asked, referring to the first message that drew Kara to contact Lena.

“Yes. A server with hundreds of people. So anyone could’ve reached out. There’s no way she was targeting me for whatever reason.”

“Maybe she used some weird Luthor voodoo.”

“Alex!”

“Kara, I need you to listen to me.”

Both sisters froze and fixed their full attention on the phone. “The reason I came to Metropolis has to do with the Luthors. Your parents once collaborated with them on some research. Whatever they came up with, they deemed it far too dangerous to hand over to anyone, let alone the Luthors, and decided to lock it away.”

Eliza let out a suffering sigh. “I received a letter about a month ago, signed by ‘L.’ I knew who they were, and they knew of your identity, so I panicked and came here. Clark and I tried to go through heaps of paperwork to hand over the property to the Luthors, just to keep you safe.”

“But why Clark?” Kara asked, walking in nervous circles and nibbling at her nails.

“Because he is an El. But the Luthors didn’t want ownership of the research. They wanted to open the safe, and…he couldn’t do that.”

“Wait, hang on.” Alex interrupted, her eyes flickering to the screen which still displayed the contents of the virtual safe. “Do you think the Luthors were responsible for…Kara’s parents?”

“…We’ve always had a strong suspicion. Jeremiah and I. It’s why we chose to change your name and make your adoption as quiet as possible. But…you were found anyway.”

Three bullets. There were three bullets, but only two pictures.

“I’m packing my things right now. I should be home in an hour. I never met with Lena Luthor, so I don’t know what her intentions are. If what she said truly is a warning, then…Please be careful, girls.”

Kara tasted blood, and realized she’d sunk her teeth into flesh rather than nail. She hid her bleeding thumb inside her fist and turned to Alex. “We should call the police. Get a patrol car here. Or something.”

She quirked an eyebrow. “And tell them what? That you were spooked by a scary game?”

“I have to go. Alex, don’t do anything reckless.” Click.

“This is insane.” Kara collapsed against the wall of her room, sliding all the way down as if surrendering to the pressure of the situation. “This is literally…just- random.”

“You’re telling me.” Now her sister stood in front of her, towering above her with her hands on her hips. Her scolding stare was unbearable and forced Kara to face the floor.

“Please stop judging me.”

“I’m not judging you. Come.” She held out a hand. “I always lock everything before bed, but we can double check.”

Kara rolled her eyes, then let Alex pull her to her feet.

As two adult women, sharing a bed was a little odd, but they made it work. Alex’s bed was sufficiently large for them to lay on opposite sides.

It took a while for sleep to finally take Kara.

Her dreams were vivid. She was at her old home, the house of the Els.

But it was daytime and, when Kara looked out the window, the skies were blue. Her mother was tall at her side and her larger hand pulled her along. Every detail of her face was crisp and defined. The shape of her smile, every lock of her hair, the crinkle at the corners of her eyes…

She took her to a door. Behind it, her father waited by a desk, where a safe rested.

They made it a fun family affair as they pressed their thumbs to the machine for it to scan. For a young Kara, this was little more than a game. Except they never played with this toy again. It was quickly forgotten.

The safe felt cold to the touch, even in her dream. As cold as a gun pressed to her forehead.

Kara opened her eyes and stared down the dark eye of its muzzle. She followed an arm encased in dark clothing to meet the glare of the masked figure that threatened her.

Then, glancing to the side, she saw Alex, sunken to her knees, hands raised by her head. Her gun was on the floor.

Alex’s eyes pleaded with Kara for forgiveness. Kara shook her head from side to side as subtly as she could with a gun aimed at her face. It’s okay.

“Bring them downstairs,” commanded a woman’s sharp voice.

Between shouts and shoves and fingers digging into their shoulders, the sisters were dragged across the dark of their own home. Kara bit her lip and tried her best to move onward. Without her glasses and with the tears pouring down, the world was a terrifying blur. She couldn’t hold back a scream whenever she heard Alex grunt.

“Shut up and move!” Shouted the man that pulled her, digging the gun into the back of her head.

They were forced to sit in their living room, on the couch where they spent relaxing evenings watching nonsense shows over pizza. “Keep your heads down!” One of the men barked.

All Kara could see were the black heels and the pencil skirt of the woman that stood before them, hip cocked in disinterest.

“Kara.” Alex whispered to her, “Kara, everything’s going to be okay-“

“Quiet!” The woman snapped, then took a deep breath to collect herself. “I don’t usually participate in these affairs, but you see, this has been an ongoing issue for- what, ten plus years? At some point you have to do things yourself.”

The tip of a gun pushed her chin up to face the woman. She was masked from the nose to the chin, and her hair was pulled up into a bun. Her hand toyed with a gun of her own.

“I need something from you, Zora El.”

Kara gasped. She hadn’t been called by that name in over a decade.

The woman’s heels clicked as she stepped closer. “You have something that belongs to me. Something your parents tried to steal and lock away. And believe me, we’ve tried to break in. If we had succeeded I wouldn’t be doing this…dirty work.”

“I’ll give it to you.” Kara finally found her voice. Her nails dug into the fabric of the couch.

“Good girl.” The woman’s voice dripped with venomous honey. She leaned in, head tilted in a facsimile of affection, “because if you declined, we would’ve had to take your fingers from you.”

Kara flinched at the hiss of steel. One of the men held a saw which glinted at the corner of her vision.

“Why go through all of this?” She asked, staring the woman in the eye.

“This is the simplest way, you see. You were meant to go down that night. With them.” The woman stood tall. “Anyway, you’ll come with us-“

“No!” Shouted Alex.

“Yes, keep bitching, and I’ll feel a lot less inclined to bring her back alive. Take her.”

A rough hand took her by the arm and forced her to her feet. Incessant hammering filled her hearing as blood rushed through her system.

This wasn’t happening, couldn’t be happening.

“Alex!” She implored.

In response came a gunshot. Then another. Echoes of the blasts ricocheted from the walls. The man that held her crumpled to the ground, writhing as he gripped his side. Kara’s eyes flicked towards her sister to see her pouncing towards the other wounded assailant and taking his weapon.

Alex aimed at the woman, the woman aimed at Kara, but her eyes flickered towards the third party.

A figure dressed in black from her toes to the top of her nose stood by the entrance to the Danvers’ household, her own gun fixed on the woman. They moved slowly towards the man by Kara and kicked his gun away before he could reach it with a bloodied hand.

“How dare you?” Growled the cornered attacker. “I’m doing this for you.”

“No. You’re doing this for yourself.” The figure shifted; the barrel of her gun trembled. “Drop the gun.”

An unnerving laugh left the woman. “You were never a real Luthor. Your brother would be ashamed.”

The green of the figure’s eyes became obvious. It sparkled like jewels, like the magic of a fairy. Lena?

Kara unfroze. The world crashed around her. No longer was this a horrible nightmare she could only beg to wake from as she watched on.

This was real, this was life, and the woman was distracted.

Kara ducked and tackled her to the ground, mustering a strength and courage she didn’t know she had within. A shriek rang loud, a gunshot even louder, and both bodies tumbled to the floor.

“Get up!” That was Alex, pulling her back by the shirt. Kara stumbled back and left her room to point at the woman. “Kara, go call 911.”

“Okay-“

“I’ll do it.”

The figure procured her own phone from her pocket with her firearm pointing at the ground.

“Lena?” Kara tried, voice breathless.

With the hand that held the phone, she pulled down the mask. Those sharp features were unmistakable, even in the dim moonlight, as was the hesitant smile. “Hey.”

“Why the fuck didn’t you call for help if you knew this was going to happen?” Alex chimed in, far less pleased to see her than Kara.

“You think Lillian doesn’t have the police in her pocket? Only a Luthor can stop another Luthor.”

“You wish you were a Luthor, you bastard child.”

“911, what’s your emergency?”

Kara watched the world unfold before her eyes, arms wrapped tightly around herself. The events that transpired in the span of less than twenty-four hours rushed through her mind. It was a lot to take in.

One moment things were fine, the next they weren’t.

One moment she was asleep, the next she had a gun pointed between her eyes.

One moment the three attackers were making demands, the next they were being taken by police, hands cuffed behind their backs.

And, of course, Lena was here. Lena, her internet friend, just a few feet away from her, in her living room.

Red and blue flashes poured into their house. Alex was doing a much better job at coping with the situation and carrying on. She and Lena were talking to the police while she sat there being useless. In shock. Paralyzed. Their couch now had bullet holes. The couch that had been there since she was first brought in as a new member of the Danvers family.

The whole scene was all the more surreal with everyone wearing masks. Standing a bit too far from each other. Just pairs of eyes emoting at one another, hands waving to punctuate words and inflections. Something was missing, a hole in a dystopian simulation.

Lena’s were particularly mesmerizing, however. She made fidgeting seem regal and imposing. Her presence owned the room, the cops, even Alex, though she was visibly in a pissy mood.

And then Eliza arrived with Clark in tow, just in time for the cops to leave them to a tearful reunion.

Even then, Kara couldn’t quite believe that it was all over. Something was just so… wrong about her surroundings, about the sound of Clark’s voice bouncing around their kitchen as he and Alex exchanged empty platitudes. This was the first time in several years his words weren’t distorted by some sort of static over a call. He was just…there.

And so was Lena. Kara looked up from her knuckles resting on the kitchen counter and out a window. The sky was shifting to the muted blues of dawn. Lena was standing outside, leaning by the railing. She had been reluctant to come inside; Alex’s protectiveness might’ve had something to do with it. Eliza was talking to her about who knows what.

Kara reached for the coffee maker and poured into four mugs, for everyone but herself. She was awake enough. Caffeine would send her over the edge.

Clark liked a scant teaspoon of sugar, Alex wanted the bitterness to be completely erased by any means necessary. Eliza liked just a splash of milk.

Kara grabbed two of the mugs and placed them on the kitchen island, right in front of Clark and Alex. The motion interrupted some generic talk about the current state of the world.

Perhaps Clark knew what was coming. Perhaps he felt guilty. He was already meeting her steely eyes before she spoke his name, “Clark…you knew about that game. Lex Luthor’s game.”

“I didn’t know anything about the…message.” Eliza must’ve relayed all the information Kara gave her.

Alex grabbed her mug and leaned in. “What did you know then? Weren’t you his…best friend?”

He crossed his arms and clenched his jaw, uncomfortable to be between the intense stare of the two sisters. “I was. Up until about a year before his death. I was there when he got ill and weak. The disease was unlike anything anyone had seen before, and no amount of money made anything better.”

“The Luthors do have a lot of that.” Alex shrugged.

Kara glared at her to shush and listen.

“Yeah, well…when he became bedbound, he decided to hire a studio to make him a game. To escape his reality, I guess. I played a few rounds.”

Kara’s eyes went wide. “A whole studio? Lena was working on that all on her own!” To be fair, Lena did have her motives to keep the game to herself and might’ve had the choice to go down a similar route. Just paying someone else to see it through.

Clark shrugged his shoulders. “I don’t know much about these things, Kara. I did…see Lena a few times in the hospital. We never spoke beyond greetings. I…I don’t know what she’s like.” A sigh. “The last time I spoke with Lex, he was programming some things into the game himself, and he gave me that code. Beyond that, I knew nothing.”

“I see…” A wave of fatigue settled on top of Kara. She leaned her elbows against the counter for support.

“You okay?” Alex was immediately at her side. “You can go upstairs and sleep, you know? It’s over.”

No, not with Lena outside, so close yet so far away. “I have to-“

The sound of the front door opening drew three sets of eyes towards a tired Eliza. She managed a weak smile. “Is everything okay in here?”

Kara stood straighter in an attempt to not worry her. “Yes.” In truth, her feet were shifting, aching to rush towards Lena outside.

“What were you doing with the Luthor?” Asked Alex.

“Talking to her.” She sauntered closer to the trio. “Poor girl. I really do not think she’s like the rest of her family.”

Kara threw up her hands. “It’s what I’ve been trying to tell you all along! She saved us! But no, she’s a Luthor so she has to be cruel and awful and-“

“Kara!”

“I’m going to talk to her.” Before leaving, she hugged her mother. “Thank you for trying to keep me safe.” Then, it was Clark’s turn. “And you too. Thank you.”

He offered a tight lipped smile and a nod. Kara then moved to retrieve a barely warm cup of dark coffee. Avoiding questioning gazes, she marched towards the front door, took a deep breath, and pushed through.

Just in time to see Lena walk away, down the steps of the porch, towards a sleek black car hastily pulled into their driveway. Her heart ached so intensely, as though she was ripping it from her chest and taking it with as the distance between them grew.

“Lena!”

She spun around mid-step, strands of hair flicking wildly. Her emerald eyes were so bright and wide. “Kara…”

“Here.” Kara rushed towards her, offering the mug in her hands. “Coffee.”

Lena hesitated, eyes darting between the drink and Kara herself.

“I didn’t add any-any sugar or milk, I don’t know why I figured you liked it dark- but if you want I can-“

Lena removed the mask from her ear. Her fingers were so soft as they brushed against Kara’s, taking the mug in her hold. Pale lips twitched into a weak smile. “Do I have it written on my face or something?”

God, it’s really her.

Lena was really standing right in front of her, one foot a step above the other, bringing Kara’s favorite mug in for a sip. Kara was frozen in place, mouth dried into a desert as her brain struggled to process all the details that hadn’t been there before.

Every strand of hair, every pore, every subtle – yet very telling- shift and flicker of emotion was crisp and clear. No delays, no pixelation. Just Lena, haggard and sleep-deprived and conflicted and so very gorgeous . Her magical beauty exceeded the boundaries of technology. The camera did her no justice, ever.

Kara couldn’t think about anything else, really. And it was ridiculous that this is what being pointed at with a gun led to: staring agape at Lena Luthor as she nursed some lukewarm coffee and studied the grass in her front yard.

She had to say something eventually. “Lena-“

“Kara-“

“Um,” she tucked a strand of hair behind her ear, “you go first.”

Lena’s gaze darted. She seemed every bit uncomfortable having the low-ground, but then their eyes met and Kara had never seen her so exposed, so naked before. “Kara…I am so sorry for everything my family has put you through. For everything I have put you through…”

Lena was too far away. At least four feet.

“…and if there’s anything, anything at all I could do for you, to make it up to you, just let me know.”

But she could still…reach out to her, if she wanted. “How did you know that this would happen?” Kara asked, twisting her fingers.

“I received a call from Lillian last night, as you opened the safe.” She glanced at the cup, now half-empty, though she seemed disinterested in its contents. “She said she was going to Midvale to finish some business related to some tech. Sounded really smug about hunting a loose end. I pushed and prodded until I got a name.” Her green gaze locked with blue. “Kara Danvers. From there I figured the rest out.”

“So you got into a car and drove all the way here with a gun, in the middle of a pandemic to…save me. From your own mother.”

Lena shook her head. “I wasn’t-I wasn’t going to just sit there while-“ Deep exhale, and she closed her eyes shut to hold back emotion.

Kara could reach out to her. She could touch her shoulder, she could hug her, completely disregarding the recklessness of the action.

She could take the mug from her hands, dig her fingers into the fibers of her shirt, lean in to feel her intoxicating scent, and kiss her.

It was all very real, and it felt so very right.

They came apart far too early. It might’ve been a trick of the lighting, but Lena seemed to be blushing as furiously as Kara. No, she definitely was, and Kara smiled knowing she caused it.

“I-I, Kara, that…that was risky. I could be a carrier and-“

Kara pecked her lips to halt her stream of words, “you’re worth it.”

She narrowed her eyes with skepticism. “Probably not, considering there’s evidence for long-term side-effects of the-“

Whatever followed was lost to a kiss that made it all fade away.

Kara dismissed the coffee mug by placing it on the railing, so she could hold Lena’s wrists in her hands. Armed with her most powerful puppy eyes, she begged, “stay.”

“Kara, I can’t.”

“Lena-“

“I need to go back to Metropolis. That’s where my studies are, where LuthorCorp is, which I will be taking over as CEO soon and you would have known about this if-“

“If I read your Wikipedia article?” Kara shrunk her shoulders. “I think I made it pretty clear how I’m not gonna do that. I want to get to know you right.”

“And that’s why I…”

“Why you…?”

Lena pressed her lips together, then let it all out in a single breath, “why I like you.”

Kara bit her lip. Her heart felt so heavy and full it might explode if she didn’t convince this woman to get a full night’s sleep and a hot meal before leaving. She squeezed her hands, tried to pull her closer. “ Stay, Lena. It’s already daytime. You probably haven’t had any sleep in close to twenty-four hours.”

“Those are rookie numbers.”

“ Lena.”

“I can’t.” She glanced past Kara’s shoulder, at her house. “I don’t think I would be very welcome, anyway.”

“Did Eliza say something to you?”

“No, she actually offered me the same thing, but…you should be able to relax with your family, I…please treasure them.”

“I do, trust me. I owe the Danvers so much.”

“You are a Danvers.” Lena’s hands slipped from Kara’s hold to land on her shoulders. “You’re as kind as your mother, protective like your sister. You…deserve each other.”

“You deserve kindness and protection. You protected me!”

She stepped back. “I have to go, Kara.”

Someday, Lena would stop denying herself a slice of paradise.

Someday, she would have far more than the cursed crumbs of faux love her family left behind for her.

Kara licked her lips. “But…but what about the safe? My parent’s safe? Do you want it?” Part of her wanted to get rid of it ASAP. The other thought of her mother and her father, and knew they wouldn’t appreciate that choice.

She stood a little straighter, a little squarer, like a woman who was raised to fight her way through a cutthroat industry. “I want…for you to learn what that safe contains, and if you believe you can trust me with that tech, then you can hand it over to me if you so choose. But I won’t kill you for it.” Her tone turned icy and sharp. “And if anyone else tries, I’ll take care of them.”

Perhaps Kara should be more worried, more afraid. But it was hard to think of anything else but the sheer incredibleness of the woman that stood in front of her. All she could do with her face was smile stupidly.

“Well…” She started, trying to regain the ability to speak. “I can already tell you’ll make a great CEO. I got shivers.”

Lena loosened and shrugged. “Some just say I’m dramatic. But I like your take better.”

Kara let herself laugh at that, then gazed at her longingly, pulling at a thread on her shirt. “You’ll reach out to me, right?”

And Lena gently held her face to kiss her, yearning. A symbol of reassurance. Her eyes twinkled with pooling tears. “Of course.”

For now, Kara had to watch her leave, watch her take a piece of her heart with her back to the city.
