Echoes of the Forest

She wished she were calling Kara.

She had tried, of course - Kara wasn’t picking up. Lena left three voicemails asking Kara to call her back, all the while fighting to get her trembling under control. Can’t have Supergirl seeing me like this, Lena thought to herself, trying to embrace the Luthor motto of “show no weakness.”

Kara would know exactly what to say in a situation like this - how to get Supergirl to see Lena’s side, to understand that the kryptonite wasn’t about harming her, that none of this was about the super at all. This was about saving Sam, not a personal attack.

But it seemed Kara was wrapped up in something else that day. Lena stared at her phone. Really, this was the sort of thing one should go to their partner for anyway. As much as she wanted that to be Kara, she knew the blonde didn’t see her that way. Besides, James was a good man, and he understood what Lex was like. And maybe he knew enough about Superman to help her figure out what to say to Supergirl.

Lena sighed, and made the call.

Who are you?, Kara thought, trying and failing to take her mind off Lena. This conversation with Brainy was too important - she needed to know if she could project her mind into the valley of Juru, so she could talk to Sam - but all she could think about was the woman next to her. Why would you keep kryptonite? Why wouldn’t you tell Supergirl?

But Brainy’s rapidfire response was coming to an end. “-and you want me to do this all before the solar eclipse achieves totality,” Brainy said, “Which will happen in less than two hours.”

“Yes,” Kara replied, pulling her mind back to the problem at hand.

“Okay. No problem, sounds fun.”

Kara blinked, but Brainy and Alex turned to head back to the Legion cruiser, and she began to follow. That was easy, she thought.

“I’m coming with you,” Lena said, quickly following the rest of the group.

Kara’s heart skipped a beat. “Not a chance,” she said. “It might not even be possible-”

“Oh, no, it’s possible,” Brainy replied.

Kara could’ve strangled the coluan on the spot, but she crossed her arms instead as Lena turned to face her. “I spent the last month working with Sam and Reign, every day,” Lena said, her face stern with determination. “I know what makes them tick. What’s more, I know Sam, and you don’t.”

Kara’s jaw tensed, her mind scrambling to figure out if there was a way to say I know Sam too. Anything to keep Lena from coming…

But it seemed Alex was on board. “With the sun darkening, your powers are going to weaken,” she said to Kara. “You might not have any powers in that… valley-mind-realm thing. You're gonna need somebody to watch your backs.”

Kara swallowed harshly. “Well,” she said, “I guess we’re all in this together.”

“Strength in numbers,” Lena replied.

“Lena has kryptonite,” Kara said.

James was defensive, but Kara didn’t care. She could feel it, the memory of her veins screaming, the sharp cuts in her lungs, how hard it was to think. Lex could only have kryptonite for one purpose - to kill Kal.

Why wouldn’t she tell Supergirl about Sam?, Kara kept thinking. Why would she hang on to the one thing that could kill me?   “I need you to find out if there really isn't any kryptonite left,” Kara said.

“Kara, you're asking me to betray somebody that I really care about,” James said reluctantly.

“I really care about her, too,” Kara said, stepping towards James, hoping that he wouldn’t read too much into her words. This wasn’t about that. “So, let's put this to bed. Once and for all.”

James nodded, resigned to the situation. Kara turned to leave - she needed to get back quickly before she was missed, and it was time to go to the valley of Juru.

Lena gasped upon waking, trying to make sense of the spinning dark sky and branches above her. The shock and vertigo from Brainy’s device was utterly sickening, and the freezing air in her lungs felt no better.

She sat up, noticing Supergirl and Alex to her left doing the same. If the trip to the forest hadn’t set off Lena’s adrenaline, the forest itself surely would’ve. The forest was dark, concealed in mist aside from the sharp trees rising through a black horizon. I’m in an alien forest, Lena thought, a sense of fear and awe fighting each other. This is an echo of Krypton.

But there wasn’t time for curiosity. Supergirl’s powers were continuing to weaken with the eclipsed sun - and Brainy made it clear that even her biology couldn’t withstand serving as an interdimensional portal for too long. The sooner they got going, the better. 

The trio rose from the ground, dusting themselves off and trying to get their bearings. Alex found makeshift weapons in the form of sticks, and it didn’t take long for them to stumble upon Grace’s body. Lena was nauseated at the sight - and for the first time, she wondered if she should’ve brought in the DEO sooner. Maybe there’d be one less corpse, she thought regretfully. Then again, maybe not.

They continued to trudge their way through, as Supergirl panted for air, and learned that she could now bleed. “It’s no worse than light kryptonite poisoning,” Supergirl said. 

“Come on,” Lena said, frustrated. “There's too much at stake for you to still be mad at me.”

“I said we would work together,” came the super’s haughty reply. “Never said it would be sunshine and rainbows.”

Lena’s jaw tensed as they continued to argue. Haven’t I proven myself enough?, she thought, as the super snarked at Lena’s secrets. Haven’t I shown that I’m not Lex?  “Right, you don’t like secrets.”

“Nope, I don’t.”

“Good,” Lena replied. “What’s your real name?”

Lena felt victorious that the super looked at least a little abashed. “It's not a great question for a Luthor to ask someone in my family.”

So I am just a Luthor to her, Lena thought, as even Alex sniped back. It made the forest somehow feel all the more oppressing, Lena feeling all the more lonely. She wished more than ever that she had been able to talk to Kara, and she prayed that the reporter was safe from the chaos being wrought around the world. Would Kara understand me?, Lena thought. Or would she lose faith in me too?

But there wasn’t time to think about that. “You have secrets,” Lena said. “I can get on board with that. I have secrets too.”

The three glanced around at each other, the cold settling on their bodies, meager sticks still in hand. In unspoken agreement, they carried on walking through the dark forest, tension and anxiety marking each one of them as they continued the search for Sam and Julia.

It was only a few moments later that Alex suddenly spun around, her alarmed eyes landing somewhere over Lena’s shoulder, back into the darkness of the forest. “What was that?” Alex asked.

Lena frowned, glancing at Supergirl, whose own brows were crinkled in a familiar way that itched at her mind. But seeing that the kryptonian was also clearly confused, Lena turned back to Alex. “What was what?”

“The voice? Didn’t you hear it?” Alex asked.

“No,” Supergirl said. “What did they say?”

“It was a man’s voice,” Alex said, baffled. “I thought… I thought he said your family motto.”

With increasing wariness, they continued on through the forest, trying to find the other women. Lena was growing to hate it here - she felt cold and clammy, and everything was eerily quiet aside from their crunching boots and panting breaths.

Lena’s eyes were on the ground, careful to not trip over a tree root or fallen branch in the dark, when she heard the murmur of a strange woman’s voice to her side.

Lena watched the back of the super’s shoulders, wondering how she was feeling. Lena knew this forest was kryptonian - but was it familiar to her? Was it just a spot on a map, or had Supergirl been here when the forest was real and alive? Did kryptonians camp, did a young child scamper around these woods as it was bathed in red sunlight?

She found herself wondering who the speaker’s voice was, and who Oliver was - but, she supposed, Supergirl had a life of her own, it’s no surprise that Lena didn’t know who was in it. Other than Kara…

And the phrasing. Take your heart? It couldn’t be literal, of course. But there was something menacing about Supergirl’s voice.

It hadn’t escaped Lena’s notice that all the echos seemed emotional in nature - about love, about Supergirl’s parents. It was almost a cruelty of the forest, that Lena and Alex could listen to the voices that Supergirl would without a doubt love to hear again.

Kara turned back.

“Is… is your family alive?”

Kara smiled - a complex and wistful thing. “My mother,” she said. “My mother, some of my friends, neighbors… they’re alive.”

Lena smiled. Kara got to hear her mother’s voice again after all. “I’m happy to hear that.”

Lena’s heart twinged, saying goodbye to Kara as she went “on assignment.” Knowing who Kara was… it was clear that she was visiting her people, off to Argo for an extended period of time. And Lena almost came clean then about knowing who Kara was. 

But she couldn’t.

She supposed her cold feet paid off, in a way. With Reign’s unexpected return came Kara’s. And with Kara… came her mother. Lena’s top priority was keeping Sam alive, but it was fascinating to hear Kara’s mother talk about kryptonian culture, the Fountain of Lillith, the odd comment here or there about her daughter growing up.

Sending Sam into the valley again was emotionally difficult - Lena knew what it was like there, and the thought of Sam being there alone sent shivers up her spine - but it was necessary. And Sam pushed through.

And when all was said and done, Lena finally handed off a case of harun-el to Alura, including a recipe to make more. Argo would survive.

Because Lena didn’t want to stack another secret onto the pile of secrets, she told Alura of her plan to experiment with the harun-el - it had untapped potential to help so many on Earth, and she swore to keep it safe in the process. “Be careful,” Alura said. “And thank you for everything, Lena.”

“There’s something important I need to tell you,” Kara’s text said. “Can I come over?”

Lena stared at the message, and stared. She had told Kara yes about 20 minutes prior, and she could hear the alert that a visitor was making their way up the penthouse elevator. After only a few more moments, Kara finally entered, and Lena’s heart pattered strongly inside her chest. Because if this was the conversation she thought it was…

“There’s, um. There’s something I should tell you,” Kara said, her fingers fidgeting in front of her as she looked at Lena. “Something I should’ve told you a long time ago.”

Lena’s eyes widened. And after a moment, she smiled softly, stepping forward as Kara’s expression shifted from nervous to curious. Lena’s hand drifted up to caress Kara’s cheek, until her fingers landed lightly on the frames of Kara’s glasses, just at her temple. “Can I take these off?” 

A small yes falls from Kara’s lips, her eyes wide with disbelief as Lena slipped the glasses off from her face. “You knew,” Kara said, her voice small. 

“Yes.”

“How long?”

“A few weeks.”

“I-” Kara swallowed harshly. “I’m so sorry, Lena. I should never have treated you that way. You have the right to be angry-”

“I was,” Lena confessed honestly. “I was angry. And hurt.”

Kara looked down and nodded, her shoulders tensing nervously.

“And then it didn’t matter anymore.”

“What?”

“You care about me,” Lena said. She couldn’t be sure if Kara cared for her romantically, but she cared. “I know you were scared. I know you lashed out. And we should talk about that over time. But I also know you care about me.”

Kara grinned weakly. “I feel like you’re delivering my apology much better than I would’ve.”

Lena laughed.

It wasn’t all smooth from there. There were times when Lena’s anger and hurt bubbled up, causing her to deliver the occasional cutting remark… or more often, simply close up.

But they worked through it. It helped Lena to remember that Kara had almost died to protect her. It helped to remember her own voice shouting back at her on Edge’s plane. The two were deeply flawed - Lena knew there were lines she could cross, if circumstances pushed her, and she knew Kara was flawed too - but the genuine compassion in Kara’s eyes proved a salve over time.

And Lena wondered if there might be some adoration there, too.

“Why did you respond that way?” Lena asked. 

It was a bright Saturday morning in National City. Kara had just brought back a pile of scones from Dublin - it had become a weekly tradition - and had just passed Lena hers as Lena brought over her cup of coffee.

“Respond how?” Kara asked.

“The kryptonite,” Lena said, her voice small. “Did you really think I would hurt you? After everything?”

Kara winced, not quite looking Lena in the eye. “I’ve just never felt pain like that before,” she answered honestly. “And up until that moment, I hadn’t even considered that it could be used to do something good.”

Lena nodded, glancing down at her coffee. 

“And honestly… part of it is that it was you.”

“A Luthor,” Lena whispered.

“No, Lena,” Kara said. “It’s because my best friend made something meant to kill me, and only me. And she didn’t even talk to me about it.”

Lena’s eyes widened.

“I know how stupid that is. But at the time, I couldn’t see past my fear,” Kara said, gingerly reaching her hand across the table on top of Lena’s. “I knew to trust you in the forest because I trust you. I just needed to come to my senses. And for that, I am so sorry.”

Lena was turning red with effort, panting to catch her breath. “Why are you doing this?” she gasped.

Kara looked over from a few feet away, where she was lazily turning the exercise bike to the highest resistance, her pedaling speed even the entire time. “What do you mean?”

“Why do you come here?” Lena said with a puff. “You clearly don’t need it.”

“I just like being with you!”

Lena decided to focus on catching her breath instead of speaking, so she rolled her eyes and smiled.

Kara was grinning widely as she landed on Lena’s balcony, more scones in hand. “You’re in a good mood,” Lena said.

Kara set down the bag at the kitchen counter. “Brainy made some improvements on Argo’s shielding system. It’s working now - comms between Argo and Earth are much easier.”

“You talked to your mom?” Lena said, the thought a cheerful one on her mind.

“Yeah,” Kara responded. “Sometimes I just… I still can’t believe it. They’re alive. My people are alive. I’m not the last anymore.”

The last, Lena thought. Though Kara never said it, Lena wondered how much that influenced the kryptonian’s actions. To fear death was one thing - to fear the death of every memory of a culture was quite another. Kara seemed so much lighter, after the discovery of Argo. So much more at ease with her place in the universe.

Kara eyed Lena bashfully. “Would you…”

Lena tilted her head.

“Would you like to visit Argo sometime? My mom says you’re a hero up there.”

Lena smiled. “I’d like that.”

It was an easy decision to make. 

They had settled into an easy rhythm. Kara often brought Lena scones from Dublin in the mornings. Their Friday nights would end in a movie night at Kara’s place. Lena talked about her experiments, and Kara talked about her articles, and Lena tried not to worry too much when Kara was called away to deal with a threat.

Which brought her to that evening, as she approached Kara’s door. The reporter had certainly missed it the first time, when Lena filled her office with flowers. Lena hoped that the small bouquet of plumerias and roses would be enough this time. But if it wasn’t, she was at least going to say what she felt, and let the chips fall where they may. 

Lena cautiously opened the door, hearing the closing of the oven door just as she entered, looking forward to Kara’s hopefully-unburned pecan pie. The kryptonian glanced up, smiling at Lena as she started to voice a welcome-

And then she saw what was in Lena’s hand.

Lena watched as different, small expressions flit across Kara’s face - pain, and hope, uncertainty, all wrapped in just a few passing moments. “They’re lovely, Lena,” Kara said.

Lena set her bag down, approaching Kara, fidgeting with the flowers in her hand. “One of the memories I overheard,” Lena began, “was someone trying to convince you that you were worth loving.”

Kara’s eyes widened in realization, as Lena reached forward to give Kara the flowers, which she silently took. “I love you,” Lena said. “I love you more than words can say. And however you feel about me, I need you to know that you are worth loving.”

“You’re worth loving,” Kara said softly. “You’re worth being loved by someone who won’t hurt you.”

“The only thing that will hurt is if you keep this from happening due to a misguided sense of nobility,” Lena said. “If you don’t feel that way about me, Kara, I can understand, and we can keep being friends. But please. If you’re worried about hurting me again, the worst hurt will be us both left wondering.”

Kara’s eyes drifted between Lena’s, as she considered Lena’s words. She glanced down at the roses and plumerias, before a slow smile spread on her face as she caught on to the symbolism. “Love and new beginnings,” she said.

“Love and new beginnings,” Lena agreed.

A quiet moment passed, before came Kara's shy question. “Can I kiss you, Lena?”

Lena smiled. “I thought you’d never ask.”

Truth is, visiting Argo was wild.

And that wasn’t even counting the day spent traveling in space. Kara pointed out the different stars and constellations, constantly orienting them throughout their travels. They spent some time with the gravity simulators turned off, and Lena learned what it was like to float and fly, weightless in an unending void. 

But Argo itself - Lena was given a hero’s welcome. Everyone in the city knew her name, and many thanked her profusely. Kara’s heroic feats on Earth hadn’t really made much of an impression to the kryptonians. They knew she used her yellow-sun-granted powers to help her new home, and could appreciate the drive to do so. But there wasn’t the awe of Supergirl in Argo City. Instead there was the awe of Lena Luthor. And Kara would simply say that she had been in awe of Lena since the moment they met.

In the evenings, she retreated to Alura’s home, the three having a quiet dinner of plants Lena had never seen, sauces she had never tasted. It sent chills up Lena’s spine to hear Alura call Kara inah in a string of kryptonese, to hear Kara sing melodies in a language Lena couldn’t understand. Kara would bring home pickings of strange new flowers and delicacies that reminded Lena strongly of chocolate and jam, as Alura told Lena old kryptonian stories.

And in the nights, Kara and Lena would snuggle close, chatting excitedly about their day until words gave way to touch and reverence, spent until the morning came. 

And when the last morning arrived before their voyage back to Earth, Lena found herself curled up against Kara in the dawning light, the kryptonian’s eyes drowsily closed as Lena rubbed circles on her hips. “What are you thinking?” Lena asked.

Kara’s eyes opened, and she turned her head towards Lena with a small smile. “Zhao,” she whispered, leaning up to press a small kiss to Lena’s lips. “I’m finally using my heart now.”

Lena smiled back. “Me too.”
