Kara’s just finally drifting off to sleep, exhaustion settled deep in her bones, when her phone starts buzzing. She groans, turns her head to face her nightstand where her phone lays on charge, and spends the next few moments debating whether or not she can get away with not answering and just pretending she didn’t hear it; for a while there, that option’s really winning out, her eyes drooping as she weighs her options, but eventually, the curse of her kindness reigns supreme, and she leans over and blindly grabs at her phone.

When she sees who’s calling, she couldn’t be more glad.

“Lena,” she says as soon as she answers, painfully aware of how desperate her voice sounds, even through the haze of her sleepiness.

“Kara,” Lena murmurs, voice low. Hearing it again feels like wrapping herself in a warm blanket after years spent in a tundra; the relief it fills her with is so strong it’s overwhelming. “I’m sorry, I didn’t mean to wake you.”

“No, no, it’s…” She sits up straight in her bed, rubbing at the sleep in her eyes with a fist. “It’s fine, don’t worry about it. I wasn’t even asleep yet anyway. What’s up?”

Lena’s quiet for a few moments, but Kara can hear her breathing softly on the other line. She squeezes her eyes shut and savours the sound. Before her time in the Phantom Zone, she’d taken the ability to just tune into the sound of it so much for granted, and then as soon as she couldn’t, it was one of the things she’d found herself missing the most about home; she knows now, with more certainty than anything, she won’t be doing that again anytime soon.

“I’m sorry, it’s… it’s silly. I shouldn’t have called.”

“Lena, the only thing that’s silly here is you thinking you can’t tell me whatever’s on your mind.” When Lena still doesn’t say anything, Kara frowns. “Please?” she implores softly.

Lena lets out a soft little sigh, and then there’s the sound of her shifting, before she speaks. “It’s just… I’ve been thinking a lot about where I come from lately. My family, my… my mother. And seeing you with your father today, it just… It really cemented to me what I have to do.”

Kara can’t help but hold her breath. “And that is?”

Lena’s quiet again, but this time, it’s different. Kara’s a self-certified expert on all things Lena Luthor, including all of her various kinds of silences -- this one, it’s clear, is Lena attempting to word whatever it is she has to say as best as she can.

“I think… no, I- I know, I have to go home.”

There’s not a shred of doubt in Kara’s mind about which home Lena’s referring to. She knows it as surely as she knows her own name, or the sound of Lena’s heartbeat. Even just the thought of losing Lena just as she’s gotten her back again, of having to say goodbye again so soon, fills Kara with a pain so visceral she can feel it like a wrench in her heart.

It’s selfish, she knows, to make this about herself, even if she’d never voice these thoughts out loud to Lena. To take this as some kind of affront against her, like Lena’s trying to hurt her, rather than just trying to do something for herself for the first time in her whole life. Kara can’t help but feel like it’s a little personal, even if she knows logically it isn’t.

It’s just, while the thought of getting out and being with her friends again had been the thing that had kept her going through the hell of the Phantom Zone, it had always really been Lena she’d come back to. No matter where she was, what she was doing, when she would let her mind drift off to something comforting in an attempt to take herself out of the horrors of her reality, it was only ever thinking of Lena that had brought her any real amount of comfort.

She’d think about Lena in the mornings after a sleepover, drowning in one of Kara’s hoodies with stolen sweatpants rolled up around her ankles, rubbing tiredly her eyes with huge sweater paws; Lena at game night, staring at a Monopoly board with the same intensity she approaches her work projects with, as if conquering Main Street was the same stakes as creating life-saving technology to her; Lena tucked into her side on the sofa, blanket pulled up under her chin, insisting that she’s awake and watching the movie even as she drools on Kara’s chest.

The knowledge that, should she ever manage to get out, Lena would be there waiting for her, and they’d be able to pick up where they left off getting their old relationship back… sometimes, it had been the only reason she’d bothered to continue fighting rather than just give in to the Phantoms.

And now… now Lena wants to go home, right when Kara’s finally got what she’s been craving for so long, and the worst part is, she can’t even grudge her it, because she knows exactly how she feels. When Kara had found out that Argo was still out there, that there was something out there that remained of her old life that was more than a memory, the need to go back and see it had been a real, tangible thing, niggling away at her from the moment she’d heard the news till the moment she was there.

“But?”

“But, for all the parts of me Ireland has, for everything there is there for me to find… there’s one thing -- one person -- it doesn’t have.”

“It’s- it’s silly, I know it is; I mean, you’ve already been away from work for so long, and you’ve probably missed Alex so much, and, God, Alex would probably kill me, and- and I mean, this is your home, but I just- I just thought, maybe-”

“Lena,” Kara says, amusement clear in her voice, unbelievably, overwhelmingly fond of the ridiculous woman on the other end of the line. “Breathe, please.”

Lena lets out a singular, shaky breath. Kara’s heart swells.

“Ask me.”

“Would you maybe… want to come with me? To Ireland.”

“Yes,” Kara says before Lena can even fully finish her sentence.

“A-are you sure? You can take some time, think it through, I-I know it’s a lot to think about, and-”

“Yes.”

“Kara, I-”

“Lena. Stop trying to talk me out of this. I said yes, and I meant it. I want to come to Ireland with you.”

“I… okay. Okay.”

And that’s that.

Kara’s packed and buzzing up to Lena’s apartment within the hour. With her, she’s only brought one suitcase, packed full of her favourite button-downs and flannels, her two pairs of good jeans, two pairs of pajama pants, and all of the sweatshirts and hoodies from her closet that she can ever remember Lena stealing. Slung over her shoulder is a backpack containing her phone charger, an assortment of books Lena’s leant her over the years that she never got around to reading, a cosy blanket Eliza’s mom once knitted her for Christmas, and Lena’s personal favourite of her hoodies, just in case she wants to change into it now.

When Lena opens the door, she looks vaguely surprised, like she hadn’t quite actually believed Kara when she said she’d be right over. A smile blooms onto her face, like sunlight creeping in after a night of darkness, beautiful enough to be blinding and make Kara, for a brief moment, forget about everything else that’s going on with them. 

For just a second, when they first meet eyes, everything else fades into the background, and all there is is Lena, and Kara thinks she’d probably recognise this as a problem if it hadn’t been exactly the same way since they first met all those years ago.

“Hi.” Lena’s voice is so small, so shy, and when she sinks into Kara’s open arms and clings on as tightly as she can, burying her face in Kara’s shoulder and inhaling sharply, Kara’s struck with the sudden realisation of just how fragile Lena is.

All this time, Kara had been so certain that if either one of them had had the ability to break the other, it had laid in Lena’s hands. Now, standing in Lena’s doorway, chin resting on top of her best friend’s head and hands stroking gentle circles into her back, Kara’s not so sure.

“Hey there,” she murmurs into her hair. “Are you all packed?”

She feels Lena nod against her chest.

“When are we setting off?”

Lena pulls back just enough to peer down at her watch, but Kara makes sure to keep her grasped firmly in her arms as she does so, unwilling to let her go just yet. “In about forty-five minutes. George is just around the corner now.”

“Okay,” Kara hums against her head, pulling Lena back against her and carding her fingers through her hair gently. “We’ll go out in a minute. Just… want a little more of this.”

She may not be able to see Lena’s face, but she swears she can sense the blush on her cheeks anyway, and she grins against her at the thought.

By the time they end up on the plane -- Kara with her head resting against the window, Lena with hers buried into Kara’s side, eyes squeezed shut in order to avoid actually having to see the plane itself, the hood of Kara’s sweatshirt pulled up over her head -- it’s nearing midnight, and Kara’s torn between her body’s want to succumb to sleep, and her heart’s want to savour every second with Lena.

Obviously, Lena wins out.

“You know, I really don’t see why I couldn’t just fly us over there myself. We could be there in like, five minutes. Ten tops, if I was being careful.”

“And I told you,” Lena says, voice muffled from where she’s smooshed against Kara’s side. “I do not want to risk being carried through the open air by someone also attempting to carry several suitcases and bags as well as me. Plus, I don’t trust any method that can take us five thousand miles away in five minutes.”

“I’m just saying, we could be there already.”

“And I’m just saying no,” Lena says, poking Kara sharply in the ribs. “Now shush.”

After they’ve been safely in the air for a little while, Kara leads Lena over to one of the many sofas on the private plane. She takes a seat herself at the edge, tucking her legs under herself, and then urges Lena to lay down with her; once she’s firmly settled in, her head rested on Kara’s lap with the blanket pulled up over her body, Kara takes one of the books out of her bag, and starts reading.

Lena’s asleep within twenty minutes, snoring away softly in a way that Kara knows she will absolutely, unequivocally deny when she wakes up. She puts the book away carefully, dog-earing the page she finished on in case Lena wants to return to it later, and then takes the opportunity to just… look.

She runs her fingers through Lena’s hair as gently as she can, rubbing her thumb in small circles against the side of her face. She looks so peaceful in her sleep: brow unfurrowed, frown lines nonexistent. When she sleeps, Lena looks for the first time in so long like a woman of her actual age, so much so that it makes Kara ache to think about someone having gone through so much in such a short time.

Eventually, she nods off too, and they only wake when the pilot starts preparing for touchdown. Lena’s adorably grouchy when she’s groggy, grumbling quietly under her breath and burrowing as deep as she can into Kara’s hoodie, trying to retain all the warmth of sleep as she can.

“Is that it? Are we there?” Kara asks.

“Mm, I think so,” Lena murmurs, nodding.

“We must’ve slept the whole way.”

Lena’s cheeks pinken a little. “Must have.” She starts rooting around in her carry-on, rubbing absent-mindedly at where the hair on the back of her head is sticking up, probably from having it buried in Kara’s lap for over ten hours.

“My mirror, I can’t find it anywhere.” Lena frowns. “My hair must look a mess.”

Kara laughs quietly under her breath; apparently, Lena really has no idea how perfect she is. “Come here.”

“Huh?”

“Come here.”

Hesitantly, Lena shuffles back over to where Kara’s sat on the couch, and looks at her expectantly, brow furrowed a little in anticipation. She clearly isn’t expecting Kara to lean over and start fluffing her hair out with her hands, smoothing out the knots as gently as she can, and then rearranging it to look as normal as possible, because she inhales sharply as soon as Kara makes contact, although doesn’t pull away.

“There,” Kara says eventually, moving away slowly from where she was leant over Lena’s shoulder, but still far closer than is probably appropriate for normal conversation. “Good as new.”

Lena takes a tremulous breath. “How do I look?”

They’re so close Kara can see every feature on Lena’s face in almost startling clarity: her slightly-blown pupils, the pretty blush on her cheeks, the chap on her lips. Kara can practically feel every breath Lena takes as if it’s her own.

“Beautiful. As always.” Kara’s voice comes out as a whisper, too scared to ruin the moment, whatever it is, by speaking any louder and throwing things off even a little.

Slowly, almost torturously so, Lena’s eyes drop down just a little, until Kara could swear she’s staring at her mouth. She can’t breathe, she’s sure of it. She knows she could try, but the thought of it is almost blasphemous -- to try and breathe right now would be to take even a shred of her attention away from Lena, and that’s just unfathomable.

If this is how she’s going to die -- staring into Lena Luthor’s eyes on a plane descending into Shannon, Ireland -- then it’s probably a pretty good way to go.

Thankfully, however -- or unfortunately, she’s not sure how to look at it -- before she can pass out from oxygen deprivation, they’re interrupted by the plane finally touching down on solid ground. Immediately, they’re snapped out of the moment, Lena squeezing her eyes shut to avoid the sensation; without giving it a second thought, Kara bundles her back into her arms, holding on tight enough to hopefully distract her.

“Just hold on to me,” she says gently. “We’re nearly there.”

Instantly, like she hadn’t even had to ask, Lena cuddles in closer.

By the time they finally arrive at Lena’s new house, a little cottage in a village about an hour away from Shannon, Kara is once again completely exhausted, despite having slept for at least ten hours on the flight there, which she puts down to a combination of plane-sleeping, worrying over Lena, and, y’know, the whole thing where every time she sleeps, she’s wracked with nightmares about the Phantoms.

Her phone is dead, which makes sense, considering it had only been on thirty percent when she’d left for Lena’s apartment last night. She’ll have to plug it in soon, call Alex -- she’d left a lengthy voicemail last night as she’d been packing explaining what was going on, but she knows her sister, knows that probably won’t have satisfied her for even the length of the voicemail itself. She’ll have to check her emails, see Andrea’s response to her announcement of resignation, effective immediately, which she’d sent as Lena’s driver had whizzed them off to the airport. God only knows how many calls she’s going to have to have with J’onn to help him come up with some plausible explanation for why Supergirl is still gone.

But all of that… she pushes it right to the back of her mind. She can deal with it later.

The house itself is gorgeous. It’s much smaller than any place Kara has seen Lena live in before, in fact it’s probably the smallest place she’s lived since she was in Ireland the first time around; it’s built entirely out of white brick, with a brown thatched roof. The door and windows are both painted yellow, and there are big green vines winding all around the building. There’s a small garden in the back -- the grass is a little overgrown, she must admit, and the house itself needs a little bit of work, but from the moment she sees it, Kara knows that she made the right choice choosing to accompany Lena here.

They settle in almost instantly.

For the first few weeks, they work on building up a new routine: every morning, Kara rises with the sun, and gets up and goes for a run along the beach. On her way back to the house, she stops in at the local bakery, where she always picks up two scones for Lena’s breakfast, and whatever the owner recommends for herself that day. When she gets back, Lena is always still asleep, curled up in a tiny little ball in the middle of the bed that ostensibly belongs to her alone but in reality is both of theirs.

She stops in the bedroom as soon as she gets in, looks down at Lena’s sleeping form for a moment, and then brushes a kiss over her forehead before pottering back into the kitchen to get a start on breakfast. She fries up rashers upon rashers of bacon, fries three eggs for herself and makes a portion of scrambled for Lena, and then gets started on that day’s attempt at not butchering the tea (she’d like to think she’s getting better).

By the time she’s just finishing up, Lena will come shuffling out of the bedroom, hair sticking up everywhere and looking adorably ruffled. She gives Kara a sweet, sleepy smile and slumps down at the little table in the corner of the room. They chat over breakfast, Kara telling stories of the people she encountered on her run and Lena telling Kara about her latest dreams. Inevitably, Kara finishes both all of her food and half of Lena’s. Inevitably, Lena deems the cup of tea undrinkable, pokes fun at Kara about it, and gets up to remake the pot herself, which she’s apparently great at -- clearly some Irish trait she inherited from her mother’s side.

After breakfast, Lena retreats into the bedroom to get ready for the day while Kara cleans up, and when she comes out, Kara says her goodbye for the day with a kiss on Lena’s cheek and a promise to be back soon, before heading out to the post office, where she works a few hours a day throughout the week. While she’s out, Lena stays at home and gardens, or reads, or sometimes attempts to bake (often to disastrous results).

When she finishes her shift, Kara makes a lap of the village, popping her head in just about every door she can and offering any help she can give. After she’s finished fixing a leaky pipe at the local pub, or collected and delivered their elderly neighbour Agnes’ shopping, or whatever it is she’s been pulled up to do that day, she returns back to the house, where Lena is always, always waiting for her with a smile.

They cook dinner together every night, swaying together to the low sounds of Lena’s music playing through her phone’s speaker. Usually it’s Kara teaching Lena how to make something, guiding her through the basics of simple pasta dishes and stews and casseroles, all things Kara had taken for granted having Eliza teach her how to make, an experience she’d never considered that Lena wouldn’t have had growing up. As the weeks progress, Lena starts experimenting with dishes of her own, usually meals common in Ireland, either recommended to her by one of the neighbours, or torn from one of the recipe books she’s taken to hoarding.

They eat dinner together, curled up close on the sofa, usually with Lena’s legs sprawled across Kara’s lap. This almost always devolves into Kara reading aloud from Lena’s latest book, or Lena attempting to demonstrate her newfound knitting skills, of which she’s picking up from Maggie, while Kara taunts her mercilessly.

Every night like clockwork, Alex FaceTimes on her lunch break, which is eight thirty for them. They catch each other up on their lives, Alex describing the latest exploits of the Superfriends, and Kara telling story after story about Lena, going into what is probably excruciating detail about funny things Lena said at breakfast that morning, and how her knitting is actually improving, and how she’s started feeding a little orange cat that sneaks into their garden every morning but refuses to name it, and isn’t that so cute, Alex?

Often, Kelly joins in on their calls, sometimes their other friends too. Occasionally, Kara even manages to coax Lena into joining, who acts all shy and bashful whenever she does, like she’s afraid she doesn’t quite fit in anymore, as if Kara doesn’t know she keeps in regular contact with them all.

Once every two weeks, on a Sunday, when Lena’s at her book club with the other women in the village -- mostly middle-aged mums, if they’re being honest -- Kara flies over to National City and spends a few hours with Alex, always returning right in time for Lena to walk through the door, even though she knows exactly where she’s been.

And then, finally, every night, they crawl into bed no later than nine thirty, and huddle together in the middle of the bed. Lena always steals all of the covers, whining about Kara’s superhuman internal heating anytime she complains, and always burrows in as close as she can to Kara’s chest, like she’s trying to climb into her body and live there. Kara thinks of it as a very sweet, if not slightly creepy, sentiment, which she thinks speaks to her level of love for Lena, because she’s not sure anybody else would see it that way.

Lay together in the darkness, that’s when they finally talk properly. Of course, they talk all throughout the day, but when the curtains have been drawn and the lamps switched off, that’s when everything really starts to come out. Kara loses track of the amount of hours they spend dissecting the minutiae of their lives together; she goes into horrifying recountings of the things she saw in the Phantom Zone, everything that haunts her every time she closes her eyes. Kara tells her all about growing up on Krypton, describes all the cultural differences between it and Earth, all of the different traditions she’d had as a child. Describes how it had felt to watch everything she’d ever known, everyone she’d ever loved, burn, at such a young age. Despite knowing now that her parents are both safe, both now only a short journey away, the memories of how it had felt at that age, to think that she was completely alone on this Earth, make her eyes burn with tears every time she thinks of them.

She tells Lena all about adjusting to life in Midvale; sometimes, those conversations are funny, stories about her and Alex’s petty bickering and pranks on each other. Others, she can hardly bear to go into, hating to dredge up the memories of how terrifying it had been to be thrown onto a whole new planet, unable to make any sense of what was going on around her, overwhelmed and terrified out of her mind the whole time.

On her side, Lena tells her all about her childhood. Kara comes to learn every memory Lena has of her mother, sparse as they may be; she has a mental picture of the house they lived in, not too dissimilar to the one they live in now, of the school Lena attended and the parks and fields they used to play in together, clear as day and idyllic as ever.

When she gets to the part of her story where she moves in with the Luthors, Kara has to physically restrain herself from flying back to National City and starting war on Lex and Lillian all over again. Instead, she settles for tightening her grip on Lena, cradling her trembling body as close to her chest as she can and pressing gentle kisses into her hairline.

All of that, however -- the pain that came with telling Lena every aspect of her trauma, the difficulty that came with holding herself back from reacting when Lena told her hers -- has nothing on the agony that comes with discussing what happened between them last year. 

Oftentimes, those discussions end unhappily, both going to sleep facing the opposite wall after saying less than favourable things to the other. Always, though, do they wake up tangled together again, Kara’s arms wrapped around Lena’s waist and face buried in her neck, and always, then, do they try again the next night.

Every friend they ever lost, every breakup they ever went through, all of it ends up being discussed in that bed, tears often openly flowing between both of them as they do so. 

And, eventually, slowly but surely, they begin to heal.

Somewhere around the six week mark of their stay, they make the decision to start looking into Lena’s mother together.
