Lena had not taken finding out The Secret well.

 

The Secret, of course, being that Kara was Supergirl. She had really not taken it well. Lena had dumped Kara so fast and it had broken the superhero’s heart. Kara didn’t tell anyone, but took off from National City to live in Metropolis. She eventually called Alex back, after almost two weeks of non-stop calls.

 

It was torture, not knowing what to do for the longest time. Then it hit her; maybe if she could show Lena she meant the apology.. Then Lena would come back. So she did the one thing she knew would probably get the message across.

 

Kara gave up the cape. 

 

It didn’t work. Lena still didn’t want any contact, either from Kara or to Kara. It was killing her inside, but Kara respected Lena’s wishes. She had to, what other choice did she have? 

 

It had been two years now, since anyone had heard from Supergirl. Little did Kara know, Lena had also moved to Metropolis after their disastrous break up. She’d regretted her decision almost as soon as it had happened, but there wasn’t a way to get in touch with Kara to fix it. Alex had refused to help her.

 

(“My hands are tied, Lena. I’m sorry.” Alex had spoken in hushed tones into the phone, muffled as if she were hiding the fact she was on the phone.

 

“What is that even supposed to mean? Come on Alex. I’m sorry.” Apparently, losing Kara had meant losing her dignity. A Luthor is reduced to begging? Lionel would roll in his grave.

 

“Kara doesn’t want me to give her number to anyone.” Alex said. “I’m sorry too. For what it’s worth, I was rooting for you both.” And then she hung up.

 

Oh, how Lena had cried. On that day, Lena admitted to herself she’d made a mistake in letting Kara go.)

 

Since then, Lena had been doing okay enough for herself. She kept in touch with Alex, despite the challenges. She had also adopted a little boy. She called him Lukas Elias Luthor. She remembered her ex in that name —Elias—  for the El… El Mayarah. Stronger Together. She hummed to herself at the thought now, looking around her kitchen. Her son, four years old, was a beautiful boy.

 

There was once a time she could have had this with Kara. She sighed at the memory, still painful, of talking about children with her ex. She loved children and had always wanted one, and now that she had one—she was happy.

 

But if she had to watch Barney one more time…

 

“Lukas, honey, your fifth birthday is coming up. What do you want to have your party theme be?” she asked, already dreading the answer, during a commercial. Lena knew better than to talk when Barney was on.

 

“Barney!” Lukas charged at his mother, a bottle of water in hand, and hugged her tightly. “Please, Mama.”

 

“Of course, my little dinosaur. How silly I was to think you’d want something else!” Lena chuckled, squeezing his shoulder. His eyes (blue, green, and so bright) scrunched closed as he smiled. “I’ll arrange everything, promise.” she vowed. “Now come on, darling, we’re going to be late for work and school.”

 

“‘Kay Mama." Lukas released her, rushing to grab his lunch box before he ran back to his mother. “Let’s go!” He stuck his hand out and Lena grabbed her keys and purse with one hand, taking his hand in the other. 

 

“Let’s go,” she agreed.

 

Once Lena had dropped Lukas off at school, she sent out a text to Jess, her secretary. Jess promised to set everything up by the end of the business day.

 

A Barney themed party it would be. On the bright side though, judging by the few minutes here and there of the television show she watched with Lukas, the new Barney show had a female lead. Lena somewhat recognised the voice, but couldn’t quite place it.

 

Ah well. That would be a problem for another day. Lena focused herself on driving to work instead—she’d find out about Barney later, when she actually cared. For the moment, all that mattered was solving this problem at the Metropolis office for L-Corp.

Meanwhile…

(Two years ago; National City)

 

Kara was pacing back and forth in her living room. Lena had just stormed out, muttering words like ‘lost trust’ and ‘never again’ and, damningly, ‘this is over.’ They were all words she couldn’t deny; words Kara couldn’t get her to take back. Kara would have done anything to change the outcome of this. She wanted to see the smile Lena saved for her again.

 

Kara sobbed, throwing an empty punch to the wall. It crumbled underneath her fist—kind of like the relationship that she’d had with Lena. Kara had held too tight to the love she shared with Lena - but damningly, she hadn’t trusted Lena enough with her biggest secret. Kara hated herself for it, knowing that there was nothing she could do to take that back - nothing she could do to show the trust was there. She needed to get out of here.

 

Alex would be over later, after all it was sisters’ night. She had to get out of here before Alex came, so that the brunette couldn’t stop her. Kara grabbed stuff from her closet, throwing it all out on her bed, and stuffed it in a suitcase in record (super) speed. 

 

Once the suitcase was packed, Kara didn’t even bother to change into a super suit (nor did she pack one) before she hightailed it out of there, falling off the balcony face first—then taking flight at the last second and heading straight to Metropolis.

 

She had a plan.

—————————  

 

(One year ago; Metropolis)

 

After spending a year moping and helping Lois and Clark raise their twin boys, Kara decided it was enough. She had to move on.

 

Kara cleared her throat in the silence of her room at the Lane-Kent household. Her heart was still obviously broken (“Clark, it doesn’t just mend overnight.”) but there was going to be a noticeable change in herself - starting today.

 

At least Lois understood the heartbreak.

 

She applied for a job—well okay, she applied for several. First, she wanted to be a teacher—but principals tended to wish for degrees in education. She didn’t have the experience, but she still applied for being a daycare worker—anything to shape the world.

 

The only one that really caught her interest was one where she’d become a children’s show actor. One show in particular was undergoing changes in their industry and looking for a female mascot as its lead. The show itself was educational, which was a plus for Kara.

 

Even without the cape she had once donned, she wanted to make a difference in the world. What better way than to shape the world’s youth?

 

A few days later, she found out that she had gotten the job. Kara as Supergirl was no more. Kara Danvers, the new Barney, was who replaced her. She smiled at her reflection in the mirror, humming to herself. She was proud.

 

—————————  



(Today; Metropolis)

 

When she wasn’t filming, Kara was still trying to be a person who was worth something. She volunteered at homeless shelters. She helped tutor kids at the library. She was happy. Not as happy as she had been, but no longer thinking like that either. She had found a large part of herself in playing Barney - one that she hadn’t realised before.

 

Kara was helping Barry Allen, one of the children she tutored in the library, with his homework when her phone rang and the other library patrons glared at her. She blushed and went to take the phone call, apologising to Barry. It was her boss, Veronica.

 

“Yeah, I can do that! A birthday party? That sounds awesome. What’s the address, is it here in Metropolis?” Kara asked eagerly.

 

“Yep! Last name Luthor. Real cute kid,” Veronica said. “Rich one too. They live on the ritzy side of town, you’ll love the food there,” Veronica teased her. 

 

Kara swallowed hard, her throat dry. A rich Luthor? What are the chances….

 

“Of course, yeah. So excited!” She tried to infuse her voice with joy. “I can’t wait. Just forward the details to my email. I’ll see you tomorrow, Vero.”

 

Kara slid down the wall as she ended the phone call. Two years was a long time, and even if she felt like she was pretending most days, she had moved on from Lena. It was only fair that Lena had too. If she was even the child’s mother, of course. Kara wasn’t going to put much thought into it though, figuring there weren’t that many Luthors in the world.

 

She cleared her throat and focused herself on breathing properly before walking back into the library. “Sorry about that, Barry!” She exclaimed, “Where were we?”

 

—————————  

 

It was approaching the day of the birthday party for Little Luthor. She still hadn’t done research other than the basics of what she’d need to know for her role. Kara forced herself into reading the file. Indeed, the child’s mother was Lena. Lena looked nice, and Kara allowed herself twenty seconds to linger on Lena’s portrait—noticing the slight silver in her hair—before she looked down at the boy, Lukas.

 

He had blonde hair, and green and blue eyes. His name was Lukas Elias Luthor, and he was turning five. Kara smiled as she traced the little family portrait in the file. How many times had she wanted this very future with the woman she saw in the folder? How many times had she dreamed about it?

 

Lena apparently didn’t have a husband though, Kara noticed—or at least not one on file. She cleared her throat, smacking herself on the forehead. She didn’t need to know that part. All that mattered was making this kid happy on his birthday. If Kara took extra pains, when it came time for the party, to look nice underneath her costume, well, who would know? It wasn’t as if Lena would care.

 

Kara didn’t care either. At least that’s what she kept telling herself…

 

—————————  

 

Lukas gave his best puppy eyes up at his mother. 

 

“Mama,” he persisted. “ Why can I have cake later and none now?”

 

“Because it’s your birthday, silly. You could have cake for every meal, but it’s best when you share it,” Lena explained patiently. “Remember, we’re always stronger together.” She forced the words out with a smile. Even if she wanted to, she couldn’t stop the tears springing to her eyes as she tried not to think about her ex.

 

Why had Lena let Kara just get away?

 

Whatever, she didn’t need Kara. All she would need was her son, and that would be enough to keep her frozen heart thawed.

 

Ah, the lies she told herself to make her feel strong enough to get through the day…

 

A few hours later, there were twelve screaming children in her backyard. Lena chuckled to herself, even if she didn’t have friends—Lukas did. That was enough for her. 

 

“OK Lukas! It’s almost time for your big surprise to get here.” Lena beamed at her son as she got off the phone. 

 

Veronica Sinclair had come through once again, and Lena was sure she’d be receiving a medal for best mother of the year . The all too real Barney was coming to their home for the party. Lena had to hand it to Jess, she did good.

 

“Hi! I’m Lena, the birthday boy is over there.” Lena greeted the woman who was dressed up as Barney as she opened the door. “He’s the blonde one, I think you got his picture?” Lena questioned.

 

“Hi Lena. I’m Barney,” Barney said in a familiar voice. Lena couldn’t place it, but got goosebumps all the same. “The birthday boy is where, now? Yes, I had the picture. Very cute family.” Was that a touch of wistfulness in Barney’s voice? Lena wasn’t sure.

 

“Lukas! Honey, look at what Mama’s got for you.” Lena called her son over. Lukas shrieked as he ran, launching himself at Barney. “BARNEY! Mama, you got me BARNEY for my birthday?!” Lukas yelped. “Is that why I can’t have a bouncy house?”

 

“Yes.” Lena laughed. “I hope you forgive me one day.”

 

“I forgive you!” Lukas squeezed Barney’s leg. Barney squeezed Lukas’ shoulders, and wow, okay… Lena felt herself get all tight in the chest. “It’s so great to meet you, Lukas! I know you must be your Mom’s special friend, but can we be friends too?” Barney asked him. Lukas nodded enthusiastically. “Yeah! We can be best friends, Barney! Come on, you have to meet EVERYONE! Uh.” Lukas paused.

 

“Can you get through the door, Barney?” he asked uncertainly, scratching his head curiously.

 

Barney laughed, and damn that was a musical laugh that Lena still could not place and yet. Here she was. Laughing. It was contagious, okay? 

 

“I can’t fit through this door, but I bet I fit through your fence door! Come on, lead the way, Lukas!” Barney exclaimed.

 

Lukas practically dragged Barney out of the house. “Ok! Come on —  Mama, you too.” he called after her.

 

“Coming, Lukey,” Lena promised with an amused smile. “Let me just get my camera, and then I’ll meet you outside.”

 

Lena hummed to herself as they walked outside, and was only a normal amount thrilled when she heard so many squeals of joy. She did well. Lena finished grabbing the camera she wanted, and then walked out herself.

 

“So everyone!” Barney clapped her hands together. Large, purple hands. “I know we’re all so, so excited for Lukas’ birthday and we’re going to get to that in a minute, but first! What are the rules for sharing?”

 

“Ask nicely!” “Sharing is caring!” peppered throughout the crowd. Barney somehow managed to look thrilled.

 

“Excellent! Now, what about if someone gets hurt? What do we say?”

 

“Sorry!” “Are you okay?” “What happened?!”

 

“Good!” Barney praised them again. “And if someone asks for forgiveness, what do you say?”

 

Lena watched as they went through several moments of silence. “You forgive them!” Lukas finally shouted out, and Lena swelled with pride.

 

“That’s right. Or at the very least, you try to!” Barney stressed the words out. “Good job, Lukas. Ok guys, let’s have some fun!”

 

The crowd erupted with happy squeals and cheers. The party had officially begun!

 

—————————  

 

Being here was hard.

 

Kara had expected hard. Yes. But she hadn’t planned on it being this hard. Watching Lena from inside the purple dinosaur suit, talking about forgiveness… Kara sighed. If only it was as easy as Lukas had said.

 

Kara made her way over to Lena— deep breaths. Just take deep breaths, she reminded herself. “Hey, I have to use the bathroom. Is it okay if I go in there for a minute?” she gestured to the house.

 

“Go for it, yeah. Let me distract Lukas whilst you do though.” Lena said. 

 

Barney nodded, and walked back into the house. “Thanks!”

 

Kara walked into the bathroom after undoing her Barney suit, taking off the head for a breather. It was hot under the suit.

 

Once she’d done her business and splashed water on her face, Kara exited the bathroom, still only half in costume. She heard a gasp, and quickly turned around—all the while thinking please don’t be a child who’s seen me.. . 

 

She went to grab the costume’s head, and only halted when she felt a hand yank her into a bedroom.

 

Lena. It was Lena.

 

—————————  

 

Kara was Barney.

 

Kara was Barney and in her room and oh god, Lena did not remember how to breathe.

 

“Breathe, in and out. Come on, with me now.” Kara’s voice was distant but the hand on her shoulder was steady.

 

Lena pushed her hand off. She didn’t need to be steady. She needed Kara to make sense of this—whatever this was—so that she could file it in a box and put the box away in her mind. 

 

“What are you doing here?” she haltingly asked, choking the words out.

 

“I was hired.” Kara said with a helpless shrug. “It’s good to see you.” She offered a signature, thousand watt Kara smile. Lena hated it instantly. Or did she hate herself for melting so quickly underneath its rays?

 

“No, no. You don’t get to do this. Not here, not no,” Lena snapped. “I don’t want to see you, I thought I made that clear.”  Lena hated herself immediately for lying like that. But she was scared, and Kara shouldn’t be here. Kara’s face fell, but then hardened.

 

“You made it perfectly clear, but I was hired to do something for your child, and I’ll be damned if I let another Luthor down.” Kara said stiffly. She grabbed her costume’s head and placed it back on. She cleared her throat and put on her Barney voice as well, clearly becoming a stranger once more that Lena only dreamed of knowing. “I’m sorry to have disappointed you again, Lena, but I’ve got to go perform a song now.”

 

Lena swallowed. She opened her mouth to say something, anything, to keep Kara there. But Kara silenced her with a firm, “Time to sing for Lukas’ cake. I hear he was very excited about it.”

 

Lena nodded in a small, frightened movement and watched as Kara exited the room. She took a moment to put everything in a mental box, before exiting as well to grab the cake and a butter knife to cut it with.

 

“Lukas!” Lena called in a hoarse voice, pretending not to notice Barney turning to her in concern. She wouldn’t make it out alive if she did. “It’s time to cut your cake, baby.”

 

“CAAAAAAAAAAAKE!” Lukas shouted, his face beaming as he ran for it. It was a chocolate cake with chocolate icing.

 

“Alright everyone!” Kara, Barney, whoever she was, called out. “Can we all sing together now? I’ll start. Happy birthday to you!”

 

“Happy birthday to you, happy birthday dear Lukas…. Happy birthday to you!” The crowd continued as Lukas finally blew out his candles. 

 

“Can I eat now?” he asked. Lena was startled when Kara laughed.

 

Lena nodded, “Here baby, I cut you a slice.” Lena said with a soft smile towards her son as she handed the plate to him. “Everyone, dig in!”

 

Kara, still in the suit, made her way over to Lena. “We need to talk.”

 

“Lukas, come say bye-bye to Barney. It’s time for her to go back to the Playhouse,” Lena announced. She didn’t acknowledge Kara’s words.

 

“Thank you for coming to my party,” Lukas said charmingly, hugging onto Barney’s leg. Barney hugged him tight. 

 

“You take care of your Mama now, okay? Happy Birthday, Lukas.”

 

Tears blurred Lena’s eyes as she led Barney out of the backyard. As soon as they were out of sight, Lena watched Kara take off the costume’s head. 

 

“You wanted to talk?” Lena raised a signature eyebrow.

 

“Yes. But not here, I don’t want to get caught.” Kara explained. “Barney’s important to the children, and they’d never—if they saw Barney outside of her costume, it’d be bad. Can we meet up this week?”

 

Lena nodded slowly. “You can talk to Jess about when I’m free,” she stated wearily. “But I’m not able to carve time out for just anything.”

 

“Not even some closure?”

 

“I don’t need your closure.” Lena snapped without thinking. She felt like a deer stuck in headlights. Lena could only imagine what she looked like.

 

Kara chuckled humorlessly. “But I need yours. So I’ll be in touch. Bye, Lena. Give Lukas Barney’s love,” she said carefully.

 

And so Lena watched her leave. She felt the pieces of her heart, the ones that scabbed over when she left, break all over again.

 

And so she broke, there out on the curb beside her house.

 

—————————  

 

Kara walked up into L-Corp a week later, swallowing her pride as she made her way onto Lena’s floor. The top floor. She greeted the secretary and announced who she was, and why she was there.

 

“Ms. Luthor will be with you in a moment.”

 

“Thanks.” Kara smiled tiredly at the secretary. She was about to take a seat when Lena walked out and inclined her head, offering her entrance. Kara swallowed thickly. Okay. Closure. She had to do this.

 

“Hey,” Kara greeted as she walked past Lena.

 

“Thanks for coming,” Lena responded as she moved to sit on one of the chairs in her office. Kara noticed there wasn’t a couch. Blue eyes closed, trying not to think about all the time spent together, cuddled up on the couch in Lena’s L-Corp office back in National City. She bit back a comment about it, knowing that it wasn’t her place. Not anymore.

 

Instead, Kara nodded, “Sure.”

 

“So.” 

 

“So.”

 

They said it at the same time. Lena broke out in a harsh laugh. “Can I start?” 

 

Kara nodded.

 

“Yeah, go ahead.”

 

“I owe you…. A lot of apologies.” Lena started. “I don’t know if Alex ever told you, but a few weeks after I left, I tried to come back. She wouldn’t say where you were, so I couldn’t find you and…. I gave up. Your sister wasn’t helping, you wouldn’t answer my calls.. I mean, I’m a Luthor. And fuck, you’re a Super. We were practically doomed from the start, Kara. Why did you even approach me back then?”

 

Kara swallowed, her eyes flashing. “It wasn’t like that. It’s still not like that.” Kara told her firmly. “I’m not Supergirl anymore. And even if I was, you’re not like the other Luthors and I wish you would realise that.”

 

“You’re not Supergirl anymore,” Lena repeated back in surprise.

 

“Is that all you retained from that? You’re not one of the Luthors, Lena. You’re so much more. I saw it back then, and I see it now. Lukas is the poster child for politeness, and you obviously love him so much— Have you heard of Supergirl since two years ago? No. I gave it up.”

 

“... I gave it up as soon as I got serious with you, but that was the day you found out and had it out with me,” Kara confessed. There wasn’t a single ounce of falsehood in Kara’s voice, and that’s what ended up breaking Lena.

 

“Lillian told me…” Lena swallowed thickly. “Lillian showed me that you only strung me along and played me for the fool.” Now though, she looked uncertain. She looked more like Kara’s Lena again. Like she was back then—when none of the shit mattered because she was with Kara.

 

“You trusted her judgement?” Kara looked horrified. In the years she’d known Lena, Kara had tried so hard to undo years of Luthor-induced trauma. It hurt because she knew now that it had all been for nothing. Even still, no one compared to Lena … She couldn’t move on until she had the closure she craved.

 

“Well, she was bloody right about Supergirl, wasn’t she? Why else would you have kept it from me, Kara?!” Lena only got loud toward the end. Even when she was furious with Kara, Lena couldn’t risk letting that Secret out into the world. She couldn’t risk somebody coming in at the wrong moment and knowing the truth..

 

“Because I loved you!” Kara shouted back at her, standing up. “I loved you and I had to show you who I was in the right way. I loved you, and I was so ready to lay it down with you that night. You just didn’t give me the chance. Rao, Lena. I knew that I was doing wrong by you, but you never even gave me the chance to explain for myself why I did it.”

 

Lena swallowed again, finding it harder to breathe when Kara was shouting. She stayed seated. “I loved you too.” She whispered in a small voice, and found herself looking anywhere but at Kara. “That’s why it hurt so much. Because I loved you.”

 

Finally trying to meet Kara’s eyes, Lena watched as Kara started to pace. “Well I’m, I’m glad you’ve moved on. I have too. We’re both fine, and we have the closure now too—right? I guess we’re good here.” Kara moved to leave even as her heart broke into pieces. But Lena grabbed hold of her wrist, twisting her arm to make Kara come ever closer.

 

Kara was close enough to kiss. Lena wouldn’t even have to speak, wouldn’t have to inch closer—All she had to do was brush her lips downwards and open her mouth just the smallest inch, and there would be Kara. Kara, whose eyes were wider than saucers as she watched Lena wildly, blue eyes that knew her, flickering from Lena’s eyes to her lips, back to her eyes, and it took everything in her to not lean in.

 

Lena breathed out as Kara breathed in, ever in sync. Her breathing got heavier, and it became a chore to breathe—honestly anything that wasn’t kissing Kara was hard to do right then.  Kara looked like she was a predator ready to pounce at any moment, so secure in their prey. Lena tried to avoid looking her in the eye as she breathed out damning words: “Let’s be honest Kara, how long were you really going to stay with me?”

 

Kara stepped back like she’d been hit. “Well, forever was my starting point, but I guess that wasn’t good enough for you.” The moment was gone. Soon, Lena knew Kara would be gone as well.

 

Lena curled in on herself. “I’m sorry, Kara. Of course it would have been good enough!” She tried to swallow her sobs. “God, I know I was wrong but I thought you’d be bored of me. Everyone gets bored of me eventually. And you’re bloody Super, how could I have hoped to stand a chance?”

 

“You should have trusted me.” Kara murmured, but there was no bite to her words, no malice. Just.. resignation. “You’re gonna have to work on that.” She said slowly, kneeling in front of Lena. Lena wiped her eyes, watching her with a look of wary surprise.

 

“With someone else, probably. Yeah. Or I’ll stay single.. Honestly Kara, you’re it.” Lena admitted slowly. She didn’t look at Kara at all, and wouldn't meet her eyes, but she continued: “I know I made mistakes, but I can’t make another one by sitting here and saying I’ll move on with no problem. You’re—  very hard to forget.”

 

“With me. Lena, I love you. I’m not going to let you go just because you made a mistake that’s coming up on three years old,” Kara explained. She cupped Lena’s face. “I won’t let you go. Not until you tell me to.”

 

“Don’t.” Lena whispered. “Don’t let me go, please.” she begged. And then she surged forward, and she’s kissing Kara like her bloody life depended on it. And then she’s not, she’s not kissing Kara anymore. She pulled back. “I’m sorry, oh god, I’m so sorry.” She knew she had misread things, but the way Kara’s lips had felt against hers made it hard for her to care.

 

Kara swallowed Lena’s sobs, pressing her open mouth against hers instead—hard and insistent but slow to let them shut. She stood, bringing Lena up with her, and wrapped her arms around Lena around the waist. Even after the past few years, she was ever-mindful of the way she had to be careful when she hugged Lena. Still—she had to bring Lena closer, she just had to. Nothing would be allowed to separate them—not ever again.

 

“No more apologies.” Kara whispered as she pressed her forehead against Lena’s and stole another soft kiss. “Only love. Okay? Just love me.”

 

“I can…. I can do that.” Lena promised quickly, thinking that it was the easiest promise she’d ever made. “I’ll be good at doing that again. I promise. Give me a chance,” she pleaded softly.

 

“As many as you like, Lena. As many as you like.” Kara’s quick to reassure her, and Lena loves her for it. She just loves her.
