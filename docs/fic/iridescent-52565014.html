    Iridescent

    A chirp sounded from the tablet on her nightstand.

    Lena frowned mid-thought as she put her new copy of Journal of Neuroscience down on her bed - her mind still mulling over an article’s implications on Non Nocere - as she picked up the tablet. It had been a high-priority alert that had chimed, and now Lena could see why. Why is she here?

    The video feed showed a blonde on her balcony, causing Lena to bristle with anger. After all, the last time the blonde was there, she had told Lena quite emphatically that she didn’t give a damn about her, so long as Lena was the villain. 

    Kara tilted her head down, a telltale sign that Lena had learned indicated that Kara was listening - and Lena realized with some fury that Kara was listening for her. For her heartbeat, at least. Why the fuck does she care?, Lena thought.

    She observed Kara for a moment longer, noting a brief tremble in Kara’s shoulders as she stood in silence. But then Kara turned-

    Lena’s jaw tensed from her place on her bed. She wasn’t even here to talk to me?, Lena thought angrily. “Kara,” Lena said, knowing the blonde could hear her through the walls.

    She could see Kara halt on the feed, and Lena gave an aggravated sigh as she threw her blanket off her body, walking down the short hallway until she reached her living room, making her way to her balcony where she could finally see the blonde’s guilty face.

    “I’m sorry,” Kara said quietly, “I can go.”

    “I want to know what you’re doing on my balcony in the first place,” Lena said coolly. “Spying on me?”

    “No, I-” Kara stuttered, taking a moment. “I just…”

    “Just what, Kara?”

    Kara looked at her forlornly. “I wanted to listen to your heartbeat.”

    Lena swallowed, fighting the twin feelings of wistfulness and rage. Not that she thought Kara returned Lena’s romantic leanings, but Lena wished more than once that she could at least have her best friend back.

    Her best friend that never existed. “Don’t do it again,” Lena muttered.

    “I won’t,” Kara promised, somehow a bit too sincere. “Goodnight, Lena.”

    But as Kara turned, before she could lift off into the sky, she stumbled forward - clutching her chest, a pained gasp escaping her lungs.

    “Kara!” Lena shouted, instinctively rushing forward to catch the kryptonian, her annoyance suddenly snapping to worry. But the kryptonian stood, pushing Lena away. “I’m fine, I’m fine.”

    “You’re not fine,” Lena said. “Just tell me. What’s going on?!”

    Kara dragged her eyes to Lena’s, resignation clear on her face. “I’m dying.”

    “Your heart rate is through the roof,” Lena said, removing the small oximeter from Kara’s finger. “And your oxygen level is low.”

    “It was slower on Krypton,” Kara said, from her position on Lena’s couch. “The disease, fahrosh zhor. It makes people exhausted, until their hearts fail. No one knew-” Kara coughed- “No one knew why, but it runs in my family.”

    “Heart transplants?”

    “Won’t work on this.”

    Lena took the damp cloth she had gotten and ran it across Kara’s forehead, where sweat had gathered from Kara’s struggling walk to Lena’s couch. “I need to get you to my lab,” Lena said. “I want your EKG readings. I can rig up a red sunlamp for blood samples-”

    “I already got scanned at the Fortress,” Kara said, “It confirmed what I have, Lena.”

    “But the Fortress didn’t have the cure,” Lena said. “I can run a workup, see what’s happening with your cells-”

    Kara shook her head. “If the Science Guild couldn’t cure it-”

    “Kara,” Lena pleaded. “Let me try.”

    Kara panted heavily as she pulled herself onto the cot, after the seeming endless time it took to get down to Lena’s lab. Lena tapped at a panel, and Kara was surprised to find the room bathed in red light. Just like home, came the stray thought.

    Lena pulled out supplies on the table. Wire leads for an EKG machine, needles for blood. Kara fought the dizzy sensation as she glanced away from Lena, instead examining more of the lab. Lena’s Earth Prime lab looked similar to Earth-38’s - right down to a strong futuristic pod reminiscent of where she had kept Reign.

    But though the equipment - the microscopes and medical tape and tubes - all looked distinctly human, Kara couldn’t help the sensation of being in her father’s lab. I always created such a mess, Kara thought to herself, smiling.

    Lena worked quickly, murmuring to Kara when she was about to insert a needle for blood, but Kara’s mind was elsewhere. On her father’s projects, on projects of her own. On a goofy-yet-functional airfoil she had built just before Krypton’s destruction - that somehow survived the collapse of her father’s lab. It now sat in her childhood bedroom on Argo.

    Something about the thought made her laugh softly.

    Lena glanced up, a quizzical look on her face. “Sorry,” Kara said softly.

    “Don’t be sorry,” Lena said. “I’m just confused.”

    Kara shrugged. “Just a bit of nostalgia, I guess.”

    “Nostalgia?” Lena asked.

    Kara bit at her lip. “The red light- I just- it just reminds me of my father’s lab.”

    “Your father was a scientist,” Lena said.

    “Yeah,” Kara responded. “I thought I was going to be one too.”

    Lena eyed Kara curiously. “Would you have wanted that?” she asked.

    Kara shrugged. “I enjoyed it. I was good at it.” But I’m not sure how long I would’ve lasted, Kara thought - painful memories of the Science Guild’s lies, and Aunt Astra’s misguided attempts to solve the problem, rising to the surface. Who knows who I would’ve become… 

    It wasn’t the first time she had mulled over how destiny had shaped her. For so long, her goal on Earth was to be unnoticed, to hide who she really was - for her safety. And then she had become Supergirl, using her newfound gifts for good.

    But even then, she had been hiding. Supergirl - the mask, the persona she had invented well into her adult life - had never been Kara. Kara Danvers was nothing special. Kara Danvers was completely unremarkable. She had only thought to become something more when…

    I’m not a reporter.

    You could’ve fooled me.

    Not that it mattered anymore.

    “I’ll find something, Kara,” Lena said, sensing Kara's weariness, her hand clasping the kryptonian’s hand in a now-rare gesture of comfort. Kara smiled. I’ll let you try, she thought wistfully.

    Lena let go of Kara’s hand, gathering up her blood samples. “I’ll need to analyze these down the hall,” she said, tapping the control panel to resume white light. Kara nodded back, and Lena exited, making her way to the other room.

    Kara stared after her, already missing the red light - though feeling at peace that she could hear Lena’s heartbeat.

    The loneliness is what got to her, the past few days. Her legacy crystals were recorded, and soon she would be gone. She couldn’t tell Alex - couldn’t tell anyone. The goodbye would be too hard.

    She wondered what would happen in the aftermath. She wondered how the world would fare. It wasn’t the first time she had wondered - that had been when she lifted Fort Rozz to space. 

    But then, she knew that there would always be heroes. There would always be those who would fight, no matter the odds. She saw it over and over again, in the superfriends, in the Crisis, in Lena. Super or not, there would always be people fighting for what was right.

    Kara smiled softly. She would die. But hope never would.

    “Your cells have chloroplasts,” Lena said, practically running back, turning to the light controls to cast the room in red light again before reaching to the keyboard and monitor at Kara’s bedside. “Or something like it - they use a carotenoid dye instead of green chlorophyll. But they function like plant cells-”

    “It’s how I process sunlight,” Kara said, a crinkle on her brow as she watched Lena type. Her cellular differences had come up years ago with Reign, but she didn’t understand why this mattered now.

    “They’re absorbing too much sunlight,” Lena said, pointing at a section that her diagram labeled caroplast for the carotenoid-based chloroplasts. “When I shine sun spectrum light on them, some of them detonate. The scan shows that your cells are trying to modulate by shifting the energy to your heart.”

    Detonate?, Kara thought, her eyes widening in horror, a wave of realization washing over her as the image of another Kara flashed before her eyes - one dressed in nazi regalia, with a sneer on her face. She died of fahrosh zhor?

    Lena turned to Kara. “The cell death would’ve happened more slowly in red sunlight. It would’ve been much harder for your scientists to see this in action. You’re dying at a rate of days, but for them it would’ve taken years.”

    “And the red light?” Kara asked, gesturing in confusion at the ceiling. “That won’t stop this…”

    “No,” Lena said apologetically. “But it may buy us a day. I still need to investigate-”

    “I need to leave,” Kara said, standing suddenly.

    “I haven’t figured out a cure-”

    “There is no cure,” Kara said, ripping the electrodes from her chest. “I’ve seen this before.”

    “On Krypton.”

    “On Earth,” Kara said, her chest rising and falling rapidly in panic as she stood. “Another Earth. Another… me. This isn’t like Krypton. My body is going to go nuclear.”

    “Another Earth? Another you- Kara, wait,” Lena said, grabbing Kara’s arm.

    Kara paused. “I need to get somewhere where I won’t hurt people when I die,” she said, staring at the ground in front of her.

    “Give me time,” Lena begged, “We have a day. Maybe two. I can try to fix how your cells are processing sunlight.”

    “Lena-”

    “Please,” Lena begged.

    Kara stared at Lena, aching second after second passing by, before she slowly nodded.

    “Your predecessor,” Lena murmured, as she finished taking another blood sample. “How large was the blast?”

    Kara shrugged. “I couldn’t tell. I took her up into the atmosphere.” 

    “She died in your arms?” Lena asked.

    “She was a villain,” Kara said, her eyes angry and far away. “It’s not what you think it was.”

    I will do everything in my power to stop you. Just like I would any other villain.

    Lena froze, and Kara realized the mistake she had made. “No-” Kara said. “Lena, I didn’t mean-”

    “I’ll be back soon,” Lena said, scrambling to gather her supplies.

    “Lena-”

    But she was already gone.

    Lena frowned at the cell sample in front of her, watching as they lit up in fluorescent green. 

    Kryptonite slows the caroplast energy transfer, Lena realized, growing nauseous as she watched Kara’s cells glow. Lowgrade kryptonite poisoning will slow down how fast she’s dying.

    Her stomach turned at the thought. The last thing she wanted to do was torture Kara - especially when Kara already thought of her as the villain.

    Can I modify it?, Lena thought, fidgeting with the microscope to examine the cell membrane more closely. If she could modify kryptonite to slow the energy transfer, without melting cell membranes or clotting blood or the myriad of other terrible side effects that caused pain…

    But until Lena could make and test it… green would buy her more time.

    “Lena-” Kara started to explain, standing as Lena entered the room.

    “Kryptonite,” Lena said, causing Kara to pause. “A low dose of kryptonite will… kill you more slowly than what you have now.”

    Kara’s expression was surprised - but too tired to be shocked, leaving her subdued. The kryptonian slowly sank back onto the cot. “Kryptonite disrupts how I process sunlight,” she muttered to herself.

    “With a low enough dose, we can minimize the other effects,” Lena explained gently. “The energy transfer is hurting you the most.”

    Kara flexed her fists nervously.

    “I can put you under, Kara,” Lena whispered.

    Kara shook her head. “I need to be able to leave. If I go nuclear-”

    “This facility can contain the explosion.”

    Kara glanced back up at Lena, trying to quell her own panic, while noting that Lena was trying to hold her own. “Okay,” she said.

    It didn’t take long for Lena to gather the equipment needed. A device to sublimate the kryptonite into vapor. A breathing mask to deliver it to Kara’s lungs. An IV drip to replenish her nutrients as the kryptonite fought for her life. With the red sunlamp on, a human-grade anesthetic would render Kara unconscious.

    Lena began laying equipment out on a table in front of Kara, as the kryptonian reflected quietly. “The other me, on the other Earth,” Kara said softly, “She wanted to hurt people, Lena. She wanted power. She’s not like you.”

    Lena stared at Kara, before tearing her eyes away, her hands trembling as she moved Kara’s arm into position, prepping Kara’s arm with antiseptic.

    “I know- I know you’re angry with me. I’m hoping that-” Kara’s voice cracked- “when I’m gone, the fog will clear. Working with Lex, this isn’t you.”

    On any other day, Lena would’ve bristled at the implication. But she couldn’t find the rage in her now, not when she was becoming less and less certain that she would ever speak to Kara again. “Maybe you don’t know me at all,” Lena said quietly.

    “I know you,” Kara said, “I know you’re not a villain. I know that’s not who you set out to be.”

    “You’ll feel a small pinch,” Lena murmured, ignoring Kara. It ached, that these were the final words Kara wanted to say. But Lena wasn’t willing to fight, not anymore. If Kara needed to get this off her chest before going under, Lena would bear it.

    Evidently, Kara was not done. “I love you.”

    Lena froze, the IV still in her hand, looking up. “What?” she asked.

    “I love you,” Kara repeated, staring at the floor. “That’s what I came to tell you. And I wasn’t sure whether to- whether it would make things worse when I-”

    But Kara’s shoulders sagged, and Lena found herself unable to speak. “Khap zhao rrip,” Kara said, and Lena didn’t need to know kryptonese to understand its meaning. “Your heartbeat is my favorite sound.”

    Lena said nothing, only staring at the kryptonian for a moment before finishing the IV. She gestured, and Kara laid herself down on the cot, looking up at the ceiling. “Don’t wake me up,” Kara said. “If you can’t find a cure, don’t wake me up just so I can die.”

    Lena nodded, continuing to work, avoiding Kara’s eyes just as much as Kara was avoiding hers. Until she was finally done, the last step before putting Kara under. “I fell in love with Kara Danvers,” Lena murmured. “I still… don’t know if she’s real. But the person I thought I knew, I loved.”

    Lena finally dragged her eyes up to Kara’s face, her heart twisting with the wistful smile that Kara gave back. “Thank you for trying, Lena.”

    Lena coughed back her tears, trying to focus on the microscope ahead, and the glimmering gold rock to her side.

    Injecting a comatose Kara with kryptonite had been one of the worst moments in her life. As she watched the kryptonian sleep, Lena fought off the memory of Agent Liberty’s kryptonite poisoning that had once brought Kara out of the sky. The kryptonian had laid unconscious in front of Lena then, too. But Lena hadn’t known it was Kara.

    Why am I working with Lex?, Lena thought to herself. She didn’t want to be alone - it was either work with Lex, or work with Kara, neither of whom could be trusted.

    Lex had… brought her back. Brought her back. He could’ve easily let her be mind-wiped along with the rest of Earth Prime. But he loved her - or as close as he could come to any sort of love - and he made sure that she was herself.

    He gave her control of her mind. Somehow, the irony had escaped her until now that she was using that gift to control the minds of others. But it will make them better people…

    With trembling fingers, Lena flipped on the sunlamp at her lab bench, aiming it again at the cells on her slide. Slowly, she brought the gold rock - a new type of kryptonite - closer. This should reduce the solar load on the caroplasts, Lena thought to herself, watching the cells with bated breath.

    Her heart pounded as the caroplasts lit up under the yellow light, glimmering momentarily as the gold kryptonite came closer. And then, to Lena’s astonishment, the caroplasts dimmed, the tautness loosening - the pressure relieved. It’s working!, Lena thought.

    But to her horror, the caroplasts rapidly began to shrink. Lena watched with astonished eyes as the organelles shriveled in front of her, going dim. No no no-

    Lena removed the golden kryptonite, refocusing the sun lamp on the cells. But the caroplasts weren’t coming back.

    Lena slammed her fist angrily on her table. Just another rock that will kill her.

    Lena watched as Kara’s chest rose and fell, jaw clenching as she looked back at the monitor. She had hoped the green kryptonite would buy her days. But it was only buying her hours. She needed a resolution soon, or else…

    Desperation washed over her, as she vowed then and there that she would never work with Lex again. Not when she could imagine his delight at the sight in her lab, as the kryptonian fought for each breath.

    Lena reached over, smoothing out the crinkle on Kara’s brow, wishing she could cast away the green tint that ran through the kryptonian’s body. Wishing that she had some answer, any answer, to bring the kryptonian back to life. But the days had dwindled to hours, and all her research came to nothing.

    I love you. That’s what I came to tell you.

    Lena knew that if she ran out of time, she should leave. That the domino effect that would lead to Kara’s demise didn’t necessarily need to take Lena with it. But Lena shrugged off the thought. She would give Kara every last moment, whatever the cost.

    If there was any chance she could see those blue eyes again, she would take it.

    She had an hour left, give or take. And all I have is this fucking gold kryptonite, Lena thought, her head in her hands, as she leaned over her lab bench in exhaustion. Useless.

    None of her other modifications proved useful. She had made plenty of inert rocks, a supercharged green kryptonite that she immediately destroyed, even developed a type of kryptonite that could give off q-waves - also destroyed - but nothing else seemed to work.

    There was just no way to moderate the gold kryptonite’s effect on the cells, no way to get it to slow the caroplasts instead of destroying them. She could avoid most of the deadly effects of green kryptonite, but if it destroyed Kara’s caroplasts and killed her anyway… 

    Lena stilled. 

    Will it kill her?

    Brows furrowed, she pulled over her box with the earlier samples, examining each cell under the microscope. They didn’t deteriorate… Lena mulled. These cells are still alive?

    Lena turned to her keyboard, scrolling through her data, beginning to code a simulation - caloric intake, power output, number of mitochondria per cell - aspect after aspect of Kara’s biology painstakingly captured and analyzed in computational form, minus the ability to process sunlight. When she was finally done, she started the simulation, praying that it would get her results soon.

    “Please please please,” Lena whispered. Would Kara even want this?, she thought.  

    Does it even matter?

    If the choice was between Kara dying of her own solar energy, or living without her powers - Lena would take it. Perhaps it was selfish, and awful, to take away such a core part of who Kara was. But to let her die… there was no decision to be made. If Lena could save her, she would.

    SIMULATION COMPLETE
    Life Expectancy: 47.83 years remaining

    Lena choked in relief, and grabbed the gold kryptonite. 

    It hadn’t taken long - thankfully - to sublimate the gold kryptonite into vapor, replacing the green kryptonite in Kara’s breathing mask with the new variant instead, causing her veins to shift from a fluorescent green to a softer amber. Lena watched her monitors with increasing excitement, worry melting away as she noted the slow death of Kara’s caroplasts and the decreasing power surges in Kara’s body. It’s working, Lena thought in relief.

    Letting out a slow breath, Lena decided to call Alex. And then, she’d wake Kara up.

    Kara’s eyes fluttered open. She found herself in Lena’s lab, looking up at normal white lights.

    She turned to her side, finding Lena anxiously looking on. Kara’s eyes drifted down to her hand, which was clasped in both of Lena’s. “I’m alive,” Kara whispered.

    “Yes,” Lena said stiffly.

    Kara’s eyes darted up again, confused by Lena’s reluctance. “Am I dying?” Kara asked.

    “No…” Lena croaked. “But it came at a cost, Kara.” It was my only option, Lena thought, deciding if Kara determined she was her enemy… Lena would accept it. As long as Kara was alive.

    “A cost?” Kara asked.

    “I… I had to destroy your powers,” Lena said reluctantly. “Gold kryptonite. You can no longer process sunlight.”

    Kara stared at Lena, unblinking, unmoving. Lena wasn’t sure what to make of the kryptonian, as her eyes shift across Lena’s for an indeterminate period of time, processing, thinking. Lena watched as the hint of tears built in Kara’s eyes, a slow and aching relief passing over her face. “I can be Kara,” she finally whispered. “Just Kara.” 

    Kara started gasping as the tears gave way. Lena leaned forward, wrapping her arms tightly around the kryptonian as she began to sob. “I’m kryptonian again,” Kara cried, her shoulders shaking in relief. “I’m kryptonian.”

    In the whirlwind after Kara woke, they hadn’t had time to talk. Alex arrived a sobbing mess, grasping Kara tightly as Brainy spoke to Lena about her data and findings, which she also transmitted to a server he had set up.

    The superfriends took Kara back to examine her themselves - Lena didn’t know where they would go on Earth Prime, other than it wasn’t the DEO - and Lena found herself alone in her lab again. There was nothing left to do.

    Lena was surprised to find it was just past 7am - 33 hours after Kara landed on her balcony. Exhausted, Lena collapsed on her bed, falling into a deep sleep.

    She woke in the afternoon, groggy and tense and disoriented. And… lonely. Her body wandered her condo like a ghost, as her mind settled on Kara. Staring out onto her balcony, a sort of panic arose within her - What if I hadn’t caught her? What if she didn’t collapse just then?

    The trembling in Lena’s hands just wouldn’t let up. She almost died…

    But finally, she glanced at her phone, surprised to find a text from Kara. “Can we talk?” 

    Lena should’ve given warning.

    But she couldn’t. She couldn’t text Kara back. A shot of espresso and she was out the door, making her way across the city to Kara’s apartment, going without a word so that she could turn back if she decided. Kara would never know.

    But it seemed her courage hadn’t failed her when she reached Kara’s door.

    She hesitated, taking note of the voices inside - she could make out small murmurings from Kara, from Alex, some TV noise in the background and a jingle that sounded all too much like the Wizard of Oz. 

    With a trembling hand, Lena finally knocked on the door.

    There was a quick - by human standards - shuffle to meet the door. Lena could hear footsteps coming towards her along with a clatter in the background and the TV noise stopping. And then the door swung open.

    Kara stood in front of her - her expression shifting from tense to relieved, yet still worried all the while - as Kara’s eyes landed on Lena. Her mouth opened as though there was something she was trying to say. But she couldn’t.

    “Hey,” Lena said.

    “Hi,” Kara said, taking a few seconds longer before her brain processed a better response. “Do you- do you want to come in?”

    Lena entered, noting that Alex was picking up her backpack, giving Kara a strong hug. And then she turned to Lena.

    Lena flinched, but she found herself wrapped up in the agent’s arms - the hug was fleeting, but strong, and not without a tremor of her own. “Thank you,” Alex said, before breaking the hug and nodding. “Text me?” she said as she turned to Kara, who nodded in return, and the agent went out the door.

    “She wanted to give us space to talk,” Kara said awkwardly. “If- if you want to talk.”

    Lena glanced down at the floor. “How are you adjusting?”

    Kara looked around her apartment. “It’s- it’s so strange. It feels like half my life was a fever dream.”

    Kara paused, gesturing to the couch, where Lena followed. “I-” Lena swallowed. “I wasn’t sure if you would hate me. Supergirl is who you were for so long.”

    Kara slid down onto the couch, Lena taking her place next to her. “It’s… it’s important to me. But it’s also gotten in the way,” she explained. “Everything I ever wanted to do. Every job I’ve had. Every… friendship.”

    Lena looked down at her clasped hands.

    “It helped me save people,” Kara continued. “And I- I want to help people. In the ways that I can. But…”

    “But?”

    “But sometimes it was too much to bear.”

    “You don’t think this will be?” Lena asked, her voice tense with guilt. “Losing the ability to fight?”

    Kara’s gaze softened. “I think… it’ll take time to adjust. But I thought I was going to die before, Lena,” Kara said. “You’ve given me another chance.”

    Lena’s gaze wandered over Kara. “I’m not working with Lex anymore,” she said. “I don’t-” Lena's voice broke. “I don’t want to fight you anymore.”

    Kara smiled gently. “I don’t want to fight you either.”

    Lena awoke in Kara’s bed.

    It was disorienting, waking up in a place both familiar and strange. Lena had half-collapsed after her conversation with Kara - having gotten nowhere near the sleep she needed - and Kara had somehow still been strong enough to carry Lena to her bed, leaving her to nap. 

    Lena realized that the sun was setting when she kicked off the covers, remembering the feel of Kara pulling the blankets over her body just before she succumbed to much-needed sleep, and she felt her heart flutter at the thought.

    It had gone unspoken between them, the confessions before. But Kara’s gentle touch and whispered “sleep well” had felt so…

    Lena smiled as she noted the water glass to her side, taking it to her lips. And finally, she rose, peeking her head out into the living room.

    Kara sat at her kitchen table, hunched over her laptop and a pile of papers, deep in thought. Lena leaned against the wall, watching the blonde quietly. She can’t hear me anymore, Lena thought to herself.

    Moments passed as Lena watched, feeling a warmth bloom in her chest, before she ambled towards the blonde, her footsteps finally catching Kara’s attention. “Hey,” Kara said, turning slightly. “How are you feeling?”

    “Better,” Lena replied, taking the seat next to Kara. “What are you doing?”

    “Working on an article.” Kara nodded her head in her laptop’s direction. “How large corporations shift the burden of environmentalism on individual consumers, and why that’s ineffective. I’ve been wanting to write this for ages.”

    “Looking for another Pulitzer?” Lena said teasingly.

    Kara smiled at Lena, before glancing down at her hands. “It destroyed my planet,” she answered honestly. “There’s just so much I want to do- so much I want to say. I’m not done saying it.”

    “The world will be better for it,” Lena murmured.

    Kara nibbled at her lip, glancing back up at Lena. And Lena’s heartbeat skipped.

    Khap zhao rrip. Your heartbeat is my favorite sound.

    “Is this really who Kara is?” Lena asked softly.

    Kara’s eyes drifted to her notes. “I… I didn’t know how to do anything but hide, before you,” she explained. “I didn’t know I could be liked for being me. I- I could do things with my life, besides be a wallflower. I could do things that mattered, as myself. As Kara.”

    Lena bit nervously at her lip, causing Kara to put a comforting hand on Lena’s shoulder. “I’m glad I was her,” Kara said, knowing what was on Lena’s mind. “But there are other things I can do. Other ways I can help. And it’s okay that I’m not her anymore.”

    Lena let out a breath she didn’t realize she had been holding, as Kara dropped her hand.

    But Kara had one last thing to say, and Lena could no longer deny the earnestness on her face. “I… I really do love you, Lena.”

    Lena glanced up shyly, before taking Kara’s hand into her own. “I love you, too.”

    Things changed. But they didn’t. 

    Lena started coming to game nights again, and was surprised to find that the awkwardness quickly faded away. Alex would pull Lena into the tightest hug whenever she saw her, and everyone else acted quite normal, right down to Brainy’s longform analyses on everyone’s gameplay. Kara burnt her first batch of cookies in a panic when she ran off to find a towel to use to pull them out of the oven, and Lena showed up the next time with a new pair of oven mitts.

    Kara had quietly invited Lena to sleep over the second time, and their relationship progressed slowly, both women cautious that things might blow up again. But despite their nerves, their relationship bloomed, and they knew they’d never turn back.

    The only thing that hadn’t gone smoothly was the very adult activities that Alex had walked in on, after one game night where she had forgotten her keys. Kara and Lena never spoke of The Incident, and Alex mulled over the existence of J’onn’s mind-erasing powers.

    The superfriends found their way out of jams, often with Lena’s technological help. Kara was in the odd place of worrying about her sister for once, but she found herself also putting more and more stories into the world. Her article on Lex’s manipulations of alien sentiment was long forgotten on Earth Prime - but there were other tides to shift, and no one was surprised when more Pulitzers landed her way.

    But more than anything, Kara felt fulfilled seeing the world come to terms with so much that had been swept under the rug. It didn’t escape Lena’s notice that Kara was on a mission to fight for the systemic changes that might’ve spared her own planet, and perhaps it became obvious that there was more than one way to be a hero.

    And when each morning came, Lena would wake to find Kara snuggled on top of her, the blonde’s head nested in the valley between Lena’s breasts. Lena would smile, thinking of Kara’s words. “Your heartbeat is my favorite sound.”
