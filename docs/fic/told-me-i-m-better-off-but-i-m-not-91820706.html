    Chapter 1

    then what?

    aka, the one where rio teaches agatha how to ghost. after recovering from how PISSED she is about it. it goes about how you might think Rio stayed in the yard for what felt like days after her wife faded into flowers. Billy was long gone; the second Death granted him permission to leave, he wandered in a sort of daze back to his Subaru. She knew that, after some time pretending she couldn’t care less, Agatha would have gone after him. Her soft spot for that boy broke Rio’s black heart, but she stayed right where she was; after an eternity of being impartial, she was allowed to be selfish for a moment. Agatha Harkness had always been the exception to every single one of her rules.

    Her death wasn’t about to change that.

    Their souls still being bound had given her insight into the kind of thoughts that, if Agatha realized Rio could hear them, would have had her sifting through ash trying to tape pages back together and resurrect the Darkhold, piece by piece, to hide again. She was married to a con woman, one of the most spectacular liars the world had ever seen, but her truth was that she ached with the knowledge that Billy was everything Nicky could have grown into if he’d been given more time. She was jealous of Wanda for getting everything she wanted without even trying, and being able to create it for herself if the world didn’t let her have her way. She was furious that, with two perfectly healthy, living, breathing ten year olds, Wanda shut down just because she couldn’t play with the dolls she’d made out of her neighbors well enough. She isn’t anything anymore, Rio reminded herself. Despite just creating the memorial, she plucked one of the few magenta flowers mixed in with the sea of purple from the grass and twirled it between her fingers.

    “Surprise…m’lady,” she whispered, turning the flower into a wilted husk and blooming it again in her palm, holding it out toward the grave the same way she’d presented a near identical flower to Agatha in the woods in a very successful attempt to piss her off. It was repetitive motion, something she could control, something that would never bend her rules. Would never leave her before she had a chance to open her eyes and drink in her face one more time. Just a clean cycle. Death. Life. Death. Life. “You surprised me. That never happens.”

    These weren’t human feelings.

    Death couldn’t be surprised.

    Death couldn’t grieve.

    Rage over the unfairness of the shortness of life, of still getting to exist while someone else didn’t…that was Agatha’s game, not hers.

    A few hundred years was supposed to be no more than a blip in her existence. Cosmic entities weren’t supposed to bask in the memory of stepping into a first home, bridal carrying the mortal (though Agatha often claimed otherwise) witch they fell in love with.

    They weren’t supposed to remember that said witch had a spot just above her rib cage where she was ticklish, or that even in the worst of her moods, a quick jab there would send her into a fit of giggles.

    They weren’t supposed to look back on the time spent teaching her magic, watching her abilities surge lightyears ahead of some of the most notable witches in history, as the greatest source of pride she’d ever known.

    They weren’t supposed to stand under the stars and run their hand over soft skin as they murmured “to have and to hold” promises, and slid an amethyst ring onto a trembling hand. They weren’t supposed to feel baby kicks and plant gentle kisses on a swollen belly right where they knew tiny feet were about to press.

    “I was never supposed to have you,” Death whispered, more to herself than anyone else. “I was never supposed to have you.”

    The sun had come back out and, though real witches knew that these movies were complete and utter bullshit, there was an Ozian “the witch is dead” air to how quickly the clouds had parted once no one was shooting purple lightning across the sky or throwing teenagers through greenhouse roofs. It made the Rio parts of Death ache, a realization that shocked her a little, since she had assumed everything that made her Rio was now buried with Agatha; her face was still only half skeleton to honor the self-made promise to look at her wife’s grave with the same eyes that used to devour every inch of her perfect body.

    The angry red crescent-shaped mark on her elbow (because how does a witch get stabbed so severely with a knitting needle?), perfect. The stretch marks webbed across her abdomen from Nicky and the ones that came before him and never made it earthside, perfect. The silvery scars on her back from her mother, the ones with a full origin she never divulged, perfect.

    “You okay there?” one of the neighbors called from across the street. Joe or John or…did Agatha say his name was Herb? Then again, she never was one to pay attention when people she didn’t care about shared their names. Case in point, Mrs. Hart.

    Death didn’t answer, simply turned her head and nodded in smug satisfaction when HerbJohnWhateverHisNameWas jumped back at the sight of her face being half skull.

    It wasn’t until his front door closed behind him that a realization sank in. When a mortal’s life ended, there was a soul to escort out of their physical life and into whatever eternity meant for them. Death hadn’t moved from the spot since the flowers bloomed, since Billy picked up the locket with Nicky’s hair in it and returned to a life of suburban mediocrity, blissfully unaware of what his mind had created. If she still sat there and her eyes hadn’t drifted from this spot…

    “What the hell did you do?” she growled.

    “God, I was wondering how long it was going to take you. I was getting bored, I thought we were going to be sitting here forever.”

    “If I remember correctly, you liked it better when I took things slow. Gave you more time to enjoy it.”

    At the sound of that voice, the lower half of her face had become Rio again, but her eyes were closed. It was delaying the inevitable, but whatever she saw when she opened them was going to piss her off, break her heart, make her day, and leave her wondering if it was possible to murder someone who was already dead, all at the same time.

    “Still. You didn’t have to drag it out that much.”

    Rio’s eyes snapped open and the first thought in her head was, in fact, how to strangle a ghost who was making flower angels on the ground. Ineffectively so, her limbs were passing straight through them, but the action was enough for Rio to take the pink flower still in her palm and squeeze it until it was dust.

    “How would you prefer I mourned you, mi amor? Oh, wait. I can’t. You just made me watch you die, and what, made yourself invisible until I noticed something wasn’t right?”

    Agatha sat—floated?—up. “Pretty much. I’ve always been able to find your loopholes, you know. You said you wanted to see me horizontal, in a grave. You never said I had to stay there.”

    “It was implied,” Rio snarled through gritted teeth. “You know I h-”

    “Hate ghosts, yeah, yeah, I know,” Agatha said with a wave of her hand. “Hate ghosts, hate Billy, hate Wanda…I’ve done worse than the rest of the rule breakers combined and yet, you never seemed to hate me. What gives?”

    “I hate you plenty, thanks.”

    “Mmm. Not the best liar though.”

    Agatha was messing with her cuticles now, purely for the theatrics, seeing as she couldn’t even touch them, and definitely waiting for Rio to seethe even more at how casually she was kicked back in the grass next to her own grave. It was so Agatha, in a way that was almost painful. Of course she’d sit next to the spot where she died like it was nothing. That only meant there was an Achilles heel somewhere.

    “Where was the lie?”

    “I don’t think you hate me. You’ve tried. And really, good effort. You put a lot into that for a few centuries of no results. I think you missed me.”

    Rio didn’t say anything. How could she, when for the first time ever, she was looking at a ghost and only wondering how she was supposed to bridal carry someone without a corporeal form. Her face was misleading, she was able to make a mask out of anger, but the nagging “does this mean I don’t have to say goodbye?” was starting to drive her insane.

    “Why did you do it?”

    Agatha sighed. “Not denying you missed me. Noted.”

    “Agatha. Why?”

    “I spent three years ruining this house. I’m not about to move out now.”

    “Agatha.”

    “I mean, no one likes moving. Putting shit in boxes, trying to pretend the candy dish your mother-in-law gave you for Christmas is something you want to save instead of letting it fall out of the U-Haul.”

    “You don’t have a mother-in-law.”

    “Ralph’s mother. Sweet lady, not impressed that I had no answers for where her son ran off to.”

    Rio buried her face in her hands. “You’re telling me that after everything the world put you through, your first act of freedom is to stay exactly where you are? No. Bullshit, Agatha. I’m calling bullshit.”

    “You know why.” Agatha’s voice was low and quiet now, in the way that signaled either danger or devastation, sometimes a turbulent mix of both.

    “You know I…never mind.”

    “No, say it.”

    “He asks about Mama every time I see him. He’s happy just knowing you’re alive and well.

    Or…you were. If you saw him, he’d be okay. You’d be okay.”

    Rio hadn’t planned to go for the kill shot so soon, but a look that flashed through Agatha’s eyes for a moment told her she’d hit her target.

    “I can’t. The things he saw. The things I made him see. It doesn’t even matter that he didn’t literally see, he only had two hands. Not watching just means he couldn’t cover his ears, those witches didn’t exactly go quietly.”

    “You were buying him more time. I think.”

    “Trying to, anyway. But if I had known it wasn’t working and had the choice to stop, I don’t think I would have. It was more than that, it was like I…it felt good. Like I was high or something.”

    Rio nodded, focusing her eyes on Agatha’s left hand. She always fidgeted with the first thing she could reach when she was vulnerable, and even in this form was still trying to wrap her fingers around blades of grass.

    “Hypothetically, if I let you stay here…”

    “Hypothetically? You heard Lilia. R.I.P. Emotion ties them to this plane. I’ve got unfinished business here, baby. You aren’t letting me do anything.”

    The grieving mother was gone as quickly as she had appeared, replaced immediately by snarky, arrogant bravado. There would be no getting further into her head once she had decided it was closed for business, not unless a miracle happened.

    “If I let you stay here, what would you do? And don’t say operating Westview’s only haunted house.”

    “I suppose you also don’t want me to say that I’m pretty sure if I centered and focused hard enough, I could turn all the sprinklers on in Jen’s store.”

    “Jesus, binding her wasn’t enough? You have to flood her local business too?”

    “I can’t believe you’re calling that idiotic scam a local business. Overlooking the lawsuits and burn victims, are we? Besides, I’d be doing her a favor. I bet those sprinklers haven’t been tested in years. Fire safety is very important to me.”

    “Fire safety, or soaking your nemesis’s head to toe designer outfit? Give me something real. Give me a good reason to keep you from getting banished from this realm.”

    “You love me.”

    “I do. But not a reason, try again.”

    “I know how you’ll feel about this,” Agatha started, and immediately, without need for their soul bond, Rio knew exactly what she was about to say.

    “You want to teach the abomination.”

    “You saw the Road. As much fun as you had reading a fake newspaper while everyone lost their shit and tearing the edges of the woods open with that dagger you take everywhere, you have to admit he has potential. And uncontrolled chaos is only fun when I’m the source, someone has to teach him how to rein that in every time he remembers that he’s a teenager.” “You’re going to help him find his brother.” Rio sighed. At this point, arguing with her was pointless.

    “What can I say?” Agatha’s hand brushed against—or through—Rio’s thigh. “I’m a sucker for a reunion.”

    “You’re going to need some help, then. And be nice, or I might change my mind.”

    “I’d fight you on that, but you can see the house through my body. I’ll let you have this one.” “Ghosts can manipulate energy the way humans can.”

    “I cannot believe you’re telling me this,” Agatha interrupted. “Miss That Is A Ghost, I Hate Ghosts…how quickly things change when the ghost is irresistible.”

    “Paper thin ice, Agatha. Come inside with me, I’ll show you.”

    It took all the neck control Rio possessed not to shake her head affectionately at Agatha teaching herself how to fly. It was a little like watching a baby deer learn to walk; she had the maneuvers down, but overshot almost every turn, launching herself into the kitchen and almost back outside via the wall before she stopped herself.

    “Still…figuring out the rules here.”

    “When have you ever tried to learn rules?”

    “Well, I thought it would be the day hell froze over. But you’re allowing a ghost to be in your presence with minimal complaint, so I guess that’s today. Big day for Lady Death.”

    “You are impossible,” Rio groaned, but she was laughing this time. “Lights are probably the easiest. Try…I’ve never been a ghost, so I’m not sure how to tell you to do this. Try to use your powers. Focus, draw in the energy around you, and let it go. Like breathing, almost.” She, infuriatingly, got it on the first attempt. The lightbulbs pulsed, growing brighter and brighter until the one in the tallest lamp shattered.

    “Little too far, but good start. And really, that’s on you for having so many lamps. Open the blinds sometime.”

    “First of all, they already called me the nosy neighbor. That was bad enough. Do you want them to call me the naked neighbor too?”

    “Why would they call…never mind. Don’t answer that.”

    “Second. Agnes was responsible for the interior decorating here. It’s not my fault someone let her run wild in the lighting section of IKEA.”

    Rio bit her tongue, priding herself on her restraint when she easily could have pointed out that the art on the walls, the mismatched patterns on the wallpaper and furniture, the fact that Nicky had a bedroom in this house, all suggested that Agnes was a little less dormant during the shopping process than her vessel could have ever realized under a spell.

    “Focus on the lights again.” Rio clicked a few of the lamps back on, sidestepping broken glass that sprinkled the floor like confetti. “Now you’re going to draw the energy out.”

    “Siphoning from a lightbulb. How the mighty have fallen.”

    Rio held back a thousand retorts about personal choices and free will. “The difference is you don’t get to keep this. You’re going to use it to do something else. Once you get this down, you can get the energy from something weaker than a lightbulb, but you need to do this right if you want to master something harder. You…what are you doing with your arms?”

    If ghosts could blush, Agatha’s face would have been a few shades redder than it started. “I was siphoning.”

    “Bring those back in, closer to your body. This kind of siphoning isn’t a performance. It has a purpose. It’s…if you master this, you’ll be able to pick things up. Stand on solid ground. You may not be able to hold it for very long right away, but it’s a start.”

    “What do you want me to do with it?”

    Rio glanced at one of the bookshelves, deciding that starting with something as heavy as one of those books would be cruel. Funny, but cruel. She walked over to the fireplace, not even attempting to hide her laugh at what she found on the floor next to it.

    “Now…I know you didn’t do this.” She held up a piece of paper and the smile slid right off of Agatha’s face. Billy’s ‘welcome coven’ sign.

    “I tore it down, that’s something.”

    “The fact that this kid worships you. If I hadn’t been a witness, I wouldn’t believe it. You’re going to make it up to him.” A mischievous glint appeared in Rio’s eyes as she placed the banner on the coffee table. “When you harness the energy you need, you’re going to pick it up.”

    Agatha opened her mouth to argue, but Rio was faster.

    “Uh uh. Don’t even think about whining about it.”

    For someone finding out she could get some of her human skills back, no one in the world had ever looked angrier than Agatha did trying to take a deep breath, remembering that she couldn’t, and watching in absolute despair as her fingers passed through the sign.

    The noise she made could only be described as a growl, and Rio let every last bit of her amusement show when the lamps turned back on with the brightness of the sun, all of the burners on the gas stove lit up with crackling blue flames, and the faucet popped off of the kitchen sink and began to spray water everywhere.

    “I meant to do that,” Agatha muttered as Rio waved a hand and eliminated all the safety hazards.

    “Right. I’m sure you did. Try not to burst the pipes next time, I think after the Salem Seven and the backyard war we just had, this house can’t take much more before they’ll just condemn it.”

    Agatha braced herself to try again and just for a moment, Rio could see the nineteen year old in Salem learning transmutation spells under Death’s watchful gaze. That fire…it was the one thing that was never able to be taken away from her.

    By the end of the day, she had mastered not only picking up the sign (and throwing it away), but moving pens and coming very close to stroking Scratchy’s fur with her fingertips. When she failed to make contact, he stomped one of his back feet and looked up at Rio as if to say, “You brought her back to me like this?”

    “She’ll get there,” Rio said, but Scratchy continued to glare at her. “You always were a quick study.”

    Agatha sighed and stretched her ghostly form out on the couch, hovering a couple centimeters above it to keep herself from going right through it. “I had a good teacher.” “You’re still infuriating, you know that, right?”

    “What, any more of your rules I’ve broken today? Aside from the whole…ghost thing…I thought I was doing okay.”

    “You’ve got me thinking…you’ve actually got me considering putting you back in your body.”

    Agatha searched Rio’s face for a sign that she was joking, but didn’t find anything there but her usual familiar sincerity.

    “You know that doesn’t just break a rule. That breaks all the rules.”

    “Teach the boy, then. Find his brother. At this point you’re more his mother than Wanda ever got to be, no matter what I have to say about that. It’s chaos magic, for all I know, he’ll do it to you by accident one day. You’d teach him better if you weren’t see-through. You wouldn’t be using him, not really, because as much as I hate to admit it, he not only needs you right now, but misses the hell out of you. If he’ll be sticking around, he could use a friend. I’m just saying that…if it happened, I wouldn’t actively put a stop to it. That’s all.”

    “You know that would mean you’d have to tolerate me again.” Agatha searched her face one more time. “Brutal honesty, what are you thinking right now?”

    “That I never stopped missing you. Death isn’t supposed to…but I guess all my rules go out the window when you’re involved. You’re a little too good at that.”

    “I suppose if I had a body, I wouldn’t...” Agatha sighed again. “I wouldn’t try to run from you this time. At least not right away.”

    Rio sharpened her gaze, immediately triggering a giggle and that snort she loved so much. Oh, how she missed her wife fucking around with her.

    “I would be willing to entertain the thought of maybe considering thinking about the possibility of us…” her voice trailed off, and even in ghost form, she looked uncharacteristically shy. “…being us again?”

    “Got used to me being around again on the Road, did you?”

    “Never.” A pause. “But I didn’t hate that hug after Alice’s trial. Maybe…you could be here sometimes and hug me more.”

    “Honey, if you were back in your body, I’d be doing a lot more than hugging you.”

    “Easy, tiger. We have to be ready before we risk having an oopsie baby.”

    “Someone’s getting ahead of herself, I just said more than hug,” Rio teased, but her smile was beginning to fade. She had ignored the souls tugging at her, demanding she do her job, for too long already. But she knew deep, deep down, that in any other life, she’d be happy with a stereotypical house, a picket fence, a dog or two. Scratchy, surely he’d exist in every universe. But it didn’t matter what else. Job, no job. Pissed off rabbit and everything. As long as the woman on the couch was the same one Rio was staring so intently at, memorizing every detail of her face, every curve of her body. As long as it was her, not one goddamn other thing mattered.

    “You have to go, don’t you?”

    Always intuitive, her witch.

    “I’ll be back. Promise. Someone needs to keep an eye on you so the house doesn’t explode. Will you be good while I’m gone?”

    “Probably not. But just for today, I’ll leave Jen alone. Give her one last day of freedom thinking I fucked off for good.”

    “You’re kind of the worst.”

    “And that’s why you love me. Hey, test me one more time,” Agatha whispered, closing her eyes to concentrate, and if Rio hadn’t seen it happen right in front of her, she would have sworn she was seeing things. Agatha wasn’t quite corporeal, but when Rio bent down and felt her lips make contact with a (very cold) forehead, there were tears in her eyes.

    She was going to get her wife back. And she was never going to let her go again.

    “Go, you’ll be late for work,” Agatha teased, becoming transparent again and waving her to the door. “I still don’t know who you report to, but don’t want to piss the boss off.”

    Rio just shook her head. No, her job wouldn’t hurt them this time. It would be a fact of her existence, but this life would be, too. Death never got second chances, not ever. She’d be holding on to this one forever.

    “What’s the thing stupid couples say? ‘I hate seeing you leave, but I love watching you go?’” Rio paused at the missing front door (she’d have to fix that) and turned around. “What did you just say?”

    “You have a nice ass, babe. Just appreciating the view.”

    Rio knew this well, the side of Agatha that would creep into vulnerable, freak herself out, and run to hide behind jokes and innuendos. The side where every single one of those innuendos really meant “I love you.”

    “My view’s not so bad either.” She pressed her fingertips to her lips. “Te veo…mi amor.”
