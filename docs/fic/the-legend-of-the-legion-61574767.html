Brainy goes back to the future.

the legend of the legion

 

The tale is old, as it is time. 

 

The tale is old, as are the dark ages. 

 

Perhaps it is predestined, because the tale— ancient as it is— bears with it the beginning of the dark ages. 

 

And darkness, after all, is the only answer to the absence of light. 

 

 

May, 2020.

 

There’s blood on his hands. 

 

There’s blood on his hands, figuratively and literally— maybe more of the first one. He should have known, he should have seen it coming, that working with Lex Luthor wouldn’t help— helping a man known for his misdeeds never helps anyone. He’d learned that before, with his mother, with Mon-El, now with Lex— for a 12 level intellect, he seems to fall too much into the same mistake. 

 

And now there’s blood on his hands. Dripping. Falling into the floor and forming a small red pond he can’t look away from. 

 

Red and green are usually a happy mix. Gives away a good feeling, a nice feeling. People don’t think of death and destruction when they see green and red. They think of Christmas and watermelons and flowers and frogs and birds. But the dark red of Lex’s blood contrasts against the green of his hands and he can’t think of anything nice .

 

The fight against Leviathan was easier than anyone expected. Nia comes to save him, everyone close behind. There’s yelling. An explosion. A fight he’s too weak to participate in. And then there’s Lex Luthor, all powerful and all knowing, in the fortress. But there’s a fight that ends up with the man on top of him, trying to squeeze the breath out of him. And then there’s a bang.

 

A shot that runs through the usually quiet fortress and Lex’s eyes loses all life right on top of him. He could only watch as the blood pours out of the human’s chest, staining Brainy’s suit, his hands, his face. And then he falls to the side, no grace as he does. And Brainy is left, breathless, with the view of Lena, hands raised holding the gun that killed her brother. 

 

All over again.

 

Guns aren’t dirty, Brainy believes that. Guns are elegant, in how they are built with the single purpose of ending stories with the cleanest crack of a projectile. They don’t leave your hands drenched in red, if you’re smart while using them. They do leave a trace, but it’s easier to steer clear from the law than it is from the confines of one’s mind. 

 

Death induced by a silver bullet to the chest. Remarkable. Especially when shooting someone it’s all brawn compared to the brains of the brightest of Brainy’s type, as plans to shut down the polluted systems of the computer tyrants his planet was once infested with were conjured.

 

Public electrocutions kept the entertainment industry afloat throughout all of his childhood. He’s used to hacking and rebooting, to meddling with interfaces until Coluans were forced to disconnect from the Big Brain as punishment.

 

The warmth of Lex’s blood seeping through his suit and pooling around them is a whole new story. 

 

In the midst of a whirlpool of emotions, his eyes flit over to Lena, who’s lost her brother to her own hands. And, for the first time after battling the same battle from two opposite sides, their camaraderie feels more palpable than ever.

 

Brainy trusts the woman behind the trigger to be more than efficient while hiding the trail. That’s for sure. Nonetheless, he doesn’t trust Lena, nor himself, to escape the emotional scarring of what just happened without leaving part of their souls behind to freeze over with Lex’s corpse at the fortress. 

 

And the concept of having a soul as a techno-organic being tastes a bit weird when he mentions it aloud to himself – feelings are a different breed – but he knows leaving it behind won’t leave a tangible trace, per se. Not a bloodied imprint that could be traced back to them and the scene. 

 

Yet, acknowledged the existence of his essence beyond inhibitions, the part of his soul that seems to be crawling out of his skin? It’s a small piece of something bigger in the scale of things – infinitesimally bigger.

 

Blood.

 

It’s too much of a new concept to have blood on his hands. 

 

Long after the brigade of superheroes leave, Brainy securing Nia on his arms the same way he saw Kara do with Lena, the Martians with both Alex and Kelly, it feels like he blacks out for a small eternity before he comes back to himself, sitting down with the pulsing weight of guilt next to him.

 

“Here,” Nia says, snapping him away from the confines of his own madness, “you should clean that up.”


The woman is holding a white cotton towel to his hands, she sits beside him on the steps of the tower and doesn’t give Brainy the chance to take it before she’s holding his hands in hers— slowly taking the time to wash away the red from his hands. Slowly giving way to green, almost as if she’s carefully taking the pain out of him. But it doesn’t work like that, he wishes it’d work like that— that way he’d be free from any burdens and be able to make his own hands stop shaking. 

 

Instead he’s stuck not only with his memories but the ones before him, too. The big brain. Both a blessing and a curse. Because after all, all knowledge comes with a price and none of his ancestors were delicate enough to know when not to take. They took and took and took. Cities. Bottles. Memories. Lives. And they all live on, inside of him. Inside his mind. And the burning of his boxes along with the disappearance of his inhibitors makes it a thousands times more present inside of him.

Who’s to say he won’t become another one in his family tree that doesn’t know when to stop? After all, he didn’t choose the right path in this fight. 

 

“I failed. I failed everyone,” his voice doesn’t break. It’s just still, factual— he’s just saying a fact. But he whispers it anyway, fearing that if he says it too loudly someone will come and force three more dots on his head again.

 

“You didn't fail, Brainy. You defeated Leviathan and Lex.” 

 

“There was too much bloodshed.” He shakes his head. “I should have let the other Brainiac-5's take on Leviathan because clearly I was too stupid to find a way that didn’t end up with Lena shooting her brother again, Kelly breaking her ankle and you getting hurt.”

 

Nia leans closer to him, effectively squashing down the guilt that was separating them, and cradles his face in her hands. “You are not stupid. You were put in a no-win situation and you did the best you could.”

 

“I hurt you anyway.”

 

“That... was not the best. You did. But I still care about you.”

 

“How can you be so kind after... after everything? It's not logical.” 

 

How can Nia spare kindness to someone like him after everything he has done?

 

“The heart isn't logical.” She sighs, lays her head on his shoulder. “When we thought you were dying on the Leviathan ship, you said that you loved me. If that's still true… you need to start engaging with me honestly, no matter what. Even if you think you're protecting me. Because, Brainy, I am not a woman who needs to be protected.”

 

”Of course. You're a woman who stops tidal waves... and who dreams impossible things.” 

 

“And loves you too. But…” There’s enough wisdom within himself to know nothing good comes after that interjection. “I am also a woman who knows when the time isn’t right. I love you, Brainy, but I don’t think I’m ready to fully let you in right now.”

 

“I understand.”

 

“This isn’t… final, we’ll find each other again. But, I just–”

 

“–you need time. I get it. It’s the most appropriate choice to make, some time with myself comes in handy at this very moment, too.”

 

Nia nods, presses the sweetest of kisses on the corner of his mouth and leaves, one more time, but there’s no presence of the rage that was simmering in his veins the weeks before. Resignation, more so, and amounts of hurt bleeding from the voids where his inhibitors should’ve been.

 

Lena told him, he all but broke down over it. But boxes… tiny itty bitty boxes— shoving his emotions into a cube sounds better than whatever he’s feeling right now. He won’t this time, though. He will never work through this pain, this guilt, this rage he feels if he pretends it’s not there. 

 

He’s sure there’s people way more capable that will have an answer for him— probably Kelly, with her patience and kindness, maybe J’onn with his years and years of wisdom— but his feet walk on their own to Lena, right until he finds her curling into herself in the middle of a secluded room.

 

“So, this is it?” The blaze of Kara’s question is frozen out by a slight tremor in her voice, one that Brainy faintly picks up as he slides over to the lab and sees her standing in front of Lena, arms wrapped around her chest. “This is where you and I— are we over?”

 

“Kara, this— all of this, already happened once, and nothing changed for me. In fact, everything was worse after I killed Lex. How can I expect to get different results if I’m following old actions?”

 

“You can’t leave me, please. I just got you back.”

 

”God, it barely feels like I have you.” Lena’s eyes are leaking, Brainy realizes, but he can’t quite put a finger on why. “But I want to, I’ve never wanted anything more. And I have to do this on my own, if something is bound to happen between us... in the future, this is the best course of action for us to follow right now.” 

 

“I can’t do this without you.” Kara’s hands circle Lena’s wrists, letting her hands rest on top of the crest on her suit. 

 

“You can and you’ll have to. And I will have to, too. We must learn to be apart, and if there's a chance of coming back together... I’m hoping you’ll know where to find me.”

 

"Where? When can I see you again?"

 

"I don't know," she whispers, "not soon. I want to learn how to be without you and live, and I can't do that if you are wherever I go."

 

"I don't want to learn that. Ever."

 

"Kara—”

 

“—please, I’ll do anything you ask me to. I— National City is big enough for two people to co-exist. Even if it’s you and me. Just… please, please stay."

 

“Anything is a serious promise, don’t you think?”

 

The flicker of hope in Kara’s face is enough to make Brainy’s stomach drop, the despair in those blue eyes pulling the air out of him. “I would do anything for you.”

 

Lena smiles a broken smile, eyes flitting downwards before she looks up again, resolution set in her jaw. "Then let me go."

 

When Kara bolts forward and holds Lena hostage in her arms, face screwed up in a grimace boosted by the desperation in her kiss, Brainy knows it’s time to turn around and let them have their last moment for themselves—  but the kiss tapers off to shared sighs before he even gets the chance to flee, Lena cupping Kara’s face not to draw her closer but to gently push her away, leaving the blonde dejected and confused as a defeated and desperate Lena, please leaves her lips. 

 

At length, he spins one-eighty when Lena breaks all points of contact from Kara’s grip. Too intimate a situation, too heartbreaking a moment to stay and watch his best friends wipe away their tears. 

 

But if he catches the tail of a teary I won’t stop thinking about you, then he blames the eavesdropping to proximity.

 

Boxes.

 

That’s a concept he is familiar with.

 

One that he almost reminds Lena of when she walks out of the improvised med bay, breathing hard and stopping in her tracks when she sees Brainy standing three steps past the doorway, both arms affixed to his back.

 

“Were you listening to that?” Lena asks. She’s changed the suit jacket spattered with her brother’s blood for one of Kara’s sweaters, but there are rivers of the same scarlet shade swirling in her eyes. 

 

Brainy frowns, then nods. “I was, indeed. Why else would I be standing here?” he says, brutally honest and unaware of boundaries as a term.

 

“I guess you’re right.” Lena laughs. Yet, it is not the same melody from the few times he’s heard her laughing— with him, or with Kara. “How much did you he—”

 

“—are you okay?” 

 

She chokes on a sob and still nods. The contrast is confusing and—

 

“Remember, little boxes,” he adds, placing a hand on her shoulder.

 

“Not this time, Brainy. We were wrong about that.” She drops a hand atop of his, squeezes once. Something warm and nice buzzes inside him. He can trust Lena, he’s always known. “It’ll take some time, but we— you and I, we will get there. I think we’ve earned some peace, after all.” 

 

Why does he feel like they lost? Why is the sense of loss looming around when the villain has been defeated?

 

“And you?” Lena interrupts. “Are you okay?”

 

“Uh, I am. I have a sore throat, otherwise, I’m perfect.” 

 

“How’s your head doing?”

 

“My head?” he touches a hand to his skull, sure he’ll find nothing but sweaty hair, but Lena’s words make the need to check pulse. “I was choked, and although lightheadedness is a common symptom of strangulation, I’m yet to—”

 

“Brainy, I mean emotionally. Where are you emotionally speaking?”

 

“ Oh. ” 

 

“I have a plane with a one-way ticket waiting for me,” she says at his lack of a real answer. “If you’re interested, I think you would make a great copilot.” 

 

“You really are leaving, then.” 

 

Shoulders hunched, Lena sighs. The fight drained from her. “I have to do this.” 

 

“And… you think it’d be good for me? Flying overseas and leaving this place after what happened.” 

 

“I’m sure they’ll manage without us. They did so for most part of this year. But that is not a decision I can take for you, Brainy.” She looks down. “If you’re up to it, there’s a seat with your name on it.”

 

He nods and there’s a flash of disappointment on Lena’s eyes before she nods back and starts to walk past him. There’s things to do here, things he should be around for, he thinks. Can he really fix his relationship with Nia if he’s not around for it? And yet space and time seems to be the one common factor between all the breakups in all the movies he’s seen on this time. So, can he really give his heart to the woman once again when he’s not even sure how it’s working, what’s in it, right now?

 

“How’s Europe this time of the year?” he asks, stopping Lena from walking into the elevator.

 

Camaraderie. 

 

That’s a concept he hopes he gets to explore with his newfound friend.

 

Something like a smile pulls at Lena’s mouth and he can’t help but reciprocate, uncertain but willing to carry on. “I guess we’ll find out.”

 

 

Thus, they all, children and adults alike, regarded the Red Bird as a creed. The light, shining upon them in the sky. A shimmer of hope, surrounding everything and everyone around them. Even more so, casting a golden glow on the so-called genius, backlit by sunshine and the desperation of time wasted to grief and pride.  

 

 

May, 2020.

 

There’s 8,750 kilometers between National City and London. The flight takes roughly around ten hours but on Lena’s plane it takes eight, she tells him proudly as she shows him around— explaining she was the one that designed it. 

 

The private jet is gorgeous, no one would expect anything else from Lena Luthor except perfection. A marvel to 21st century society with its expensive white leather chairs, autopilot and self-cleaning. Lena walks straight to the minibar on the right side of the plane, right beside the smart TV, she takes out a bottle of wine and picks up two of the glasses hanging from the wooden structure on the ceiling. 

 

She pours them both a drink and gives one to Brainy. The clink of their glasses is the only sound on the plane and when he sees the small glimmer of excitement on Lena’s eyes, he realizes this is both a new beginning and a breath of fresh air for the two of them. The plane slowly starts to speed up before it effortlessly picks up into the air, barely making them lose their balance.

 

“Your engines seem to be shaking too much,” he says distractedly as he sits down on the couch opposite Lena. The woman’s hands stop halfway through tightening her seatbelt to look at him bewildered.

 

“What? It barely makes a sound.”

 

He sets the glass on the little table under the window, “The wine is shaking.”



Lena scoffs, “That’s nothing, have you ever been to an airline plane?”



“I have not, I’m sure it’s worse,” he takes a sip of his wine, letting the still-strange taste cover his tongue. He looks at Lena again, “Humans don’t develop completely silent mechanics until a hundred years from now anyhow.”



“A hundred years?” Lena rolls her eyes before muttering into her wine, “Give me ten.”



Brainy smirks at it, “It’s still astonishing. I’m positive it has come in handy many times.”



“Not really,” Lena shrugs, eyes suddenly finding the oval window, watching as National City is left under the clouds, “I used this plane with Kara once, when we went to Kasnia.”



“On your search for Red Daughter,” he nods.

 

“Yes but… it was more than that.” Lena touches her glass to her flushed cheeks. “We hadn’t spent that much time together in a while, so it was nice to just… have my best friend around,” she drowns the rest of her wine before she scoffs, “or— I guess we had spent time together, but between the charming caped crusader and the white lies it was hard to know it was my— it was Kara behind all of that.”



The plane goes silent. The sound waves of the engines become a buzz easier to ignore under the whirring of Lena’s mind. He’s not sure what’s the etiquette speech to voice in this particular position, he doesn’t know how far and how deep the lies and deceiving between Lena and Supergirl and Kara went. 

 

There was only war. Bits of a silent fight with two people he considered more family than most. He was a quiet spectator, all along, watching as his family tumbled down into a pit of nothingness, then something three shades darker than that. 

 

Sorrow. It had been there all of this past year, settled right in everyone’s eyes. But it shone through in Lena, bent Kara’s spine to her guilt. He never knew the specifics, just remembers catching both of them teary-eyed on individual occasions, whenever their names were mentioned to each other.

 

Rage was part of the common denominator, too. But as a subdued substance that spent more time brewing than actually blazing across the masses.

 

Lena had done wonders of keeping her boxes sealed. Even if there was a time where her knees wobbled and she let emotion override her.

 

Brainy is still proud of the performance.

 

He’s heard that there’s a fine line between love and hate. And Lena had done a perfect show of how to blend them, so everything ached a little less.

 

“I wanted to take Nia on a trip,” he says, hoping it’s a good enough distraction and it must be, because it catches Lena’s attention, “It was all planned out. She loves to travel and she loves the beach so I organized a trip with the best beaches in the world, according to experts on reddit, and—” he frowns, stopping himself— “we’re not doing that anymore… I guess.”

 

“And what, exactly, did this itinerary include? During the trip,” Lena explains herself when the only answer she receives is a tilt of the head.

 

What did he want to do? Feels like a rather simple question and he’s always been one to have the answers for everything. And yet— Lena asks and there’s a heavy weight on his tongue that leaves him confused because he should be able to answer a simple question. He’s a 12th level intellect.

 

Why does the near future look so blurry when it’s sitting in front of him?

 

“I uh… I read that humans practice the art of performing together as each person plays a desired instrument, all in tune with each other as they play an ancient piece, I’d like to see that,” he says, after a while, a bit of wonder in his voice. 

 

Music, a trivial thing back in his time, one that was just rhythms put together and songs sung by anyone that dared. No professionals. No security. No way to know what is expected. His probabilities failed one too many times, enough for him to hold a grudge on the street music of the 31st century. 

 

He had confided this desire to Nia and while the concert she had taken him to was fun, it also left his ears ringing, his nose filled with the smoke of all the cigarettes stomped down around them— but Nia had send an euphoric smile his way, kissed him goodnight under the blinking lamp post of her building. Despite the lows, he considered the night a success.

 

“An orchestra, you mean.” Lena smiles warmly. “I’m a big fan of the London Symphony Orchestra, and also kind of own a big percentage of their concert hall. Just say the word and I’ll take you there, if you’d like.”


He smiles back, excitement bubbling up inside himself now that there aren’t inhibitors or mental boxes to bound him to coldness. It’s great, it’s been a fun ride to have ditched the blues and seized the lush green of authenticity.

 

“I’d love that, actually.”

 

“Then it’s settled. Is there anything else Mr. Brainy would like to see?” Lena asks. and confusion sews a frown on her face, “I never asked but… that’s your name, right? Brainy?”

 

“No.” The taste of his beverage becomes overwhelming after the fourth sip, nothing compared to the saccharine richness of the ginger juice he orders when they meet at Al’s bar. He pushes his glass aside, joining his fingers to form a triangle across his lap. “It is a nickname given from my fellow Legionnaires. My birth name is Querl Dox.”

 

“It’s my pleasure to make your acquaintance, Querl Dox,” Lena says, stretching her hand out for him to take. And he does, albeit still confused with her cryptic comments.

 

“We have known each other for a long time now, Lena.” 

 

She laughs, shakes her head with what Brainy can tell looks a bit like fondness. “It’s just a dumb expression. However, let me remind you how grateful I am that you are a friend, and that you decided to come with me. I’m starting to feel like this journey is almost synchronized by fate.”

 

“There’s some truth to your words, but fate is not something to be toyed wi—”

 

“—let’s be glad we’re here. I have some ginger root for you, American Alien, what do you say?”

 

That perks him up. “I’m in.”

 

 

 

 

It only took a second for Lena to realize it. 

 

One second in which he extended his ID to the man in the airport and he laughed, boldly and unashamed, at Brainy’s real name. Barney Fife. It usually never bothered him, even if he had gotten an odd look once or twice, he knows it’s not the best combination of names for a human. But it’s what the DEO gave him, the one thing he has to pass inspections and use credit cards and go into human bars, so he uses it and lowers his eyes every time someone laughs in his face about it. 

 

It took one second for Lena to realize his discomfort when he moved his eyes to the floor, it took her five entire minutes to shut up the man with her stern words and it took her three days to get him a new ID.

 

She shily passes the envelope over the table of the cafe they’ve chosen that day, he looks at it confused for a couple of minutes until Lena’s it’s fine, you can open it sets his limbs in action again. 

 

The ID is extremely similar to the one he had before, same picture and same generic font. The birth date was different too, although his birth date is always different to the real one— it’s not like he can add a date that hasn’t happened yet. But the nicest part of the small piece of plastic is he can see his name, his real name, on it. Querl Dox.

 

“Is it okay?” Lena asks, the croissant in front of her suddenly the most interesting thing in the room.

 

He clears his throat, moves away that heavy feeling he gets every time someone does something so considerate and so selflessly for him.

 

“It’s very much alright.”



“Which one do you prefer?” she asks, “Brainy or Querl?”



“I think… in the spirit of new beginnings… Querl would be nice. It’s been a long time since I last heard it.”

 

“Querl it is. I also took the liberty of making up a birth certificate and a credit card, in case you ever need them.”



“I don’t mean to overburden you with these triviali–”



“You’re not.” Her fingers tap the side of her cup of coffee. “I’m the one who offered you to stay away from everything you know, the least I can do it’s make sure you’re taken care of. Plus, it’s amusing to think of Lex rolling on his grave because an alien is using his money.”



A sudden laugh leaves his lips before he realizes it, the comment catching him off guard. The imagine of the man suffering, even if just in spirit in whatever hell he’s in, makes him embrace the whole charade. He takes out the birth certificate out of the brown envelope and reads it over. Same date as his ID, same name and a city called Dublin. There’s not two names under the parents section, instead, there’s just one.



“Elizabeth Kieran Walsh. Who’s that?”



“My mother. She died when I was young,” she says. Eyes glancing away from his face, the easy smile she wore drops into a straight line. “I figured it’d be easier. No sudden people with the same descendant or anything showing up to ask who you are since it’s just me.”



He nods. “You share her middle name.”


“I do.”



“She must be proud of the way you’ve carried it.”

 

“I– thank you.”

 

 

 

But, you see… even in the midst of sorrow, the darkness of desolation, the genius embraced the belief that they, too, only carried the light as a reflection of the Red Bird— set alight by this being’s undying benevolence. When in truth, there was such brilliance to the genius— only seen with the right eyes — that it was their light the one that guided the bird itself.

 

 

Year 3027.

 

He’s snapped away from the consoles by a hand waving in front of his face. Imra rests her back against his table, making Brainy purse his lips in discomfort at the clear evasion of his space. The woman’s five-year-old ring is not as shiny as it once was, but it still sits heavily on her finger— whether it’s because of love or to keep the peace, Brainy is not sure. Although his calculations slowly and surely leaned into the first option as the years passed between them. 

 

She’s wearing her usual black and maroon uniform, her saturn girl symbol sitting proudly on abdomen. She smiles down at Brainy in hello, and he knows, by the way she’s forcing the edges a little and her breathing is not completely relaxed, that she’s about to ask him to do something.

 

“Hey, Brainy, could you go check on Nura? Garth says she’s been talking in her sleep a lot lately, that’s never a good sign.”



“I am certain Dream Girl can understand her own premonitions,” he says, eyes turning back to the screen in front of him.



“That’s not why I’m asking you. You’re her closest friend, and this is the most restless and uneasy she’s been in ages.”



Brainy frowns. “I would not consider us friends. By definition a friend is a person with whom one has a bond of mutual affection, I am not sure that’s the case here. Did you know that the word friend comes from the Old English word freond. Back then you had to be careful to not confuse freond with feond, which meant fiend or enem—”

 

“—Brainy,” Imra presses and the heavy intonation makes him stop his research to pair her with an unfazed look. “Can you go check on her, please? Maybe you’ll figure out what the gibberish is about.”

Sighing, he saves the updates of his work and pushes back on his chair, nervously drumming his fingers against the edge of the desk before scrambling to his feet. 

 

“Thank you,” Imra says, turning left and leaving Brainy alone with the thunder of his thoughts.

 

The trek to Nura’s chambers doesn’t take long, per se. What stops him from reaching her sooner is the sweat gathering in the crook of his palms, how did it even reach there when it is biologically impossible for him to sweat? What stops him from wanting to see Nura’s face, is the probability that this could be one of the last few times he gets to do so, gets to be around her without feeling his heart breaking for the impending doom.

 

They’re not even friends. Why does it feel that way?

 

Brainy stands, shoulders slumping, in the doorway to her bedroom, waiting for the green light to blink and the glass doors to part open. But even with the crystal barrier and the cacophony of the teenage clique training in the gardens below, he can hear the stutters of nonsense spoken aloud.

 

“The red bird is lost… No escape… Jingle bells, jingle bells…”


“Dream Girl?”

 

The door remains open behind him as he stumbles upon a bedridden Nura, the dark gray of her eyes only seen between the slits of time where she opens them to beckon Brainy in with a weak smile. Gulliver sits on the kitchen island, swaying its tail as his eyes follow Lena— calling it it had started to feel weird after the cat had claimed the smallest inside couch as his bed and had officially become the third roommate. Now he happily sits everywhere in the house, his lovely red bell necklace announcing his movements.

 

Lena repeats her ceremonial speech with ease, taking small breaks to sip on her coffee or take a deep breath. 

 

“You have done this before,” he says, sitting on the stool and becoming Lena’s second morning spectator. “Plenty of times,” he adds.

 

“I know,” Lena sighs, her hand raises to touch her hair, moving it from one side to the other in the way she always does when she’s nervous or tired, but stops herself. She’s dressed already, tailored suit and perfectly applied make-up. “You’ll be there, right?” she asks, “It’s at ten am, remember.”



“When have I ever forgotten something?” he asks, “or be late?” 

 

Lena rolls her eyes. Then sighs as her shoulders deflate. “They’ll be there, you know.”



He nods, “I know. Are you ready?”



“Are you?”

 

“I don’t know,” he picks up one of the scones and quietly shoos Gulliver away when he walks over to him and starts sniffing his breakfast. He swears there’s annoyance and anger in the cat’s eyes as he turns away and places his head under Lena’s hand, who instantly starts petting him. “You’re spoiling him.”



“He’s my baby,” Lena says, a smirk on her face as she rubs Gulliver under his chin, a happy sway in his tail. “I will see you later, my little traveler,” she says, softly to the cat before she smiles at Brainy, “And I will see you later, too. I want to check on the lab one more time before the ceremony.”

 

Brainy nods, “I’ll be there in an hour.”



Lena walks out, leaving only the trail of her expensive perfume behind. Gulliver meows in grievance when the door closes, his green eyes asking why isn’t Brainy complaining along with him. 

 

“We will be back tonight at eight at the latest, you pampered furball,” he says, the nerves in his stomach finally turning into excitement, today is the first great day of the rest of his great life helping save the world with his best friend. “It’s not the end of the world,” he tells Gulliver.

 

The cat meows back. 

 

 

 

As he watches the crowd of reporters circle Lena, he’s glad he’ll mostly go unnoticed by most of them, even as a co-founder of Legere. This is Lena’s territory, clear in the finesse of her step as she greets her guests and charms her way into possible sponsors’ graces, her smile as firm as her handshakes.

 

“She was born for this, wasn’t she?” Nia, standing to his left, asks. The wonder he’s felt whenever it comes to Lena Luthor mirrored in her voice. 

 

“Groomed, more like. Built for greater things than the human brain comprehends, of course, but tailored to her family’s needs,” he says, a shy grin curling his lips. “Still, it’s nothing short of a marvel to watch her in her element.”

 

Scratching the string of her reporter lanyard with her thumb, Nia is unassuming as she brushes the back of her hand against Brainy’s. And he’s glad for it, swaying slightly where they stand in the last rows as they wait for Lena to take over the stage. 

 

“I’m happy you’re here,” he confesses, refusing to make eye contact with anyone. Especially not Nia, not when looking into her dark brown eyes makes his skin prick.

 

“I’m happy I’m here,” she answers, coy as she’s ever been. “We are happy to be here. Kara almost cried when Lena finally called back, I swear.”

 

They both turn to look at Kara. Standing tall and stiff, shoulders spread open as she surveys the whole park, always in the lookout even when in her civilian clothes. But despite her alertness, her combat-ready stance, there’s a kind of bewilderment in her eyes, a spellbound fondness as she follows Lena everywhere with her gaze.

 

This. Them. It’s not something Brainy nor Lena can afford to lose. Not when they waited so long for them. Not when he remembers how warmth shaped Nia’s face after he mentioned how beautiful she looked, or how he is sure Kara floated for a few seconds when Lena, swarmed by the sea of suits, spared her a fleeting smile.  

 

Now, it’s December 23rd. The birds are flying high in London's sky, and Lena Luthor’s genius brought them all back together. So if they had to be apart to be okay again, be it. There’s no distance in the world Brainy wouldn’t breach for his loved ones, no amount of time he wouldn’t travel to come back. 

 

“I’m glad Lena did,” Nia whispers, “there are a lot of things unsaid between them.” 

 

Brainy thinks of the long nights trying to convince Lena to leave the lab and go to bed instead, the awkward mornings after drunken heart-to-hearts, the muffled, quiet crying seeping through the walls of their apartment, the fake niceties whenever one of their company’s benefactors joked about Supergirl coming to help the Luthor’s new endeavors. 

 

“Indeed, there are.”

 

Lena walks into the podium with the grace only someone that has walked those steps a million times before can muster. Her winning smile receives the applause of multiple camera flashes and her green eyes soften when they find Kara again, who’s proudly looking up at her like she’s the most important person in the world— and maybe, in this exact minute, she really is. 

 

“It took us quite a while to finally decide on a name,” Lena stars, foregoing the introductions and the pleasantries, they all know who she is, they all know what they’re here for. “It took us a second visit to the history museum to find the right word: Legere . Lost between the shinier and more popular exhibitions. We read the word in one of the recovered letters from the ancient roman empire. Back then, legere used to mean to gather . Which, in a way, it’s the purpose of this company— we strive to gather more information for the world, we want to gather the brightest minds that we can and with them create a better world. We want to gather ideas that would help everyone and maybe, in a way, we can bring peace in the world.” 

 

Lena smiles brightly, her eyes now finding Brainy. He smiles back, an excitement he hadn’t really felt before forming right above his heart. Not the same excitement he’s felt before— not that quick rush of adrenaline everyone feels when they get excited but instead, he feels the thrilling anticipation of the future. 

 

The curtain behind the podium falls and it proudly displays the company’s logo. It’s a squared black stone with a simple circle carved on gold on its edges and in the middle of the circle, a capital archaic latin alphabet L— basically, a turned L. It’s delicate, it’s simple yet striking. Under the circle, in cursive, it says legio honoris, scientia et pax . 

 

“Legion of honor, knowledge and peace,” Lena says, translating the words for everyone around her and ending her speech. “Thank you.”

 

The applause around them overwhelms his ears, yet he smiles brightly at Lena.

 

This is their magnum opus. This is the fruit giving everything up has born.

 

Brainy in his naivety, flooded by happiness and the illusion of pieces finally falling into place, trusts the world to spin in their favour. Once. Just this time. And that , he realises too late, becomes his fatal flaw – the cross he will carry for the rest of his life.

 

It’s not until his knees crack against the pavement that his mind seems to register what’s going on, a sickening growl filling his ears as he bows above Nia’s body, protecting her from the sudden cacophony of anguish coming from the first rows of the press conference. From the corner of his eye, he sees a red blur speed towards the stage, dodging the bodyguards posted there to cover the pale frame on the floor, growing lifeless with each passing second. 

 

“Lena!” 

 

Hearing Kara’s horrified shout split the morning, Brainy scrambles to his feet, Nia right behind him. Disoriented, his breath quickens and panic seizes him by the throat, knees buckling beneath the weight of his distress. It’s a haze after that, his ribs protesting as his heart slams against his chest with a force he never thought Coluans were capable of, and as the officers recognise him, part to allow him and Nia to approach the scene, he has the audacity of wondering of who he is going to be after this, of what he’ll become after Lena. 

 

Eventually, it’s Nia who pulls him from the shock, tugging at his wrist as she stands back to make a call. 

 

“It’s— is she…?”

 

He doesn’t dare to take a look at Kara, her suit already stained beyond repair with the thick, rich scarlet of Lena’s blood. The white button up he had ironed for Lena last night is no longer pristine, a sleeve torn and wrapped tight around her arm. 

 

“They’re poisoned, the bullets,” Kara grits through her teeth. 

 

Fumbling for her hand, he’s desperate to find a pulse. Bile rises up his throat as Nia cries into her phone, only listening to the parts of the conversations where she says it’s Lena and it’s really bad, Alex and you need to get here now. And if he wasn’t so consumed by guilt, by dread, by pain, he would’ve done anything, anything but stare at Lena — gasping for breath in Kara’s lap, life scurrying from her fingers as blood trickles from her chest. 

 

“Hey, hey, it’s okay,” Kara coos, threading bloodied fingers through black hair. “You’re okay, you’re going to be okay.”

 

Brainy stays quiet. Knowing that Kara knows it’s not okay at all, well aware that she’s probably listening to Lena’s heart beat its final beats in this lifetime. 

 

“I— I…” Lena coughs, ashen but still smitten. As in love as she had been the first time Brainy saw her and Kara together, the same heart-bleedingly openness Lena wore when it came to her. “I missed your beautiful face,” she says, her fingertips leaving red imprints on Kara’s cheek. 

 

Kara laughs. A broken, hollowed sound that will haunt Brainy the days to come. Leaning into the coldness of Lena’s hand, she shakes her head. “I missed all of you, every single day. You have no idea.”

 

“I’m sorry I— we lost so much time.”

 

“No, no. Don’t say that,” Kara whispers, her hand having given up on pressing the wound, now cradled around Lena’s nape. “We have— we have our entire lives ahead. You’ll see… we’ll be the happiest someone—” she’s cut short by a sob wracking her entire body.

 

In spite of everything, Lena smiles, bright green eyes swimming with tears. “I’m so sorry, darling.”

 

“No, Lena, you don’t understand. This time— if you… I won’t be able to— Lena, I—”

 

“I know,” she says, resignation written all over her face. “I’ve always known. I love you, too.”

 

“I don’t understand.” When Brainy’s gaze finally breaks away from the red coating his hands, drying under his nails, the breath gets kicked out of him as he sees Kara. Bone white, numb, hot tears overflowing as she bites the side of her cheek. “Why you?”

 

“Someday you’ll understand,” she addresses them both, “and I’ll cross my fingers so when— when that day comes… we’ll find each other again,” Lena rasps, her voice rough. 

 

When Lena squeezes his hand back, right before her grip begins to loosen, he knows. It’s over. 

 

Her face grows slack as Kara presses her lips to her forehead, a tender kiss to seal their fate. She hugs Lena tight to her chest, muttering I love you and you can rest now and a gut-wrenching no, no, no that makes Brainy flinch, sagging as agony washes through them— a world they held so dearly torn apart from their hands. 

 

“She’s gone,” Kara mumbles, listlessly staring into Lena’s closed eyes. “She’s— uh, she’s gone.”

 

However, Brainy doesn’t let go of his best friend’s hand. Not even when Kara gently places Lena’s body on the floor, trembling as she climbs to her feet and clutches her chest with stained hands, breath hiccuping as she glances dead-eyed ahead. Not even when she pushes on her heels, a red blur soaring into the sky after being forced to say her last goodbye to the woman she loved. Not even when Nia grabs onto his shoulder, fruitlessly attempting to tear him apart from… Not even when a portal opens in the middle of the stage, the voices of Alex and J’onn joining the suffocating weight of their grief. 

 

Lena Kieran Luthor, world-saving genius, dies on December 23rd. 

 

The deafening loss of her leaves a gaping void in Querl Dox’s heart.

 

 

And there’s the thing about ambiguity: it shakes the core of everything you believed in until it is indistinguishable and, something you once were fond of, becomes a grudge that holds you back. That’s why perfection is needed, that’s why everything ought to be corrected so the course of time keeps flowing without hindrance.

 

The traveler knew, he always knew. 

 

That’s why he devoted his life to keep it that way.

 

 

December 30th, 2020.

 

No one refutes him when he suggests she should be buried in Ireland. Mostly because the one person he knew that could change his mind, the only other person in the universe that Lena held in high regard, disappeared from the face of the earth a week ago.

 

A rushed love confession. A heartbroken no goodbye. Nothing, then. 

 

Brainy understands. He would’ve done the same, wasn’t guilt threatening to eat him alive. 

 

It’s an intimate affair. No more than a dozen wooden chairs lined up across the grass, all pointing towards the grave waiting for the coffin to be lowered. All familiar faces, too. Nia and J’onn sat to each of his sides, James next to Kelly, and Alex in between her girlfriend and Mrs. Danvers, the concern radiating from them why Brainy’s gut twists uneasily. Far away, two women he barely recognises sit together, twin desolated grimaces on their faces – Sam Arias and Andrea Rojas, Nia reminds him, Lena’s childhood best friends. Even a contrite-looking Lillian Luthor shows up, dressed in black and sporting a gauntness about her features that wasn’t there the time she spent with him and Lex. 

 

Everyone, it seems. Except for Kara Danvers.

 

For hours on end— the morning mist on the headstones dried from the sunlight overhead, the trees howling with the bitter winds of winter— there’s a chair that remains notoriously empty, but when it’s clear Kara will not show up, Brainy stands up. Attempts to get hold of himself, holds onto his eulogy instead. 

 

He is not remarkably good at public speeches, never had been, never will be— he will never know what words to use to soothe the hearts of the people at this funeral like Kara’s would. He will never know how to tell them to reach their heart like Lena’s would. But the absence of Kara means it’s his duty to talk, to honor his best friend with some parting words. 

 

“Living with Lena is quite like living with a hurricane. She leaves cups of tea and coffee on any surface available. She loses pens and papers with scribbles on them because she wrote down her idea on a napkin and then accidentally threw it out. She rushes out to the lab without breakfast and rushes back in with a prototype on her hand hours later. These past few days I’ve—” he swallows a sob and breaths out. “I’ve found post-it notes with random thoughts all over the apartment. Smoke filter for firefighters’ suits. Buy Ruby’s birthday present. Self cleaning cat box,” he chuckles to himself. “I’ve left every single one on a notebook just so as to not lose them and maybe, eventually, make them come true. I’ve put all clothes into boxes and sent it to charity and I’ve donated all her books to the library, just as her will tells us to do. I’ve been quite thankful for it because even if living with Lena was like living with a hurricane, she was anything but chaos. The problem is…”

 

He trails off, looking up and finding the tear stained eyes and cheeks of his friends. The sight finally allows his own tears to fall. 

 

“The problem is,” he chokes out, “it has allowed me to do something for the past few days and… and I’m quite terrified of tomorrow when I’ll have nothing to do but miss her.”

 

He vaguely hears the sob Nia lets out when he picks up one of the roses on the side of the coffin and places it on top of it. His green hand contrasts against the polished black wood, “Goodbye, my friend,” he says, a tear falling beside his flower. 

 

There’s nothing wrong with his brain. Nothing physical in his circuits and joints and yet, he cannot move away. Because the second he does, the moment he moves away it will be too final, too real. Set in literal stone. 

 

It’s not until Nia’s hand places a flower beside his and softly pushes into his shoulder, a kind come on on her lips, that he manages to walk away— giving way for everyone to say their last goodbyes to Lena, no matter how reluctant he is. As it stands, their party of ten is diligent in the farewell, standing in queue while waiting for the burial, for the dirt to fill the grave out. 

 

Exhaustion prevents a reaction from him, yet his stomach churns with nausea the moment Kara lands next to the grave, the earth quaking beneath her feet. Eerily quiet, a ticking time bomb, she becomes enraptured with the wooden box, unaware of all the eyes poised on her, everyone holding their breath — the pallbearers for different reasons to them.

 

“Supergirl,” Alex whispers, Mrs. Danvers’ arm in her middle stopping her from reaching out. 

 

Kara’s blonde hair is matted, the suit now hanging off her frame. Said suit, to his horror, remains covered in blood — same as her face, hands and neck. The rust of it streaked across her cheeks, the last place Lena ever touched her. It’s not intentional, he suspects, not worn as a token from a war lost. Not when Kara seems so bone-deep in shock she’s barely breathing, only moving to leave a flower on the coffin with shaking hands. 

 

A plumeria, Brainy realises.

 

Her eyes, glued to the flower, widen for a second, then squeeze shut tightly. Kara jumps back, agitated, skin greyish. It’s unbearable to watch. To feel, to see second hand the agony that’s been chipping away at him since the worst came to pass. 

 

“Supergirl, are you okay?” this time it’s J’onn who tries, getting no answer back. 

 

Apparently drifting away from consciousness, Kara turns her back to them, tripping on her feet until she’s far enough to take flight. But the moment she flexes her knees in a move as familiar to her as breathing, ready to fade from existence and face grief on her own, Kara crashes to her knees instead. She lurches to her feet unsteadily, betrayed by her powers when she collapses again — the sheer force of her heartache bringing her down. Hunched over, disturbingly small, she breaks into sobs. And only then she’s allowed company, Alex and Eliza rushing after her.

 

Brainy once prided himself on never allowing anyone to kill his friends. But now his closest friend, his sister, lays six feet under, and Kara… his treasured Kara… he is paralysed. 

 

“I couldn’t tell her I loved her,” she mourns between gasping breaths. Her fingers dig into the earth, forehead pressed against Alex’s chest, face crumpled in pain as tears soak her sister’s blazer. “She’s dead! My Lena… she’s gone, Alex. She’s gone forever and I can’t—… I— I don’t… I can’t do this without her.”

 

“Oh, sweetheart,” Eliza murmurs brokenly, carding her fingers across her daughter’s hair. “I’m so sorry.” 

 

Someone sniffs beside him— Samantha. Sam, who Lena once admitted she trusted with everything. Everything she couldn’t tell Brainy before, not when he was close with Kara too. 

 

He has to fix this. He has to fix this. He has to.

 

He makes his way back to National City in some sort of haze. He barely feels Nia’s hand in his, pulling him into her as to lead him back to her apartment. He shakes his head in answer, the heavy weight on his tongue makes him unable and he desperately hopes whatever his eyes are conveying are enough to convince Nia that he needs a moment alone. 

 

She drops his hand and nods— there’s a certain uneasiness in her face as she does so but she lets him walk away into the dark National City streets on his own. 

 

His contingency plan is exactly as he left it— halfway done. A machine lost to time rather than a time machine. He had started working on it the second the legionnaires left— in case they needed him to return, in case the past wasn’t as he hope, in case something like this ever happened. 

 

But the past turned out to be the best thing to ever happen to him and so, in between game nights and date lunches, he left the pieces of his machine to collect dust.

 

He picks up his tools and sighs. 

 

He has to fix this. He has to fix this. He has to.

 

 

However, the fatal flaw about our beloved traveler is that he never guessed, right up until the second it all came crashing down, what it was that he was meant to do. 

 

A whisper. A guiding hand. A kiss. A city. They all collapsed into one, just like hours collapsed into days and days into weeks and weeks into months, to let him know what he was supposed to do.

 

Save the genius.

 

 

Year 3020.

 

The clubhouse’s hallways are exactly as Brainy remembers them. His legs take him to Nura’s room like it’s second nature— a rush on his steps he has never felt before. A desperation on his fist as it collides against Nura’s door. The watch connected to the machine beeps, letting him know the first minute of five has gone by, he sighs. 

 

“Brainiac,” she smiles, the door opening with a whoosh, and walking back to her desk. “Back so soon?” 

 

He had forgotten the right tone of her voice and it takes him by surprise anyway. And seeing her, without his inhibitors for the first time, he realizes they have actually been friends for quite a while now and just how much he’s missed her. 

 

He smiles, “It is quite good to see you, Nura Nal.”

 

She looks up from her chair and smirks, a knowing glint in her eyes. “There you are, traveler,” she says, “You took ages.”



The door whooshes back into place when he comes in and he sits down on the chair beside Nura. He sighs. 

 

“I need your help,” he says. 

 

She nods, “I know.”

 

“I— I have no idea how it happened. I couldn’t figure it out. I’m a twelve level intellect and I couldn’t save my best friend,” he says, quietly, like he’s confessing a sin. He puts his hands through his hair and sighs. 

 

He looks up to Nura when he feels a tentative soft hand on his shoulder. There’s a fleeting thought about how little Nia there is in her features— dark hair and dark eyes lost to time and generations and changed for blonde ash hair and gray eyes. 

 

“The future won’t be very kind to you, will it?” Nura asks, softly. 

 

“On the contrary,” Brainy says, sad small on his face, “I have never been as loved and appreciated. Which is why I need to fix this, Nura. You have to help me.”

 

“Don’t you already know?” she asks, curious. 

 

He shakes his head, “I think I might know. I’m not… sure. I am not used to this feeling but I only have three possible time jumps, I cannot miss this.”

 

“I’m still not sure how you expect me to help, Brainy…” she trails off. “I see the future, not the past.”

“You will,” he says. “It was you who warned me, in the future, that the timeline had changed. It means that you can, too.”



“Then why didn’t you go back to that Nura?” she asks softly, so softly she must already know the answer. Brainy looks away. 


She nods again. She’s not using her silver Dream Girl suit, instead in a simple sleeping gown she sits down on the floor and motions for Brainy to sit in front of her. She places her hands on his temple and breaths out— the light breeze of her breath turns blue and a mist covers them both. 

 

Nura’s eyes roll to the back of her head. The mist grows around them until the room is completely dark. There’s no noise, no time and no space anymore. And then. A flash in the darkness like a lightning in the clouds. Brainy makes out his own face in the drawings, the phone belonging to the man outside of time and his satisfied smirk as he leaves on a spaceship, the sound of the bullet that killed his best friend and then. A song. 

 

“Jingle bells, jingle bells,” Nura murmurs, distractedly, as her fingers fall from his temples. A disoriented look in her eyes as she looks at Brainy. “Jingle all the way,” she laughs. A cough cuts her off, the mist around finally dissipating. 

 

There’s a tiredness on Nura’s face that he’s come to associate with Nia— that look she has on her face after using her powers too much. Like the will it brings to her every limb has been absorbed into her premonitions. 

 

“Thank you, Nura,” he says, softly, “You did well.”



He slowly gets to his feet, helping Nura up and into her bed. She grabs his wrist, weakly, “I’ll keep an eye on him,” she says, “until you fix this. I’ll keep an eye on him.”



Brainy nods, “Thank you, my friend.”



She chuckles, “I knew we were friends,” she says, distracted, “I’ve told you already.”

 

“That you have,” he whispers but Nura is already asleep, quietly sleep-singing her way through Jingle Bells one more time.

 

 

Year 2017.

 

The sleepless hours are starting to get to him. By the time he makes the second time jump, there’s an exhaustion he’s not used to on every single one of his limbs. Leaden, barren, hardly a shadow of the man he used to be. And that helplessness in his heart, weighing him down after not being able to save Lena, had grown roots within his entire system. Withered, yet intrusive on their journey. The type of anger that kills overwhelming him the moment Nura had confirmed it had been Mon-El all along.

 

So much for calling himself a hero. 

 

He walks the corridors of the DEO with strange familiarity. It’s been months since he’s stepped foot on this place but for the 2017 Brainy, it’s pretty much the only place he knows. And so, he traipses through hallways until he’s face to face with Mon-El. 

 

“Hey man,” he greets Brainy, who leans back just in time to dodge his touch. Grossed out at the thought of coming into contact with the man who ordered his best friend’s assassination. “What’s up?”

 

And perhaps it’s the build up and simmering of his feelings for months, years even, of his seemingly harmless yet hurtful jokes and dismissive comments. Or perhaps it’s the way he walks in complete and plain confidence, secure in his own suit and superpowers, as he had just condemned Brainy’s best friend to death. Maybe Brainy’s exhaustion has to do with it, too. 

 

“You tell me,” Brainy says and leans against a workbench. Arms crossed across his chest, legs crossed at the ankle. “Have you thought about your plans for the near future?” 

 

Sighing, a muscle in his jaw jumping, Mon-El answers, “We’ve discussed this many times, Brainy, I won’t be coming back anytime so–”

 

“And I said before that the future needs leaders, didn’t I? That the future needs a hero, to fight,” he spits out, teeth gritted. “I ran the projections, as you asked me, I figured out what happens if you do go back to the future instead.” 

 

“That’s not what I asked you to find out.” He barely pays attention to Brainy’s words, entertained as he is with one of Winn’s gadgets. “What is it anyway? Will it be okay?”

 

“No, not at all. Not even close.”

 

“I’m busy, so if you…”

 

“You’re well aware that in spite of being an organic lifeform, I’m also half-computer and thus a technopath, right?”

 

Mon-El looks at him, annoyance clear in the frown between his brows. “You’ve said so a thousand times bef–”

 

“And that even for Coluan standards, I’m quite smart,” he concedes, already seething with rage. “Which means it was a… piece of cake for me to transport myself into the Legion’s interface in order to get access to everyone’s digital prints. So by all means, imagine my surprise when I discovered it was you who got in contact with Floyd Lawton from the beginning.” 

 

Right away, Mon-El grows taut. And although he tries to conceal it with his faux naivety, Brainy is not a specialist in 31st century psychology for nothing. 

 

“What are you even talking about, man?” he laughs, shaking his head. “Turns out Nura’s lunacy was actually contagious, after all.”

 

“Imagine my surprise when the autopsy report stated that two bullet impacts, a graze in her arm and a bullet lodged in her chest, both laced with a neurotoxin called curare, were what killed her.” 

 

Brainy is used to anger. To sit with it to the point it is so intertwined with his DNA he gets scared of himself. But this is different. 

 

Moving in tight, controlled movements, he turns around to face the man who might as well have taken his best friend’s life. The man who orchestrates the pull of the trigger is not far behind from the one who triggers it. 

 

Mon-El scoffs. The cynicism further fuelling his wrath. 

 

“Imagine my surprise when Floyd Lawton was taken into custody with tens of poisoned bullet magazines, a rifle and a tattoo reading Lena Luthor fresh on his nape.” Brainy purses his lips. “How long did you think you could take me for a fool?”

 

Brainy loathes to think that evil has permeated him as it has most of his relatives. But the second his fist collides against Mon-El’s face, the clean sound of bone cracking sending shivers up his spine, he believes it’s completely justified. The universe is lucky that, through it all, he still couldn’t care less about guilt. 

 

The satisfaction of seeing him knocked-out and sprawled on the floor, will be nothing compared to the relief when he eventually gets Lena back. 

 

He is on time. His watch beeps, hurrying him up. He has the chance to call off the job. He will be able to stop this madness and reverse all the damage. 

 

He has to.

 

 

December 23rd, 2020.

 

It’s risky, he knows. He’s never been one to be risky— all his actions have a calculated outcome. And he knows, as he runs down the London streets, hoping to catch up to Lena, that what he’s about to do will only up her percentage of survival one percent. 

 

“Lena!” He yells, catching the attention of two or three people that are walking down the street. But, most importantly, he catches Lena’s attention and she turns around. 

 

She frowns. “What are you doing here?” she asks, “and dressed like that? You have to get ready.”

He’s wearing the disheveled white shirt and black dress pants of the funeral. Lena’s funeral. They’re dirty and worn out from days of constant use. But Lena is right here, in front of him, wearing the pristine looking suit she died on. He sighs and walks into her space before wrapping his arms around her. 

 

Confused, Lena wraps one arm around him. “Are you alright?” 

 

“I’m—” he cuts himself off. He takes a step back. “Here,” he says, shoving his hand into his pocket and placing a small button on Lena’s stretched palm, “A bullet proof vest.”



Lena rolls her eyes and something akin to a laugh and a scoff mixed together leaves her lips. “That’s not necessary,” she says. 

 

“I know,” he says, hoping there’s truth in his words and every step he took to ensure Floyd Lawton won’t show up today, had stood in place for three years. “But still,” he says, softer this time, “please.”



Lena nods, as her eyes cautiously observe him. “Are you sure you’re okay?”



He shallows the lump in his throat. Lena’s eyes get a tint of suspicion on them when his watch beeps and she doesn’t recognize it, she opens her mouth to ask about it but Brainy doesn’t give her the chance. “I’ll see you at the press conference.” 

 

 

December 31st, 2020.

 

I’m running out of time, he thinks, when a loud ring wakes him up. 

 

Except , there’s no watch on his wrist anymore. And the ring was a meow— Gulliver’s meow, waking him up and asking him for food.

 

He jumps out of the bed, barely noticing the smell of fresh pancakes in the air and the light coming out of the windows even though he hasn’t opened the blinds in days. He runs to Lena’s room and his heart drops when he sees her bed empty— no sheets, no pillows and most importantly, no Lena.

 

“I put on a batch of laundry,” Lena’s voice says from the kitchen, “Did you have to wash yours too?”

“Lena,” he breathes out. Once more, he’s stuck in his own body, as though frozen in time, for all the right reasons. Swamped with relief, overjoyed beyond belief. He doesn’t know what to do with himself, how to manifest his love for this woman without being accused of losing his mind. Even if for a second, he almost did. “You’re here.” 

 

Confused, she frowns. “Well, I know you for some reason didn’t spend the night. But I haven’t gone anywhere.” 

 

Brainy loses his stiff posture, hands going to fix his hair. Laughter spills out of him so freely and unabashed that it has his best friend staring at him as if he was a madman. But insanity is a small price to pay if it means Lena is safe and sound. Here, in their time. Alive. 

 

There’s no universe that can afford to lose her. 

 

Lena has seen him cry, has soothed his heartache and scared his fears away. Still, not sure how to proceed, he steps forwards and wraps his fingers around her arm. Firmly squeezing, making sure it’s not his brain playing tricks, he settles for a tentative smile. One he hopes says you died in my arms and I travelled through time to get you back.

 

“I’m glad you’re here.” 

 

“And I’m not letting you off the hook that easily,” she says, pointing the spatula at him. “I had to find out through someone else you were out on a date.”

 

The amount of pancakes Lena plates is too much, even for two people. And as he studies her, notices the lightness in her step, the brightness in her eyes, the fondness with which she fishes a third plate from the cupboard, Brainy wonders…

 

He clears his throat, scratches Gulliver’s head on his lap. “It is a long story.”

 

“Good thing we have the rest of our lives ahead.”





As expected, Legere takes off like a rocket. Just entering the third quarter, most of their goals are solidified and the projections for the months to come are all positive. Of course you couldn’t expect less from them. A woman who has more experience in the field than years of age, a man who’s as neurotic about perfection as they come. A shared intellect and a wish to do good, to atone, that transcends space and time. It was always meant to happen that way, in spite of everything it took to get there.

 

But… it still feels like something is missing, like a limb lost to the inscrutability of the choices made a year ago. 

 

It’s the distance. Between them and the people they call home. 

 

It’s not working anymore, the back and forth. The crossing of oceans every couple of months for dates that feel surreal, that leave a taste of being stuck because it’s hard to juggle a company and a relationship that are continents apart. It won’t ever work that way. Not when the adoration he has for Nia is lodged deep inside his ribs, a reminder of who and what awaits him. And he’s aware Lena feels it too, how it manifests in similar ghosts for the two of them.

 

As he closes his laptop, done with his daily video call with Nia, the main door opens to the sound of Lena’s voice, the wheels of her suitcase dragging against the tiles. 

 

“Yes, yes, I just walked into the apartment,” he hears her say, probably to the phone. “Okay, I’ll tell him you said hi. Yes darling, I love you too. And I’ll miss you, it’s going to be a few long weeks.”

 

Even with sunglasses, it’s glaring that she is sunburnt – flushed red from head to toe after a long weekend spent sunbathing in Italy. However, Lena looks relaxed as she hangs up the call and slides open the glass door to the terrace, sitting next to Gulliver on the outdoor couch. 

 

For a couple of minutes, they watch the fire pit in silence. Thinking the same thing and yet not knowing how to voice it. 

 

It’s Lena who speaks first. “Kara says hi.”  

 

Brainy turns to watch the skyline, then. How it slants across the horizon, how the city lights make it shine. And he thinks, not for the first time in the past months, that London has been a good friend, but it’s time to come back. It’s time to go home. 

 

“I’ve been meaning to ask you,” he starts, taking a deep breath, “what about a one-way ticket home?”

 

Lena smiles. It’s that easy. 

 

 

December 31st, 2770.

 

“And save the genius he did,” Brainy says. 

 

Eyes from each side of the galaxy, in all shapes and forms, look up at him with the same feeling when he tells the story— curiosity, awe, wonder. The kids are all sitting crossed legged on the floor of the spaceship, forming a circle around Brainy. 

 

The story had been a perfect distraction, making the kids sit still for hours until they reached the peace point and giving their parents a much needed rest. They’re all dirty, some of them even injured, but the story has lifted their spirits in a way only the legend of the dark ages does. 

 

In truth, the dark ages had never happened, not on this universe and not on this timeline— but time is always a fickle thing and there’s memories and cracks that certain species kept even when the timelines changed. And so, a remnant, a fear of it ever becoming true, stuck around the universe and the story of hope, of Supergirl and the genius, kept their fearful hearts and minds at bay.

 

“So, the genius lived?” A young girl asked, the tears tracks on her cheeks dried. 

 

“She did,” Brainy nods, “for many many decades, happily.”

“And the Red Bird?” another one asks, a little older, a little bit more in tune about what they’re talking about, his eyes travelling to the far corner of the ship, where sitting in front of the controls, he can see the red cape and boots of Superwoman. 

 

“They lived together and in love, you couldn’t see one without the other close by.” Brainy answers, “I am not one for cliches and I would not expect all of you to be, either, after all you’ve been through. But, sometimes, there is for us all, a happily ever after.”

The lack of constant talk makes the younger kids lose attention, scattering along the board of the spaceship to play some game or another. The older ones ran out of questions eventually and so, like he’s done so many times before, he talks towards the consoles, to Kara— his last and longest standing friend. 

 

They’ve accompanied each other in camaraderie and trust for so many decades along each corner of the galaxy, their knees are starting to crack and their heads are now filled with gray and white hairs instead of their usual blondes. 

 

Kara’s wedding ring and bracelet shine as perfectly as the same day Lena had put them on her, even hundreds of years later. Brainy cannot judge, he had never taken off his wedding ring either. 

 

“You know, I heard someone on earth changed Lena’s company name to The Legion,” Kara says, placing the ship on autopilot as she speaks. When she’s alone, she always steers the ship manually, even when they’re in peaceful parts of space— Brainy’s pretty sure she needs something to do or maybe it’s just a residual action of having been raised in a time when most things were manual. 

 

“It hasn’t been Lena’s company in a couple of centuries, no?” he answers, sitting down on the copilot’s chair. “Or mine.”

“It’ll always be Lena's company,” is the simple answer he receives. He nods.


“Yes. It is an interesting choice for a name,” he says, throwing a subtle smile at Kara. “I’m guessing it’ll eventually turn into a legion, one we’re quite familiar with.”

 

Kara snorts, “You don’t guess, Querl.”
