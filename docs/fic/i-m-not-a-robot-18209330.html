There are three things that Lena loves about the barn house.

Technically, it is more of a contemporary barn house. Jess had rolled her eyes when Lena showed her photos of it, but Lena loves it. It’s one of the rare things she had splurged on through the years. The barn house is a spacious two-floor, two-bedroom and one bathroom of wood and bricks. The floors are made of marble and hardwood, the design a lovely intersection of countryside and industrial. The place had high ceilings and fresh air, around it the many trees and plants that the caretaker she hired planted in the months after her purchase. Underneath the house’s rustic exterior is its wonderful mechanical workings—one of the reasons Lena bought the place. The machine makes a low humming noise as Lena presses the button to bring up the wooden walls and let the morning sun filter through the tall glass windows. It gives a view to the smaller wooden house connected by the doorway by the stairs. Lena’s workshop.

There are three things that Lena loves about the barn house.

One, it’s literally so far away from other houses that she could blast Supermassive Blackhole while dancing with a glass full of the best wine this side of the country. She knows, because she tried last night and the sheriff didn’t come to her driveway with a noise complaint. It also means she can be in an old, baggy MIT shirt and panties and no one would bat an eye.

Two, she can sit on her dinner table first thing in the early morning, mug of coffee in hand, and watch the trees and plants outside sway in the breeze. It feels and looks calming, and maybe Jess had the right idea when she sent her here to get away from Lillian’s and Lex’s pressure to finally join Luthor Corp.

Not that she didn’t want to. Lena loves science and engineering. She loves reading, creating, and tinkering with things, but joining Luthor Corp meant dealing with her brother and mother on a daily basis about business. She never really enjoyed that. She would rather stay in her workshop on day’s end than deal with that. She’s an inventor. A brilliant one at that. And that’s all she wants to do in life, not meddle with the already messy family business.

Lena’s final favorite about the barn house is the peace and quiet. Quiet is something Lena never really liked, but something about this place is invigorating. Maybe it’s the fact that she was miles and miles away from work and the stress from her brother and mother.

Peace and quiet. It’s nice.

Something crashes right onto her backyard. It causes a loud rumble that has the glass windows shaking.

“What the fuck?”

Lena almost drops her coffee mug as she stares out the window, where the dust settles. She blinks a couple of times. Is that—

“Oh my god.”

Lena hops off the table and sets her mug down before padding nearer to the window. A meteorite. But from what? It definitely looks shiny and metallic from where Lena stands. Wind blows some of the dust—and smoke?—away, giving Lena a glimpse of glass and a sliver of gold.

Blonde hair.

Lena’s eyes widen, and before she could really think about it she is running her way out of the house barefoot. She makes a beeline for the crash site. She couldn’t believe her eyes even as she stands in front it.

It being a small ship. More specifically, a one-person ship that housed inside it a blonde woman. Unconscious, from the looks of it. The ship fizzles and Lena steps back when it sparks.

“Fuck.”

Lena gives the ship a quick glance. It doesn’t look familiar, and she has read even about ship prototypes from government projects she shouldn’t know about. Being a Luthor had its perks sometimes. She doesn’t dwell on it; instead, she works on trying to open the ship. She feels the metal, still warm, until a beep sounds when her hands press on a small knob and the pod hisses with air.

The glass opens and Lena immediately reaches for the woman. “Hey. Hey, are you okay?” Lena shakes her slightly. “Can you hear me?”

A quick glance at the woman shows no injury. At least, until green eyes settle on her left arm and finds burnt flesh and- and—

Metal. Shiny metal underneath burnt flesh.

Lena feels sick all of a sudden. The dashboard of the ship is burnt, too, which might be the cause of why this woman—stranger—was injured. Still unconscious. The front of the pod sparks some more and, panicked now, Lena reaches for the woman again.

“Come on. We need to get you out of here,” Lena says, but when the blonde doesn’t budge, doesn’t even wake up, Lena says fuck it and tries to muster all her strength to pull the woman out of the pod.

She manages. Barely. She avoids the injury arm and tries not to think about it while she drags the woman out of the ship. Lena hoists her over her shoulder and slowly carries her to the house, muttering under her breath the whole time how she was never skipping strength training again.

Lena is out of breath by the time she manages to put the woman on her couch. She likes to think that she carefully put her down, but she here legs were shaking that she was sure she dropped the poor unconscious woman.

An small explosion sounds from her backyard again, and when Lena looks out the window, she sees that the front of the pod is up in smoke.

Crap.
