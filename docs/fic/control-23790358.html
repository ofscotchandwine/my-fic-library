“I heard your talk with Kal.”

Lena pauses and blinks at Kara who was still avoiding Lena’s eyes.

“And you agree with him? Do you want to go to Smallville with him?”

“No.” Kara says, deflated.

“I don’t want to bother you anymore, Lena. Lena carefully drops her salad-filled Tupperware on her desk and straightens herself in her chair.

“Kara Zor-El, you listen to me,” Lena says, her voice commanding, steady, and low. “You are never a bother nor an inconvenience to me. This is your journey towards healing and I’m just along for the ride. If you want to take this turn and go, then you are free to do so.”

“You’re not going to try and stop me?” Kara asks, and there is something small and broken in it that almost makes Lena want to run to Kara and gather her friend in her arms—to hold her and never, ever, let her go. But she doesn’t…she couldn’t.

“No, Kara. I mean, I’m gonna be sad, sure. Mostly because I’ve grown used to your presence here. But this is a decision you must make and I must respect. All I ask is that you make sure that you are making this decision, independent of what anybody expects of you. This decision must be yours and yours alone and for no one else but yourself. Besides, if you change your mind, you know where to find me. My name is on the building, after all.”

The rest of the evening is spent in silence.

Lena continues to work until midnight when, like clockwork, Kara says “The Day the Saucers Came,” and Lena reaches out towards her battered copy and begins, as she has done every night for nearly five months, to read Kara to sleep.

 

But for the first time, Lena does not stop when Kara inevitably falls asleep. She reads the entire poem and weeps silent tears when it ends. She wonders then if Kara knew…

 

 

 

Lena arrives the next morning and finds the lab and the chamber empty. She shouldn’t be surprised. In fact, she expected it. When she woke up that morning, she braced herself and silently reassured herself that whatever she finds in her lab will be the thing that Kara wants and is therefore the best decision at the moment. Lena was sure, that morning, she is going to be fine.

So now she questions her convictions as she walks towards the silent chamber, the echo of her heels clacking too loudly in the cavernous underground lab. And then she sees it. And it shatters her heart in a million pieces that for a moment, Lena does not understand why her heart is still beating.

On top of Kara’s bed is a small piece of paper with a single word in Kara’s careful handwriting:

Smallville.

 

And Lena wishes she could be angry or sad or… just anything.

Instead, all Lena feels is this sudden onslaught of sheer emptiness. It is intense, gnawing, and deeply overwhelming. It is nothing and everything all at once and Lena couldn’t put a handle on it.

It is a void.

Lena decides that that is what she feels; a deep hole of absolute nothingness that threatens to engulf anything and everything around her in an effort to be something, to be anything.

She tells herself that she should be happy. This is Kara’s decision, after all. She pushed her the night before, told her that she would support her in whatever she decides. And now, she’s being a fucking hypocrite by being this confused, selfish friend who wishes Kara never went away.

Inhale.

She carefully folds the note and almost reverently places it in her pocket.

Center.

She calls Jess to cancel all the security passes of people in Kara’s list.

Exhale.

And that she will not be accepting any walk-in visitors for the foreseeable future.

 

 

 

That evening, and for the first time in a few months, she finds the blue-eyed blonde sitting on her bed once again. Lena strips down to her underwear and tells the blonde that she will not stop on a safeword, only a broken paddle. She prepares her bath beforehand, fills it with nothing but ice so that by the time they are done, some of it has melted.

The blonde hesitates but eventually does as she’s ordered and brings down the paddle again and again. And Lena begins to feel… something.

Pain.

She begs then, not for the pain to stop. No.

She begs for forgiveness for being a shitty friend, for being a hypocrite, for not being happy for her best friend.

And when the paddle finally breaks, and the blonde has left, and Lena crawls to her bathroom and, with great effort, hauls herself in the ice water, Lena begs again.

“Please, come back.”

All of them with the word “Safe” whispered like a prayer in the crook of Lena’s neck.

And there it is, the only answer left in Kara’s puzzle. The only one she still couldn’t put a finger on because her strong, gentle, loving Kara will never let her see it. Because it is a battle Kara fights every day and she won’t Lena see it.

Lena presses another button on her phone, lowering the barrier and activating the red sun lamps.

Kara stands, tentatively, but sneers. “You are about to cross a line, my queen. The last time you gambled like this, I sent you to the hospital for a couple of months… and that was me letting you off easy. I told you, red sun lamps or no, I could ruin you still.”

Lena centers herself and walks towards Kara. Carefully, she wraps her arms around Kara’s shoulders and draws her girlfriend in. “Safe…”

“What are you doing, my queen?” Kara asks, and Lena could feel uncertainty and tension run all over the infected Kryptonian.

“Crossing a line. I would do it again in a heatbeat. I love you, all of you. And there is no line in this universe I wouldn’t cross to make you feel safe, worthy, and loved.”

There is silence then, for a long while, as Lena held her infected Kryptonian before she mumbles back, “Safe… Now, where are you hiding your ser—“

Kara never manages to finish her question as Lena plunges the serum on Kara’s neck.

Lena feels Kara’s body go limp and is grateful she was near the couch. She manages to half-carry her girlfriend to the couch and let Kara’s head rest on her lap.

The serum has been perfected a long time ago. It shouldn’t take more than five minutes now, and the pain is nearly non-existent.

 

 

True enough, Kara groans awake five minutes later.

“Hi, puppy. How are you feeling?” Lena asks, stroking Kara’s hair.

“A little bit mad at the DEO for seriously just not getting rid of all the Red K they have. And also a little bit mad at you for risking yourself, again,” Kara mumbles, her eyes still closed.

Lena hums thoughtfully. “I may have a few secure canisters I can lend to the DEO if they insist on handling Red K somewhere.”

Kara opens her eyes only to roll them at Lena, “Seriously? That’s your takeaway?”

“No, my biggest takeaway is you’re still second-guessing yourself in our relationship and it honestly makes me feel inadequate. And I have to take advantage of your infection to learn about it,” Lena says it in her most patient tone.

“I’m sorry. I can’t help it. I don’t like feeling this way. I know you said you see us as equals but I think about you and I see the most precious being in the universe and how did I ever deserve you, Lena?” Kara asks, as she raises a hand to run a gentle thumb tracing Lena’s jawline.

“Silly puppy, I see you the same way. How did I ever manage to be with the kindest, most gentle, most loving, most generous lover in the whole known existence? But I trust your words when you say you love me, when you promise to stay. You taught me that.”

“I’m sorry, Lena. I didn’t want to make you feel like I don’t trust you. I do, you know that right? I trust you with all my heart,” Kara says, sitting up.

Lena could trace all the tiny specks of gold in Kara’s eyes.

“Marry me.”

Lena pauses. “Sorry, could you—“

“Lena Kieran Luthor, mother of my future half-kryptonian genius twins, marry me,” Kara insists, hope and love brimming in her eyes.

“Is that my title?” Lena asks with a raised eyebrow.

“What?” Lena wants to smile at the confusion in Kara’s eyes, but she decides to stand firm and let her girlfriend suffer just a bit more.

“You’re going to propose to me without a ring and you’re not even going to give me the decency of saying my complete title? How rude…” Lena says with an eye roll and mock disdain.

Kara stares at Lena and for a second, Lena fears that she may have taken the joke a little too far.

“Lena Kieran Luthor—bed sheet changer, laundry washer and folder, excellent cook, cold black coffee drinker, kale-eater, maker and breaker of jails or containment chambers, inventor of wonders, one most petty, best sex I have ever had and will ever have, goddess and love of my life and mother of my future half-kryptonian genius twins—will you marry me?”

“Ugh, you forgot about being your queen, but I guess that will do…” Lena says, trying—and failing—to hide her smile.

“So… is that a yes?” Kara asks tentatively.

“One second,” Lena says as she pads to the kitchen and opens her herb stash. “I figure the herb stash is the one place in this house you will not dare look at,” she says as she hands Kara a small box.

“Lena..?”

“I was going to propose on our anniversary but I guess you beat me to it,” Lena shrugs as she sits beside Kara. “May I?” she asks, gesturing to the small box. Kara nods and hands back the box to her. She, kneels in front of Kara, takes her hand and slides in a simple silver band with an odd red gemstone.

“This,” Lena says, her voice slightly trembling, “is painite. It is currently the world’s rarest gem. It is not as hard as diamonds, and with your line of work, I decided to encase it in a thin layer of nanofibers, a modified version of the one we used in your suit so now it is nearly indestructible. But at the end of the day, you are still so much more precious than this gem or this ring.”

Lena hears a slow exhale from Kara. “Oh Rao, Lena… it’s beautiful.”

Lena smiles and kisses Kara lightly. “I’m glad you like it.”

“Wait, but this means you will marry me, right?”

“No, I just wanted to show off and give you a ring.”

Kara groans. “Lena…”

Lena chuckles, cups Kara’s chin, whispering “I will marry you, Kara Zor-El Danvers,” before capturing Kara’s lips in a kiss that is familiar and new at the same time.

Kara deepens their kiss while gathering Lena in her arms. And when lack of oxygen forced Lena to pull away, she grins wickedly and whispers.

“I’ve always wanted to do that.”
