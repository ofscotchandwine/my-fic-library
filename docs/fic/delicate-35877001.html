Delicate

    Chapter 1

    With a pained groan, Kate pushed the door of her apartment open. She quietly walked inside, trying to ignore the way her vision kept blurring in and out.

    The fight on the rooftop was insane, to say the least. Honestly, most of it had already faded out of her memories. It just happened so fast. One minute, she was investigating an apartment. The next, she and Clint were fighting to stay alive.

    As thrilling as it was, Kate would be lying if she said it didn’t scare the shit out of her. She was confident in her archery skills, but shooting a target was different than hitting a person. Ending someone’s life didn’t feel like her place, even if they were trying to take away hers. The girl on the roof was almost her first casualty. Her arrow was pulled back and ready to fly, but something inside her told her not to shoot. She didn’t know if it was her moral code or Clint’s prior speech, but she couldn’t do it.

    When Kate looked at her, she didn’t see endless rage as she did in Maya. The only thing she saw was deep-rooted grief. The young woman had clearly lost someone. She’d been in her element, but it didn’t seem to bring her any peace.

    Whatever situation she was stuck in, Kate believed she wanted out. She wouldn’t kill someone who was still aware enough to know better.

    After walking over to her burnt couch, Kate threw herself onto it. Sometime during the fight, she’d taken a hit to her stomach. Her jacket was stained with blood, but she didn’t feel like dealing with it. She was still thinking about Clint, who basically told her to fuck off. God— Did he really think she was going to give up just like that? They hadn’t known each other long, but they knew the basics about each other. Once Kate was involved with something, she didn’t back down. Not until it was over.

    She knew the situation went from bad to worse, but that should’ve made their partnership even more valuable. Clint was powerful on his own, but he had blind spots. She was more than happy to cover them, so why wouldn’t he let her?

    Just as Kate closed her eyes, a loud clang echoed through her apartment. She immediately opened them again, sitting up straight as she looked towards the noise. Another clang. Someone was in her kitchen.

    She gradually stood up, her hand reaching for a glass bottle. It wasn’t the best weapon, but her bow and arrow were by the door. By the time she got to them, the intruder would probably be aware of her presence.

    She planned to sneak into the kitchen and throw the bottle at whoever was standing there. She managed to move two steps forward before a familiar blonde walked out of the kitchen, a soft grin settled across her cheeks.

    Without thinking, Kate threw the bottle. She easily caught it, raising an eyebrow as she moved it away from her face.

    “Kate Bishop,” she stated. “Is that how you greet all of your guests? Because—” The smile suddenly dropped from her lips. Her gaze darted down, staring at the blood on Kate’s clothes. Kate looked down herself, wincing at the sight. Her blood should’ve been inside her but instead, it was smeared across her favorite cover-up. It was definitely one of the downfalls of being half a hero.

    “You’re bleeding.” She walked forward, gesturing to the spot on her jacket. “Looks like it hurts.”

    Kate didn’t know what she was supposed to say to her. Normally, she’d be able to fish out a witty remark, but she was exhausted. The wound on her stomach hurt and Clint ripped her heart to pieces. Her mind didn’t have room for any more problems.

    She decided to ignore the other woman’s obvious observations. “You’re in my apartment.” Kate stepped back, sitting on her couch again. A small part of her believed she wasn’t there to hurt her and for right now, that was enough. “Let me guess— My window’s broken?” The other girl laughed, shaking her head. “Oh, you have to give me more credit than that.” When she pointed to the window, Kate glanced up. It was open, but it wasn’t shattered. “Am I supposed to be impressed?” Kate brought her hand to her stomach, doing her best to hide another wince. “Congrats on breaking and entering without actually breaking anything.” The other woman began laughing again, walking over to the couch. “Maybe a little.” When she was close enough, she reached her hand towards Kate’s jacket. “May I?” “Why?” Kate questioned. “So you can finish the job?” Just a couple of hours ago, she’d tried to kill Clint. Was she supposed to trust her after that?

    She groaned dramatically, rolling her eyes. “If I wanted to kill you, I would’ve—” Kate didn’t let her finish. She didn’t want to give her the satisfaction. “You would’ve killed me when I walked in. Yeah, yeah— Whatever.”

    “Smart girl.” The other woman stayed where she was, her hand still hovering above Kate’s jacket. “Now can I take a look? It would be a shame if you bled out before we talked.” With a dramatic groan of her own, Kate admitted defeat. “Fine,” she forced out. “Not like I can really say no.”

    “Smart girl,” she repeated. After Kate moved her hand, the other woman slowly lifted her jacket. She tried to stop herself from grimacing, but it only half-worked. The blonde stranger scooted closer, taking her time to look over the wound. If Kate didn’t know better, she’d say she looked concerned. Maybe it was worse than she originally thought.

    “Why didn’t you bandage this?” She clicked her tongue, letting go of her jacket. “I have gotten infections before. They are disgusting.”

    Their conversation felt so… casual. It was difficult to believe that a little while ago, they were fighting each other.

    “I was a little busy,” Kate quipped. “You know, trying to keep you from killing my partner.” The other woman rolled her eyes again, standing up straight before walking off. “You really think he’s your partner?”

    Kate closed her eyes for a second. When she opened them, the stranger was in front of her again, her concern no longer questionable.

    “You’re starting to look a little pale, Kate Bishop.” She lifted her jacket again, then began wiping away the dried blood around her wound.

    Kate didn’t exactly give her permission to help, but the towel she was using was warm and she couldn’t get herself to move away.

    “What’s your name?” Kate suddenly asked, holding onto her jacket so the other woman wouldn’t have to. “Think I deserve to know since you threw me off a building.” The blonde stranger paused, her lips parting slightly. She looked offended and Kate almost laughed. “I hooked you up, didn’t I?” She cleaned off the rest of the blood, then set the towel down. “I could’ve let you fall, but I didn’t. You should be grateful.” “I should be grateful?” Kate threw back. “You should be grateful. I almost killed you.” An obnoxious laugh echoed through the room. “You really think—” Her own reaction cut her off. “Oh, Kate Bishop. You’re so funny.”

    Kate scowled, but she didn’t add anything. After ripping open a small packet, the other woman ran a wipe over the wound. Despite her best efforts, Kate whimpered from the contact.

    “Sorry, sorry,” the stranger murmured. “Trust me, it feels a lot better than an infection.” “You didn’t answer my question,” Kate choked out. Their conversation was turning into a well-needed distraction.

    She put the wipe down. “Yelena,” she answered. “My name’s Yelena. Happy now?” Yelena. Kate had never met a Yelena before. She didn’t know much about the one in front of her, but it suited her somehow. For the first time in hours, a small smile reached Kate’s lips. “Yes,” Kate added. “Ecstatic.”

    Yelena released a short snort. “Don’t get too excit— Oo, liquid stitches.” She held up a bottle, her eyes darting over the packaging. “You got some of the good stuff.” Kate watched as Yelena opened the bottle. As she applied it over the wound, she was almost too careful. She didn’t think someone like her was capable of being delicate. What other secrets was she hiding?

    When Yelena began packing the first-aid kit back up, Kate cleared her throat. “Thank you,” she stated, letting her jacket fall back into place. “I don’t know why you helped me, but thank you.”

    After standing back up, Yelena threw the kit onto the coffee table. “Why wouldn’t I help you?” She sat down next to it, stretching her arms out. “Have I killed a few people? Sure, but I’m not a monster.”

    “Right.” Kate shook her head. She moved the pillows to one end of the couch, then slowly laid down. If Yelena was still looking for a real conversation, she wasn’t going to find it. Not tonight.

    Yelena tapped her fingers against the table. “Your jacket is stained,” she commented, even though they both knew it.

    “Mhm,” Kate mumbled. “Thanks for pointing out the obvious.”

    Yelena groaned again, standing back up. Kate watched as she disappeared, only to come back with a new shirt. She didn’t like the idea of a stranger rummaging through her things, but she didn’t have the energy to complain.

    “If it sticks to my awesome handiwork, then it will all be for nothing.” She held out the shirt until Kate took it.

    Before Kate could say anything, Yelena turned around. With a low moan, Kate sat up, taking her time to change.

    When she focused back on Yelena, two capsules and a glass of water were held out to her. “Tylenol from your cabinet. See?” She tilted her head towards the table, where a bottle of Tylenol was sitting.

    Kate hadn’t noticed her leave again. God, when was the last time she slept? “Thanks.” After taking the pills down, Yelena took the empty glass from her. Kate laid back down, her eyes immediately falling shut.

    “Well, Kate Bishop…” Yelena trailed off. “This did not go as expected.” Kate was the one to laugh this time, even though it hurt. “Tell me about it.” Yelena laughed with her. When she didn’t say anything else, Kate opened her eyes. She was standing under the window, one of her gadgets in hand.

    “I thought you wanted to talk,” Kate reminded her.

    Yelena snorted. “I do want to talk,” she answered. “But I don’t want to start my big speech if you’re going to fall asleep on me. What’s the point if I’m going to have to start over? Such a waste of time.”

    Kate smiled a little, shutting her eyes once more. “Smart girl,” she echoed, in a sad attempt to match her accent.

    “Are you mocking me? Here I was, trying to compliment you.” Kate heard a tiny snap. “Sleep it off, Kate Bishop. Tomorrow is going to be a big day.”

    A rope retracted. Kate didn’t bother saying anything— She knew Yelena had already made her escape.

    She pulled the blanket she kept on the top of her couch down, carefully wrapping it around herself. When she first saw Yelena, she was so sure she wouldn’t be walking out of her apartment. She didn’t know what to think now. She attacked Clint and broke in, but she also fixed her up and apologizedwhen it hurt.

    She didn’t know what would happen tomorrow, but she did know one thing. She was glad she didn’t take that shot.
