A Hero's Journey (Or Something Like That)

Lena Luthor liked to think that she was a fairly mild-tempered woman. She never snapped at waiters or baristas when her order took too long, and she kept her cool while the investors left over from Lex’s days called her incompetent and leered while she attempted to steer the company in a direction that didn’t include the mass killings of innocents. However, her patience was spread thin when she was woken up, yet again, to loud music blaring from her new neighbor’s wall. Give how much Lena paid in rent every month for the damned apartment, she was a little upset that apparently the walls were thin enough that she could hear right through them. That didn’t change the fact that she had been woken up every. Single. Day. For the past week by loud music and off-key singing. Even then, it wouldn’t have been unbearable except for the fact that it was always about twenty minutes before her alarm went off. Those twenty minutes Lena needed to function, and it was always just short enough a span that when she started drifting off again, the alarm went off.

So, more than a little irritated and slightly sleep deprived, as soon as the shower cut off and Lena knew her neighbor would be able to answer, she stormed out her door to bang her first on her neighbor’s. It took a couple of moments before there was a response, but eventually, a sleepy-looking blonde answered the door, eyes slightly glazed with exhaustion, and a cup of coffee in her hand. Still, the fact that her neighbor was adorable wasn’t enough to deter her, and Lena took a deep breath. “I feel that I have been very patient with you, but every morning for the past week at this ungodly hour, you seem to feel the need to play your music very loudly, waking me out of a dead sleep. I am the CEO of a Fortune 500 company. I don’t think I need to impress upon you how badly I need my sleep. If I hear the music playing this early again, I will be filing a complaint and calling in a disturbance. Do you understand me?”

The blonde, who didn’t look much more awake at this point, stared at her for a very long moment before pressing the coffee mug into Lena’s hand, like that was her only solution to the problem.

Despite her confusion, the CEO curled her lip, dredging up a sarcastic response. “I’m lactose intolerant.”

“It’s soy.”

And then the door was slammed in her face.

Lena’s first reaction was fury, ready to bang on the door some more before the scent of coffee wafted up her, and she found what looked like a perfectly untouched latte in her hand, complete with whipped cream and some kind of drizzle. Aside from the aforementioned lactose intolerance, Lena didn’t have any allergies, so with a final glare at the door, she walked back to her own apartment with the coffee. She took a sip out of the blue metal coffee mug that had ‘Liquid Motivation’ on the side in a swirly print, and nearly moaned out at the rich flavor that danced over her taste buds.

If coffee was provided for lost sleep, Lena might be tempted to let it all go.

It was ridiculous. She owned a two-thousand-dollar espresso machine that spit out the perfect latte for her on a timer every single morning, but it didn’t taste nearly as good was whatever the concoction was that her neighbor had provided. She had only woken up to her own alarm that morning, so she was in a good mood, but it quickly vanished upon realizing that she clearly wasn’t going to be happy with whatever she usually had and sighed heavily before grabbing the mug she’d cleaned the night before and dragging herself to her neighbor’s door.

She only had to knock once this time, and lightly, before it swung open, the same blonde from the previous morning greeting her, slightly more awake, but no less ruffled. With a small smile, Lena held up the mug. “I think I owe you an apology.”

Her neighbor smiled, taking it from her. “Pretty sure you’ve got that backwards. It was really my fault. Well, not my fault, my sister’s fault, but the whole thing was my idea. It’s a whole story,” she took a breath, fumbling with glasses perched precariously on her nose. “And you probably don’t want to hear it. Besides, she was only here for a week and she left last night.”

Contrarily, Lena could listen to her go on all day, but she had come over there for more reason than one. “I actually had a question for you.”

“Shoot.”

The little smirk Unnamed Neighbor was giving her almost made her melt, but Lena held her feelings in. “How do you make your coffee? Because it was freaking amazing, and my espresso machine disappointed me this morning.”

The other woman laughed, perhaps a little too loud, but unrestrained, and Lena kind of liked that. “I worked at Noonan’s for four years. I learned a thing or two.” She bit her lip, smiling a little beneath it. “If you have the time, you could come in and I can show you.”

She didn’t have the time; not really. But Lena Luthor was a useless lesbian at the best of times, so she grinned back. “I’d love to.”

She missed two meetings and a phone call with her investors but learning the proper way to make her coffee was far more important. Or perhaps it was the time spent with the Not-So-Unnamed-Neighbor, Kara Danvers, that was important. Either way, Lena had zero regrets.

Morning coffee became a regular thing when Lena proved hopeless, both at the coffee machine, and in her ability to string one particular question together for her neighbor. After yet another failed latte attempt, Lena knocked lightly on Kara’s door, Liquid Motivation mug in hand. It had unofficially become her cup after the third or fourth time Kara had sent it home with her, and they no longer questioned it. When the door swung open, Lena gave her best pout and held up the cup. “Please?”

Kara’s laugh bounced around the hall before she let Lena in, plucking the mug from her fingers. “Lucky for you, I’ve already got the machine set up.”

She swirled together a couple different kinds of syrups, adding espresso, and then the milk. It was all domestic enough to make Lena sick and her chest ached with the pure want of it all. She tried psyching herself up to say the words, to ask her out. What came out instead was, “So how did you go from Noonan’s to living in a building like this?”

Kara smiled at her (kept smiling, she never seemed to stop when Lena was around, and the CEO didn’t really know how to take that). “Well, between working at Noonan’s and this, I was actually Cat Grant’s assistant.”

Lena’s eyes widened. “Wait, I thought her assistant’s name was Kiera.”

“She never could get my name right. Pretty sure it was on purpose.”

“You do realize you’re like some kind of corporate legend, right? If anyone wanted something done, they just needed to call in a favor with Cat Grant or ask for one because Kiera would make it happen.”

There was that laugh again, this time accompanied by a blush. “Yeah, my cousin is a reporter, so I have a lot of connections through him.”

“I didn’t realize Cat paid this well.”

“She doesn’t. I just happened to be working on my manuscript when she sent me on some file run, probably on purpose so she could see what I was writing. I guess she liked what she saw because she encouraged me to finish it and pushed to get it published. Through CatCo, of course. And it was kind of popular. Pretty much blew up overnight and I ended up with enough money from it to be able to move here.”

Lena grinned at her. “Wow. What’s the book called? I haven’t had the time to read lately, so I haven’t checked the best-seller list in a while.”

Kara’s blush deepened. “A Hero’s Journey.”

“That sounds like it’s a reference to something, but I can’t place it.”

“It’s basically the framework for every heroic tale. You have the hero who started in a boring place and ends up going on this whole adventure and then returning triumphant.” It sounded like an oversimplification, but Lena could do her own research on it later. “The story is kind of a spoof on the whole thing. I spend about four hundred pages making fun of the guideline for adventure stories. I guess it was popular, though. Ms. Grant’s been pushing me to write a sequel, but I don’t know about that yet. Have to see if I can think up a plot for it.”

Lena smiled at her. “Well, I’m sure you’re far more talented that you give yourself credit for.”

They share coffee and laughs and smiles until Lena has to go to work.

As soon as she gets in, she tells Jess to get her a copy of the damned book, despite her assistant’s confusion. Lena hadn’t picked up a book in years, especially not something in that genre, but she was more than willing to do so for Kara.

She promptly finished the book in one night, and all she can do is be thankful it’s a Friday because it was long after two AM when she was through and could finally fall asleep.

A Hero’s Journey followed an idiot that continuously made the wrong decisions because of his stupidity and drags along the female side character after her initial refusal. He follows along the steps of the books title while somehow managing to make bad choice after bad choice and eventually manages to go up against the villain, an evil sorceress that is written as a beautiful badass and what was really what made Kara break the internet, the art published of her alongside the novel succeeding in flustering just about everyone.

In the end, the hero makes the choice to trade his friend for what he thought was needed to complete his journey, though at that point, the woman had all but volunteered to get away from him. He leaves with a vial that he thinks is a cure to poison, friend in the clutches on the sorceress, not even looking back.

The epilogue was really what had Lena intrigued, however:

            As Estella watched Jeremy walk away, no thought to their friendship or what had transpired to get them here, she frowned. While they had no lost love after the disaster of their trip, she had still expected something. Perhaps some regret at trading her life away for some puny vial that they probably could have had Jefferson brew for them? Had he really cared about her that little?

            A finger underneath Estella’s chin drew her attention away from her former-friend’s retreating figure, as it pulled her face towards the woman she was standing next to. “He was a fool to let you go so easily, dearest.”

            Estella arched an eyebrow. “As if I would let anyone take me easily. I’m here because I want to be.”

            “Oh, I am well aware." The witch smirked. “I knew you wanted to be here. It’s why I asked for the exchange. I would never actually trade a human life.”

            “You wouldn’t.”

            “No. And I despise all those that do. The little boy will get what is coming to him. After all, it wasn’t his hero’s journey. It was yours. And every hero has to suffer a sacrifice.”

            “What are you talking about?”

            “The tasks were all for you. Tailored for you to better succeed. It was you who initially refused the call and had to be convinced. You who connected better with the mentor, who listened to his advice. You who is truly facing the villain of the story.”

            Estella scoffed. “Oh, I don’t think I’d consider you to be a villain.”

            “I’m not.”

            That stumped her. “If not you, then who? You were fighting us at every turn, trying to keep us away from the antidote. You poisoned our town.”

            The sorceress chuckled. “You have managed to say every fact wrong.”

            “What?”

            “I was preventing him from coming to me. I attempted to keep him away from you, lest he hurt you too. It was him who poisoned your town. My only offense was having the power to save them.”

            Estella shook her head. She’d expected manipulation on some level, but this was beyond that. “No. Jeremy wasn’t a good guy, but he would never hurt people.”

            “Wouldn’t he?”

            And then she thought back to their childhood, and why they hadn’t been friends back then. The times she caught him throwing rocks at animals only to tearfully take them to the vet and say he’d stopped people from hurting them. How he’d let a rat loose in the classroom so he could catch it later and play hero. That was all he’d ever done. Cause problems and then find a way to solve them so he would be the hero. This whole trip. All because… “He really killed his own mother so he could be a hero?”

“Indeed, my dear. And then he created a villain to go with his own deluded story.”

Estella swallowed thickly. “We have to stop him. He’s only going to do it again.”

“I never gave him the antidote. Just a vial of water.” The witch waved a hand, purple magic clearing to show a blue vial. “This is the true antidote. And it will save the people. Your real journey will be exposing his treachery. Are you up for the task?”

Estella took the cure, studying it for a moment before tucking it into the pocket of her jacket. “I am.”

“Good.” Kind brown eyes stared deeply into her own as she was handed a bag. “Provisions for your trip. And,” the adventurer was surprised when full lips pressed against her own. “A protection spell. To keep you safe.”

Estella’s heart raced, but she couldn’t tell if it was from the kiss or the excitement of magic. “You’re not coming with me?”

“I would only cause distress after the slander of that man-child. My spell will protect you, but should you desire my help, you need only say my name, and I will hear you.”

“And what is your name?”

“Mazikeen.”

A chill blew through the cave where they stood and when Estella blinked, the witch was gone, though her name echoed in the heroine’s mind.

Without another thought, she stepped out into the light.

Lena’s jaw dropped, blinking dry eyes and startled to see that it was now early morning, faint light coming in through the window. She stumbled out of bed, taking her coffee cup more on instinct than intention, and stormed next door, banging on the wood.

When a sleep ruffled Kara opened up, rubbing at tired eyes, Lena didn’t bother to hold back. “You just left it there, and act like you can’t think up a sequel?!”

Kara blinked at her a few times before it registered and Lena was waving her book around, and yawned, leaving to door open as she walked towards the kitchen. “I thought what came next was kind of implied.”

“Implied?!” Lena shut the door behind her with a groan. “You tease us all with romance between Estella and Mazikeen and then you just leave it there! You know all the straights are going to call them gal pals if you don’t write a sequel where they kill Jeremy and live out a happy gay life! Or even worse,” Lena almost gagged at the thought. “They’ll try to say Jeremy can be redeemed.”

Kara’s nose scrunched in distaste. “I literally made him a puppy-killer. There’s no coming back from that.”

“Yeah, but people are dumb. You have to keep the story going.”

The author chuckled a little, taking Lena’s mug to start her coffee. “And you practically broke down my door at five in the morning on a Saturday to tell me that?”

Lena pursed her lips. “To be fair, I just finished it and I had no idea it was this early.”

“You’re lucky I love you.”

They both froze when Kara said the words, Lena with her jaw wide open, and Kara with the mug halfway outstretched. While they’d had this weird coffee-friendship thing going for almost six months now, they hadn’t even had a date, and oh gods, what if she just meant it platonically. Lena was going to self-destruct.

Kara wet her lips, back straightening. “Go out with me?”

Okay it was happening. Stay calm, Lena, keep your cool. “Yes.” It would have been a success if not for the fact that Lena sounded like she’d been punched in the stomach.

“Yes?” The blonde sounded like she couldn’t believe it.

“Yes.” More confident this time. “I’d love to go out with you.”

A wide grin bloomed on her neighbor’s face and Lena couldn’t help but return it. “I’d invite you for a coffee, but I think we might be a little past that.”

“Good for you, I happen to know a great Italian place that also serves coffee, so we can keep up the tradition. I don’t think it’s as good as yours, but I don’t think anything could really compare.”

“Tonight, at seven?”

“I’ll be waiting.”
