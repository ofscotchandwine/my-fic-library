            They were all spread out throughout the living room of J’onns house, sprawled on sofas and armchairs or on the floor, the coffee table a mess of beer bottles and wine glasses in varying states of emptiness. It had been, for a while, while the DEO still stood. Now, they were truly vigilantes and the work had never gotten any easier.

Now, there were enemies in every corner, somehow impossibly stronger with each new one, testing her limits. She knew eventually she’d fail, but she hadn’t yet, through good fortune or the benefit of being best friends with a genius billionaire with enough time on her hands to create life-saving technology, but things with Lena weren’t the same anymore.

 

            But they weren’t bad. Not as bad as they’d been before, with the deceit and distrust, the anger and wounded feelings on both sides. They’d come to an arrangement now, a secret one that no one in the room knew about, and Kara knew that they never could. Her friends hadn’t forgiven Lena for the part she’d played in her brother’s schemes, but they also didn’t know her like Kara did. They didn’t understand . And Kara did understand, which was why she could so easily forgive Lena, because she’d put her through so much, knowing how much it would hurt her.

 

            It hadn’t been easy for them to come to a truce, to acknowledge that they couldn’t do without each other, but they’d done it. In secret, they had their uneasy friendship, overshadowed by their past together, but they also had more . Because through all of it, the one thing that had become startlingly clear to them both was that they loved each other. 

 

            And not in the way they’d thought before, when Kara would tell Lena, the words rolling off her tongue with ease, shared with all her friends a thousand times before, with Lena’s wry acknowledgement of them, even though she could never say it back. For all she’d been through in her childhood, the trauma making it unbearable for her to even imagine letting herself be so vulnerable with someone else in such a manner, Lena still loved Kara though and had known that she loved her too. But it wasn’t just in the way friends loved each other, because while Kara might’ve loved all her friends, Lena hadn’t. There had always been that distinct divide, but with Kara, it was blurred.

 

            It had taken her a while to parse through that knowledge while they tried to fix what had been so easily ruined, but the one thing they both came to understand was that it had hurt so much because they truly did love each other. In every sense of the word, even if they couldn’t say it. But they didn’t trust each other, not yet, and so, in secret, their relationship had evolved into what little they could give each other.

 

            In stolen moments, they would meet up at Lena’s, the chances of being caught slimmer that way. And for a couple of hours, they would put it all aside, tangled up in bedsheets together, and in itself, it was a way of healing. They weren’t dating , but the safety and comfort that came with that were there, the love, the knowledge of understanding each other completely, for all their flaws too.

 

            She wished that Lena was with them at the moment, their game nights were never quite the same without her, always an empty seat or space where they all knew she should’ve been. The worst part was that they pretended like there wasn’t , barely mentioning her name, never bringing up stories involving her when they reminisced, or if they did, quickly trailing off and changing the topic as a pall of awkwardness was cast over the room, all of them casting sheepish, furtive glances at Kara. And she tried to tell them it was fine, that things would be okay, but how was she supposed to tell them that she loved Lena when they were still harbouring grudges?

It was Kara that she’d endangered, and while she knew her sister loved her dearly, she couldn’t understand why it was so hard for Alex to forgive Lena when she herself didn’t hold it against her. Instead, she had to sneak around to circumvent the discomfort of admitting what she was really up to, knowing that no one would be happy for her, that they’d all be suspicious and judgemental. In a way, Kara was glad for the secrecy, because at least then she didn’t have to offer any explanations. Not that she owed them anyway, but she knew the fuss it would cause within their circle, and she didn’t want to divide them further by choosing sides.
