The Villain’s Journey (Or Whatever It’s Called)

Author's Note:

Y'all blew me away with the response to A Hero's Journey, thank you so much! I tried to get this out as quickly as possible for you guys, and I hope you enjoy <3

Kara Danvers loved her sister. Like, a lot. But she didn’t necessarily love when she came to stay with her. Alex wanted to be up early and started her day with a run followed by a hot shower. Which was cool. Fine. Whatever. The problem was that Alex tended to get lost in fake arguments and debates while in said shower and both jacked up the water bill and drained all the hot water. Again, fine in her own place, but this was Kara’s apartment, and while bills weren’t a problem anymore, she didn’t enjoy cold showers. So, when Alex came to help her move in, Kara put in a rule. With the shower speaker she’d ordered off of Amazon especially for that occasion, Alex had to put on some music, and she had three songs to take her shower. That was it. It kept her focused enough to shower up while not dulling her mood, and she could easily keep track of how many songs she’d listened to. Yes, she was a little loud, but Kara had invested in ear plugs back when they were teenagers, so it didn’t really bother her.

She forgot that she had neighbors, though. Or, rather, one neighbor since they were the only ones on the floor. It was that kind of an apartment building.

On Alex’s last day with her, Kara had just woken up when her sister had shaken her awake to get breakfast and spend some more time together before she went back to her apartment on the other side of the city. After the stint she did at Noonan’s she didn’t bother buying coffee for either of them since she could make it just as well, and as soon as A Hero’s Journey really started making money, a fancy espresso machine was her first investment. With her favorite latte in her favorite cup- a metal travel one from Target that said ‘Liquid Motivation’ on the side-, Kara finished with her drizzle and smirked proudly for a second. It was always too hot to drink right away, and she put it to the side while she started on Alex’s. Her sister was so damn boring and just wanted espresso and a little cream. Where was the fun in that?

Admittedly still half asleep and more than a little groggy, it took Kara a second to process that there was someone banging on her door. The second knock came even harder, and Kara frowned, subconsciously snagging her coffee as she padded over to the entryway, pulling open the door only for her heart to skip a beat. She was gorgeous, all dark hair and full lips and piercing green eyes. Kara wasn’t ashamed to say that she missed most of what was being said due to what Alex would definitely call her PAN-ic. Because pan panic didn’t sound as good. Or good at all, really. And what was she saying again?

“-music very loudly, waking me out of a dead sleep. I am the CEO of a Fortune 500 company. I don’t think I need to impress upon you how badly I need my sleep. If I hear the music playing this early again, I will be filing a complaint and calling in a disturbance. Do you understand me?”

Oh, damn Alex and her loud ass music at six o-freaking-clock in the morning. She couldn’t just listen to it at normal volume, she had to go piss off the neighbors. Kara was no stranger to CEOs and their hours, and she almost felt a little bad that she was keeping this beautiful woman from her sleep because she probably needed it. Oh crap, she probably was waiting for some sort of reply, wasn’t she? With no caffeine in her system, but lots of PAN-ic, she could only recognize that the woman in front of her was both sleep and coffee-deprived and so she did what came as an instinct after working for Cat Grant for two years and in a coffee shop for four years before that. She put a coffee in her hand. Just took the mug and put it in her neighbor’s hand.

It was clearly the wrong move because she sneered and rolled her eyes. “I’m lactose intolerant.”

“It’s soy.” And then she slammed the door. Right in her face. Oh gods, what was Kara thinking? That was so rude! She wasn’t raised like that! By the time she got the nerve to look through the peephole, the mystery woman was gone, and Kara was feeling even stupider.

Alex came out of the bathroom with a cloud of steam just as Kara plopped down at the table, dropping her head onto its surface with a thud. “Who was that? I thought we were getting raided.”

“You’ve already managed to piss of my neighbor.”

Her sister drew an affronted look. “I haven’t even met them.”

“Apparently she doesn’t like Abba at six AM as much as you do.”

“I’ll have you know that Mamma Mia is a classic.”

“Not this early! And not when I’m this freaking dumb!”

Alex frowned, staring her sister down for a long moment before a smirk twisted her lips. “You had PAN-ic didn’t you? And you said something dumb.”

“It was worse than that.”

“Oh, I’m all ears.” Kara groaned loudly, pressing her face further into the table and mumbling. “What was that? I don’t speak table.”

The author threw her head back dramatically. “I shoved coffee at her and slammed the door in her face.”

Alex’s hand shot up to cover her mouth. “You didn’t. You threw coffee on your neighbor?!”

“No, I was holding my coffee cup and she looked exactly like Ms. Grant when she hasn’t had her coffee so, I just gave it to her and slammed the door.”

“And she didn’t say anything?”

Kara covered her face with her hands. “She said she was lactose intolerant.”

“And you slammed the door?”

“No, I said it was soy and then I slammed the door.”

Her sister wasn’t even trying to hide her cackles by that point and Kara was wondering if having her stay with her had even been worth it. She supposed the free labor was a plus, but Kara was kind of rich now, so she didn’t really need to worry about that. “I can’t believe your obsession with non-dairy products came of use.”

“It’s not an obsession.”

“You refuse to have anything with milk in it.”

“What about the Ben and Jerry’s we had last night?”

“The exception, not the rule.”

Kara glared. “I just don’t like milk, what’s the big deal?”

“It’s not a big deal except when you force coffee on your neighbor who just so happens to be lactose intolerant. I’m calling it now, you’re soulmates.”

“You’re full of it.”

“Nope, you did this when Lucy showed up at my door in the same band shirt that I was to our first date. It’s my turn.”

“It wasn’t a date! She came to yell at me!”

Alex grinned. “Mark my words, Kara Danvers, you will marry this woman.”

“I hate you.”

“Stop pouting or I’m not taking you to the pancake buffet I found.”

Kara’s eyes widened. “You found a what now?”

“That’s what I thought. Now, go get ready.”

“Yes, Ma’am, Agent Danvers.”

“Just for that I’m kicking your butt at our next training.”

“No!”

Kara truthfully wasn’t expecting to see her neighbor ever again and mourned the loss of her favorite cup that night, right as she finished doing all the dishes Alex had left behind from the experimental concoction she called ‘dinner’. It wasn’t edible. At all. They ended up ordering Thai.

Then, the next morning, right as she was finishing her second cup of coffee (in a mug that was usually reserved for Alex because it said ‘I have the vocabulary of a well-educated sailor’ on it), there was a knock at her door. It was a lot lighter and far less angry than that of the previous day, and Kara answered it without a second thought, a little surprised to see her sheepish neighbor on the other side, metal mug in hand. She held it up like a peace offering, bashful smile in place. “I think I owe you an apology.”

Damnit, why’d she have to be so darn cute. Kara took the mug. “Pretty sure you’ve got that backwards. It was really my fault. Well, not my fault, my sister’s fault, but the whole thing was my idea. It’s a whole story.” Oh, my gods, Kara, shut up. No one want to know about your sister’s showers. She fiddled nervously with her glasses. “And you probably don’t want to hear it. Besides, she was only here for a week, and she left last night.”

Neighbor gave her a dazzling smile. “I actually had a question for you.”

“Shoot.”

“How do you make your coffee? Because it was freaking amazing, and my espresso machine disappointed me this morning.” She looked almost nervous to ask, all blushes and darting eyes. Adorable.

Kara’s own laugh surprised her, and she cringed internally. Loud, much, Kara? “I worked at Noonan’s for four years. I learned a thing or two.” Kara bit her lips, desperately trying to smother a grin and the rising question, failing in record time. “If you have the time, you could come in and I can show you.” She probably had way better things to be doing than making coffee. Just make the damn latte for her, dummy.

“I’d love to.”

Surprising, but so damn welcome. Kara opened the door a little wider to let her neighbor in, and that reminded her. “Oh, I’m Kara, by the way. Kara Danvers.”

“Lena Luthor.”

She said it like she was expecting Kara to get mad, but all Kara could thing about was the spinal implant produced by none other than the woman before her that was currently helping her foster mom walk. But she was here for coffee, not for Kara to gush about how amazing her work is. She and Alex could do that later. “Nice to officially meet you. Now, the trick with the coffee, is really in the syrups…”

They talked for hours over coffee lessons that Kara could tell were going nowhere but was completely okay with. She’d make a thousand cups of coffee if it meant spending time with Lena.

Morning coffee was their thing, and Kara loved it. She loved every second of it, in fact, even if she had to get up earlier than usual so she could hang out with Lena a little bit before she had to rush off to be a big important CEO. She didn’t really mind, though. She enjoyed their time.

After about six months of their little routine, Kara was dealing with daily teasing from her sister about her not-relationship, and Lena was finally finding out about her book. The blonde was a little surprised that she hadn’t looked her up sooner, but also appreciated the respect and privacy that offered. Not to mention that it was a little amusing that Lena knew her as Kiera, but not Kara. It shouldn’t have been all that surprising, though. Through Clark she knew Diana Prince and Bruce Wayne, her cousin’s multiple interviews of them leading to a friendship of sorts, and Kara had practically grown up around them. Not only had the connections helped get her into places others couldn’t, but it meant that when she’d gone for that interview to be Ms. Grant’s assistant, it was without the fear of a powerful CEO that most others had. Kara wasn’t scared of her and was willing to put up with most of her BS, while not giving into the smaller things. Really, she could thank Bruce for that. He was a little scary.

That day was much like all the rest. She was too wired on caffeine and Lena’s morning visit to go back to sleep, so she puttered around for a bit, stared at an empty word document for a while, and then went to her weekly lunch with Cat.

“You’re late.”

“I’m actually five minutes early.”

Her former boss rolled her eyes. “Well, I was here early and therefore made to wait. And you know I hate waiting.”

Kara chuckled good-naturedly. “And I also saw you walk in here about thirty seconds before I did. But nice try.”

The media mogul’s façade cracked, and she smirked. “Caught me. Now, tell me about your sequel. It’s been almost a year, Kiera; if you plan on keeping your name up there, you need to think about what comes next.”

Kara sighed. “Maybe I don’t have a sequel planned. Maybe I just want to write something else.”

“Don’t even try that with me. I know you know that their story isn’t finished. You just need the proper push. And unfortunately, it seems that you are immune to mine.”

“I’m not immune. I just don’t have any kind of thought towards it right now. I felt like I could have written a whole new novel right after I finished the first one, but I left it for too long and now I feel like I’ll never get it back.”

Cat sipped at the martini that had just been set in front of her. “And does that have anything to do with this mystery woman you refuse to tell me about.”

“Not this again.”

The older woman sighed heavily. “Kiera, I work eighty-hour weeks and take care of a teenage son outside of those hours while dealing with running an empire by myself. Whatever plans I had for dating dried up ages ago because I simply don’t have the time. And that means I need to live vicariously through you. Understand? Because you are young and pretty and clearly have someone in your life. So, tell me about her. Chop, chop.”

With a slight roll of her eyes and a fond smile, Kara started, “So, my new neighbor was pissed about a week after I moved in…”

~ “And this neighbor you’ve been fawning over. Does she have a name?”

They’re both a little tipsy from their respective drinks and Kara would absolutely admit she was fawning over- “Lena Luthor. My neighbor’s name is Lena Luthor.”

Cat’s eyes widened slightly before she broke out into a toothy grin. “You’re kidding me. I just got a call from her assistant Gina this morning for a signed copy.”

“Her name is Jess. But you know that because she’s an excellent assistant and I know you tried to poach her after I published.”

“I have no clue what you’re talking about. Jeanine, did ask for the copy, but I said we were out.”

Kara’s brow creased in a frown. “I just signed, like, eight billion copies yesterday.”

“Once again, your eloquence astounds me, Kara. First of all, those will be sold out by the end of the day, I guarantee it. Second, even if they weren’t, I was far more interested in setting up a meeting between the two of you, so I said no.”

“You tried to set me up!”

“Oh, like you really expected anything different. I’ve been trying to get you and the non-homicidal Luthor to meet for the better part of a year. I think you’d complement each other. But of course, she would be your neighbor.”

A horrifying thought crossed her mind, and with two glasses of wine in her, her brain-to-mouth filter was shot. “Alex can never find out about that.”

Cat rolled her eyes. “I doubt your sister would be upset that I was trying to set you up.”

“No, Cat, you don’t get it. After that first morning, Alex was all ‘you’re soulmates, I’ll tell this speech at your wedding’.” It was a poor impression of her sister, but it got the point across. “If she finds out you were trying to get us to meet, she’ll never let it go.”

The CEO was silent for a moment, looking a little stunned, before she let loose a laugh (Kara had never heard her really laugh, she was scared), following with an evil grin. “I have never been happier to have your sister’s number.”

“No!”

Someone was banging down Kara’s door.

At least, that was what it sounded like. It was exactly 5:03 AM, and Kara was going to kill whoever it was. However, rather than it being someone easily shooed away, Kara opened the door to find a slightly frazzled Lena, book in one hand and coffee mug in the other. “You just left it there, and act like you can’t think up a sequel?!”

She took a moment to process that sentence and then the fact that it was her book in Lena’s hand, and OH. She read her book! Well, that called for a whole discussion. Trusting Lena to follow her, she stumbled off to the kitchen and turned on the espresso machine. “I thought what came next was kind of implied.”

“Implied?!” Her door slammed. “You tease us all with romance between Estella and Mazikeen and then you just leave it there! You know all the straights are going to call them gal pals if you don’t write a sequel where they kill Jeremy and live out a happy gay life! Or even worse. They’ll try to say Jeremy can be redeemed.” Lena sounded truly disgusted at the thought, and truthfully, Kara was too.

“I literally made him a puppy-killer. There’s no coming back from that.” Honestly, people these days.

“Yeah, but people are dumb. You have to keep the story going.”

She wasn’t exactly wrong, but damnit, writing was hard. Especially when you didn’t have any motivation. Still, as Kara took the mug from her friend, she had to laugh. “And you practically broke down my door at five in the morning on a Saturday to tell me that?”

Lena gaped for a moment before raising her chin a little. “To be fair, I just finished it and I had no idea it was this early.”

Kara would later blame it on the lack of sleep and lack of caffeine, but what came out of her mouth next was completely unplanned, much like her actions that first morning they met. “You’re lucky I love you.”

Silence.

Dead.

Deafening.

Silence.

Well, Lena hadn’t run screaming from the building, yet. So, maybe it was time for Kara to put on her big-girl panties and do something. “Go out with me?”

She prepared herself. She took a breath and prepared for the negative answer. “Yes.” It was a little breathless and airy and so fucking beautiful.

And Kara couldn’t believe it. “Yes?”

“Yes.” There was more steel to this one. “I’d love to go out with you.”

Alex and Cat would never let her hear the end of this one, but she couldn’t hold back her grin. “I’d invite you for a coffee, but I think we might be a little past that.”

And Lena, perfect Lena, had the perfect answer. “Good for you, I happen to know a great Italian place that also serves coffee, so we can keep up the tradition. I don’t think it’s as good as yours, but I don’t think anything could really compare.”

Kara couldn’t wait. Like at all. “Tonight, at seven?”

“I’ll be waiting.” And then she sashayed out of the apartment, coffee cup still clutched in Kara’s hand. She said yes. Kara was going on a date with Lena. She had to tell Cat. Alex… Alex would wait because she’d never hear the end of it, but Cat would be able tell her what to wear, at least.

Now more than a little wide awake, Kara sipped coffee out of the mug she’s practically gifted Lena over the months, and grinned. She had an idea.

Pulling her laptop close, she opened a word document and started.

            The trip back towards town was entirely uneventful, and Estella had to wonder how much of that was due to the witch. Mazikeen. Her thoughts drifted to the other woman more than a few times on her journey-

Kara was there at seven. Lena was waiting.
