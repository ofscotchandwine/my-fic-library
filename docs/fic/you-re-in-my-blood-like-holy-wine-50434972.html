Chapter 1

October 1980

Fortune Bay, Massachusetts

 

 

When Lena is 11 years old, she meets her best friend.

“Witch! Witch! You’re a bitch!”

Small-town America, Lena has thought since the moment she arrived here, must be the worst place in the world to grow up different. And small-town coastal New England, with the most judgemental and puritanical adoptive family possible, must be the worst place in the world to grow up a witch.

"Can you just let me through, please?" Lena says, her eyes fixed to the ground. Seven of her least favourite schoolmates are lined up in front of the gate she needs to go through to get home, and the last thing she needs is for them to take her books again. Her assignments had ended up being late last time on account of needing to re-write them all, and Lillian still hasn't let her hear the end of it.

"Or what? You'll turn us into frogs?" Siobhan Smythe sneers. She's the worst of them – she's hounded Lena's steps since the first day Lena stepped into their third grade class, fresh off of losing her mother and relocating continents to a home that clearly didn't want her. She's picked apart every aspect of Lena, from her clothes to her accent, since the moment they met. 

"I just want to go home," Lena says softly.

Not that the Luthor manor is home, really. It never has been. Home is still a cottage on the coast of Ireland. Home is her mother putting on an autumn simmer pot, filling the house with the fresh scent of cinnamon and rosemary. Home is learning how to spark a candle into flame with a gentle breath.

But that home died with her mother. The house she's lived in for the last three years, with its long drafty corridors and her stepmother lurking around every corner waiting to criticize her for something, is just the place she happens to be.

Her mother had told Lena of her heritage long before she died and left Lena alone in the world. She’d taught Lena spells and charms, showed her how to harness her magic for healing and occasional mischief. And she'd told Lena of the curse that runs through their veins – the one that made her mother send Lena's father away before he could get hurt by it. The curse that dooms anyone they dare to love to an early death. She had told Lena the tale of her ancestor casting it like a bedtime story until Lena had it practically memorized.

It's the spell of a broken witch. Meant to keep her heart safe after a heartbreak. But it turned bitter with time and loneliness. Now all in her line are cursed, my love. We aren't meant for that kind of happiness.

Her mother had thought sending Lionel away would save him, letting him move on and go back to the family he left for her. But he came back. He kept coming back over and over throughout Lena’s childhood, until he didn't. And when he didn't, it was her mother who got sick.

Lena knows that it isn't scientifically possible to die of a broken heart. But sometimes she wonders.

In her mother’s will, apparently, was a stipulation that in the event of her death Lena was to be brought to her father. She showed up to that big, lonely house on a brand-new continent with nothing but a teddy bear and her mother’s spellbook hidden in her backpack, given to Lionel and Lillian Luthor like a package they didn't order, and she’s never left. Even when everyone from her stepmother to her classmates have made it their mission to beat the magic out of her.

It’s become more and more literal lately, Lena thinks, closing her eyes and trying to shield her face with her books as she’s pelted with sticks and small rocks as an answer. They could be more creative with the rhyme.

"Witch, witch, you're a bitch! Witch, witch, you're a –"

The chant ends in a yell, the crash of fracturing wood, and a voice Lena doesn't recognize.

"Who the heck throws rocks? What is this, 1750?"

Lena opens her eyes. A girl is standing in front of her, tall and willowy with long brown hair and brandishing her backpack like a flail, and just beyond her Siobhan is lying in the splintered remains of the fence Lena had been trying to get past.

"Who the hell are you?" Siobhan snarls, holding out her hands so that two of her friends can help her to her feet. Her arms are scraped up from the wood, but she seems more angry at being stood up to than anything else.

The mystery girl snorts. "Someone who thinks bullies are dumb."

"Are you calling me dumb?"

"I'm looking at you, aren't I?"

Siobhan lunges at the girl. The girl holds her own for a minute, her height clearly giving her an advantage, but soon enough Siobhan's friends jump in and with seven against one Lena's rescuer doesn't have a chance. Lena stands rooted to the spot with her heart in her throat, wanting to help but knowing she'd be nothing but a liability, and the paralysis lasts until Siobhan hits the girl so hard in the face that Lena sees blood start to drip from her nose.

Terrified, driven by pure instinct, Lena throws out a hand.

"Stop!"

The seven girls scatter like marbles. They're sent flying through the air by the force of Lena's spell, hurled away with enough power to knock the breath from them on landing - Siobhan lands the furthest away, rolling a few feet and then unsteadily picking herself up, and when she sees Lena standing over the new girl with a hand extended something comes over her face that Lena has never seen before. Terror.

"I'm telling my mom!" Siobhan shouts. Her voice cracks on the last word, tears visibly welling up in her eyes, and with her friends behind her she scampers down the path in the opposite direction and finally, blessedly, away from Lena. Lena is sure she'll be hearing from Lillian about this latest altercation, but at least for now she can have a moment of peace.

The girl who defended Lena so unexpectedly stands up slowly. Her eyes are fixed on Lena's hand, wide as saucers, and when she's drawn herself up to her full height – almost a full head taller than Lena – she opens her mouth to shout.

"That. Was. Amazing!"

Lena blinks. She'd been expecting to be abandoned as soon as this girl realized who she was defending, or worse – instead, the girl looks like Christmas has come early.

"How did you do that?! I mean, you just put your hand up, and bam!" The girl says, brandishing her hand the same way and seeming disappointed when it doesn't bear the same results. "What was that?"

"Magic," Lena says quietly. The girl takes it in stride.

"Cool! You saved my butt, magic girl. Thanks!"

"You saved mine first," Lena points out.

"Fair point. I'm Sam," the girl says, sticking her hand out for a shake. She has a split lip, and a quickly-swelling red abrasion around her eye that's likely going to turn into a truly spectacular bruise. "Sam Arias. Just moved here from National City."

"Nice to meet you," Lena says mechanically, giving Sam's hand a polite shake but too thrown off to do much else. She'd heard about a new girl two grades above her, but she hadn't expected an eighth grader to come to her rescue.

Sam grins. Her handshake is enthusiastic, and she doesn't let go until she's given Lena a hard clasp. "Introductions go two ways, you know. What's your name?"

"Lena," Lena says, blinking. "Ehm. Luthor."

Sam Arias walks Lena home that day, and every day after. She can't stop the relentless targeting that happens while Lena is in class, but at recess and on the way home Siobhan and her cronies steer clear of the two of them from that point on. Sam is never afraid of anything.

 

 

When Sam visits the Manor, Lena feels for the first time like perhaps she isn't simply being ungrateful by not being happy there.

"Wow," Sam says, whistling low as Lena shows her the drawing room and the corridor that leads to the kitchens before heading up the grand stairs towards her bedroom. "This place is massive. Are your family in the mafia or something?"

"I actually have no idea how they got their money. I try not to ask too many questions."

"It's spooky," Sam says, running her finger along the shiny mahogany panelling lining the walls. "All this space for three people?"

"It was four before Lex left," Lena says, though she knows it hardly helps. Sam spins in a wide circle, arms outstretched and still not even close to touching either wall.

"What do you even do with all this space?"

"Honestly? Nothing. My father stays in his study," Lena says, shrugging. "Lillian keeps to her wing, unless she's found something criticize me for that can't wait until dinner."

"And I thought my home life was miserable," Sam mutters.

"Do you not get along with your parents?"

"Don't have parents," Sam says easily. "Just an adoptive mother who hates me. So, ya know. You're not alone."

Her blasé tone softens on the last sentence, and Lena feels such a wave of surprise and relief at finding just one person who understands that she doesn't notice Lillian looming around the corner until they've almost run headlong into her.

Sam makes a noise, drawing up to keep from barrelling Lillian over, and Lena pulls her backward by the back of her shirt until they've both given Lillian a wide berth. She sets her eyes on the ground near Lillian's expensive shoes.

"And who is this?" Lillian's cool tones make Lena's chest seize with a familiar anxiety. "A distraction from your studies?"

"We're studying together," Lena says quickly, without raising her eyes. "Sam is in the 8th grade. She's teaching me some higher-level math." She doesn't look at Sam, but she hopes against hope that her new friend will get the hint.

Thankfully, Sam does. The older girl nods, standing up straighter and clasping her hands behind her back. "Yeah. Linear equations. Pretty advanced for a sixth grader."

"Advanced," Lillian says, looking Sam up and down from her scuffed sneakers to her messy ponytail. Judgement reeks from every pore, but Sam holds fast against it in a way Lena has never managed to. "I don't quite see the point in trying, since Lex was doing linear equations by age 7. But the effort is…quaint."

Lena tries not to react to that. She should have expected it – it doesn't matter what she does. Lillian's approval will always remain out of reach.

"Make sure she's gone by suppertime," Lillian says, brushing past them and towards the stairs. Her heels click into the distance, and only when they fade out of earshot does Lena let herself relax.

Sam blows a loud raspberry.

"Jeez. What a jerk."

"Shhh," Lena says, hurrying Sam down the hall, but she can't help but giggle at Sam's pure audacity. "Don't let her hear you."

"She's not magic like you is she?"

Lena shakes her head darkly. "No. And don't mention it in front of her, okay?"

Lena's room is just about the only place in this house she's ever felt close to safe. There's no lock on the door, but with Lex away at college she's the only one who sleeps in this wing of the house – unless Lillian is seeking her out, an uncommon occurrence when she has full access to give Lena criticism during dinner, Lena is usually alone up here.

It's smaller than Lex's room ever was by a long stretch. But it's still large, full of all the little things she's been able to accumulate over the years – books, scraps from Lex's projects to cobble together into her own inventions, even a pewter chess set from her father's library that nobody has noticed went missing. She has French doors that lead to a small balcony, and a good view of the garden. The little place she's carved out for herself here is one of the few blessings she's grateful for in this house.

Sam looks around, seeming to take stock of her surroundings before sitting heavily on the bed and grabbing Lena's teddy bear.

Lena only gets in a moment of embarrassment for having her childhood teddy on such open display before Sam grins, holding it to her chest and setting her chin on top of his soft head. There's no judgement in her eyes.

"I don't actually have to teach you linear equations, do I?"

Lena snorts. "Oh, please. I've been doing them for years."

From that point on, Sam always visits by climbing in through the balcony.

 

 

"She's perfect," Sam says, infatuation punctuating every syllable. "She's so strong, and she doesn't want people to know how much she cares about stuff but she's so enthusiastic about things sometimes. And her hair, ugh. I love auburn hair. I'm so in love with her, Lena."

They're lying with their heads at the end of Lena's bed, hair dangling to the floor, blood rushing to Lena's brain as Sam outlines her crush. It's a girl in Sam's grade that Lena hasn't met, but she's seen her in the schoolyard playing soccer – Tonya, Sam said. Lena might be more prone to take her seriously if Sam hadn't been so in love with a boy on her street named Mark only three weeks before, and a girl she saw at the coffee shop a month before that. Sam gathers crushes like Lena gathers books.

"Isn't she like, 6 inches shorter than you?" Lena drawls. "Are you going to have her stand on a box?"

"So? She's compact. I love it."

"You're not even in high school yet," Lena says. "How do you know it's love?"

Sam shrugs. "I feel it. Every time I look at her I feel it."

"That's just your brain releasing chemicals."

Sam scoffs. She nudges Lena's knee with her own. "You can't make love into a science, Lena. It's like magic!"

"Magic has rules," Lena says firmly. "Just like science. And love is not magic. Love is an illusion."

"You are the most unromantic person I have ever met," Sam sighs. Lena, rather than bristling, takes it as a compliment.

"That's all the better. I'll never fall in love."

Sam sits up. Lena follows, feeling all the blood move out of her head in a dizzying rush, to see Sam looking at her like she has 3 heads.

"What do you mean, never? Like, never ever?"

Lena nods. She saw what love did to her mother after her father stopped visiting. And she sees now how Lillian's love for Lionel, faced with his infidelity and clear love for another, has added to the cruelty Lillian enacts on everyone in the house. It only got worse when Lex left for college. Lionel doesn't love Lena – he only looks at her like she's something he's lost, a cruel reflection of the woman he loved who haunts his house like a ghost. Lex might have loved Lena in his own selfish way, but not more than himself. Not enough to stay and protect her.

Lena vowed a long time ago to stay away from the whole business. She's cursed, after all; what is love, besides a way to get people hurt?

"Why would I ever fall in love? I've never seen it do anything but make people miserable," Lena says. "It killed my mother. It's the reason my stepmother hates me."

"None of that was caused by love," Sam argues. She fusses with Lena's hair, starting to make an idle braid in it while Lena scoffs. "Your mother got sick, and Lillian hates you because she hates herself and needs to take it out on someone."

"I beg to differ."

"I don't think you'll be able to stop yourself when it happens," Sam says. She finishes the messy braid, combing her fingers through it until it's untangled again. "You're only 11. What happens when you grow up?"

"I have magic," Lena shrugs. "I'll just stop it from happening."

"I don't think even magic can do that. You can't just hit an off-switch."

Lena's eyes narrow. She twists around to look at Sam, the cogs in her mind already turning.

"Actually, I think I can."

It's true that no spell exists to take feelings away. Once it's taken root, her mother's book says, love is a stubborn weed. It grows in all the cracks of the heart until it's taken over every inch of fertile ground. But what do exist are spells to give love shape – to mould it, direct it, make it brighter. To call for it to find you. Such spells are complicated, but Lena has always had a natural aptitude.

"So to keep yourself from getting hurt by love, you're…casting a spell to…find it?" Sam says, her face twisted up in confusion as Lena wanders the garden gathering the herbs she needs. It's dark outside, and they're darting between shadows – the garden is here for the kitchen staff to pick fresh ingredients from, and the last time Lillian found her snipping rosemary she was confined to her room for two months.

"I'm calling for Amas Veritas," Lena clarifies. "I'm binding myself to a person that doesn't exist."

Sam steps on a pile of crunchy leaves, and they both freeze – a chill breeze rustles the grass, kicking up the leaves and ruffling their hair, but Lena can't hear any movement from the house. After a few seconds she relaxes again, rifling through the garden bed for some mint.

"How do you know they don't exist?" Sam asks in a loud whisper.

"They can't. No person can fit all my criteria. And if they don't exist, I can never fall in love," Lena says, sifting through her pencil box full of herbs and flora. "And nobody can get hurt. A curse needs a target."

"But what if they do?"

"They don't," Lena says firmly. "I think that's everything I need. Come on."

It's too late for Lillian to be awake, but they tiptoe through the halls anyways until they reach Lena's room. The spell needs open air to work properly, so Lena sets up a circle of lit candles on the balcony, sitting on one side of it while Sam sits on the other playing with one of the lit wicks.

"So who is this impossible love you're calling for?" Sam says, hissing when she doesn't move her finger fast enough to keep from getting singed. "Lena Luthor's soulmate?"

Lena takes a deep breath, trying to summon the peace and concentration needed for such a complex spell.

"They need to be kind," Lena starts, setting one ingredient after another into a wooden bowl in the middle of the circle. They rustle in the breeze, but don't blow away. Oregano, lavender, violet. "Kind to everyone. Loyal. And they need to hear my call from miles away."

"Miles?" Sam says, leaving the candle alone to watch Lena work the spell. Lena has done magic in front of her before, but never anything this intensive. "That's pretty demanding,"

"They'll hum my favourite song," Lena says. A snipping of chamomile. Comfort.

"Which is?"

Lena doesn't answer.

"They're strong. Strong enough to lift…" Lena trails off, thinking. A car? A house? Lena doesn't care necessarily about measurements like that so much as what that strength could do. She wants someone strong enough to keep Lillian away. Strong enough to take her away from here.

"Strong enough to keep us safe," Lena finally decides, adding a clipping of lovage. "And they can make pancakes in funny shapes. Flip them in the air."

"Okay, that's at least reasonable," Sam says, munching on a few leaves of mint that Lena isn't using.

"They love my favourite pizza toppings."

"And that's asking too much," Sam finishes, tossing the stem over the balcony. "Olives and mushrooms? Anchovies? Nobody likes that but you."

"They wear glasses," Lena decides in the moment, "And they have blue eyes. The brightest blue eyes."

"I'm more of a brown eyes person," Sam muses. Rather than distracting her, Lena finds that Sam's presence is actually helping her concentrate – keeping her mind happy, rather than dwelling on the anxiety that the idea of such a love brings her.

"And they need a great family," Lena says, her voice cracking a little as she set down the last few herbs. "A family that loves them. Who can love me, too."

Sam smiles, sad and a little soft. "Get me in on that one too, will you?"

Lena returns the smile. She sets a single daffodil in the bowl. Resilience. "They need to be indestructible. So that even a curse can’t take them away."

And finally, the one request that Lena knows will clinch it. The final requirement that nobody could ever fulfill. She gathers a handful of rose petals, cupping them in her palm.

"Anything else?" Sam drawls.

"Yes," Lena says quietly. She looks up at the sky, the great big empty expanse she's dreamed about escaping to ever since the doors of the Luthor manor first closed behind her. Freedom. "They need to touch the stars."

The magic starts to gather as soon as the last requirement is spoken. It swirls around her, lifting the spell's ingredients and floating them around her in a gentle spiral, making the candle flame flicker, before floating skyward. And to her alarm, as she sends the spell into the heavens, Lena feels a slight tug. Like a string tied to her ribcage has been pulled towards the sky, urging her somewhere else.

But it passes quickly. The string dissipates, her call to the sky fading, and Lena knows the spell didn’t work. Couldn’t have.

Perfect people don’t exist.

 

 

Lena learns when she turns 15 that not even death can stop a curse from finding its target. Lena doesn't love her father. But her mother loved him. Her mother loved him so much that it killed her, and now it's killed him, too.

Listen for the sound of the deathwatch beetle, her mother had told her. When you hear its call, the person you love is doomed to die. Lena hears its chirping in the wee hours of the morning, kept awake by the constant noise coming from her windowsill, and by the time the sun has risen the household staff have given her the news.

The funeral is bleak. It's a drizzly September morning, cold and dour, and the funeral is sparsely attended – a few business associates, the staff, and Lillian and Lex, standing near the grave as the casket is lowered. They're both emotionless, their designer coats and black umbrellas wicking raindrops away while Lena tries to blink the chilly water out of her eyes; she keeps to the back, holding tightly to Sam's hand as her father disappears into the ground and trying to stay out of Lillian's way.

It doesn't work. Once the first handful of dirt is sprinkled and the prayers have been said, Lillian's first move is to turn around and pin Lena with a look that says everything her words won't in a public place, but Lena fully expects to hear regularly from this point onward.

That somehow, this is Lena's fault.

And maybe it is. Maybe it's not Lena's mother's curse that found Lionel, but Lena's; without any other target to latch onto, maybe the curse decided to take away the only sense of shaky safety Lena ever had in the Luthor household. Lionel had hardly even looked at her, but at least with him there Lena knew Lillian would never do anything to hurt her. That guarantee is gone after his death, and Lena finds that she preferred his mournful silence to what follows.

Lionel’s death twists Lillian even further into the cruel shell she was becoming before it. Lex disappears again to grad school, visiting home even less often than before, and when he does visit all he seems to want to do is rant about aliens. About their abuses of power. About Superman, whose new notoriety and feats of heroism have taken the world by storm in the last two years.

Lena couldn't care less about Superman. Lex's obsession doesn't take hold in her, besides to remind her how little Lex now seems to resemble the brother she once knew. Aliens are the least of her worries. Something else takes most of her attention in the days after Lionel is buried.

Sam scales her balcony in the small hours of the morning a few weeks after the funeral, knocking gently on the glass-panelled doors with a tear-streaked face, and when Lena opens them to catch her in a hug that Sam collapses into she sees a small piece of white plastic clenched in her best friend's hand.

It's the first time Lena has ever seen her afraid.

"Lena," Sam chokes, clinging to her shoulders, "I'm pregnant."

 

 

“Do you really have to go?” Lena whispers. She shivers in the April chill, Sam’s rusty car idling in the shadowed back road behind the Luthor house.

"According to my mom, yes. She even packed my stuff for me," Sam says with an air of forced nonchalance, gesturing over her shoulder at the trash bags stuffed into the backseat. Lena bites at her lip.

"You could stay with me. Lillian hardly ever comes to my wing anymore, you could -"

“I can’t stay here, Lena. Not for long, anyways. Not like this.” Sam’s pregnant belly fills the space between them, and as much as she hates it, Lena understands. While Sam might be able to hide away in Lena's room for a while, there's no way to hide a baby. And besides, this town is small. Sam's mother is cruel. There's no bright future here for a pregnant teenage girl. Especially one who, after the single night that got her pregnant, apparently realizes that she's gayer than she thought she was.

“I have a friend back in National City who can take me in. I’ll get my GED, and go to college, and…” Sam is obviously trying to sound confident, but Lena can hear the fear in her voice. Her hand splays instinctively over her stomach, and Lena puts her own over it.

“You’re going to do amazing things, and you’re going to be an amazing mom. I know you are,” Lena says fiercely. Sam's smile is halfhearted.

“Are you sure you don't want to come with me?”

 “Of course I do," Lena says heavily. "But you know Lillian. It doesn’t matter where I go – she’d just find me and drag me back here. She can't torture me from so many states away. You have a better chance without me.”

Sam nods, visibly holding back tears. Her grip on Lena's hand tightens.

“I know you won't say it back, but I – I love you, Lena," Sam says, those tears finally making their escape. "You're my sister. You know that, right?”

Sam is right – Lena can't say it back. She's never been able to, in all their years of a friendship that feels more like sisterhood. Lena's love is a death sentence, and she can't lose anyone else. She can't ever lose Sam.

“Yeah.”

Sam rubs her belly. “And we’re going to stay in touch. You’re going to be an auntie to whoever is in here.”

Lena lets out a watery laugh, nodding. “I am. I promise.”

Sam's car keeps idling. The smell of the exhaust mingles with the salty scent of the nearby ocean, carried on a damp breeze. Sam glances back at it, but doesn't seem eager to leave – she won't let go of Lena's hand. Not because she wants to stay here, Lena knows, but because she's worried about what will happen when she leaves. What will happen to Lena without the barrier of her presence.

Lena won't let herself be the thing that holds Sam back.

Reaching into Sam’s purse and pulling out the Swiss Army knife she knows is hidden there, Lena flips it open and submits to the fit of impulse. “I can make sure we never lose each other. That we always protect each other. Hold out your hand.”

Sam does it without question. Bracing herself for pain, Lena makes a decently-sized cut on her own hand.

“My blood,” Lena says, letting the blood fill the creases of her palm. Next she cuts Sam on the same hand, a short but deep cut that makes her hiss, but Sam doesn’t jerk her hand away.

“Your blood,” Lena says, and Sam nods. She can feel the magic building in the air, the buzz in her skull that means the spell is working even though she's not working from anything she remembers from her mother's book. An ad-lib spell. A shot in the dark, born of a desperation not to lose the only person she has left.

Finally she grasps Sam’s cut hand in a firm grip with her own, pressing their wounds together – a grip that’s comforting in its familiarity, and the steady pressure of it makes the spell easy to finish.

“Our blood,” Lena says decisively.

She can feel the moment the spell takes hold – the buzz dissipates with a warm feeling, and the pain in her hand eases off. When she lets go of Sam's hand the smeared blood remains, but the cut itself has already scabbed.

It'll leave a scar. But the pain and the permanent reminder is worth it for the smile on Sam’s face.

Lena can't say I love you. But she can do this.

“This way we'll always be linked. We'll keep each other safe no matter where we are. Sisters by blood,” Lena says, realizing she’s failed in her attempt to keep her tears at bay. They're streaming down her face; but Sam is crying too, and they share a tight hug before she finally clambers into her old Toyota and drives away. Far away, to the other side of the country, to start a life of freedom that Lena aches to follow her into.

But Lena stays. She turns back to the Luthor Manor, using Sam's old path to climb up to her own balcony and crawl into bed.

She feels the emptiness of Sam's absence every day.

 

 

Lena finishes school with her head down. She gets accepted to MIT, and for the first time she gets a taste of the freedom Sam must have felt when she left Fortune Bay – Lena is still in the same state and Lillian insists on weekly phone calls and full access to Lena's grades, but for the most part Lena is on her own. Nobody here knows about her magic. Nobody here throws sticks when she walks home from class.

Lena is 20 when she meets Jack Spheer.

Jack isn't perfect. He doesn’t believe in magic, putting Lena's eccentricities down to luck or coincidence. He's more interested in the tangible nature of the the stars than he is in touching them, and more likely to theorize about what Superman's arrival means for scientific research than he is to worry about the Kryptonian's powers. His pancakes are truly terrible. He's allergic to anchovies. But he's kind. He shares Lena's passion for helping people. He encourages and supports her. He's gentle and attentive and, it turns out, he loves her.

Lena puts her mother's book into a box. It gathers dust under her bed as she turns instead to science, to the perceptible world, to the things she can put under a microscope or into an experiment, and she leaves the magic behind. In time, she forgets about the spells she cast in childhood; she forgets about curses and candles and amas veritas.

The one piece of magic she can't shake is the scar in the centre of her palm.

Through all the years of their separation, through the birth of Sam's daughter Ruby and both of their big life changes, Lena still hasn't yet had the chance to visit Sam in National City. Lena has always kept in touch with her through letters. They cover as broad a range as their conversations always did, and the night Jack reveals his feelings, the night Lena accepts them with no small amount of fear, Lena pens a new one.

Dear Sam,

Sometimes I feel like there’s an emptiness inside of me. An emptiness that at times, seems to burn. It sounds dramatic, but I think that’s just who I am. There’s a ring around the moon tonight – according to old superstitions, it means there’s trouble not far behind. I’m scared of what that might mean.

Jack told me tonight that he loves me. That he wanted to be with me. I told him yes, but I couldn't say the words back. I do care about him, and I told him so, but it doesn't feel the way you always described it. It's like something is missing. Like something is broken in me.

I miss you, Sam. You’re the only person I could ever talk to about this. Even Jack doesn’t understand this feeling – I have this dream of being whole. Of finding something that no curse can break. Something that cuts through the fear. Something even time would lie down and be still for. Do you think that exists?

I’m sorry I’m being so existential. Maybe I’m just not meant to be happy.

Lena stops writing, putting down the pen and flexing her hand. It feels good to get all these feelings out, but she doesn’t want to unload everything on Sam. She has her own life to live. A daughter to raise. School to get through. She's far enough away now that Lena worries less about the curse taking her, and Lena intends on keeping it that way.

Lena fills the rest of the letter with easier topics – asks after Ruby, and talks about school and Lex’s recent work and Jack – and when she mails it, she feels a little bit lighter.

 

 

When Lena is 23, Lex loses his mind.

She watches it live on TV. Jack holds her and stares at the tiny screen in horror as station after station covers the chaos in Metropolis – Lex, in some kind of mechanical suit, shooting ray after ray of sickly green light at Superman. Taking out buildings. Hurting innocent bystanders. A rush of inexplicable violence, tearing the city apart just to get a chance at hurting the Kryptonian.

He's apprehended, of course; as brilliant as Lex's mind is, even his technology can't negate his own hubris. Lena testifies against him in court. She tells the jury of his mania, his hatred, his changed nature. They give him 15 consecutive life sentences, and convict Lillian as an accomplice.

His psychotic break is blamed on anti-alien xenophobia, but Lena can’t help but feel like somehow, some way, it’s her fault. Her fault for loving him, for letting his brotherly affection into her heart before he left Fortune Bay and started to change. The part of him that welcomed her into his home when she arrived as a scared child from Ireland is gone, that much is clear when she visits him in prison – he looks at her with the same blank, emotionless stare she’s always associated with Lillian.

Any vestige of the brother she knew is dead. Lost to his obsession with being the most powerful man in the world.

Journalists hound Lena for statements. They camp outside her apartment. Offer her millions for exclusive interviews about her brother's downward spiral. Theorize in the papers about what she could be hiding. Lena ignores them all.

She throws herself instead into work. Jack's nanobot project has the potential to change the world; maybe if she can help others, if she can get his ideas off the ground and use them for good, she can scrub the stains from her own soul.

The Luthor estate goes to Lena. Stocks and bonds. Patents. Properties. She hardly makes note of it. And when the opportunity to take over Lex's company presents itself, Lena turns it down. The dark path that Lex took started and ended at LuthorCorp. Instead Lena stays with Jack in their little garage lab, and she comes to terms with her future.

Jack loves her. Lena cares for him deeply still, always, just not quite the way he wishes she would; but that understanding is there, and he seems content with what she offers him. He loves her like she's the only thing he'll ever need. She loves him from a distance. She finds an engagement ring in his sock drawer, but it never ends up on her finger.

In the end even Lena's distance doesn’t protect him. The stain of her curse touches everything.

 

 

Lena gets the call in her 26th year, just as the September heat is starting to break.

She hears the deathwatch beetle make itself known as she's making coffee. She woke up to a note on the fridge saying that Jack went out to pick up some equipment he ordered from the post office, and Lena takes the opportunity for a slow morning – she opens the windows to let in the fresh air, taking a deep breath of it as she pours a mug and takes her first sip. She picks up the newspaper that Jack must have brought in, glancing at the first pages, and it's as she's flipping to the centre section that she hears it. The same sound she heard the morning she lost her father.

Chirp, chirp. Chirp, chirp.

A cold, cruel hand wraps around her ribcage and squeezes.

"No," Lena breathes, whirling around as if she'll be able to spot it crawling across the floor. But there's nothing there. No beetle. No spectre of death.

Maybe she imagined it. A conjuration of allowing herself a singular happy moment. Her mind might have summoned the sound as a way to remind her not to get complacent – there are only two people in her life that she cares for enough to be targeted by the curse, now, and she can't bear to lose either.

"It's just a stupid bug," Lena whispers, squeezing her eyes shut. It's what Jack has always said, since the day Lena admitted to him what really killed Lionel Luthor. He had been so sure of it, so steadfast as he told her it wasn't real. It couldn't be real. The mug in her hands is so hot that it's burning her palms, but she holds it tighter. "It doesn't mean anything. It's just a bug."

Chirp, chirp. Chirp, chirp.

It's everywhere and nowhere. Lena tears the house apart looking, her desperation mounting with every failed minute, praying to a force she doesn't believe in anymore that it's not what she thinks it is – or at the very least, that she can find it and kill it before it does its job. She rips apart the couch, tears through the cupboards, rips up half the floorboards in the kitchen, but still it eludes her. Chirping, chirping, chirping –

It stops just before the phone rings.

Jack's mother is sobbing on the other line as she tells Lena what happened. Something so simple, so fucking simple – a car taking a blind corner. Jack carrying too many packages. A hit and run with no explanation. Jack didn't even make it to the hospital.

The phone slips from Lena's fingers, hanging from its cord as Lena sinks to her knees surrounded by splintered boards and nails. Jack's mother keeps talking, distantly, sobbing the cruel truth over and over.

"He's gone. Lena, he's gone. Jack is – he's –"

Jack is gone. And it's Lena's fault.

Lena pays for the funeral and all associated burial costs. She attends the service, quietly sells off everything they owned together, packs up the bare essentials, and then she drives to Fortune Bay, Massachusetts without stopping.

The Luthor Manor is empty, but well-maintained. Lillian hasn't lived there since Lex's trial ended. Lena has continued to pay for upkeep on the outside; the grounds are well-kept, the leaves just starting to change from green to bright late-September oranges and reds, and the garden is as lush as it's ever been. But the interior is dusty and disused. Hollow. Around every corner a ghost that Lena never dealt with. It feels like a just punishment, coming back here. She never deserved to leave this house in the first place.

She wanders its halls for hours when she arrives, giving herself one day to feel everything – the pain, the guilt, the crushing weight of the curse that runs through her veins – before she shuts off the valve for good.

Lena leaves her childhood room untouched. She moves into a larger guest suite in a different wing, and she hires no staff; she lets the gardener continue his weekly work outside, but Lena shutters the Manor windows and retreats to the basement lab that Lillian had built for Lex in the hopes that he would come back home after grad school.

Her mother's book stays in its box, and Lena locks herself in. She doesn't tell Sam where she's gone. Sam's letters, if she sends them, will bounce back from Lena's old address until her first and only friend will finally presumably give up on trying.

If Lena loves nobody – if nobody loves her - nobody can get hurt.

She knows that Fortune Bay's residents have noticed the house being slightly more occupied than it used to be. She sees people point and whisper as they walk by through the cracks in the shutters; she sees kids daring each other to knock on the door of the town's haunted house, sprinting up the path and pulling on the bell before turning back with shrill, terrified laughter. Lena has groceries delivered and left on the front porch, and if ever she needs to venture into town, it's clear that she's been labelled as the resident crazy recluse. She even once sees a grown-up and immaculately dressed Siobhan Smythe shielding her children from Lena as they pass each other on the street.

Lena prefers it that way. The further away everyone stays from her, the better.

It takes close to a year before Sam's letters start to arrive. The first one is a question, a shot in the dark based on the possibility of Lena doing exactly what she's done and coming back here, and their arrival becomes almost bi-weekly once Sam seems to realize that they aren't bouncing back anymore.

Sam talks about everything and nothing in them – Lena gets updates on Ruby's life, pictures of her growing from a little girl to a teenager with a wicked twinkle in her eye that reminds Lena painfully of the Sam she remembers, brandishing a backpack at Lena's school bully.

Lena opens them, reads them voraciously and keeps them safe in a lockbox on her desk, but she never sends a reply.

 

 

The seasons change. Time passes slowly in the town Lena thought she'd escaped for good, day after day, as Lena cooks her meals and reads every book in Lionel's library and tinkers with projects that she and Jack hadn't had time for, and she keeps herself isolated from the world. Or, more accurately, she keeps the world safe from herself.

On Lena's 30th birthday, someone breaks into the house.

She's boiling the kettle around lunchtime when she hears footsteps upstairs. It's such an unfamiliar sound that for a moment Lena wonders if the metaphorical ghosts of her past have somehow become real ones – but when she hears a door open and close on the floor above, her brain finally catches up and she snatches up a kitchen knife from the block on the counter.

Her palm tingles under the handle.

She tiptoes up the grand staircase, finding that even 15 years later she still hasn't forgotten which steps are creaky and which are silent, and follows where she heard the source of the noise. It seemed to be coming from the wing she used to sleep in, somewhere near her old bedroom. It's cold up here – Lena doesn't often turn the heating on, doing it only enough to keep the pipes from freezing and choosing instead to use the fireplaces in the kitchen and her room to ward off the October chill. Up here, she can see her own breath.

She's just turning the corner when she sees the intruder. They're walking slowly down the dark hall towards the stairs, seeming to be looking up at the ceiling and mostly in shadow until Lena steps out with the knife held out in front of her with both hands.

"I know this house looks abandoned, but there's really nothing here worth stealing," Lena says, keeping her voice as steady as she can. "So if you leave right now I'll agree not to call –"

The intruder steps out of the shadowy hallway and close to a window, illuminating their features. Lena almost drops the knife.

"…Sam?"

"There you are!" Sam says, as if Lena is late to an appointment they made together rather than in her own house, finding her old friend wandering around uninvited. She completely ignores the sharp steel Lena is pointing in her direction.

She looks different than when Lena last saw her, 17 and pregnant and terrified of the future – she looks different even than the last picture she sent of herself and Ruby with one of her old letters, years ago. Older and more mature, but not without the spark that Lena remembers. She still has that brightness that kept Lena going through so many miserable years in this house. Her eyes crinkle at the corner when she smiles.

"Do you realize this house is now even creepier than it used to be? The cobwebs aren't doing you any favours."

"What – how – how did you – what are you doing here?" Lena stammers. She's not entirely sure this isn't a vivid hallucination, maybe born of loneliness. She's thought about what seeing Sam again might be like, meeting Ruby, catching up and sharing a conversation with another person – but Sam is actually here. In Lena's house. Lena's dusty, creepy, miserable house.

"I'm here to see you," Sam says, like it should be self-evident.

Lena could almost laugh. The reflex feels rusty, and it comes out more like a croak. "I mean – in Fortune Bay? I thought you were - how did you even get in?"

"The balcony, dummy. You still keep it unlocked," Sam says, jamming her finger over her shoulder at the door to Lena's old room. "And even after all these years I still remember the layout."

Lena gapes at her.

"Could you put the knife down, maybe?"

Lena drops it. A small cloud of dust erupts from the carpet where it lands. Sam steps closer; she seems taller than Lena remembers.

"Sam…" Lena whispers. Her voice cracks – her eyes are starting to burn, and her breath hitches in the barest beginnings of a sob that she claws back with great effort. Sam opens up her arms, her face soft and empathetic.

"Happy birthday, Lena."

Sam steps into her space. Lena sinks into her first hug in what feels like a lifetime, enveloped in the somehow still-familiar and comforting smell of her best friend in the world, and does her best not to break down completely.

The kettle is long cold when they make it back to the kitchen. Lena re-boils it to give herself something to do – now that the shock of seeing Sam has worn off, it's becoming more evident that Sam is here for a reason beyond saying happy birthday.

“You’ve been avoiding me,” Sam says matter-of-factly, perched with delicately crossed legs on one of Lena's kitchen stools. Lena fiddles with the gas knob.

“I’ve been busy,” she insists, but unfortunately even after years of separation Sam can still read her like a book.

“For 4 years? Bull. I’ve been in town for a week and you didn't answer the door once.”

"That was you?" Lena says, turning back around and crossing her arms defensively over her chest. She'd heard knocking on the door a few times this week, but as usual she'd ignored it – everyone in town knows that Lena won't open up. "I didn't even know you were in town."

"I've come by every day."

“I don't answer the door. It's usually kids playing pranks.”

The silence stretches thin. The kettle whistles behind her at the tail end of it, breaking the stalemate between them. She bustles over to it, carefully pouring two mugs and taking as much time as possible to do it – anything to avoid the conversation Sam is insisting on having.

"I've sent you about 100 letters," Sam says more softly. "I'm so sorry about Jack, Lena. I didn't even hear until after the funeral, and when I went to your old place you'd already sold it. I made a guess that you might have come back here, but I was hoping I was wrong."

Lena swallows. "The mail carrier never comes up here. Nobody likes to come near the crazy Luthor house." She steeps the tea, toying with the teabag and resolutely ignoring the second part of Sam's statement. Talking about Jack only reminds her that Sam needs to leave as soon as she can. Before any magic can take hold. Before the curse can reach out its tendrils. "Why are you here, Sam?"

"My mother died."

Lena turns back around, tea forgotten. "Oh. Fuck."

She hadn't given it much thought until now, but Lena hasn't seen Patricia Arias around in the whole time she's lived here. Not that she'd want to – Sam's mother had never taken kindly to Lena, nor to anything else that might have allowed Sam a bit of freedom. She and Lillian had been eerily similar in that way. But a loss is a loss.

Sam shrugs. "She was a cruel bitch, but she was the only mom I had. She left me her estate, small as it was. We had to come up and get everything sorted."

"Is Ruby here?"

"And Alex."

Lena frowns. Sam's more recent letters have made it seem like she and her newest girlfriend had only been seeing each other for a few months, though Sam seems crazy about her. "Alex came all the way here with you? Already?"

"Huh. No 'who's Alex'?" Sam says, arching a brow. "I thought you didn't get my letters?"

Lena turns back to the tea, duly chastened. "I'm sorry about Patricia."

Sam accepts her own mug. She blows on it gently and then sets it on the table without taking a sip.

Lena taps her fingers nervously on the ceramic. The dull sound seems to echo through the huge, empty house.

“I can feel it, you know," Sam finally says, her voice low and soft. "How much you’re hurting.”

It’s a startling enough sentence that Lena breaks her self-imposed stony silence and looks up sharply.

“What?”

Sam nods. “When Lex was locked away, when Jack died – I felt it. I felt you, as if you were screaming in my head. It's quieter now, but sometimes…”

Lena stares at her, still trying to compute exactly what Sam means. “How –"

Before she can form the full question, Sam holds up a hand; there, in the centre of her palm, is a short white scar. Even after 15 years it’s still visible, matching the longer scar on Lena's own palm that still stands out starkly against her skin.

“Apparently blood oaths are serious business, even if you make them when you’re a teenager,” Sam jokes, trying for levity. Lena can’t find it in herself to laugh. She rubs her own scar, staring at Sam's hand.

"Then…why haven't I felt you?" Lena says, feeling her voice go thick and strained. Sam doesn't put her hand down. It's as if she's waiting.

"Maybe you have. Maybe it's what kept you from going crazy here all alone."

Lena snorts, trying to blink away the tears that fight to escape again. "Who says I'm not crazy?"

"I do," Sam says simply. "Sad and under-socialized, maybe. But still sane."

Lena stares at Sam’s hand for a moment, at the scar that's linked them since she cast that impulsive spell, and then holds up her own. Sam grasps it palm-to-palm just like always. Just like the night they said goodbye all those years ago.

As Sam links their fingers Lena leans her head onto their gripped hands and lets it all hit her – all the loss, the pain, the grief, the guilt she's been packing away since Jack died. It rolls over her in waves, sobs clawing their way out of her chest, and she doesn’t realize she’s shaking until Sam pulls her close and smooths her hair, whispering reassuringly.

“Shh, come here. I’ve got you,” Sam murmurs, her voice soft. The voice of a caretaker. A loving mother. A side of Sam that was always there, but Lena hasn't given herself the opportunity to know. Lena gives into the comfort, craves it after her years of self-imposed solitude, and finally admits to herself exactly how much she’s missed the woman she’s always thought of as a sister. "You don’t have to be alone anymore."

Lena cries for what feels like years. It’s like someone has tied every awful, hidden feeling inside her to a chain and is now pulling it forcibly from her body to be laid bare in the light, link by devastating link. Sam holds her through all of it, and when she’s finally lost what seems like all the moisture in her body and is reduced to shaking and sniffles, Sam helps her to do what she hasn't done since she moved in.

Lena opens the shutters.

 

 

“You have to leave the house, Lena. How do you get groceries?”

“I get them delivered, and I leave when I have to. Right now, I don’t have to,” Lena argues stubbornly, but she can feel her will starting to wane.

Sam has already shoved her arms into her coat, and she’s standing impatiently with the front door open. It's windy outside, but the sky is blue – the sprawling front lawn is scattered with dying leaves, blowing away from the growing piles the gardener has left. Someone nearby is burning piles of them. The musky, smoky smell drifts over the whole estate, lending a sharp edge to the crisp air.

For 4 days now Sam has been dragging Lena out of the house in short stints – first going for morning walks around the garden and then for a quick coffee in town, during which Sam got a full demonstration of just how unsettled the town at large is by her presence – and now it’s becoming clearer why.

“Come on. I want you to meet everyone! Ruby has been begging to meet you. She's going crazy with excitement.”

At the thought of meeting the people that Sam loves – her girlfriend, her child – Lena recoils, backing further into the darkened house. To do so would only be introducing more people she could hurt. People her curse could take from her. Take from Sam.

“No,” Lena says firmly. Sam frowns.

“Why not?”

To Lena, the answer is clear as day. She absolutely, unequivocally refuses to put anyone else in danger. Sam might not believe in the curse, but Lena knows it’s real, and she can’t take the chance – if she gets to know these people, gets too fond of them, she’s putting them in harm’s way. It’s dangerous enough having Sam around again. She won’t let anyone else in. She can’t.

“Because I’m dangerous.”

“What? Because you have magic? Jesus, Lena, you’re not going to light my girlfriend on fire –"

“That’s not what I’m afraid of,” Lena says darkly.

A look of realization dawns on Sam's face in the stifling silence that follows. She steps closer to Lena, who still tries to shrink back into the safety of the house.

“Your father and Jack…they weren’t your fault,” Sam says softly.

Lena shakes her head, that familiar cold hand gripping her heart at the knowledge that Sam is wrong. She heard the beetle. She's heard her mother's voice in her dreams since the day Jack died, echoes of one of their last conversations before she had gotten too weak to tell Lena stories anymore. With the sweet comes the sour, my darling. Love is a potent force. But we Walsh women bring ruin to those we love.

“They were my fault,” Lena insists, her voice breaking slightly. “I’m cursed, Sam."

“Lena, you need to stop blaming –" Sam tries to interrupt, but finally, Lena loses it.

“No!” Lena says more loudly, and Sam startles at the volume, stepping back. “Everyone I love dies, Sam. Do you understand? Everyone anyone in my family loves dies. Romantic love, familial love, platonic – it doesn't seem to matter! They either die or they go insane and try to kill Superman, which is essentially a suicide attempt."

Sam is quiet, and Lena keeps going. It's cathartic to let all of this out now that she's started.

"You know I have magic, you know it’s possible, and I know that it’s real," Lena chokes on the last word. "I’m so scared – I’m so scared it’ll take you or Ruby, and I can’t lose anyone else.” The tight hold Lena usually has over her emotions starts to waver. She can feel tears forming; she shuts her eyes tight against the flow, feeling Sam’s arms come around her.

“I’m not going anywhere. Not without you,” Sam says, her words full of conviction. They distract Lena momentarily.

“What do you mean, not without me?"

“I’m not going to let you rot away in this shitty town,” Sam says, as if it’s a simple concept. “Everyone here is backwards, and they’re all afraid of you. You don't deserve to be alone like this.”

“I'm fine,” Lena starts, but Sam isn’t having any of it.

“A child threw a pumpkin at you yesterday when we were walking down the street. A pumpkin, Lena. These people all either think you’re a crazy witch –"

“Which I am,” Lena interrupts, but Sam holds up an impatient hand.

“Or, they think you’re going to be like Lex, both of which are untrue.”

“I can’t just go back to National City with you,” Lena says, shaking her head. The thought of being surrounded by so many people, people who could be hurt because of some stupid Irish curse her birth mother passed down – it makes her shudder.

“Why not? What’s keeping you here?” Sam challenges.

“Everything I touch falls apart. I stay here, alone, because it’s safer for everyone else,” Lena finally admits, staring hard at the carpet to avoid seeing the pity she knows is in Sam’s eyes. Sam seems to let that sink in for a moment, but finally she speaks up, saying exactly what Lena expected her to.

“You can’t stay holed up here, miserable, just because of what might happen.”

“It will happen,” Lena says, unshakeable conviction in every syllable. "It did happen."

Sam sighs. She sounds close to defeated.

“Look, I’m here for 3 more weeks at least. Hang out with me and Alex, meet Ruby and Kara. See that being alone sucks. And when it's time for us to go, if none of us have kicked the bucket, at least think about it.”

Sam seems to be going for a last-ditch attempt, and strangely enough, it’s this try that finally gives Lena pause. Something sweeps through her at the mention of this new name. Kara. Kara. Warm tingles spread from the base of her neck all the way to her toes, taking all the anxiety of their conversation with them and leaving Lena feeling strangely…calm.

“Who’s Kara?” Lena asks, almost dazedly. When Sam gives her hand a tug, pulling her over the threshold and out onto the porch, Lena doesn't resist. “Does Alex have a daughter, too?”

Sam smiles, shaking her head. “No. Kara is her sister – she decided to tag along with us. Apparently she’s never seen the Atlantic Ocean. I don’t know why, but she was pretty excited about coming. Alex thought it was kind of weird.”

Lena shrugs, still luxuriating in the rare absence of anxiety. In the end, she should have known she can’t argue with Sam when she sets her mind to something. “Fine. I’ll meet them. But tell them to keep their distance if they want to stay alive.”

Sam laughs, looping their arms together and leading Lena down the driveway. “God, you’re such a drama queen.”

When they arrive at Sam’s B&B just off the Main Street, making their way up the rickety stairs towards the rooms on the second floor, the tingly feeling returns in full force. It still brings the radiating sense of warmth, but this time Lena starts to feel the slightest bit of apprehension about what exactly it means.

She hasn't done any in years, but she still remembers what magic feels like. And there’s something in the air here that tastes like spellcraft.

It intensifies the closer Lena gets to the door labelled 2-B. It makes the hair on her arms prickle. Sam is talking about something, some story about something Ruby did on the road trip here, but Lena's ears are buzzing too loud to hear it.

Sam pulls out her key, but before she can put it in the lock, the knob twists and the door opens.

When Lena locks gazes with the clearest blue eyes she’s ever seen in her life behind a set of black-framed glasses, something inside her slides into place. A puzzle piece finding its home. The blue eyes are attached to a handsome face, and blonde hair, and broad shoulders, and a dazzling smile, and –

“Hi! You must be Lena,” the blonde woman says, sounding slightly breathless. She holds out a hand, and Lena takes it on autopilot. “I’m Kara. I’ve heard so much about you from Sam!”

The moment their hands touch, the magic in the air shifts on a monumental scale. Everything inside her is screaming that this is good, this is right, this is perfect – and judging by the awestruck look on Kara’s face, she feels it too. There's a cool breeze swirling around them despite being indoors, enough to ruffle Lena's hair and throw Kara a little off balance. It smells like fresh herbs – like rosemary and violet. Lavender.

And rose petals.

The final scent that drifts past her nose unlocks something in Lena's memory. A night she'd since put out of her mind – laying herbs in a circle, watching Sam chew on mint leaves, and protecting her heart by calling for a soulmate that couldn’t possibly exist.

They wear glasses. And they have blue eyes. The brightest blue eyes.

“Oh, wow,” Kara whispers, almost inaudible.

A stab of panic enters Lena's unexpected nirvana state.

Oh…oh, no.

 

 

Kara Danvers is, by all Lena's objective metrics, annoyingly perfect.

She's sweet, friendly, and relentlessly kind. She loves dogs and ice cream, she gets along famously with Ruby. And she's absurdly, superhumanly handsome. Bouncy blonde waves, clear blue eyes, bow lips that curve into a smile that takes Lena's breath away. She's taller than Lena by a few inches, with deceptively broad shoulders under her cozy wool sweater.

All of these facts Lena learns within an hour of meeting her. Everything about her, from her musical voice to her warm, genuine smile, calls to Lena like a siren's song.

Half of Lena wants to follow it to whatever conclusion it's leading her to. The other half is screaming at her to run, to flee back home and lock the doors and block out all thoughts of blue eyes and glasses and warm smiles, but she's kept in her seat by Ruby's unfiltered joy at their introduction.

Sam's daughter is every bit as enthusiastic and lovable as her mother is, and Sam wasn't lying – she's very excited to meet Lena.

"Do you like living here, Aunt Lena? Can I call you that?"

"Of course, that's…that's fine," Lena says, twisting her hands together in her lap. Ruby is perched next to her on the middle cushion of a patchy couch in the sitting-room area of the B&B suite Sam has booked – Kara has an adjacent room, but just so happened to be in Sam's when Lena arrived – and has been peppering Lena with questions since the moment she sat down. "Living here is…fine."

"Cool! I've never been to a town this small," Ruby says, her grin so reminiscent of Sam at age 14 that Lena can't help but return it. "Do you guys have a Blockbuster?"

"No?"

"That's what mom said, but I didn't believe her. We brought some movies with us. Maybe we can have a movie night! What was my mom like when you were kids?"

Lena laughs a little, trying to relax her posture. Remembering Sam's fiery nature, Lena's biggest source of protection then, is helpful. "She was a hellion."

"She still is," Alex says, giving Sam a firm kiss on the cheek. Alex hasn't talked directly to Lena yet except to shake her hand when she arrived, but she seems steadfast in her love of Sam, and for that Lena can't dislike her even if she's somewhat distant. Lena might even thank her, if that distance means she keeps Sam far enough away not to get hurt.

"Like mother, like daughter," Sam says with a wink.

"How did you meet?" Ruby asks, looking back and forth between them. "My mom has told me a lot but sometimes she doesn't answer questions because she gets too sad. Because she missed you."

"You're trying to protect us," Ruby says, far more insightful than Lena expected. "That's what families do."

Lena wouldn't know. She's never been protected by anyone but Sam. But she nods.

"I'm sorry things couldn't be different. I wish they could be."

More than anything Lena wants Ruby to understand. To know why she holds herself back. She wants Ruby to know in her heart that it isn't her fault. Lena doesn't ever want to be Lillian, withholding love from people who need it. But she just can't risk letting what happened to Jack ruin someone else's life.

Ruby sniffles.

"It's okay. I think they're gonna be."

Ruby says it with the confidence of a teenager, that unshakeable faith that Sam always had and which Lena has never really experienced. It's a confidence that feels almost prophetic. And to Lena's surprise, Ruby puts her head on Lena's shoulder. They sit in the quiet as the sun leaves the horizon and shifts from orange to bright yellow, and Lena cries silently as the birds wake up to greet the day.

 

 

Halloween has always been Lena's least favourite holiday.

Even as a child she resented the imagery. Dancing skeletons, grinning pumpkins, cackling witches on brooms – it's both inaccurate and needlessly macabre, and Lena's real life has always been stressful enough without the need to seek out being frightened. These days, Halloween means fielding attempted break-ins by unruly teens and scraping dried egg off the windows.

This year Halloween apparently means trick-or-treating with Ruby.

Lena has never been trick-or-treating, nor has a single person knocked on her door for it in the years since she came back to this town. She's seen streams of children parading past the end of her driveway through the windows, but none ever make the trek to her darkened doorstep unless it's to enact some vandalism. And now Ruby wants everyone to accompany her in going door-to-door.

As if Lena could say no. Ruby is quickly gaining the same place in Lena's heart that her mother holds, and all the teenager has to do is bat her eyelashes and throw in a pouting 'but I packed a costume!' for Lena to give in.

It actually isn't so bad at first. Being with a group means that most people pay Lena no mind, which is a nice change of pace. Ruby gets lots of compliments on her Sporty Spice costume, and Kara buys everyone a warm apple cider to sip as they follow her from house to house. Lena could almost say she's having a good time until they reach a particularly ostentatious house and a familiar face answers the door.

Lena hasn't had an interaction with Siobhan Smythe beyond avoiding her on the street since high school, and now here she is in all her condescending glory with a plastic cauldron full of candy on her hip. Lena is standing partly in Kara's shadow, so her former bully's eyes fall first on Sam.

"Shit," Sam mutters.

"Samantha Arias," Siobhan says the moment their eyes meet, her haughty smile turning sour. "I didn't think I'd ever be seeing your face in this town again."

"Missed you too, Siobhan," Sam says, rolling her eyes and putting her hands on Ruby's confused shoulders. "Are you going to give my kid her candy, or are you still the person you were in grade school?"

Ruby is looking back and forth between them, her eyebrows raised. Lena can imagine she isn't used to anyone talking to her mother this way.

"Isn't she a little old to be trick-or-treating? What would she be, now – 13? 14?" Siobhan says pointedly. It's a clear barb, but Sam just tilts her head, her expression matching Siobhan's in condescension.

"Aw, you remembered!" Sam says, sarcasm dripping from every word. "See, I knew you cared."

Siobhan's face twitches into something less controlled. "It's hard to forget the teen impregnation of the town whore."

That seems to activate everyone else. Ruby bristles, looking about ready to smack an adult with her candy bag, and Alex takes a firm step forward.

"What did you just call her?"

Sam stops Alex in her tracks with a hand on her arm.

"That is super rude," Kara says, folding her arms and moving next to Alex. She's more conversational than her sister, but still clearly in deep dislike. "You talk like that in front of kids?"

Unfortunately, Kara moving means that Lena is now fully visible. Siobhan's eyes snap to her, and that small childish part of Lena that never quite managed to stand up to her shrinks back under her gaze.

"And you're with her. I should have known you two freaks would still be stuck together," Siobhan says. The snideness in her voice has turned to something deeper, a long-standing hatred that only seemed to brew and get worse when Sam stepped in and she could no longer torment Lena as much as she'd like. "It's bad enough you came back here, but now you're just walking around with the rest of us as if you haven't gone crazy like your psycho brother?"

Kara's casual stance melts away. She straightens, and somehow seems to gain several inches in height; her shoulders seem wider, her hands balled into fists at her sides. Lena has no idea how she hasn't noticed just how tall Kara is before now. She's not Sam's height, but even so she seems to tower over Siobhan. It's as if she's been making herself small before now. Siobhan's rosy cheeks go a bit pale.

Kara doesn't say anything, but she looks back at Lena as if to ask permission. Swallowing hard, Lena shakes her head. Siobhan's words still sting. But the last thing she needs is a scene to be made with other families now waiting behind them to get their candy.

"It's fine," Lena says quietly, but when Siobhan smirks as if this means she's won something inside Lena snaps in response. "Cruelty isn't out of character for her. You know what they say. Once a bully, always a bully."

She says it loud enough for the people behind them to hear, and when they start to titter Siobhan's cheeks turn pink. But Kara smiles down at Lena. She seems almost proud, and Lena's back straightens under her wordless praise.

"Huh," Ruby says, sizing Siobhan up. "Yeah, I get why Mom punched her."

Siobhan's smug grin has turned into a glare. Sam takes Ruby by the shoulders and guides her away, giving Siobhan no more of her time. "Come on, honey. We don't need her candy. I'll get you an extra chocolate bar on the way home."

Sam fulfills her promise, buying several candy bars along with enough snacks for a small army from the general store. Lena waits outside, not eager for any more confrontation, and she can't tell whether she's relieved or not when Kara hangs back with her.

"You okay?" Kara asks softly.

"Fine. Nothing I'm not used to," Lena says, shrugging. She digs her toe into the gravel of the parking lot. "Siobhan isn't the first to say something like that. Most people here think I'm crazy."

Kara leans against the outside of the building, and Lena can feel her gaze burning against the side of her face. She doesn't speak, but her clear desire to say something is thick on the air. Lena tries to keep her own eyes fixed on her feet, but when several long moments have passed and Sam still hasn't exited the store she can't help it. She looks up.

Kara is looking at her with an expression of such painful empathy that Lena wishes she hadn't.

"What?" Lena says, rubbing her arms. The night is chilly, and despite her warm coat she has goosebumps.

"That makes me sad," Kara says simply.

Lena scoffs. "It shouldn't. You hardly know me."

"Neither do they."

Kara's words tangle in Lena's chest. It feels physical, the way they hit her – but Sam blessedly chooses that moment to appear with Alex carrying the fruits of their shopping trip and Ruby already several packets of Smarties deep in her candy bag.

"Thank god they still sell cheap wine here. I got us a bottle each," Sam says, thrusting a clinking bag into Lena's hands. "Except for Ruby. She gets soda."

"Dr. Pepper?" Kara says, grinning. Ruby throws a Smartie at her.

"Never!"

Lena only realizes when they've passed any street that might lead them downtown that they're heading not back to the B&B, but to the Luthor Manor. She hadn't thought to prepare the house for guests, but Sam is the one leading the pack, and when they get back to the house she bustles around like she's the one who lives there. Ruby wants to watch a movie, and Sam sets to getting everyone snacks and drinks while Lena turns on a TV she's pretty sure hasn't been touched in 15 years.

The screen flashes to life, dusty but useable, to a news segment.

"- causing problems in National City in the last few weeks," the anchor is saying, while behind her a photo appears. A blurred picture of a caped woman in black, a mask covering most of her face, holding a car above her head with the caption our newest masked hero?

"No-one knows who she is, or where she came from. But lately she's been stepping in for Supergirl as the city's the caped crusader," the anchor continues, her hands folded on the desk. "Supergirl has been conspicuously absent since early last week, and according to CatCo, refuses to comment."

A reel plays, then – security footage of the black-caped woman intervening in a robbery. She saves the victims, but she has none of Superman or Supergirl's control in apprehending the robbers. She grabs one by the throat, throwing the other viciously into the wall, before disappearing at the first flash of police lights and leaving them crumpled on the ground.

The rest of the group filters in from the kitchen, their chatter dying down when Lena turns up the volume.

"Is this yet another Kryptonian on earth? Is she here to help, or to hurt? Where is Supergirl, and can we expect this to be a nemesis…or an ally? More on this at 11."

To Lena's right, Alex and Kara exchange a look she can't decipher.

"I wonder where Supergirl is," Lena says, turning the volume back down when the news turns to less interesting stories. "She's usually the first person on the scene in National City, isn't she?"

"Do you…know much about Supergirl?" Kara asks. She sounds strangely nervous – Alex elbows her, but Kara seems to ignore it.

"Well, my brother tried to kill her cousin," Lena says drily. "I've done my research."

"She doesn't hold that against you," Kara says. It seems to slip out of her, an instinctive reassurance with no basis whatsoever, and Lena looks at her sharply.

"You have no way of knowing that."

"I do, actually," Kara says, and Alex elbows her again, harder this time. Kara winces, but continues to ignore her. "We…um. We know each other."

"You know Supergirl?" Lena drawls. She's sure everyone and their mother in National City likes to brag about knowing Supergirl personally, but she didn't expect that kind of sourceless bragging from Kara.

Alex's glare at the side of Kara's head could melt steel.

"She's been a source for some of my stories," Kara says, sidestepping Alex and putting her armful of snacks down on the coffee table. Lena raises a brow.

"Then do you know why she isn't doing her job?"

Kara opens her mouth, but nothing comes out. The silence stretches on past the point of uncomfortable until Sam clears her throat.

"I'm sure Supergirl is doing something important," she says, looking pointedly at Kara as she throws a towering armful of blankets and pillows onto the couch. "But more importantly, as per Ruby's request we have a blanket fort to make."

The blanket fort ends up encompassing the entire couch and more. Ruby is nothing if not determined to make it structurally sound, and once all of them are piled inside – far physically closer than Lena is used to, with Ruby cuddled up between Sam and Alex and Kara so close to Lena's other side that Lena can feel her warmth through the blanket draped over her lap – Ruby chooses a Halloween-themed Disney movie Lena isn't familiar with and they settle in to snack and watch.

Lena isn’t sure what to do with herself, if she's being honest. Having people over in this house is already foreign. Sam had been the only person to visit her here growing up, and they almost never left Lena's room. But she can't deny that it's fun. Kara and Ruby sing along to the musical parts of the movie, consuming more candy than is probably advisable, and Lena tries to settle into the comfortable feeling she usually resists.

Less than halfway through the film Kara pauses it, frowning.

"I hear people outside. Do you have super late-night gardeners or something?"

"I don't hear anything," Ruby says.

"It's probably just kids egging the house," Lena says with a careless wave. She takes another handful of popcorn, munching on it while Kara blinks at her. "Ignore it."

"Why would they egg your house?"

"It happens every year," Lena explains. "Halloween pranks. People think I'm crazy, remember? It's fine, I'll clean it up in the morning."

Kara frowns again. She looks towards where the front door is, far on the other end of the house, and then stands up, dusting the powdery barbecue popcorn seasoning from her hands.

"I'll be right back."

Kara is gone in a flash. Lena scrambles to her feet, following what Kara's path must have been through the halls until she reaches the foyer, but by the time she's at the front door Kara is already coming back inside. Her cheeks are ruddy, and for some reason she's buttoning up her shirt.

"What happened?" Lena says, breathless from the power-walk through the house. Kara smiles.

"You don't need to worry about them anymore!" Kara says cheerfully. "And they weren't kids, by the way. They were way too old to be trying to egg a house."

Kara finishes with the shirt, and then seems to realize her fingers are sticky.

"Do you have egg on your hands?" Lena asks. Kara's mouth twists. A shadow of guilt, maybe.

"Um. Yes?"

Moving past Kara, Lena opens the door to glance out at the porch. In a plastic grocery bag near the stairs are several egg cartons, mostly untouched but for a few neatly-cracked shells. And there's no egg on the house. At the far end of the driveway Lena can see distant figures sprinting away. If she squints, she could swear one of their heads was faintly yellow.

"What did you do, exactly?" Lena says, shivering in the doorway. Kara holds her hands up in surrender.

"Nothing bad! Just taught them the error of their ways."

Kara brushes past her, far closer than she needs to be given the width of the doorframe. Her shirt is cold from the outdoors, but Lena can feel the heat of her body radiating from her through the whisper of space between them.

"How?" Lena asks.

"They just needed a talking-to, that's all."

"Kara –"

But Kara is gone, headed to the kitchen to wash her hands. Lena is left by the open door staring at the bag of eggs, wondering what on earth Kara did – and above all, how the hell she did it in the mere moments before Lena got to the door.

Movie night continues on afterwards largely thanks to Ruby's insistence. They make it through two and a half before Lena falls asleep – Sam is holding her hand, Alex on Sam's other side, Ruby's head in her lap and Kara's fluffy-socked feet tucked under the pillow Lena is sitting on. They feel like a fortress around her, shielding her from the outside world in this castle of fabric and diffused light.

Maybe Halloween isn't so bad.

 

 

Lena's neck is unbelievably stiff when she wakes up to weak, cloudy sunlight through the sitting-room windows. Ruby is snoring, spread across Sam's lap and Lena's, and Sam and Alex have curled into each other in the night – it's sweet, the perfect picture of a little family, and Lena is smiling despite her aching muscles when she notices that Kara is awake.

Awake, and staring at her.

Kara looks somewhat embarrassed to be caught, but not as much as she rightly should be. And Lena conversely isn't unnerved by it like she wishes she was. It feels familiar and terrifying and perfectly natural.

"Morning," Kara says. Her follow Lena's movement as she stretches with a groan. "Sleep well?"

"Shockingly," Lena says curtly, rolling her neck from side to side. "But I'm sure my back won't thank me for sleeping upright."

Kara shuffles closer. Her smile is lopsided and quietly confident.

"Yeah, I woke up half under the couch. The dust bunnies and I became very good –"

Kara stops.

Even if her words hadn't trailed off, Lena would have noticed the shift in her expression. It's abrupt; Kara pales. Her grin doesn't fade so much as it drops, and she sits up straight as she moves closer to Lena with a sudden intensity.

"What?" Lena says, shrinking back. Kara stops, but she's still holding Lena's gaze with such concentration that Lena is having trouble looking away.

"Your eyes," Kara says. There's something charged in her voice, something emotional and unstable as she seems to drink in something Lena can't comprehend. Something urgent.

Lena blinks.

"What about them?"

Kara's eyes dart back and forth between Lena's, like she's been searching desperately for something all her life and only just found it there. It makes Lena's heart hammer in her chest.

"They're – they're different colours."

Kara is so close. Her breath is warm, and with her gazing so intently Lena can see all the details in Kara's eyes – the variation in the shades of blue, the warm flecks of hazel, the jagged ring of grey around her iris. It's like spiral hypnosis, and Lena's voice is low and whispery when she manages to speak.

"Yes?"

Kara swallows. The slow bob of her throat almost draws Lena's eyes away. Almost. "I thought they were green. But – one is…"

Kara's hand, which had been reaching out as if to touch Lena's cheek under the eye in question, drops suddenly when Lena glances at it.

"Blue."

Lena should move back to a more respectable distance, but she's frozen in place. For a single wild moment she considers bringing Kara's hand to her face herself; she wants to be touched by her so badly that she can almost imagine the feeling like a sense memory. Lena wants to lean in, to sink into Kara like a hot bath and let herself be enveloped. No matter how deeply she knows she shouldn't.

"Is that a problem?" Lena manages to say. Her voice is low and a little scratchy.

"No," Kara says in an earnest whisper. Her eyes are shimmering with an emotion Lena can't understand. "No, it's…it's beautiful."

Sometime in the last few exchanges they've drifted closer. The last word is said so close to Lena's lips that she can practically taste it, the sweet tartness of a compliment she wants to refute but can't seem to find the words – Kara's shaky breath is coming quicker, the blue and grey and hazel of her eyes overtaken now by wide irises, the warmth of her so inviting that Lena can't help but -

Behind her, Ruby stirs.

They spring apart. Lena reels back, knocking against Sam in the process, and with a sleepy grumble she wakes up too. Alex grunts beside her, kicking her feet out to wiggle her toes, and in the confusion Lena hauls herself to her feet and away from Kara, stumbling out of the blanket fort. Her pulse has jumped up further, as impossible as that seems.

"Morning, guys!" Kara says, her voice unnaturally high. Alex groans, throwing an arm over her eyes.

"Too bright."

Sam is considerably perkier, combing a few tangles from Ruby's hair. "Next time we sleep in one of Lena's 800 guest rooms. I'm getting too old to sleep on the floor. How'd you sleep, Rubes?"

Ruby, who seems perfectly refreshed in comparison to everyone else, grins.

"Great! Can we have pancakes?"

After Kara makes a (suspiciously fast, in Lena's opinion) trip to the market the kitchen is bustling with pancake-making activity – Sam and Ruby slicing fruit, Alex determinedly whipping up fresh whipped cream with the truly ancient hand-mixer she found in a cupboard, and Lena piling plates and cups onto a tray to carry to the table while Kara starts to ladle batter into a cast iron skillet.

"What shapes does everyone want?"

Lena's stomach lurches. She almost drops a plate, but Alex catches it with a quick hand, handing it back to her without looking up from her task.

"Shapes?" Lena says.

"Yeah! Whatever shape you want. Cactus, Christmas tree, motorcycle," Kara lists off, forming the batter into a configuration that looks vaguely like an elephant. It sizzles in the hot butter. "Mickey Mouse. I can even do the Statue of Liberty, this pan is big enough!"

"Can you make a big sunflower?" Ruby asks. She sneaks a few raspberries while Sam isn't looking and pops them into her mouth. Bubbles are forming on the surface of Kara's elephant pancake, and Kara loosens it with a spatula.

"You betcha," Kara says, flashing a wink at Lena. And somehow Lena knows what's coming. It feels as inexorable as the almost-kiss, and somehow just as terrifying.

Kara gives the pan a shake, and with a practiced flick she flips the pancake high in the air and catches it on its opposite side in a perfect arc.

Lena's own voice, long-repressed, seems to whisper in her ear again.

They can make pancakes in funny shapes. Flip them in the air.

"You okay?" Kara asks a few minutes later, noticing Lena's sudden silence as she sets the first perfect elephant-shaped pancake on a plate to keep warm in the oven. "You don't have to have a sunflower, you know. I can make you whatever you'd like."

"Round is fine," Lena says distantly. Kara nods, ladling two perfectly circular cakes into the pan.

"Coming right up!"

They eat in the garden, bathed in sunshine and serenaded by birds and ocean waves. The midmorning light makes the hairs escaping Kara's messy bun flare like a golden halo around her face. And Lena, despite the rollercoaster her stomach has been on this morning, eats every unfairly delicious pancake Kara made for her plus two extras – a lion, and a hot air balloon complete with chocolate chip stripes.

Any attempt at not being drawn into Kara's orbit is a resounding failure.

 

 

Ruby's solution to sore muscles is, antithetically, a hike. She's a far more active teenager than Lena ever was, and twice as convincing – by late afternoon Lena is in running shoes and comfortable pants, and they're heading back to the B&B to get everyone changed into walking clothes to explore the forested part of the Luthor property.  

Alex follows Kara into her room with a determined expression while Lena goes with Sam and Ruby, and after a few minutes of getting a bag together with some hiking essentials Lena excuses herself to the hall to let them get changed. She paces slowly to fill the time, her path getting more and more elongated, but she stops in her tracks when she hears her name from behind Kara's door.

It sounds like Kara's voice. Loud. Upset.

Lena steps closer.

"-need to focus more on what's going on at home. Things are clearly going downhill without you," Alex is saying, muffled by the wood but still audible. "You haven't been away this long in years, and with this new person causing problems -"

"J'onn can handle it," Kara counters immediately. "It's only been a week and a half. There hasn't been any mass destruction. Maybe this person isn't a threat to the city like everyone thinks."

Lena frowns. Maybe Kara is more important at the Tribune than Lena assumed, if she's apparently this necessary for the National City news cycle. From Alex's tone, Kara not being there to deal with the person from the news last night seems like an emergency.

"Kara, I've never seen you want to be away from the team. You've jumped at every opportunity to get involved ever since you started this Supergirl thing. Why now do you –"

"Am I not allowed to have a break? I'm always on duty. All the time. Can't I have a few weeks off?"

Kara sounds more than upset. She sounds exhausted.

"Are you saying that because you need a vacation, or because you're fixated on –"

"Don't," Kara says, her voice getting so quiet that Lena almost can't hear it.

The silence is so sudden that Lena's ears seem to ring.

"I'm sorry," Alex says after a few beats, more softly this time. "I know you're tired, Kara. I just think maybe you're abandoning Supergirl at a pretty inopportune time."

"J'onn hasn't called to ask for help. Maybe Supergirl needs a break, too," Kara says. "Maybe the city needs to figure out how to manage without her for once."

Kara sighs, and Lena hears the squeak of distant mattress springs.

"I know you don't want me to. But I need to tell her."

Lena frowns. Tell who what?

"I think we all have things to tell her," Alex mutters. There's a slight pause. "And you're certainly dancing pretty close to it after what you pulled last night. Supergirl is pretty prominently missing from National City, and then you risk -"

Lena hears the turning of Sam's doorknob just in time. She darts away from Kara's door, leaning against the opposite wall in a way she hopes is, and thankfully neither Sam nor Ruby question why she's halfway down the hall.

Lena plays the overheard conversation over and over in her mind as they walk through underbrush and crunchy leaves. Kara is carrying Ruby on her back through some muddy parts of the path, with Sam leading the way pushing branches aside with a long stick; the colours are beautiful, the weather perfect for a walk through the woods, but Lena's mind is elsewhere.

As much as Lena doubted it last night, it really does seem like Kara truly knows Supergirl. A partnership, by the sounds of it. Lena should find that alarming. She should be terrified of Kara Danvers the intrepid reporter with close ties to Supergirl. But she thinks back to Kara's words. Supergirl doesn't hold that against you. Kara had seemed so sure of it, had said it so easily without prompting.

Maybe it's true.

And on top of that, apparently everyone is keeping something from her. Lena doesn’t have much of a leg to stand on considering Alex and Kara have no idea that Lena is a witch with a blood-curse that puts them in danger of dying at any moment, but even so all Lena can hear as she walks is I need to tell her. We all have things to tell her. After what you pulled last night -

She's startled from her thoughts only by the pure shock of Alex hanging back to walk with her. She blinks, looking around, to find that Sam, Kara and Ruby are gone. They must have pulled ahead, and Lena has been wandering with her head in the clouds.

"Where is everyone?"

"Kara thought she could find a viewpoint over the ocean," Alex says. "Did you not notice it was only me with you?"

Lena frowns. "I didn't. When did they –"

"There's another reason we came here," Alex interrupts, apropos of nothing. Her voice is low and somewhat urgent. "Came to see you specifically. I think you need to know."

"I thought you were sorting out Patricia's estate?" Lena says. Alex barges straight through to drop what feels like a bombshell on Lena's head.

"Sam has been having blackouts."

The wave of terror that grips Lena is among the worst she's ever experienced. It's worse than when a pregnant Sam told her that she needed to leave, worse than watching Lex destroy Metropolis. It's dangerously close to the devastation of the phone call from Jack's mother, despite Sam being alive and well nearby. Fear and self-blame, her two most common bedfellows, wrap around her lungs until it feels like she can't breathe. Her knees feel weak.

"What do you mean?" Lena says, stumbling to a stop and grabbing a nearby sapling for support. "What kind of blackouts?"

"Periods of time she can't remember," Alex says. She's solemn as she so often seems to be when Lena is around, but now it almost comforts Lena. Knowing that Alex is as concerned as she is. She's taking this seriously. "Long periods, sometimes. A few times she's disappeared for hours and then can't remember where she's been." Lena can hear the worry in Alex's voice, and somehow it eases some of the terror. She clearly cares about Sam deeply. Efficient and capable.

Lena wonders suddenly what Alex does for a living. Sam has never mentioned it, but something in her quiet calm makes Lena wonder if she's in the medical field.

"Has she gotten any tests? Scans, MRIs, cognitive –"

"Yes. She's seeing specialists, and she goes to an experimental clinic in Metropolis every few months," Alex says. She kicks at some dirt, sending it scattering across the path. She seems restless. But Lena can't make herself walk again. "So far we don't have any answers, but…she's scared. She wanted to reconnect with you. In case…"

Alex's voice cracks, and Lena remembers Ruby's words from last week. I think it would be cool. To have more people around, in case…

A familiar feeling, a familiar voice, slithers across Lena's mind.

This is your fault. You've cursed her.

It's already too late.

Like always it forms a sick pit in Lena's stomach, but it's different this time. She isn't alone in her worry and grief – Alex is at Sam's side. Ruby. Kara, even. This time there's another voice too. One that tells her not to make this about herself for once. As scared as Lena is to lose her best friend, she can't imagine how terrified Sam is right now. The last thing she needs is Lena's self-flagellation.

She hasn't heard the beetle yet.

"I would be very grateful if you kept me updated," Lena says unsteadily. "And…thank you. For letting me know."

Alex nods. Her shoulders seem to relax, un-bunching in a deep exhale.

"And Alex," Lena says, catching the sleeve of Alex's jacket as she starts to walk away. "I'm sorry."

Alex pauses mid-step. And then she stays, giving Lena the ear she's asking for.

"I distanced myself from Sam for her protection. I never meant to hurt her. I wanted to keep her safe," Lena says, holding Alex's expectant gaze. Her eyes are a light, warm brown. She's so different from Kara, but Lena can see their similarities as well – her loyalty, her protectiveness. Lena has hardly known Kara for a week, but already she feels like she knows her.

Alex nods. "From your brother?"

"Among other things," Lena says, swallowing past the curse-sized lump in her throat. "But I did hurt her. And I'm sorry."

"You should tell her that," Alex says. But there's no judgement in her voice now. Lena nods.

"I know. I will."

"I can respect trying to protect her," Alex continues, starting to walk again but inviting Lena to follow. They wade through leaves together in tandem. "I've done worse things to keep Kara safe. It's what sisters do."

Alex says it in a casual tone, but it's that very fact that makes Lena's eyes sting with sudden tears. That Alex so easily sees Lena's relationship with Sam as equitable with her own with Kara. The love Alex holds for her sister is clear in their every interaction, even when they're in disagreement. And Alex understands.

"Yes. It should be," Lena says. She can't quite keep the thickness out of her voice despite successfully keeping any tears from falling, and Alex stops again.

"Oh, hey, I'm sorry. Don't cry," Alex says, wincing, as if pointing it out isn't a surefire way to make Lena do the opposite. "Kara will kill me if I made you cry."

Lena laughs wetly, pressing the heels of her hands to her eyes. "I don't see why she would care. And Kara is probably a mile ahead by now. I doubt –"

"Alex! Did you make Lena cry?"

Kara's voice is sudden enough to make Lena jump, and she moves her hands and opens her eyes to see the blonde standing in front of them with her arms crossed. The leave around them are blowing around in a sudden gust of wind, and Kara isn't even out of breath. Her shoes are caked in mud.

It's strange enough that it almost dips into the category of impossible.

"Speculation still abounds over who Reign really is, and why she's been hostile towards Supergirl –"

"Can you turn that off, please?" Sam asks quietly. Her face has gone even paler, highlighting the shadows under her eyes. Ruby keeps flicking through channels until she finds a movie she recognizes, and for the duration of it they huddle together eating their ice cream – Sam braiding and unbraiding Ruby's hair in a gesture that seems self-soothing, and Alex watching as if she can protect them simply by being vigilant enough.

Kara sits almost isolated at the end of the couch, picking at the pilled fabric of her sweater with her unbandaged hand.  Her brow is furrowed. She barely touches her ice cream.

They don't stay the night. Alex thinks that sleeping at the B&B might help, that perhaps the unfamiliar room had been part of Sam's problem last night. Lena thinks she's grasping at straws, but she understands the impulse all the same. When faced with the unknown it's hard not to seek solutions wherever possible.

When Lena lies awake in bed, unsettled by the sudden quiet in a house that had for a brief time been filled with colour and noise, she can't stop thinking about Supergirl.

The Girl of Steel. Protector of National City, after a short vacation. And Kara's friend, apparently. Kara, who Lena now remembers had disappeared last night along with Sam. Maybe what Kara had to take care of had something to do with Supergirl's fight with this Reign woman. What a journalist like Kara could possibly have done to help Lena has no idea, though – she's only human.

In the dark canopy of her bed with nothing else to distract her but her thoughts, that statement suddenly strikes Lena as…presumptuous.

Only human. Kara has no immediately evident traits that point otherwise – no antennae, no pointed teeth, nothing about her appearance that seems otherworldly. But like a series of slides on a projector Lena's mind shuffles through the other traits she's noticed – Kara's strength, far outmatching the size of her body. Her slightly unnatural speed. How she seemed to have heard Lena upset over her conversation with Alex, and appeared moments later in a gust of wind.

All the ridiculous, impossible things Lena's spell had asked for, and how effortlessly Kara fulfills them.

Lena is out of bed and grasping for the nearest notebook before she knows it. She scribbles a list – Kara's strange traits, her behaviour, even the physical traits that Lena now realizes are a perfect match. Blonde hair. Wide shoulders. Blue eyes. Lena has never seen Supergirl up close, but everyone with access to a magazine in the last 5 years has seen a picture.

She's left with pages of notes, a detailed comparison column, and a dawning realization. Kara doesn't just fit some of Lena's criteria. She fits all of it. Lena's spell worked. The person she called for, with all their impossible human traits, is real – because she isn't human. She's an alien. An alien with suspiciously specific powers.

On the news reel, Supergirl had held her right arm against her body as if injured. And Kara's right wrist has been bandaged all day.

It's the only way to explain how quickly Lena has become attached to Kara. How immediately Kara had her trust. And how absolutely terrified Lena is now to lose her. Lena's childish spell has fully wound its way around them both, pushing them together against both of their wills. Bound a Super to a Luthor. Linked Kara to Lena, whose blood is not just shared with the man who tried to kill her only family but also carries a curse that could kill Kara, too.

Even faced with the evidence, it feels absurd. For Lena's spell to have landed on Supergirl of all people? For Supergirl to be Kara? It's too much of a cosmic joke. A last laugh for the universe – after all Lex did to wipe Superman off the face of the earth, Lena could be the one to truly damn the House of El.

Lena climbs back into bed with her mind racing. She hardly sleeps, caught up in dreams of chirping beetles and red colliding with black.

When she meets everyone for lunch the next day, Kara's bandaged wrist is completely healed.

 

 

 

 

Sam is increasingly unspecific about when she plans on leaving Fortune Bay the longer their stay goes on.

There's no real reason for her to remain here. Patricia's house is emptied, and the work of putting it on the market and doing the paperwork could be done quickly. But Sam doesn't seem to be in any hurry. She's almost back to her old self a few days after the incident, but an exhaustion lingers around her that Lena notices in moments of quiet. A slow, gradual loss of hope. Physically she's doing better, but Lena can guess that she's fighting an inner battle against despair. It's a battle Lena has been losing in her own mind since she was a teenager.

It's hard to watch. Sam has always been a bastion, a buoy in the storm of Lena's life even if Lena only saw her life through letters, and to see her losing hope is harder than Lena losing it herself. Which is why despite her reservations, Lena makes an offer in the third week of Sam's stay in the hopes of keeping her closer while she still can.

"Why don't you all stay here at the manor instead of paying for that hotel?"

Sam and Ruby look up simultaneously, Sam from the book she's been reading and Ruby from her math work - Sam has been homeschooling her ever since their vacation turned into an extended stay, and Ruby has mentioned more than once that she prefers it to school.

"Really?" Ruby says eagerly. "We could live here with you?"

Sam closes her book, dog-earing the page. "Are you sure? We're paid up there until the end of the week."

"The B&B must be costing you a fortune by now. With your medical bills, it's silly for you to not stay here instead once the week is up. I have the room," Lena says. She hands Sam a bookmark. The suggestion is logical, but it still strikes a chord of unshakeable fear into her. She wants Sam close; but at the same time, she wants her as far from the curse as humanly possible.

But above all Lena needs to figure out what's happening in Sam's head. If Sam is here and refusing to go home, at the very least Lena can try to keep her safe here.

"And Kara too?" Ruby says.

Lena swallows hard. That's the most difficult part of this equation, but the one she can't deny that she wants. "And Kara too."

When Ruby immediately drops her schoolwork to sprint upstairs and choose her room, Lena turns to Sam. Her best friend is looking at her warmly, chuckling at Ruby's excitement.

"How long will you be staying?"

"I don't know," Sam shrugs. "I'm on medical leave. Alex's boss has been really good about her being gone. She's accumulated like, 6 months of time off at her job because she didn't know how to take a break before she met me."

"And Kara?"

"You'll have to ask her," Sam says, with a pointedly arched brow. "She's been a little evasive with her plans."

"Where is she, by the way?" Lena asks, aiming for nonchalance. She picks at her nails, not daring to look Sam in the face – Sam has always been able to read her like a book. "I haven't seen her today."

"She and Alex had some stuff to take care of."

"Stuff?" Lena prods. Sam tenses a little. Lena can see it in her jaw, her clenched hands. In the flash of guilt that crosses her face. She fidgets with the bookmark Lena gave her, not yet putting it between the pages.

"Work stuff, I think."

Lena has done her best to put it out of her mind since she came to the realization of who Kara might be. It's been difficult, given that she's seen Kara almost every day since then. But now she can't help but wonder whether or not Sam knows. It's probable, given the conversation she had with Sam on their first day clearing out Patricia's house – there were some things we hadn't told each other.

And in both cases it wasn't our secret to tell.

It's an easy enough conclusion to draw. Sam probably found out after she arrived here. And Sam was right then, as curious as Lena is about the situation. Whether or not Kara is who Lena thinks she is, and whether or not Sam knows, it isn't up to anyone but Kara to tell Lena anything. And it's not like Lena in any way deserves to know. The last person Supergirl should ever trust with her secret is a Luthor.

So Lena lets it go.

"You'll have to let them know later, then," Lena says. Sam relaxes, finally putting the bookmark in its place. But the guilt remains. It hands in the air between them, cloying and thick.

"Lena," Sam starts, sitting up straighter while she searches for words. "I should…I mean, there's something -"

Lena touches her wrist gently.

"It's okay," Lena says. She can only hope the small smile she flashes communicates her meaning. "Really."

Sam bites her lip. She still looks conflicted, but in the end she nods.

"Okay."

Ruby's heavy footfalls come racing down the stairs, breaking the moment cleanly.

"How many rooms does this place have?" Ruby pants, her face red from exertion. She must have run at top speed through the entire upper level to be back so soon. "I've looked in like, 30 of them already."

"Too many," Lena chuckles. She pulls Sam up by the hand, and as a group they head back up the stairs to choose the perfect one for Ruby.

 

 

 

 

It's not long before Sam gets tired of being treated like spun glass. She looks better by the day, and with no subsequent blackouts and Alex waiting on her hand and foot she gets plenty of rest until finally she insists on some kind of outing to break up the monotony of recovery.

Even as she'd hated living here as a teen Sam had always been a fan of Fortune Bay's Christmas decorations, so with warm drinks and coats to fend off the incoming cold Lena joins the group to walk around town and take in the newly-installed lights and garlands.

"It's not even snowy yet," Ruby notes, one hand swinging in her mother's and the other wrapped around an extra-large hot chocolate. "I bet they look better in the snow."

"We can come back when it's snowy," Kara says. She's drinking the same thing, with the addition of whipped cream and caramel drizzle for an added sugar boost that Lena is sure she doesn't need. Ruby nods in satisfaction, but the promise tugs at something in Lena's mind.

"It doesn't usually snow until December."

"It doesn't snow at all in National City," Kara says, neatly sidestepping Lena's intent.

"Shouldn't you be going back there soon?" Lena says. She takes a small sip of her apple cider. "I'd think you'd need to get back to work. Sam is on medical leave, but 3 weeks is a long vacation for you. You'll get fired if you stay until December."

What Lena doesn't say is what she really means. That Supergirl probably needs to go back to National City. But Kara shakes her head.

"Helping Sam is more important."

"She and your sister have only been dating six months. You're not obligated to stay this long to help her."

"She's family now," Kara says easily.

"And there's no other reason?"

Lena has taken to watching the news more often since starting to suspect Kara's identity, and all anyone can talk about is the fact that Supergirl was almost beaten and then immediately disappeared again. They speculate about whether she's hiding in shame, or injured, or simply quitting in their time of need. Lena can't help but be curious about which theory is the truth.

Kara swallows audibly. They've stopped to admire the tree in the centre of town, which is still being decorated, and while the others are distracted she turns to Lena. Her eyes are shining with something that scares Lena more than anything else.

"There might be one or two."

Oh.

Even Lena in all her determination can't ignore Kara's meaning. They're not standing particularly close together, but the tension hangs in the space between them so thickly that it feels like no space at all. She's not sure if she wants Kara to keep talking, or if she wants to run in the other direction.

And then Lena sees something behind Kara that makes her heart feel like it's stopped dead in her chest.

Just barely through the branches of the tree, past ornaments and lights, is a face she only sees now in her nightmares. Gaunt and pale, but smug. Always so smug. Staring at her, his eyes boring into hers like a dare, like a challenge, his smile promising pain and humiliation if she ever denies his requests -

It's Lex.

"What? Where?" Kara spins around, wide eyes darting around every corner of the square. She seems taller now, somehow. Her shoulders straightened in a protective stance that unfortunately does nothing to quell Lena's panic. Lena hadn't even realized she'd said it out loud.

Lena looks back to the place she'd seen him, but he isn't there. There's nothing to indicate that he ever was to begin with. But she'd felt him. Like a presence in her mind, Lena had felt him like a phantom. No, more than a phantom, more than a ghost – like he'd reached out and grabbed her by the chin, forced her to look in the same way he used to when she was a child and had refused to help him with a dissection. Holding her there while tears streamed down her face, not letting her avert her eyes from whatever small creature he was disassembling.

Like he's trying to tell her something.

"Lena?" Kara says, her voice low. "What's going on?"

Sam, Alex, and Ruby haven't noticed her distress yet, and Lena would rather keep it that way. They're still looking at the tree. Lena takes a few breaths, but her chest feels too tight to get any air.

"Must have – must have been seeing things. Stress dreams," Lena bites out. She presses a hand against her chest, slipping it through the gap in her coat to sit against her clavicle. The pressure and the sudden cold help a little.

"While you're awake?"

"He's not here," Lena says shortly. "It's fine."

"Lena."

Kara is close, now. She's close and warm and she touches Lena's chin, but it's nothing like Lena's memories of Lex. It's a gentle touch. An encouragement rather than a demand. Lena lets it happen, locking eyes with Kara and finding that it's even more grounding than the hand on her chest. Kara is solid and real, blue eyes soft with concern behind her glasses. She's handsome and sweet and safe and so definitely Supergirl.

Even in her receding panic, Lena wonders why a Kryptonian would need corrective lenses.

"Talk to me?" Kara murmurs. The scar between her eyebrows seems especially prominent next to the deep furrow of her concern.

Lena glances towards Sam. She and Alex are trading soft kisses, smiling at each other like nothing else in the world matters while Ruby stares through the window of a nearby organic skincare shop. The last thing any of them needs is to be bothered by Lena's paranoid delusions.

"I dream about him sometimes," Lena admits quietly. "It's never happened while I'm awake, but it felt the same. I saw him. But he isn't here."

"Do you think the dreams are trying to tell you something?" Kara says without a hint of uncertainty about Lena seeing visions. Lena sighs.

"I forgot. You believe in magic."

Kara smiles, her expression a little sad. "And you wish you didn't."

"With good reason," Lena mutters. Kara nods, despite Lena not explaining any further.

"Even if I didn't believe in magic, dreams tend to show us what we don't want to deal with while we're awake," Kara says. "What exactly do you see?"

Lena stares down at the lid of her cider cup. Little curls of steam are rising from it, twisting in the air and dissipating just like her vision of Lex.

"I see him staring at me. He never says anything, but it feels like he's taunting me," Lena whispers. She stares hard at the cider steam, unable yet to look directly at Kara's earnest, empathetic expression. The tension that's been between them for weeks now feels thicker than usual. "He looks at me the way he did when I gave my testimony. Anger, and hate, and…arrogance. Like he's just biding his time until he can…"

Lena scoffs, cutting herself off before her voice can break.

"Listen to me. Maybe I really am going crazy like he did. I should have asked him if he started having hallucinations before I helped throw him in prison."

Kara makes a small, sympathetic noise. "Lena…"

"Hey!" Sam calls towards them, her brow furrowed as she finally notices Lena's dark mood from afar. "You two okay?"

"We're fine," Lena says, giving Kara a look that she hopes says for the love of god, don't say anything. "Come on, let's go see the ice rink. It might be frozen by now."

"There's an ice rink?!" Ruby shouts.

The afternoon ends with a promise to take Ruby skating once the weather is cold enough. After a quick dinner Lena excuses herself, citing a headache, and the moment she arrives back at the Luthor manor she runs to the kitchen phone to dial a number she memorized years ago.

It takes close to an hour to get through to the warden of the Van Kull Corrections Facility. He confirms that Lex is in Maximum Security where he belongs, though her insistence that he actually check to make sure seems to irk him.

Lena hangs up only somewhat assured. Her dreams are restless and short.

 

 

 

 

Friday night finds Lena at the Bingo Hall, sat between Kara and Ruby and wondering how she was ever convinced to come here.

"When I said we should do something fun this was not what I had in mind," Alex grumbles, dabbing at a number with her marker. She and Sam are across the table, their hands intertwined next to their bingo cards.

"You're the one who said no alcohol. This town has pretty limited options," Sam says. Alex's suspicion that the drinking had something to do with Sam's episode has meant a dry week for Sam, and Lena agrees.

Sam hasn't gotten a single number called yet, but she still seems to be having a better time than Alex – she's been people-watching, pointing out anyone she remembers from her time living here and questioning Lena about what they've been doing in the interim years. For the most part Lena has no answers for her, having been something of a hermit, but Sam seems to be enjoying herself anyways.  

"I think it's fun," Kara says. "Right, Rubes?"

Ruby shrugs. She's dabbing a colourful design on her card, ignoring the numbers being called out in favour of doodling. "Meh."

Kara's hopeful smile turns into a pout. "Fine. I just think it's nice to be out of the –"

Kara stops talking abruptly. She straightens, her head tilting as if she's listening for something, and the pout turns into a full-on frown.

"What's wrong with you?" Alex says. The caller at the front of the hall calls out another number – he seems to take his job very seriously, with each turn of the wheel and calling of a new number from the little plastic balls coming with an ostentatious sort of ceremony. He rotates the cage, chooses a ball with a flourish, holds it above his head as if to show off his neutrality, and then reads it aloud before placing it into a numbered divot on his table.

In the quiet between number calls, Lena hears the whispers.

"What's she even doing here? She couldn't stay in her mansion where she belongs?"

The conversation is coming from a couple sitting two tables away, their heads bent close together. Older, and somewhat familiar to Lena – she can't place their names, but she's seen them around for as long as she can remember.

"Eyes on your card, Gladys."

"Look at me, I haven't won a single round," Gladys says in a loud stage whisper. "She's bad luck. Like a black cat."

Kara's fist tightens on her marker. Lena, not unused to such whispers any time she dares leave the house, keeps her head down, but Kara's jaw is tight with anger.

"You really believe in that kind of nonsense?" the husband mutters. Gladys scoffs.

"You didn't know her when she was a little girl. Strange things happened when she was around, just ask Siobhan. She always had the air of a curse around her."

Lena's stomach sinks. It's one thing to endure whispers, but quite another to have a relative stranger confirm her worst fear. That being here is dangerous for everyone.

Kara's marker cracks and splits in her fist, leaving her hand stained with green ink to match her forest green wool sweater.

"Kara," Alex says. It's not a scold, exactly, but it's tinged with an exasperation that betrays how often this probably happens. Kara losing control of her strength in public.

"Excuse me," Kara says, ignoring her sister and pushing her chair out loudly. "One sec." She power-walks towards the hallway that leads to the bathrooms, disappearing around the corner.

"Kara's really strong," Ruby notes, still doodling with her bingo markers.

It's true. Lena can't help but wonder how strong she truly is; she's only seen Kara's strength applied to small things so far. Lifting a bed, crushing a marker. Lena's wish had been for someone so strong that a curse couldn't take them. An impossible ask.

A breeze rustles Lena's hair.

"28!" the caller shouts from the front of the room. Lena, preoccupied by watching Ruby's intricate doodle take form, hardly hears it until Sam taps on her bingo card.

"That's your number," Sam points out. Lena frowns down at the paper – 28 is indeed on her card, one of the few she's heard called from her lot so far. She dabs it distractedly.

"Where did Kara go?" Lena asks. Alex shrugs.

"Hopefully to wash her hands." The rattle of the plastic balls rings out again over the buzz of conversation. The caller chooses a ball, holding it above his head –

Another breeze sends Alex's card drifting across the table.

"Jeez. Did someone leave a window open?" Alex says, snatching it again. The caller clears his throat.

"72!"

Ruby stops doodling to point at Lena's card. "That number is yours too, Aunt Lena!"

Lena frowns down at the 72 in front of her, in black and white. Her dots are in a direct line, one after the other, out of nowhere. The caller is spinning the wheel again; he reaches in, grabs a ball, holds it up –

A wind blows through the hall yet again, and Lena could swear she sees some kind of blur near his hand. The caller looks a bit confused too, glancing around him as if he's worried someone is looking over his shoulder, but after a pause he reads the number.

"56!"

"You're on a roll," Sam says, pointing at the fourth number. The last three called, along with one from earlier, have formed a perfect line across Lena's card with only one remaining.

"And I still have nothing," Alex says, tapping her marker on the table. "How is that statistically possible? There's 75 of them!"

Lena watches carefully this time when the caller reaches in for another ball, training her eyes on his hand as he holds it up. It's clenched between his thumb and forefinger. She squints, not entirely sure what she's looking for, until a focused wind sends the balls on the table scattering.

And Lena is absolutely positive she saw a hint of green.

The caller gathers up the misplaced balls, his face getting more and more red as he's forced to chase them across the floor and put them back in their places. But finally he sets down the one he most recently pulled, shouting it to the hall.

"11!"

Lena looks down at the final number remaining in her line.

"…bingo?"

A hush falls over the hall. All eyes turn to Lena, staring with everything from shock to genuine anger. And Kara chooses that moment to stroll back into the hall, wiping her damp but still slightly ink-stained hands on her green sweater.

"Wow! You got bingo!" Kara says loudly, sitting back down next to Lena with a flourish. Her ponytail is less put-together than it was before, with little flyaway wisps escaping the elastic. "Good job!"

"It's not exactly a game of skill," Lena says, looking back towards the caller, who is still holding the ball aloft. "Kara, did you –"

Did you do this is the question Lena is burning to ask. Unfortunately she can't really ask it outright without sounding insane.

"You must have good luck, then," Kara says, grinning as she puts emphasis on those two words. Her eyes flick towards the couple behind them, so fast that Lena almost doesn't catch it. "I'm keeping you near me. Like a charm, right?"

The hall is still quiet. People are starting to whisper, some looking sour while others are simply interested in the proceedings, but none speak up.

"Excuse me? The lady said bingo," Kara says even more loudly, her smile widening as she looks at the caller. "I think that means she wins something?"

Lena can't help but laugh.

The prize is a coupon for a dozen free donuts from the local cafe. By the time the next round starts people have stopped whispering, though Lena can practically feel Gladys' glare boring into the back of her head. And yet it doesn't bother her like it used to. Kara is like a shield.

Ruby and Kara soon start to make games within the game, keeping the table entertained with new and increasingly silly elaborations on bingo that infuriate their table neighbours, and against all odds Lena is having a good time. Kara keeps looking at her with that warm, affectionate expression she wears so often, and Lena is…happy.

She always had the air of a curse around her.

The words echo in her head unbidden, and terror crashes through Lena in a great wave.

Baseless terror, maybe. Repetitive, exhausting terror. But it's like a reflex – happiness leads to death. Moments of comfort and joy have only ever been followed by tragedy, and the idea of losing anyone at this table is unimaginable. Sam is already in danger. She hasn't been nearly as careful as she swore she would be when she let these people into her life.

The last time Lena was happy, the last time she let her guard down for a night, Sam got so much worse. And Lena can't even muster up her magic to help her get better.

Sam and Alex take Ruby back to the B&B around 8, and when Kara offers to walk Lena home she means to turn her down. But the idea of trudging through the cold back to the Manor alone feels worse than the risk of taking Kara with her. Kara, despite Lena's best efforts, feels safe. Even more so since Lena finally picked up the clues that have been staring her in the face.

They pick up Lena's free donuts on the way. Kara chooses the flavours, excitedly pointing out the most interesting ones, and she carries the box as they meander their way through town towards Lena's property. The trees are almost bare now, skeletal branches with their leaves scattered in crunchy piles across the sidewalks and lawns they pass by. There's a wintery edge to the wind. The clean, cold smell of the first snow just around the corner.

Lena pulls her scarf more securely around her face, but Kara seems to thrive on the low temperatures. Her coat is slung over her arm, and every so often she takes a big, satisfied breath of frosty air. Her warm hand brushes against the back of Lena's every so often. Lena can practically feel Kara's desire to intertwine their fingers, but she never makes the move.

Lena can't quite decide if she's grateful or not. On the one hand is her rational brain, the part of her shouting that these desires are stemming from a childish magic she never should have cast. That it can only hurt them both to spend more time together. But on the other, Lena has always been weak-willed. Unable to learn the lessons the universe has continually tried to teach her.

Over and over again she's been reminded that she doesn't get to have a happy ending, and yet she still aches for the chance to pretend for a while.

Kara hovers behind Lena as she unlocks the door.

"Thank you for walking me home," Lena says, slipping the keys back into her pocket. "Will you be all right on the way back?"

Kara rocks back and forth on the balls of her feet, her hands shoved into her pockets. "Oh, yeah. Don't worry about me."

"I worry about everyone," Lena says. She doesn't open the door yet, nor does Kara leave her doorstep. The moment stretches out; Lena should be going inside, ending the night, and thinking about her choices until she finally gets the reality of the situation into her thick skull. But she doesn't. And Kara stays.

"Lena, can I be honest with you?"

A cold that has nothing to do with the weather crawls up Lena's spine. She swallows, feeling for the door handle.

"If you'd like."

"Okay. Um." Kara clears her throat, taking a big breath and moving forward until they're close enough for Lena to feel the heat rolling off her. "Okay. Maybe I'm imagining things. But I feel…a connection with you."

Lena's heart stutters in her chest. Kara's voice is shaking a little; it matches the sudden trembling of Lena's hands.

"I don't know what it is," Kara continues, more urgently this time. "But from the moment I saw you, I felt like maybe you were the reason I was supposed to come here."

"Kara…" Lena whispers. Kara's eyes are wide and earnest, and slowly she reaches out to take one of Lena's hands between hers. They're warm and soft, a shocking contrast to Lena's cold fingertips.

"Does that sound crazy? It feels crazy, but it also feels right. I can't explain it."

It does feel right. And that's the problem, really. That's why Lena has let this go on for so long, the reason she's now in this position. Being faced with Kara's honesty, her bravery, and having to explain to her why this is a terrible idea.

"It's not crazy," Lena says. Her voice is more of a croak, and she can't seem to say more than that at the moment – she knows that she needs to, that Kara deserves to understand this. That she should at the very least admit to what's happening if she's going to have any hope of moving past it, activating her magic and nullifying the spell that's caught them both in its web. But it sticks in her throat.

"Don't you feel it too?" Kara whispers. The smoke of her breath drifts across Lena's face.

Lena can't breathe, let alone put together words. It's all caught up in her chest, suffocating her slowly.

"I can't."

"Because of your curse?"

All the breath Lena had been searching for leaves her in a rush. Fear is eclipsed by pure shock, and Lena's mouth falls open.

"My…how do you know about that?"

"I have good hearing," Kara says. There's a strange undercurrent to her voice, like she's hoping Lena will question it. "You and Sam talk about it a lot."

Good hearing. A massive understatement. But Lena has other concerns – her heart is in overdrive, pounding so hard that it's making her head spin.

"I believe you, if that means anything," Kara says, as if that isn't a revelation in itself. Even Sam, who has seen Lena's magic in action, has always been doubtful about it. And here Kara is accepting it at face value.

"…you do?"

Kara shrugs. "I've seen more absurd things than magic in this world. But I'm not afraid of a curse."

"I am," Lena says. Her voice breaks on the words. Kara's face softens, but her resolve seems harder than ever.

"Lena, whatever it is, I'm sure I can help. Please. Let me help?"

Lena wishes dearly that that was possible. But in that moment Lena doesn't see Kara as she is, warm and earnest and safe. She doesn't hear the surety in Kara's voice. She sees Kara pale and unmoving, and a headstone bearing her name. She hears Alex's voice on the phone, full of grief and rage at Lena for being stupid enough to put her in harm's way. She sees the world grieving Supergirl, taken from them at last by a Luthor too selfish to do the right thing.

"I want to. But I won't do that to you," Lena says, pulling her hand out of Kara's grasp. She grabs at the door handle again, finally twisting it and retreating into the dark safety of the house. "I'm sorry. I'm sorry, Kara."

Closing the door on Kara's forlorn face, standing there on Lena's welcome mat with her box of donuts, is the last straw for Lena's fraying emotions. She stumbles away from the entryway, finally collapsing onto the couch with her heart beating so fast and her breath coming so quickly that it's a miracle she's still conscious.

Somewhere in that rational part of her mind, Lena understands that she is probably having a panic attack. All physical signs point to the obvious – racing heart, chest pain, gasping for breath. It’s been threatening to burst out of her all morning, and if she’s going to trust anyone with her half-baked theory, it’s Kara.

“What possibility?”

“I think that Sam is Reign.”

Kara blinks silently for a few seconds.

“What?”

“I know it sounds insane. But in my dream, I saw her in surgery. They weren’t taking anything out. They were putting something in,” Lena says urgently. “A chip of some kind, I think. And then I saw Reign.”

“Okay,” Kara says, clearly still processing.

“Every blackout Sam has had since she got here has coincided with Reign showing up,” Lena says. She bites the inside of her cheek. “I mapped it on a calendar. I know this is all based on a dream, but –”

“God. Oh my god, if she’s – I fought her,” Kara says suddenly. Her face has gone white. “I fought Sam. I was right there, face to face, and I didn’t see. How did I not see?”

“We don’t know without a doubt that it’s her. There’s no actual proof,” Lena says.

Kara shakes her head. “No, I think you’re right. Now that I think about it, she did look like Sam, I just…I didn’t think…”

“Reign wears a mask. People don’t see Supergirl in you with nothing more than a ponytail and a pair of glasses,” Lena says, rubbing Kara’s arm. Some of the tension leaves Kara’s shoulders.

“Point taken,” Kara mutters. “But why would anyone do that to Sam? And how? I’ve fought her, Lena. She’s strong. As strong as me. We draw even every time, and she’s been getting better. How did they turn her into something like that?”

“I don’t know,” Lena says. She leans into Kara, and Kara wraps her up in a tight hug. “And I don’t think we’re going to find her until she wants to be found.”

The better part of the day is spent looking, and as Lena predicted Sam is nowhere in town or the surrounding woods. It’s only the possibility that Sam might have found her way back home while they were out that convinces Alex to head back to the Manor around sundown.

When they arrive, the windows on the main floor are illuminated with warm light.

“We didn’t leave the lights on,” Kara says.

“It must be Mom!” Ruby shouts, taking off running towards the steps, but Kara darts forward to slow her down before she reaches the porch.

Lena has a bad feeling the likes of which she hasn’t had since Jack died.

“Do you think it’s…” Alex trails off, but Lena can fill in the blank.

Seeing Lena’s expression Alex takes Ruby by the shoulders, guiding her away from the house and away from Lena and Kara. Ruby resists, still clearly wanting to run to the porch, but Alex is steadfast and Lena is deeply grateful.

“I should go in alone,” Lena says decisively.

Kara is immediately reactive. “Not happening.”

“Whoever is in there, I don’t think it’s Sam,” Lena says, lowering her voice and hoping Kara understands. “Alex needs to look after Ruby. And if this is Lex, he made kryptonite for Superman. He could have it now. He could hurt you, Kara.”

“Then that's a risk we have to take,” Kara argues.

“I can't lose you. I won't,” Lena says fiercely.

“So you expect me to send you after him by yourself?”

“I need to know you're safe. If my curse puts you in danger –”

“Lena,” Kara says, taking Lena softly by the upper arms, “you can't live your whole life based on the fear of losing people you love. That's no way to go through the world. Trust me.”

“It's different when it's my fault.”

“Whatever this curse is, it is not your fault. It's something you inherited,” Kara says. The pure conviction in her voice is almost enough to sway Lena on its own. “Jack was not your fault. And neither would it be if I died.”

Lena’s hands tangle in the front of Kara’s coat. She clings to the fabric, feeling Kara’s warmth underneath and willing it to leech into her body to eliminate the cold dread in the pit of her stomach.

“Remember what I said, when you asked me how I go on loving people after losing everything?” Kara says.

Lena swallows thickly.

“Hope.”

“Yeah. Hope.”

“Hope for what?” Lena says, shrugging helplessly. “What is there to hope for?”

“Hope that someday the grief won't feel so devastating,” Kara says. She cups Lena’s face, brushing a forming tear from the corner of Lena’s eye. “Hope that even when you lose part of your heart, there will always be people to carve out new spaces there. To keep giving life meaning.”

More tears slip free, spilling over Kara’s thumb.

“I lost everything. And then I found you,” Kara says simply. She’s so calm in the face of Lena’s doubt, but her voice cracks with emotion. “And life will go on after us.”

“I don't know how to look at it the way you do,” Lena whispers. Ruby is glancing over at them, and Lena presses her cheek into Kara’s palm to hide her tears. The last thing Ruby needs is to be even more scared.

“You let yourself grieve. You hold the people you lost in your heart. I live every day with the fear of losing the people I love. We all do. And I’ll fight to protect them to the end. But even if we lose that fight, loving them will always be worth it. No matter how much it hurts.”

Lena pulls Kara close, burying her face in the warm layers of Kara’s jacket. Her lips find skin just above the collar of Kara’s shirt, and she presses a few shaky kisses there. Just Kara’s scent is enough to calm her down a little.

Kara presses a kiss to the crown of Lena’s head.

“Everyone dies, Lena,” Kara murmurs into Lena’s hair. “All we ever have are the moments before.”

Kara’s words are a church bell in Lena’s head. They ring and ring, echoing through her and shaking her already-unsteady thoughts like an earthquake until the very foundations of her are nothing but crumbled rock.

Lena has always told herself that she avoids loving people to protect them, but Kara was right when she made her observation a few days ago. In doing so, Lena has built a wall around her heart. Pushing them away also protects Lena from the hurt of loss. Just like her ancestor, who cast the curse to begin with.

Maybe isolation isn't the answer. Maybe Kara is right, and it’s hope. Connection. Maybe it’s whatever nebulous force is fuelling her magic.

“Okay,” Lena says, soaking up Kara’s closeness while she can. “Okay. Let’s do this together.”

 

 

 

 

It takes some persuasion to convince Alex to stay outside. But protecting Ruby is paramount, and if Lena is right and Lex is in the house – possibly with Sam, possibly as Reign – Lena knows with absolute certainty that Sam would want Ruby’s safety put first.

The front door squeaks ominously when Lena eases it open.

Not every light on the first floor is switched on. The foyer chandelier is lit, while the hallway to the kitchen is dark. The upper landing is shadowy and still. But more light is spilling in from the sitting room, and when Lena focuses past the frantic beating of her own heart, she can hear music coming from that direction. Classical piano.

Together they follow the noise.

Lena half expects to see Lex seated at the piano. He’d always been ostentatious about playing for an audience, and if Lena so much as touched the keys he’d appear from nowhere to lecture her about finger placement. But the piano bench is empty. Instead a record is spinning on the old player Lena hasn’t used in a decade.

A figure is standing with their back to the door, staring out the window at the fading light over the garden. A tall figure with perfect posture. A long, fashionable trenchcoat with a popped collar covers most of their skin, except for bare calves which end in sharp stiletto heels.

Sam is lying on one of the couches, her eyes closed, completely motionless. Though the mask is nowhere to be seen, Reign’s black suit is unmistakeable.

The figure in the window turns, and Lena’s stomach bottoms out.

“Lillian,” Lena breathes.

Lillian Luthor looks exactly the way she did the last time Lena saw her. It had been this time of year, just after Thanksgiving in Lena’s final year of school. Before she’d finally gotten brave enough to cut contact with her family completely.

Lillian had seen her to the taxi waiting in the driveway after an awkward family dinner – god forbid she offer her daughter a ride to the airport – and in lieu of the hug any other parent might offer the child they haven’t seen in almost a year, she’d patted Lena’s cheek and told her to expect a call about a new initiative Lex was deigning to loop her in on.

Lex had called a few weeks afterwards, and Lena had ignored it. Eight months later he tried to kill Superman.

“Have I lost the privilege of my title?” Lillian drawls. Despite the lack of surgical mask, it’s so obvious now – the figure standing next to Reign in Lena’s dream was her stepmother.

Lena has been so caught up in her visions of Lex and figuring out how he’d managed to pull strings from prison that she hadn’t even contemplated the possibility of Lillian being his instrument. Lillian had been charged as an accessory, and Lena had assumed she was safely in jail. Harmless.

“I never wanted to call you my mother,” Lena manages to say, as her whole world tilts like a demented carnival ride. “And you lost it when you helped Lex murder innocent people. What are you doing here?”

“You always did have a myopic view of the world,” Lillian sighs. Her tone is dry and disinterested. “So focused on the insignificant details.”

“What do you want with Sam?” Kara says, solid as a rock beside Lena.

“Do you really think that Miss Arias is the goal?” Lillian says with a condescending chuckle. She moves towards the couch, and Lena takes a step towards it too. Kara sticks close to her side. “I know Kryptonians are known for brawn and not brains, but really, Supergirl. Do use your head.”

Kara freezes. Lena’s breath catches in her throat.

Lillian knows.

“You’re after me,” Kara whispers.

“We’re after Superman. But in order to craft a weapon that can take down the world’s favourite hero, it first needs to be honed,” Lillian says. Her hand alights on the arm of the couch, just over Sam’s head. “You sharpen the blade. Learn your enemy’s weaknesses and adjust.”

For some reason, Lillian’s sudden focus on Kara is enough to jolt Lena out of her haze.

“So what, Sam is your weapon and Kara is your guinea pig?” Lena snaps. “Your trial run? Is this some deranged plan to get Lex out of jail?”

“Supergirl is inexperienced,” Lillian says dismissively. “Kill her first, and her cousin will be weakened by grief. An easier target for Reign. While the world looks for a hero to fill Superman’s place, Lex will offer his expertise to subdue the villain who bested a god.”

Of course. It all comes back to Lex’s god complex. She’s been seeing him because he’s pulling the strings even from lockup, and Lillian has always done everything she can to make sure her little boy gets everything he wants.

“Her name is Sam,” Lena says fiercely. “How did you even do this to her?”

“You really should be asking how you did this to her.”

A wave of nausea rolls through Lena. Kara is practically vibrating next to her, clearly ready to fight but worried about Sam’s safety, and Lillian continues talking as if she isn’t condescending to the most powerful being on the planet.

“It’s a shame you abandoned your medical research with that boy,” Lillian continues in a drawl. “If you’d been on board, it wouldn’t have taken nearly as long to implement your research. Lex can only do so much from the inside of a prison.”

“You couldn’t have used our research. We were trying to eliminate cancer and immune disorders,” Lena says. “Not create super-soldiers.”

Lillian smiles. It’s as cold as ever.

“It’s amazing what nanobots can do if given a different directive.”

“What is she talking about?” Kara says.

“We were trying to use nanotech to cure diseases. To eliminate cancerous cells without chemo,” Lena says. Her eyes dart back and forth between Lillian and Sam. “Redirect the immune system without doing lasting damage. But the technology wasn’t ready. It was too dangerous. Jack wanted to be a test subject, but I wouldn’t let him.”

“It wasn’t ready then. But oh, the leaps and bounds science can make with a little bit of money behind it,” Lillian says, almost cheerful now.

Sam is still motionless on the couch. Lena wants more than anything to run to her, but she knows Lillian well enough to know she has a plan for that scenario. Their best chance is to encourage her to talk. Reveal as much of her plan as possible in an effort to brag about her or Lex’s brilliance, and rely on her hubris to find an opening to exploit.

Lena can only hope that her underestimation of Kara’s ability will be her downfall.

“You sold off everything after the funeral,” Lillian continues, filling the silence. “Properties, equipment, patents. Your research files.”

“To secure scientific research facilities, with a legal agreement to be used purely for medical advancement. How did you get access?”

“Lex might have been given several consecutive life sentences, but with good behaviour and a few connections I’ve been out of prison for some time. Long enough to get my hands on just about anything.”

“So, what, you waited until Jack died and hoped I’d sell everything off?” Lena says, grasping for answers.

“A happy coincidence. And you did exactly what we hoped,” Lillian says simply. “You got out of our way.”

It’s impossible not to spiral into despair. What’s been done to Sam is Lena’s own doing – for selling off her research, for being a coward, for running away and trying to pretend it all didn’t exist. And there’s nothing she can do to fix it.

“And if Jack hadn’t died. Would you have killed him?” Lena asks. “Would you have killed me?”

“I hoped it wouldn’t come to that,” Lillian says. She avoids the question, but her avoidance says all Lena needs to know.

“Lena, don’t listen to her,” Kara says. “She’s trying to make you doubt yourself, because she knows you’re capable of stopping her. Don’t –”

“Enough,” Lillian says sharply. Her hands are in the pockets of her trenchcoat. Lena hears a quiet, almost undetectable click.

Sam’s eyes open. She sits up abruptly, displacing the cushions; her eyes are a dark, glowing red.

“Let me be clear,” Lillian says, as Reign stands up with the fluidity of a jungle cat. Swift and dangerous. Lena can see the face of her best friend, but there’s nothing of Sam in her movement. “There is nothing you can do to stop this. You stopped applying yourself years ago. You’re lazy, Lena. Selfish. And now what you abandoned will be used to rid us of Kryptonians for good.”

Kara takes a step forward, like she’s trying to shield Lena with her body – her arm goes out across Lena’s chest, a protective stance, as Reign circles around.

Lillian snaps her fingers.

“Kill Supergirl.”

Reign attacks in an instant. Kara manages to push Lena aside, and Lena lands in a heap as Reign sends them both flying through the sitting room wall and into the hallway in a shower of plaster and wood.

Lena has seen Reign fighting Supergirl on television. Grainy, far-away shots, occasionally pausing to reveal the extent of their injuries. Seeing it up close is infinitely worse. Lena can hear the impact of fists hitting flesh, the grunts and the smashing furniture, and she scrambles out of the way as Reign picks up the piano and hurls it in Kara’s direction.

“What the hell is going on?”

Lena turns, her heart dropping, to see Alex sprinting into the mostly-demolished room. She takes the last few feet in a perfect knee-slide until she’s on the floor with Lena, hidden by the couch.

In the next room, through the broken wall, Lena can hear Kara shout out in pain.

“I told you to stay with Ruby!” Lena hisses.

Alex’s face is set in grim determination. She reaches behind her back, digging into her waistband, and produces a compact but shockingly military-grade handgun.

“She’s hidden, she’s safe,” Alex says, propping herself up to peek over the couch. “Who is Kara fighting? Is it Lex?”

“It’s my mother,” Lena says shakily. “And Alex, Sam is – “

Sam herself appears before them, sliding across the floor until she hits the wall. The length of her body leaves a dent in the drywall. Her eyes glow red; her face is clearly visible, contorted in anger, before she darts back towards Kara in the other room.

“Oh my god,” Alex whispers.

Lena ducks down when a large chunk of the piano bench sails over their heads. “I know. I’m so sorry. I don’t know how to fix her.”

“Fuck. Fuck!” Alex ducks too, her knuckles gone white against the handle of her gun. “How is this possible? How long have you known this?”

“I only started to suspect today,” Lena says quickly. “Lex and Lillian, they’ve been running the clinic she goes to. They’re using nanotechnology to make her this way. It controls her somehow, makes her invulnerable. Heals her instantly.”

Alex goes white.

“I have no quarrel with the rest of you,” Lillian calls over the commotion. “Lena, you’re still my daughter. It's the Kryptonian I want. Surrender to me, and I'll happily leave the rest of you in peace.”

With what Lena can only describe as a growl, Alex pulls herself up to standing to glare at Lillian. “Over my dead body.”

“I was never your daughter. And I'm not letting Ruby lose her real mother like I did,” Lena says, standing up beside Alex. “We aren't going anywhere without Sam.”

Kara lands in a heap at their feet. Her lip is broken, and there’s blood crusting at her nostril – her civilian clothes have been torn, showing glimpses of the red and blue beneath, but she hasn’t removed the shreds yet. She hauls herself to her feet only with Lena and Alex’s help.

“So be it,” Lillian says.

Reign emerges from the dusty hallway to stand at Lillian’s side. The lights flicker, fighting to stay illuminated when half the wiring in the room has been torn out; Sam’s face is as cold and emotionless as stone.

“Lena, you have help her,” Alex says, her voice cracked and broken as she clings to Kara’s bruised arm. “We have to bring her back.”

“You can do this,” Kara says under her breath. Lena can hear a wheeze at the end, and she notices that Kara’s free arm is wrapped around her ribs. “Your magic is strongest when you're tapped into your emotions. Don't block yourself off from them. Let them power you.”

“I don’t know if I can,” Lena whispers.

Kara squeezes her hand. Brief, but firm. “I believe in you, Lena. I love you.”

Kara steps forward. Reign lunges. Lillian takes one hand out of her pocket, and the room fills with sickly green light.

Kara’s scream of pain is a sound Lena never wants to hear again. It fills the room, tearing Lena’s heart in two as Kara drops to her knees and Reign puts her immediately into a headlock.

The effect of the kryptonite is instant – green lines seem to crawl through Kara’s veins, moving up from her neck and over her jaw towards the top of her head. It’s horrifying to watch. Kara can barely fight back with her strength so sapped. She struggles against Reign, against Sam, and Lillian watches with a satisfied smile.

Like so many years before when Siobhan had driven Lena to accidental magic, something snaps. Terrified, driven by pure instinct, again Lena throws out a hand.

“Kara!”

The kryptonite flies from Lillian’s hand, landing across the room, and immediately a dome of swirling light encases it like a shield to keep the poison in. The green light disappears. Kara makes a noise of relief, the kryptonite lines receding from her face, and Lillian stares down at her empty hand.

Lena has no idea how she’s even doing it, but she focuses all her might on keeping it active.

Lillian looks, for the first time in Lena’s life, like she’s lost for words – so livid that she can’t even conjure an insult.

Kara recovers quickly. She throws Reign off, elbowing her hard in the ribs and pushing her up and through the ceiling. The house creaks and groans as they rip through; by some miracle the lights stay on, even with half the wiring now dangling from the hole they left.

And then Lena hears it.

Chirp, chirp. Chirp, chirp.

Adrenaline turns to cold dread.

The deathwatch beetle. It’s a sound she hoped never to hear again; Lena can’t even fathom at this point who the curse might be taking. Sam, Kara, Alex, Ruby. It could be any of them. It could be all of them.

Lena’s lungs feel too small. They’re thin and flooded with terror. Her magic is flickering in her panic, threatening to release the kryptonite again, and as Kara and Reign land back on the ground floor Kara tries to remember Kara’s words.

I live every day with the fear of losing the people I love. We all do. And I’ll fight to protect them to the end. But even if we lose that fight, loving them will always be worth it.

It’s hard to internalize it when the threat of losing them is so imminent, but Lena tries to force air into her lungs regardless.

Kara and Reign are both in rough shape. They’re too evenly matched, with Kara powered by the sun and Reign’s strength bolstered by nanotech that now visibly heals her with every hit, and Lena has no idea how either of them will ever come out on top.

The beetle is still chirping when Kara, bloodied to a degree Lena has never seen before, throws a wild punch that lands squarely on Reign’s temple. It throws Sam back hard, so hard that she demolishes half the staircase in the foyer, and for the first time she doesn’t get up right away – she’s half-sitting up, leaned limply against what’s left of the stairs.

When her eyes open, they’re warm brown.

“Sam!” Lena gasps, sprinting over scattered debris and out into the foyer to her prone body. She can hear Alex running too; Kara is at her side in an instant, battered and bruised.

“Lena…” Sam murmurs. Her eyes are hazy. A droplet of blood is streaking slowly from a split near her eyebrow down to her cheekbone. “I think…I know what they did to me.”

Lena bites back a sob. She eases Sam’s head into her lap, stroking her hair – it’s come loose during the fight from the tight ponytail it was in when they arrived.

“I know, honey. I know.”

“You should…you should run,” Sam says. It sounds like all the pain of her injuries are hitting her now – like the nanobots aren’t active, maybe. Like Kara’s hit disrupted them.

“I’m not leaving you,” Lena says fiercely. “Not ever again.”

“None of us are,” Alex says. She’s holding Sam’s hand tightly, wrapping it up in both of her own and holding it to her heart.

Sam grimaces.

“I can’t stop it,” Sam whispers. Tears are streaming down her temples. “It’s too…strong.”

Lena’s mind races. For whatever reason, Sam is herself in this moment, and the window of opportunity to do something before Reign returns is narrowing.

Sam’s eyes flicker with red.

“Sam! Sam, don’t let her win,” Lena says, her voice high and almost unrecognizable with pre-emptive grief. She grabs Sam’s other hand; her palm tingles where they meet. “Please. Don’t leave me.”

Alex kisses Sam’s knuckles. “Come on, babe. You’re stronger than whatever they did to you. You’re the strongest person I know.”

Kara’s eyes are only for Lena. They’re wide, frightened, but in them Lena can see Kara’s belief. Her unwavering faith in Lena’s ability.

Red fades into brown again, but it flickers inconsistently. Sam is fighting.

Chirp, chirp. Chirp, chirp.

“Please,” Sam chokes, her grip on Lena’s turning almost strong enough to crush her fingers. “Kara, please – get them out, get them safe from me. Hide Ruby. You have to – if it comes down to it, you have to stop me. Promise me.”

“I am not going to kill you, Sam,” Kara says. She sounds horrified at the very suggestion, but Sam is insistent.

“You can’t let me hurt them. I’d rather – I’d rather die.”

Sam cries out in pain. Her head tips back into Lena’s lap; she twists, contorting in agony.

“Lena, do something!” Alex says, still holding Sam’s hand despite her bruising grip. “Please!”

Lena cradles Sam back against her chest. She doesn’t know what she can do. What magic will disrupt this. What can release Sam from whatever they did rooting around in her brain.

The slow click of Lillian’s heels approaching interrupts Lena’s racing thoughts.

“It’s no use. She can’t fight it for long,” Lillian says. Lena’s shield is still active around the kryptonite, but Lena has no idea how much longer it will hold. “Eventually the tech will win out.”

Faster than Lena can comprehend, Alex pulls out her gun again and points it directly at Lillian’s chest.

“Turn her back! Deactivate it!” Alex shouts.

“Not until she’s done what she was programmed to do.”

“Until she kills me,” Kara says quietly. She turns to Lillian, her teeth gritted – Lena can see blood filling the cracks. “You’re a monster.”

Chirp, chirp. Chirp, chirp.

“Why Sam?” Lena says, holding Sam tighter as she struggles against Reign’s grip on her mind. “Why – of all the people on this earth, why did you have to choose my best friend?”

“The world does not revolve around you, Lena,” Lillian says sharply. “It was a happy coincidence, but an accident nonetheless. She came to us. And she was the only person who survived every round of testing.”

“How many rounds of testing? How many people did you two kill with this project, Lillian?"

“She's subject 65,” Lillian says, like such an absurdly high number is nothing to her. “Samantha here has proven uncannily resistant to anything we do to her. No matter how many rounds of testing, she always bounced back. Almost supernatural, really. Every other test subject died before we could even implant a chip.”

Lillian sounds almost bored by the recounting of her horrifying experiments, but one word sticks in Lena’s brain.

Supernatural.

Lena’s palm has been tingling since she grabbed Sam’s hand. She opens her fingers to see that the scar which usually sits white against her skin is angry and red. Throbbing. And so is Sam’s.

Connecting them to each other.

“Kara,” Lena says, suddenly eerily calm in the midst of her epiphany, “I need a knife.”

“What?” Alex snaps.

“I need a knife. Now.”

Something in Lena’s tone must broker no argument, because Alex lifts her right pant leg to reveal a small holster. She pulls a small but deadly-looking knife out of it, putting it in Lena’s outstretched hand.

Without missing a beat, Lena slices her own palm open.

“My blood,” she whispers. Magic buzzes in her veins, stronger than ever before.

“Lena! What are you doing?” Kara says, grabbing at the knife. Lena holds on tight.

“Trust me,” Lena says, holding her hand open as the blood pools in the cracks of her palm. She hardly feels the pain at all. “Hold Sam’s hand open, and trust me. Please.”

After only a brief pause, Kara nods. Sam writhes in Lena’s lap, but Kara holds her in place and opens up Sam’s palm.

“Stop that,” Lillian says, her voice suddenly taking on a hint of fear. She steps forward, but Alex levels the gun at her again.

“Take one more step,” Alex says, pulling back the hammer with a decisive click. “I dare you.”

Lena draws the knife across Sam’s palm. It doesn’t cut cleanly – the knife glances against the skin, not cutting deeply enough to draw blood, and the superficial slice starts to heal immediately.

“Come on, Sam,” Lena whispers, pressing the knife harder. “Fight it for us.”

Sam groans. After a tense few seconds, the knife finally sinks through her skin.

“Your blood,” Lena says quickly, her voice stronger now. The magic is there, singing through her, telling her that this is right – a spell that’s kept them connected for 15 years is growing, expanding, engulfing them both.  

Lena vowed that it would help them protect each other, no matter where they were. Bound as sisters. And it has. Lena knows that now – it has to be the reason Sam survived Lillian’s deranged tests, Lex’s horrifying technology, when nobody else did. It has to be the reason Lena didn’t succumb to despair the way her mother did after losing Jack.

Her spell has protected them both.

“Our blood,” Lena whispers, and presses their palms together tightly.

A gust of potent wind blows back the debris around them. It knocks Lillian off her feet, but within the bubble of Lena’s magic everyone else is safe – Sam shouts, but it’s not a noise of pain anymore. It sounds more like relief.

The magic explodes, and Lena grips Sam’s hand in hers as images flash through her mind.

She sees Sam, young and confident in contrast to Lena’s lifetime of fear, shaking her hand after chasing off Siobhan. She sees Sam walking her home every day like a guardian angel, vowing to protect Lena like a little sister. She sees long nights locked away in Lena’s room and spent laughing, sharing, comforting, keeping Sam out of her own house where she was so miserable. Two lost girls with no support finding something solid in each other.

She sees their goodbye. Her memory reconciles the tears, the hugs, the sorrow of watching Sam drive away, but now she also Sam sobbing on the side of the highway halfway to National City. She sees the snakelike tendrils of magic connecting them even through the distance, linking their hands like a chain.

She sees Sam holding a newborn Ruby, and writing Lena a letter from the hospital bed. Getting through college while raising a perfect little girl. Showing Ruby pictures of Lena, of their childhood together. She sees Sam sitting by a window, tracing the scar on her hand just as Lena always did - a thin golden thread emanates from it, out towards the horizon.

She sees Sam returning here to Fortune Bay, bringing her family with her. Bringing Kara. Introducing light to Lena’s life again, just like she did when they were kids.

And now she sees Sam fighting the force inside her. Holding off the infringement of her own mind. Lena feel the source of the conflict, the dark space in Sam’s skull radiating malice as if her brother put his very self into the warped technology.

But that dark space is fading. It’s being drowned out by the purity of their love for each other, something even the bitterest curse can't touch. That golden thread of light wraps around it, squeezing and squeezing until it fades to nothing.

The wind dies down all at once. Sam is limp in her arms; Lillian is pinned by a large piece of what used to be the wall to the sitting room. Lena’s face is wet with tears.

The beetle has stopped chirping.

Lena releases Sam’s hand, and between their palms over Lena’s smooth white scar is a tiny microchip.

“Sam?” Alex says. Her voice is trembling.

Sam’s eyes open. They’re clear and warm and completely brown. They dart around from face to face, lingering on Alex and then Lena.

“Ow,” Sam says. She winces, shifting and seeming to take stock of her injuries. “Fuck. Ow.”

It’s Sam. Lena knows it as surely as she knows anything – her best friend is back. The relief hits like a tsunami.

“God, you had me scared,” Lena says, her breath catching somewhere between a sob and a laugh. “How are you feeling?”

“Like I got hit by a train. Did you really have to hit that hard, Kara?”

“Apparently,” Kara says, grinning and giving Sam’s arm a happy squeeze. Her eyes are shiny, too. “You were kicking my butt. Welcome back, Sam.”

“Is it over?” Alex says, still alert even as she stares at Sam like she’s an oasis in the desert. She’s still holding Sam’s hand to her chest, gripping it like a lifeline. “Is that it? She’s okay now?”

“I think so,” Lena says. She holds out the chip. It glints in the flickering overhead light. “It’s deactivated. Without this, the bots are inert.”

Not only that, though, Lena knows with a sudden clarity that it isn’t only Sam’s brainwashing that’s over. Bearing the curse all her life, she hadn’t realized how heavy it was – now she can feel its absence like a massive weight has been lifted from her shoulders. A heavy mantle is gone, and suddenly bright sunshine is warming her.

For the first time in her life, Lena knows that everything is going to be okay.

“I’m okay, babe,” Sam says softly. She pulls their joined hands to her lips and kisses Alex’s knuckles, tucking them under her chin. “I’m sorry I put you all through this.”

Kara, Alex, and Lena all speak up, talking over each other in an effort to reassure Sam that she has absolutely nothing to be sorry for, before they’re interrupted.

“That. Was. Amazing,” a voice rings out from the direction of the front door.

Ruby is standing in the doorframe.

“Ruby!” Alex hisses, quickly snatching her gun off the floor and sticking it back in her waistband. “I told you not to come inside under any circumstances!”

Ruby ignores the scolding. She’s pale and close to tears, and she sprints towards Sam and Alex to pull them into a tight hug.

Lena shifts away and towards Kara, letting them have their reunion – Alex, with the ordeal finally over, sobs her relief into Sam’s shoulder while Sam smooths Ruby’s hair, simultaneously admonishing her for being here while kissing every inch of her face gratefully.

“You did it,” Kara says. Her smile is pure sunlight, and she presses a kiss to Lena’s forehead. “I knew you could. I am so proud of you.”

Lena sinks into her. With all the adrenaline of the evening and the lifting of a lifetime of constant fear, it feels like she could sleep for 10 years and still be tired.

“Lena,” Sam says, her face streaked with tears. She reaches over blindly, still in the middle of Alex and Ruby, and Lena takes her hand. "I don't know what to say besides thank you. I love you."

"I know," Lena says softly. She squeezes Sam's hand tight. "I love you, too."

It's the first time she's ever said it to Sam, and Sam's smile says that she knows it. The fear that used to accompany the words is gone, now. 

Across the house, Lillian stirs under the debris. Whatever device she was using to deploy the kryptonite is lying a few feet away, inert, and Kara is the one to get up and kick it out of her reach.

“What are we going to do about her?” Lena says, the microchip still gripped in her free hand – she considers asking Kara to crush it, but the more rational thing to do is probably to take the thing apart and ensure it can’t be replicated.

“I’ll contact J’onn,” Alex says, wiping her face and finally relinquishing Sam to pull herself to her feet. “Messing with nanotech to create alien-killing super-soldiers for Lex Luthor probably falls under DEO jurisdiction.”

“DEO?” Lena says, looking quizzically at Kara. “Is that some kind of Federal bureau?”

“Kind of,” Alex says. Kara picks up the debris pinning Lillian. Alex hauls the older woman to her feet and pulls her wrists behind her back. “We’ll explain later.”

“You know I won’t be behind bars for long. And neither will Lex,” Lillian says, sounding far more smug than her current position warrants.

“We don’t have bars at the DEO. We have solitary high-security containment cells,” Alex says. She sighs. “Damn it. My cuffs are up in our room.”

Kara is gone and back again in an instant. A pair of handcuffs hang from her pointer finger, and her face is set in a wince.

“I really wish you had packed them in a different bag,” Kara says, letting them drop into Alex’s open hand. “I did not need to see that collection.”

Sam, still on the floor holding Ruby, laughs and laughs and laughs.

 

 

 

 

December is as beautiful as ever in Fortune Bay – the forecast is calling for a blizzard just before Christmas, but by then Lena will be long gone.

The sun warms Lena’s face from her seat on the front porch steps. It’s just about the only part of the Manor still left untouched. The whole thing is scheduled to be demolished, and Lena’s intent is to donate the land for a public park.

Everything Lena cares about from this house is in the four cardboard boxes on the steps beside her. The rest is dust.

Near her feet, a single deathwatch beetle crawls up between the boards. It inches its way over the wood, stopping when it reaches the barrier of her boot. Its antennae twitch, but it makes no sound.

“I'm not afraid of you anymore,” Lena says quietly.

The beetle says nothing.  It turns, crawling slowly away until it disappears again between the cracks of the porch timbers to its ice-free home beneath. Not a chirp to be heard.

Gravel crunches under tires as Kara pulls up in a rental car. After some healing from Lena, Sam and Alex left with Ruby a week ago to head back to National City, and Lena has only stayed here to get things in order before she leaves for good.

A cold breeze sweeps through just as Kara opens the car door, and Lena pulls her scarf closer around her face.

“Hey! It's freezing, you should be inside!”

“I'm savouring it,” Lena says, standing up and accepting Kara’s hug. They saw each other less than two hours ago when Kara left to pick up the car, but Lena missed her nonetheless. “Your California weather is going to be hell on my complexion.”

Kara laughs. She kisses the cold tip of Lena’s nose. “Don't worry, I have freeze-breath. I'll keep you cool.”

Kara bundles Lena’s boxes into the car along with her own luggage before taking the driver’s seat and turning the radio to a pop station. She takes Lena’s hand, intertwining their fingers over the gearshift.

“Let’s go home,” Kara says gently.

Lena looks back towards the Manor. It’s a shell, now, soon to be nothing but empty land, and Lena is more than ready to leave it all behind.

Fortune Bay shrinks in the rearview mirror, and with Kara beside her Lena turns towards the future.
