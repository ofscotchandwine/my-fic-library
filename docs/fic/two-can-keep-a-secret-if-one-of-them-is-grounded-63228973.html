    Chapter 1

    The Lesser of Two Nightmares Natasha Romanoff had seen a lot of things in her life. She had fought gods, aliens, and cyborgs. She had gone toe-to-toe with Steve Rogers and held her own against the Winter Soldier. But somehow, none of that compared to the absolute chaos that was Kate Bishop. She had just been looking for a lead—just a simple lead—when Kate quite literally crashed into her.

    Natasha had been watching a suspicious exchange from a rooftop, her black coat blending into the shadows. Below her, a group of Tracksuit Mafia guys were handing off something in a duffel bag to an older man in an expensive suit. She was moments away from dropping in to introduce herself when—

    THUNK.

    Something—or someone—hit her side and nearly sent her toppling off the building. Natasha spun, reaching for a knife, only to come face to face with—

    A kid?

    No, not a kid. A young woman, maybe early twenties, with wide brown eyes and a look of absolute shock.

    "Oh my God," the girl whispered, pointing at her. "You're Black Widow."

    Natasha narrowed her eyes. "And you're ruining my op."

    Kate Bishop, as she introduced herself a few minutes later—somewhat awkwardly, considering Natasha had pinned her to the roof—turned out to be both a disaster and a magnet for trouble. She had been investigating the Tracksuits on her own, following in the footsteps of her so-called "mentor" Clint Barton.

    And now, thanks to Kate, the exchange Natasha had been monitoring had gone sideways. The Tracksuits were scattering, taking their mystery bag with them.

    Natasha let out a slow breath, releasing Kate and standing up. "Okay. You're coming with me."

    Kate blinked. "I am?"

    "Yeah," Natasha said flatly. "Before you get yourself killed."

    Kate grinned, as if that was the greatest thing she'd ever heard. "Cool, cool, cool. Can I just say—this is the best night of my life?"

    Natasha gave her a look. "It won’t be for long."

    Natasha had a lot to deal with. She needed to find out what the Tracksuits were up to. She needed to figure out who was really pulling the strings.

    The one thing she didn’t need? A disaster magnet in the form of Kate Bishop tagging along. That’s where Yelena came in.

    "I am not a babysitter, Natasha," Yelena said the next morning, arms crossed.

    "You are now," Natasha replied, handing her a cup of coffee.

    Yelena scowled but took the coffee. "This is unfair. You know I don’t like children."

    "I'm twenty-two," Kate interjected from the couch. "Not a child."

    Yelena shot her a look. "You are a nuisance."

    Kate gasped. "That is so rude! You don’t even know me yet!"

    "I know enough," Yelena muttered.

    Natasha sighed, rubbing her temples. "Look, just keep her out of trouble in the meantime” Kate perked up. "Wait, does that mean we're gonna, like, hang out?"

    Yelena narrowed her eyes. "No."

    "Sounds like a yes to me," Kate said, grinning.

    Natasha ignored them both, grabbing her jacket. "I’ll check in later. Just—make sure she doesn’t do anything stupid."

    Yelena scoffed. "This one? Impossible."

    Kate placed a hand over her heart. "Wow. The trust. The faith. I’m truly touched."

    Natasha just shook her head and walked out, leaving Kate and Yelena staring at each other. There was silence.

    Yelena took a slow sip of her coffee. "Do not talk to me."

    Kate immediately opened her mouth.

    Yelena groaned. "This is going to be the worst day of my life."

    For the first few hours, things were fine. Kate scrolled through her phone, Yelena cleaned her gun, and they ignored each other like two cats trapped in the same room. But eventually, Kate started fidgeting. Then pacing. Then sighing dramatically.

    Yelena didn’t acknowledge it. Not when Kate flopped onto the couch. Not when she muttered something about wasting potential. Not when she got up and started stretching.

    But when Kate grabbed her bow and started toward the door, Yelena finally spoke.

    "Sit down."

    Kate froze. "Uh—"

    "Now."

    Kate turned slowly. "Okay, but, like, just hear me out—"

    "No."

    "You didn’t even let me say anything!"

    "Because I already know what you are going to say," Yelena said flatly. "We should be out there, helping Natasha, stopping the bad guys, blah blah blah." She waved a hand. "No." Kate crossed her arms. "Okay, but what if—"

    "No."

    Kate groaned, flopping back onto the couch. "You are literally the most boring assassin ever." Yelena smirked. "Good. That means you will stay put."

    Kate sulked. For about five minutes. Then she got up again.

    Yelena tensed. "What are you doing?"

    "Nothing."

    Yelena’s eyes narrowed. "Do not—"

    But the couch was empty.

    Yelena blinked. Looked around. Scowled.

    "Oh, come on," she muttered, already grabbing her coat.

    Kate Bishop was going to be the death of her.

    Mission: Impossible (Babysitter Protocol)

    Kate Bishop had always been good at two things: shooting arrows and getting into trouble. Lucky for her, she was exceptional at the first one, which meant she could usually shoot her way out of the second. But ever since meeting Natasha Romanoff, trouble had taken on a whole new meaning.

    Because now, trouble came with a six-foot Russian assassin breathing down her neck.

    And that assassin was currently glaring at her from across the room, arms crossed, expression caught somewhere between exasperation and murderous intent.

    “I told you to stay put.”

    Kate grinned, completely unfazed. “And I told you I have things to do.”

    Yelena Belova pinched the bridge of her nose and muttered something in Russian that Kate was pretty sure wasn’t complimentary. “No. You do not have things to do. You have instructions to follow. Natasha said you stay here while she takes care of the big guy, so you stay here.”

    “That’s boring,” Kate whined, flopping onto the couch dramatically. “I’m not gonna just sit here and twiddle my thumbs while there’s an actual crime lord running around my city.”

    “This is why babysitting you is impossible,” Yelena groaned. “Why can’t you be like other rich kids and go skiing or something?”

    Kate gasped in mock offense. “I can ski. I just also fight crime on the side. Multitasking.” Yelena let out a frustrated sigh. “Look. Natasha is handling it. We wait.”

    Kate barely refrained from rolling her eyes. She liked Natasha—a lot, actually. She was cool, competent, and had that whole "deadly but maternal" thing going for her. Kate had looked up to her even before they’d met. And meeting her? It had just made her more determined to prove herself.

    Except no one was letting her do anything. First Natasha had dragged her away from her investigation into Fisk, and now Yelena was acting like she was some bratty teenager who needed a babysitter.

    Which, okay, technically Natasha had assigned Yelena to babysit her, but that was beside the point.

    Kate needed to get out of here.

    Which meant she had to be sneaky.

    And that meant she had to be smart.

    Thirty Minutes Later

    Yelena was suspicious.

    Kate was quiet.

    Too quiet.

    She put down the sandwich she’d been eating and glanced around the apartment. “Kate?”

    No answer.

    “Kate,” she said again, standing up. “If you are in the vents, I will find you, and I will—” She turned the corner to the bedroom and found the window wide open.

    Yelena blinked.

    Then she cursed loudly.

    Meanwhile…

    Kate grinned as she dashed down the fire escape, heart pounding with excitement. Sorry, Yelena, she thought, but I’ve got work to do.

    She’d spent the last few days listening in on Natasha’s conversations, piecing together what she could. And what she’d learned?

    Fisk was back.

    And Natasha was planning something big.

    But nobody was telling her anything.

    Which meant she had to figure it out herself.

    She landed lightly on the sidewalk and pulled up the hood of her jacket, blending into the crowd.

    She had an informant to meet.

    And if Yelena caught her before she got there?

    Well. Kate was confident she could outrun her.

    …Probably.

    Back at the Apartment

    Yelena was furious.

    This was why she didn’t do babysitting.

    She yanked out her phone and called Natasha.

    “She ran.”

    A pause. Then a sigh. “Already?”

    “Yes, already!” Yelena snapped. “You expect me to keep an eye on someone who thinks she is an avenger just because she can do trick shots? No. Absolutely not. I am done. I am quitting.”

    “You’re not quitting,” Natasha said dryly. “You like her.”

    “I like dogs,” Yelena corrected. “And even Lucky behaves better than she does.”

    There was a beat of silence, and then Natasha said, “Find her before she gets herself killed.” Yelena groaned.

    She was going to kill Kate Bishop.

    After she found her.

    Kate’s Not-So-Smooth Escape Plan

    Everything had been going great.

    She’d made it to the informant, gotten some useful intel, and was just about to make her grand exit when—

    A familiar voice drawled behind her, “You must think you are very smart.”

    Kate froze.

    Then she turned slowly, already wincing.

    Yelena stood in the alleyway, arms crossed, looking pissed.

    Kate flashed a weak smile. “Hey, Yelena. Fancy seeing you here.”

    Yelena did not smile back.

    Kate swallowed.

    Then, before she could think of an excuse, Yelena grabbed her by the arm and started dragging her toward the street.

    “Nope. Nope. We are going home.”

    “Hey—ow!—I can walk, you know!”

    “Good. Walk faster.”

    Kate huffed. “I told you I had things to do.”

    “And I told you to stay put.”

    Kate muttered something under her breath.

    Yelena yanked her forward. “What was that?”

    “Nothing,” Kate said, grinning. “Just wondering if this means you care about me.”

    Yelena stopped walking.

    Then she turned, squinting at Kate like she was deciding whether or not to throw her into traffic.

    Kate smirked.

    Yelena groaned. “I hate you.”

    “No, you don’t.”

    Yelena let out a sharp breath. “I should let Natasha deal with you.”

    Kate grinned. “But you won’t.”

    Yelena sighed. “No. I won’t.”

    Kate beamed. “See? You do like me.”

    Yelena gave her a long, hard look.

    Then she flicked Kate on the forehead.

    “Ow!”

    “That is for making my life difficult.”

    Kate rubbed her forehead, still grinning. “Worth it.”

    Yelena shook her head, dragging Kate the rest of the way home.

    She was definitely telling Natasha to handle this next time.

    …Maybe.

    The Babysitter’s Curse Kate Bishop was grounded.

    Not officially, because Yelena had no actual authority to ground her. But judging by the way she was currently pacing the apartment like a caged animal while Yelena sat across from her, arms crossed, watching like an annoyed warden… yeah. Kate was totally grounded.

    "This is ridiculous," Kate groaned, flopping onto the couch. "You can't keep me here forever."

    Yelena, who was sharpening a knife just to make a point, raised an unimpressed eyebrow. "Oh? Try me."

    Kate scowled. "Natasha wouldn’t keep me locked up like this."

    Yelena let out a short laugh. "Yes, because Natasha is smart. Unlike you."

    "Rude."

    "Truth."

    Kate huffed, crossing her arms. "Look, I get it. I messed up. I should’ve told you where I was going—"

    Yelena narrowed her eyes.

    "—but in my defense, you wouldn't have let me go!"

    "Yes, because you are an idiot," Yelena snapped. "Do you know what Fisk would do to you if he caught you snooping around?"

    Kate hesitated. "…Put me in timeout?"

    Yelena groaned. "No, Kate Bishop. He would kill you. Quickly, if you are lucky. Slowly, if you are not."

    Kate frowned, chewing on that. She knew Fisk was dangerous. She wasn't stupid. But hearing it from Yelena—who had actually been in the assassin business—made it sink in a little deeper.

    "So what, then?" she asked, shifting uncomfortably. "I'm just supposed to sit here and let Natasha handle everything?"

    "Yes," Yelena said, exasperated. "That is exactly what you are supposed to do."

    Kate scowled. "Yeah, well, that sucks."

    Yelena threw her hands up. "You are impossible!"

    Kate flashed her a cheeky smile. "You say that like it's a bad thing."

    Yelena grabbed a pillow off the couch and threw it at Kate’s face.

    Kate caught it, laughing. "Real mature."

    "I am not your babysitter, Kate Bishop!"

    Kate tilted her head. "But… you kinda are, though?"

    Yelena let out a long suffering sigh. "This is why I do not take jobs with children."

    Kate gasped. "Children? Excuse me, I am a legal adult!"

    "You are a legal moron."

    Kate huffed, crossing her arms. "You’re really bad at pep talks, you know that?"

    Yelena gave her a pointed look. "I am not here to be nice to you, Kate Bishop. I am here to keep you alive. And if I have to break your legs to make sure you stay put, I will."

    Kate blinked.

    Then laughed nervously. "Okay, see, normally I’d assume that’s a joke, but with you, I actually don't know."

    Yelena smirked. "Good."

    Kate pursed her lips, contemplating her options. She could try sneaking out again, but Yelena was obviously watching her like a hawk now.

    She needed a new plan.

    Two Hours Later

    Yelena was asleep.

    Kate had been waiting for this moment all night.

    Very carefully, she tiptoed toward the door, making sure not to step on any creaky floorboards. She reached for the doorknob—

    "Don’t even think about it."

    Kate yelled, spinning around.

    Yelena was wide awake, still sprawled on the couch, arms crossed, looking thoroughly unimpressed.

    "You were supposed to be asleep!" Kate accused.

    Yelena rolled her eyes. "You think I don’t know how you work? You have the subtlety of a baby giraffe."

    Kate groaned, rubbing her face. "Oh my god, do you ever sleep?"

    "Not when I am babysitting you," Yelena deadpanned.

    Kate groaned again.

    Yelena smirked. "Now sit down before I tie you to the chair."

    Kate muttered something about Russian prison guards and flopped dramatically onto the couch. "This is the worst day of my life."

    Yelena gave her a smug look. "Good. Maybe next time, you will listen."

    Kate narrowed her eyes.

    Challenge accepted.

    The Next Morning

    "She did what?"

    Natasha sighed, pinching the bridge of her nose.

    Yelena, looking thoroughly unamused, gestured to Kate—who was currently tied to a chair with a very angry expression.

    "She tried to sneak out again, so I fixed the problem."

    Natasha stared.

    Then sighed again.

    "Kate."

    Kate glared. "She tied me to a freaking chair!"

    "You deserved it," Yelena muttered.

    Natasha shot her a look. "Yelena."

    Yelena raised an eyebrow. "What? You told me to keep her from running off."

    "Yeah, but not like this!"

    Kate smirked victoriously. "Ha! See? Even Natasha thinks you went too far."

    Natasha gave Kate a pointed look. "You also went too far, sneaking out when I explicitly told you to stay put."

    Kate opened her mouth.

    Then shut it.

    Then scowled. "…Fine. Whatever."

    Natasha sighed, rubbing her temples. "Okay. New plan. Kate, you’re coming with me."

    Kate immediately perked up. "Wait, really?"

    Yelena narrowed her eyes. "Wait, what?"

    Natasha nodded. "Clearly, she’s not going to stay put, and I’d rather not have her keep escaping and getting herself killed."

    Kate grinned. "Finally, someone with sense!"

    Yelena groaned. "Ugh. No. No, no, no. She is a liability—"

    "And she’s my responsibility," Natasha said firmly.

    Kate beamed.

    Yelena scowled.

    Natasha smirked at Yelena. "Besides, you’ll be happy to know this means you don’t have to babysit her anymore."

    Yelena blinked.

    Then perked up.

    "…You know what? Fine. She’s your problem now."

    Kate grinned at Yelena. "Aw, you’ll miss me."

    Yelena rolled her eyes. "I will not."

    Natasha chuckled, untying Kate from the chair. "Alright, Kate. Let’s go get into some trouble."

    Kate’s eyes lit up.

    "Oh, finally."

    Yelena groaned. "I am never doing this again."

    Kate smirked. "You say that now, but I totally grew on you."

    Yelena threw a pillow at her again.

    Kate barely dodged it.

    And with that, she followed Natasha out the door, finally getting to do something useful. Yelena sighed, collapsing onto the couch.

    She was definitely charging Natasha extra for this.

    The Chaos Duo (and Yelena’s Sanity Slowly Dying)

    Yelena had made a mistake.

    A terrible mistake.

    Because she had assumed—stupidly—that once Natasha took Kate off her hands, she wouldn’t have to deal with her nonsense anymore.

    That had been two days ago.

    And yet, somehow, she was still getting calls.

    Her phone rang—again.

    She knew who it was.

    She considered ignoring it.

    Then, with a heavy sigh, she answered.

    "What did she do this time?"

    "You sound tired," Natasha said.

    "I am tired," Yelena grumbled. "I was supposed to be free."

    Natasha hummed. "Yeah, about that… we need backup."

    Yelena closed her eyes. "No."

    "Yelena."

    "No, Natasha."

    "Yelena."

    Yelena groaned. "I hate you."

    "You love me," Natasha corrected. "And if it makes you feel better, this time it wasn’t Kate’s fault."

    Yelena blinked.

    Then narrowed her eyes. "Are you lying to me?"

    Natasha hesitated. "…It was mostly not her fault."

    "Ugh. Fine. Where are you?"

    "Warehouse district. Look for the smoke."

    Yelena paused. "Smoke?"

    The call cut off.

    Yelena stared at her phone.

    Then, cursing, she grabbed her gear and ran.

    Meanwhile…

    "So, technically, this is not my fault," Kate said.

    Natasha gave her an unimpressed look. "Kate. The building is on fire."

    "Right, but I didn’t start the fire."

    Natasha raised an eyebrow.

    Kate hesitated. "…Okay, maybe indirectly, but—"

    Natasha sighed, shoving Kate behind cover as bullets ripped past them.

    "This is why you stay out of things," Natasha muttered, firing back.

    Kate winced. "Okay, in my defense—"

    "No."

    "But—"

    "No, Kate."

    Kate huffed, ducking as more bullets flew past. "Ugh, fine. Just saying, if we make it out of this alive, I deserve some credit."

    "For what? Starting a gang war?"

    Kate opened her mouth.

    Then shut it.

    Then smiled sheepishly. "…Okay, yeah, I see how that sounds bad."

    Natasha rolled her eyes. "Just focus on not getting shot, alright?"

    "On it."

    Kate peeked over the crates, loosing an arrow toward one of the goons. It hit him square in the leg, sending him crashing to the ground.

    She grinned. "See? Useful."

    Natasha smirked, taking out another guy with a well-placed shot. "I’ll admit, you can shoot." Kate beamed. "High praise from the Black Widow."

    Natasha shook her head, still smirking.

    Then—

    A loud explosion rocked the building.

    Kate yelled, covering her head as debris rained down.

    Natasha swore. "We need to move—now!"

    Kate nodded, scrambling to her feet, but before they could reach the exit—

    A new wave of armed men stormed in.

    Kate sighed. "Oh, come on!"

    Then, suddenly—

    A flash of movement.

    A blur of black and green.

    And before Kate could blink, half the guys were already on the ground.

    Kate’s eyes widened. "…Did you just call in Yelena?"

    Natasha smirked. "Told you. Backup."

    Yelena, standing in the middle of a pile of unconscious goons, flipped her hair. "You are welcome."

    Kate gawked. "That was so cool."

    Yelena raised an eyebrow. "I know."

    Kate grinned. "Best babysitter ever."

    Yelena’s eye twitched.

    "I am not your babysitter, Kate Bishop!"

    Kate patted her on the shoulder. "Sure, sure. Now let’s finish kicking these guys’ asses." Yelena groaned.

    She hated her life.

    The Cost of Playing Hero Kate had been shot before.

    Not badly, and nothing serious enough to take her out of commission, but she had taken a bullet graze to the arm once.

    This?

    This was different.

    Because this time, it wasn’t a graze.

    And this time, she could feel the cold creeping in.

    It happened so fast. One second, she was ducking behind cover, loosing arrows, keeping up with Natasha and Yelena as they fought their way toward the exit. The next—

    Pain.

    A sharp, burning pain in her side.

    Kate staggered, the breath ripping from her lungs.

    She pressed a hand to her stomach, and when she pulled it away—

    Blood.

    A lot of it.

    Her knees buckled. The sounds of the fight dulled, the world tilting—

    "Kate!"

    Natasha.

    Suddenly, hands were on her, steadying her, lowering her to the ground.

    Kate tried to focus, but her vision was swimming, the pain pulsing through her.

    "Oh, that’s… that’s a lot of blood," she mumbled, trying for a joke.

    Natasha’s face was tight, her hands pressing down on the wound. "Stay with me, Kate."

    Kate blinked blearily. "Trying," she slurred.

    Then—

    Everything went black.

    The Apartment

    Kate woke up to the sound of quiet voices.

    It took her a second to register where she was. The last thing she remembered was the fight— the gunfire, the blur of Natasha and Yelena taking out enemies with ruthless precision— then… nothing.

    Now, she was lying on her couch, wrapped in blankets, her side bandaged up. The apartment smelled like antiseptic and something vaguely burned.

    Then, she heard them.

    "She could have died, Natasha."

    Yelena’s voice.

    A pause. Then Natasha, quieter: "I know."

    Something in her tone made Kate freeze.

    Yelena’s voice softened slightly. "You are getting attached."

    Another pause.

    Then—

    "You think I don’t know that?" Natasha exhaled sharply. "She… she reminds me of him."

    Yelena didn’t answer right away.

    Kate’s fingers curled into the blanket.

    She didn’t need to ask who him was.

    Even though Natasha had never spoken about him—not really—Kate knew. The way Natasha reacted to the Ronin suit. The way she sometimes looked at Kate, as if torn between amusement and something heavier.

    Clint Barton.

    The man Natasha had lost.

    The reason she had taken Kate in that night in the alley instead of walking away.

    Kate swallowed hard.

    "She is not Clint," Yelena said finally.

    Natasha’s voice was quiet. "Yeah. But she’s got his stubbornness. His recklessness. And those damn arrows."

    Yelena huffed a small, humorless laugh. "Maybe it is a joke from the universe."

    Natasha exhaled. "Some joke."

    Silence.

    Kate felt the weight of those words settle deep in her chest.

    She didn’t move. Didn’t let them know she was awake.

    Because she wasn’t sure what she was supposed to do with this.

    So she just closed her eyes and pretended she didn’t hear any of it.

    Later That Night

    Kate was not a great patient.

    That was what she gathered from the looks Natasha and Yelena kept giving her every time she tried to stand.

    "I can walk," Kate argued, trying (and failing) to push off the couch.

    Natasha shoved her back down with zero effort.

    "You got shot, Kate," Natasha deadpanned.

    "Yeah, but, like, a little bit shot."

    Yelena rolled her eyes. "You bled out on the pavement."

    "Okay, dramatic."

    Natasha pinched the bridge of her nose. "Kate."

    Kate sighed, giving up. "Fine. But just so you know, this is gonna make training a lot harder." "Training?" Yelena echoed. "No. No more training. No more sneaking out. You are officially grounded."

    Kate gawked at her. "You can’t ground me."

    "I just did."

    "You’re not my mom!"

    "Then stop acting like a child!"

    Kate spluttered. "Excuse me, I did not ask to get shot!"

    "Yet it still happened!" Yelena snapped.

    Kate shut up.

    For the first time, she actually looked at Yelena—really looked at her.

    She looked… pissed.

    But underneath that—

    Fear.

    And not just fear of Kate getting shot.

    Something deeper.

    Something that made Kate’s chest ache.

    She wasn’t used to people worrying about her like this. Sure, her mom had always kept tabs on her, but that was different. That was control.

    This?

    This felt real.

    Kate exhaled, running a hand through her hair. "I’ll be more careful."

    Yelena scoffed. "I do not believe you."

    Kate hesitated. "Okay, fair. But I mean it. I’m not trying to get myself killed."

    Yelena didn’t look convinced.

    Kate mustered a weak grin. "I can be responsible. I mean, maybe. Theoretically."

    Yelena groaned. "You are going to be the death of me."

    Kate smirked. "Nah. You love me too much."

    Yelena threw a pillow at her face.

    Kate winced. "Ow, okay, rude."

    But Natasha’s smirk had returned.

    And just like that, the tension eased—if only a little.

    Kate still wasn’t totally off the hook.

    But she was alive.

    And for now?

    That was enough.

    Natasha sat alone in the kitchen, staring at the file in front of her.

    Kate’s mother.

    Eleanor Bishop.

    Working for Fisk.

    She exhaled, rubbing a hand down her face.

    This was bad.

    Kate was strong, but she wasn’t ready for this.

    She wasn’t ready to learn that her own mother—her only parent left—was in bed with the most dangerous man in New York.

    Natasha clenched her jaw.

    She’d already seen what happened when Clint lost his family.

    She refused to let Kate go through the same thing.

    Not alone.

    Not while Natasha was still here.

    Under Watch Kate woke up feeling like she’d been hit by a truck.

    Which, honestly, wasn’t too far off.

    Her whole body ached, her side throbbed, and her brain felt like it was wrapped in fog. She groaned, shifting slightly on the couch—only to hear a very unimpressed voice say: "Do not even think about it, Kate Bishop."

    Kate cracked her eyes open and immediately groaned again.

    Yelena.

    Sitting in a chair directly across from her, arms crossed, staring her down like an angry babysitter.

    "Seriously?" Kate rasped. "Did you just sit there all night?"

    "Of course. Someone had to make sure you did not sneak out and get yourself killed."

    Kate let her head flop back against the couch. "Okay, first of all, ouch. Second of all, do you not have anything better to do?"

    Yelena smirked. "Not really, no."

    Kate rolled her eyes. "Great. Love that for me."

    She shifted again, testing how much pain she was in.

    The answer? A lot.

    Yelena noticed immediately. "Do not even try to get up. Natasha said you are not allowed to move."

    Kate scoffed. "Oh, so now you take orders from Natasha?"

    Yelena grinned. "No, but I do enjoy making your life miserable."

    Kate groaned again, throwing an arm over her face. "You guys are the worst babysitters ever." "You are the worst person to babysit."

    "That’s fair."
