    Chapter 1: Gilese 3707

    “Captain’s Log, Stardate 1941.3. We are approaching the Gilese 3707 star system after several weeks of travel at low warp from Starbase 12. Our mission is simple: to enter orbit around the lone Class M planet in the system, ascertain whether intelligent life is present, and conduct a full planetary survey.”

    Lena Luthor, captain of the USS Da Vinci, toggled off the computer on her ready room desk, straightened her uniform, and walked out onto the bridge. The gentle hiss of the automatic doors was practically deafening compared to the silence on the bridge; her crew was oddly quiet compared to past ships she’d served on. Perhaps it was the odd amalgamation of people; she had more non-humans in this crew, and their social norms hadn’t quite settled in yet.

    “Commander Rojas, anything to report?”

    Andrea Rojas, almost a full commander, smiled at her captain. They’d spent a year together at Starfleet Academy, Andrea joining the service in Lena’s final year. Lena had taken the young cadet under her wing and now, years later, they served together as much as Starfleet would permit. The younger brunette stood up out of the captain’s chair, straightened her uniform jacket, and smiled.

    “Nothing new, Captain. We’re probably a few hours away still from Gilese 3707. I believe we’ll be close enough fairly soon that Lieutenant Dox will be able to start the first stellar cartography work.” Rojas stepped away from the captain’s chair on the tiny, cramped bridge.

    Unlike the larger ships in the fleet, Da Vinci was an old NX-class vessel, one of the earliest Starfleet ships that were warp-capable. A generation prior, it had been cutting edge, but the new Constellation-class ships made it obsolete for primary missions. It was, however, perfectly capable for scientific exploration. Old or not, it was Lena’s first command and she was proud of it and her crew.

    “So, what do we know so far?” she asked Rojas as she took a sip of tea from the tumbler she’d carried from her ready room.

    Rojas shook her head, tapping on the electronic PADD in her hands. “Gilese 3707’s a red dwarf star. Frankly, I’d be surprised if we found anything at all on the planet. It’s barely in the habitable zone from what stellar cartography says.”

    “Indeed, Captain,” Querl Dox, a green-skinned Coluan and the ship’s science officer spoke up. “We are starting to receive telemetry from Gilese’s planetary object, which I shall henceforth refer to as Gilese 1. The surface appears to be mostly ice and rock.” He looked through the scope at his station, his forehead wrinkling.

    “I know that look, Lieutenant Dox. What is it?” Lena asked, wandering over to the science station.

    “It’s most peculiar, Captain. Gilese 1 is emitting an unusual type of radiation.” He pointed down at his viewer, Lena peering into it. “You will notice the unusually strong bands of epsilon radiation spiking from it, enough that it’s visible this far away.”

    Lena rubbed her forehead. “What would be causing something like that? Epsilon radiation is fairly rare, if I remember correctly.”

    Dox nodded. “Quite rare. The only time we’ve seen it be this prominent is around the Klingon homeworld’s moon, Praxis. They’ve been mining it so heavily over the last 50 years that radioactive elements in its core are emitting epsilon radiation. It generally does not occur naturally.”

    “So… the Gilese planet might be populated and mining it?”

    The green Coluan nodded. “That is a strong possibility. However, they may not be technologically sophisticated enough to realize the damage they are doing to the planet. Epsilon radiation might not even be detectable at their level of technological advancement. That is, of course, assuming anyone is even alive there.”

    Lena took another sip of tea. “Because epsilon radiation is hazardous.” Dox nodded in acknowledgment. “All right. Chief Ardeen, calibrate our shields to make sure we’re protected and let’s get a closer look, shall we?”

    The tall and muscular security chief, Imra Ardeen, nodded and keyed in the appropriate shield frequencies on her console, the myriad blinking lights all turning green. “Shield harmonics set, Captain.”

    “Very well,” Lena smiled, “Ensign Tessmacher, bring us into standard orbit around Gilese 1 please.”

    The Da Vinci assumed geosynchronous orbit above the planet, a barren and hostile-looking world. From what the crew could see, small settlements appears on the surface in what appeared to be giant hexagonal crystalline structures, refracting the dull red glow of the nearby star.

    “Captain, there are definitely signs of life on the surface. I can make out what appear to be cities of some kind, either carved into or made from giant crystals, as well as low-orbit spacecraft and life signs,” the science officer explained as he scanned the surface. “We are out of their range of detection.”

    “So, no signs of being warp-capable?” Lena wondered aloud. This world wasn’t too far from being able to reach for the stars, but Starfleet General Order 1 - prohibiting contact with pre-warp civilizations - meant she could do little more than take some photos and then fly away.

    “None presently, Captain. The planet appears to have been through multiple calamities, and what’s left on the surface now are just a few major cities, not much else. In fact, it would appear most of the cities on the surface are abandoned; only a handful have life signs. Also curious, the epsilon radiation signature appears to be getting significantly stronger.”

    Lena stood up from her chair and walked towards the wall-size window in the front of the room, resting her hand on the navigation console. “Because we’re this much closer than we were a little while ago?”

    The Coluan shook his head. “No, Captain. The radiation itself is increasing logarithmically. This is most unusual.”

    Chief Ardeen looked up with alarm. “Captain, sensors show some kind of missile launch on the surface, headed straight for us!”

    “Red alert, Chief. Can you tell what kind of missile it is? I’m thinking a civilization without warp probably can’t field any serious threat to us?” Lena looked to both her first officer and science officer for answers.

    Dox stared intently into his scanner. “You are… correct, Captain. The projectile appears to almost be a single-person shuttle of some kind with basic solid fuel propulsion. One life sign aboard, no discernible weapons. Not warp capable. But Captain, radiation levels from the planet are off the charts, and I am now detecting substantial seismic activity on the planet itself. Our shields may not hold if the intensity continues to increase.”

    Rojas joined Lena at the front of the bridge. “Something’s not right here, Captain. Let’s give ourselves a little space?”

    Lena nodded. “Ensign Tessmacher, break orbit and move us away from the planet, one quarter impulse.”

    The Da Vinci moved out of orbit just in time as the planet below fractured and exploded into rock and dust, a massive shockwave spreading out across the system. For her part, Lena struggled to stay on her feet as the explosion buffeted the small vessel; had they not had their shields up, the Da Vinci might have been lost entirely. Her first officer wasn’t so lucky; Rojas lay unconscious on the floor, a gash on her temple bleeding profusely.

    “Doctor Danvers to the bridge!” Lena shouted, holding her first officer’s head and assessing for injury.

    Chief Medical Officer Alex Danvers burst out of the turbo lift doors, medkit and scanner in hand as she took one look at the situation and knew what happened. “Captain, I’ve got her,” she reassured, carefully easing Rojas out of Lena’s arms and beginning to treat the fallen officer.

    “What the hell was that, Brainy?” Lena asked as she clambered back into her seat, using Dox’s nickname among the crew. Coluans were not shy about reminding everyone their species tended to have significantly above average intellects, on par with Vulcans. “I’ve never-” she started before looking out the window, her mouth agape. The planet they’d been orbiting was reducing to a swirling cloud of rubble in space, no trace of the civilization they’d only had a few moments to observe. Flecks of bright green crystals glowed near the center of the debris field, but anyone who had been living on the planet was gone.

    Dox cleared his throat. “It would appear that something caused the planet to… well, everyone can clearly see what happened,” he gestured at the viewscreen.

    “What would cause something like that?”

    “It is unclear, Captain, but my scans of the debris indicates that the planetary core somehow was irradiated to the point where it reached fissile mass and then simply… detonated.” Dox shook his head. Surely a civilization that had the sophistication to mine the core of their planet would also know the dire consequences of doing so, but that appeared not to be the case here.

    “Captain!” Ardeen looked up from her console. “That rocket we saw, it’s some kind of shuttlecraft and it’s about 10,000 kilometers from here. Scans show life support is failing. What should we do?”

    “Are we out of danger?” Lena asked, watching as Dox nodded his head. With his affirmation, Lena pushed the intercom on the arm of her chair. “Very well. Ensign Tessmacher, lay in an intercept course. Luthor to Engineering!”

    “Schott here, Captain. What the heck are you guys doing up there? It’s like a disco party with wrenches down here,” Chief Engineer Winslow Schott shouted amidst a cacophony of machinery sounds.

    “Sorry about that, Winn. Get our new tractor beam warmed up; we have to do a little rescue. Luthor out.” She nodded to Tessmacher to bring the ship around and capture the errant shuttlecraft. Tractor beams were a late addition to the NX Class starships; Lena was lucky to have one outfitted on her ship at all. Most of the older NX ships still had grappling hooks.

    “Any radiological or biological warnings on that shuttle, Brainy?”

    “None that I can tell, Captain. The inhabitant is alive but scans are showing they are likely unconscious. No non-ambient radiation, no signs of viral or bacterial infestation.”

    Lena stood up. “All right, let’s see what we’re dealing with. Ardeen, Dox, you’re with me. Once Doc Danvers has Andrea patched up, tell her to meet us in the shuttle bay.”

    With the grace of a hawk, the Da Vinci swooped over the drifting shuttle and bright blue tractor beams guided it into the shuttle bay of the ship, easing it down onto the deck. Ardeen stood by with a phase rifle in hand as Doctor Danvers rushed in with a fresh medkit.

    Lena stared at the small craft they’d rescued - oblong, almost resembling a silver fish of sorts, she’d not seen anything like it in their travels. The front of it appeared to be a window of some kind, but the cold of space had frosted it over, making it impossible to see inside. “What kind of shuttle is this, anyway? There’s hardly any room in it!”

    “It would appear perhaps the occupant was meant to travel in some sort of stasis or medical coma, rather than be conscious for whatever flight it was intended to take,” Brainy murmured as he waved his tricorder’s scanners over the pod. “The life form inside is definitely under some kind of sedation and is not conscious.”

    Danvers concurred with the Coluan. “This is very much like old cryogenic chambers used after the Eugenics Wars, Captain. Compared to us, it’s fairly primitive. Let’s get it out of there and into Sick Bay, I guess? I’m not detecting any known pathogens or… well, any bacteria or viral forms at all, actually. That’s odd.” The scientist and physician tilted her head as her medical tricorder softly beeped. Every complex life form she’d ever encountered had some kind of symbiotic life within it.

    Dox located what appeared to be a control panel on the outside of the pod and surmised based on the glyphs on it a logical sequence to open the door. Chief Ardeen took aim at the shuttle with her rifle; with a gentle hiss, the lid of the pod swung upwards, revealing an unconscious woman dressed in white robes, some sort of glyph imprinted on the chest.

    “I think it’s safe to put down the phaser, Imra,” Lena chuckled lightly. “This woman’s clearly very unconscious and isn’t a threat to anyone. Let’s get her to Sick Bay like Doc Danvers said, shall we?” She reached in and grabbed one side of the woman while Alex grabbed the other, then hoisted her out of the pod.

    Half an hour later, the Da Vinci had broken orbit and began its long journey back to Earth. They’d secured as many samples of the debris as possible along with the alien life form and her pod; Starfleet Xenobiology had requested they return to Earth immediately so they could investigate more closely, while Starfleet Command wanted to know how a planet just… exploded by itself.

    “Danvers to the Bridge,” the intercom chirped.

    “Go ahead, Doctor,” Lena called out, laying down a report she’d been reading on the armrest of her chair.

    “Captain, you’ll probably want to come down to Sickbay. The alien we recovered is beginning to wake up.”

    Chapter 2: Kara Zor-El

    Lena strode into Sickbay, the white glass doors sliding open to reveal the oval room with a dozen medical beds. Only one of the beds was occupied, with the female alien they’d recovered from the destruction of Gilese 1. Monitors overhead beeped softly, showing her condition to be stable.

    “How’s our patient, Alex?” Lena asked, sidling up to the bed. The alien woman had a wholesome beauty to her - blonde hair, flawless skin. At a glance, she could easily be mistaken for a human, Illyrian, or Deltan, any of the humanoid races that shared a common appearance.

    “She’s in good health, except for whatever was in the atmosphere of her spacecraft. It was one powerful sedative, and it’s finally starting to wear off. Her biology is mostly compatible with ours - you can see we’re getting a good ECG and brainwave patterns from her consistent with most humanoids.” Alex was secretly delighted at the turn of events. She’d joined Starfleet Medical five years ago, hoping to specialize in xenobiology, the study of alien species. Despite a rocky time at Starfleet Academy, she’d graduated with enough prominence to earn a spot as Chief Medical Officer, albeit on one of the fleet’s smaller ships. But a posting was better than being deskbound on Earth or Mars; she was grateful that Lena had spotted her application and chosen her for CMO, and even more grateful for a chance to use her talents more fully.

    “She looks awfully human. Is she? Were there humans on that world?” Lena wondered aloud, looking at the blonde’s features as she rested on the biobed.

    Alex shook her head, her brown hair waving. “Definitely not human.” She keyed in the biobed’s scan results, showing the patient’s anatomy on the display above the bed. “Take a look. Heart’s substantially larger than ours. Lungs are more tubular and in a different orientation. There are a couple of organs we don’t have - there’s one here in the lower right quadrant of the abdomen that is laced with nerves, but I can’t figure out for the life of me what it does. And this other one here in the bottom of the abdominal cavity… I’ve never seen anything like that.”

    Lena looked over the scans in detail. As a scientist and an explorer, this was the kind of detail she relished, academic study in depth. She zoomed in on the second unusual organ, an organ that looked like a tennis ball covered in spikes as her brow knitted together. “Alex… I don’t see any gonads or… well, actually, I don’t see any reproductive organs whatsoever. Am I missing something?”

    “I’m not sure either, Captain. That spiky-looking thing is where the uterus is on a human female, but as far as I can tell, there’s no vaginal canal to connect to it, nor any ovaries or Fallopian tubes feeding into it. Whatever this species is, they must reproduce some other way. That organ is super dense, filled with cells.” Alex was already mulling over the paper she’d be drafting for Starfleet Medical on whoever this alien was.

    “gavrrigehd… zha gavrrig,” the alien muttered as she slowly opened her eyes. Instantly, Lena was transfixed. The alien’s eyes were hypnotic, an electric blue that seemed to almost sparkle.

    “Computer, why is the Universal Translator not working?” she asked aloud, unable to pull her eyes away from those sapphire blues.

    “Universal Translator has insufficient information to construct a translation matrix,” the robotic voice called out.

    As the alien attempted to sit up, Alex scanned her, gently touching her shoulder. “Easy now, miss. You’ve been through quite a lot and your body is still trying to recover from the sedative,” she offered with a soft smile.

    Lena looked at the doctor, her head tilted. “I’m not sure she can understand you, Alex. If we don’t have her language in our translation matrix, I would imagine she wouldn’t have had ours.”

    The alien reached out a hand and gently touched Lena’s arm, her fingers brushing over the yellow uniform shirt and the gold stripes on the sleeve. “khuhtiv…threv Kara Zor-El nahn,” she spoke quietly, pointing at herself.

    “You… that’s your name, I suppose?” Lena asked, before pointing towards herself. “Lena. Lena Luthor. Captain Lena Luthor.”

    The alien smiled, testing out the syllables in her mouth. “Lee… Lee-na,” she grinned, before pointing back at herself. “Kara Zor-El.”

    “Kara,” Lena returned the smile. “Kara, welcome aboard the USS Da Vinci,” she gestured, her hands in the air to indicate the ship as a whole. The blonde’s eyes tracked her hand movements but otherwise didn’t indicate she understood.

    “Captain, there’s one other thing,” Alex noted, pressing a button above Kara’s head as the blonde turned to look. “Her DNA… it’s nothing like I’ve ever seen.” She tapped a few buttons to bring up a strand of human DNA, the familiar double helix outlined. “That’s us, right? Check this out.” In another button press, a strand of DNA with no fewer than five phosphate backbones and forty different base pairs appeared onscreen.

    “Holy crap, Alex. That’s like… five times our genetic density. There’s so much information packed in there,” she breathed, her eyes wide in astonishment. To see a living creature with such complex genetic material literally took her breath away. “Something doesn’t make sense though… she has really complex DNA but no sex organs. Does that mean she’s…”

    Alex nodded, recalling her own study of bioengineering at Starfleet Academy. “Almost certainly genetically engineered. You can see it; in our DNA, you can spot mutations and errors pretty obviously. We leave those alone for the most part because of what happened in the Eugenics Wars. But her DNA, complex though it is, shows no signs of mutation or deformation anywhere. She’s essentially a paragon of her species, a perfect genetic exemplar. There’s no way she’s purely natural like this.”

    Lena pondered whether this was unique to the woman in front of them or whether it was true of all her species. Was true, she corrected herself. Unless Kara’s people had managed to make it to the stars previously, the rest of her race had perished with the destruction of their world. Lena shook her head. The alien in front of her needed help, here and now, and they had to start with language. She picked up a data pad off the end table.

    “Computer, display Children’s Educational Primer Year 1, Federation Standard, and actively listen for translation matrix,” she spoke into the pad, its display immediately changing to that of a kid’s book. Lena handed it to Kara and touched the display, which was showing a picture of a chickadee. “Bird,” the display chimed. Kara looked at it, looked at Lena, and her eyes widened. “Ghim,” she smiled. Another picture appeared, of a couple kissing, this cartoon animated. “Kiss,” the computer read aloud after Kara tapped it. “Dhaikh,” Kara responded.

    After half an hour of back and forth through the book and four subsequent books, the ship’s computer beeped in acknowledgement. “Sufficient data acquired to infer a translation matrix with moderate accuracy. Designating new language as Gilese 1 dialect. Provide additional input to improve the model.”

    Lena brushed off her uniform jacket and stood up from the chair. “Computer, commence-”

    “Got it, I think?” Kara said in heavily accented Federation Standard, eliciting a yelp from Lena.

    “You- how?” Lena asked, her hand still on her chest as she steadied herself against the biobed frame.

    “I listened, the same way your computer did? Your language is funny, all the verbs in the wrong place are,” Kara giggled as she pulled herself up to a more seated position on the biobed.

    “All the verbs are in the wrong place, you mean.”

    “Yes! See? Totally wrong. How can you effectively communicate with each other if you don’t know what you’re talking about until after you say it?” Kara tilted her head.

    Lena pinched the bridge of her nose. “I’m afraid I’m not much of a xenolinguistics expert, Kara. But at least we can communicate a little, and I’m sure once you’re back on your feet, you’d enjoy a long discussion with our science officer about the evolution of our respective languages.”

    “You have a science officer? Is that like a representative of your Science Guild-” the blonde started, before her eyes widened and she tensed up. “My world! Lena, my planet- what happened to it? What happened to Krypton?”

    Lena pulled the chair over to the side of the bed, her eyes softening as she tentatively reached out for the alien’s hand. “What’s the last thing you remember?”

    Zor-El pushed a final sequence of keys on the crystal console, the silver pod in front of him glowing. “Your pod’s coordinates are interlocked with Kal-El’s, Kara. You will follow him to a nearby yellow star.”

    “I’m not afraid, Father,” the 24-year old woman, the youngest ever inducted into Krypton’s Science Guild, affirmed as she climbed into the pod she’d helped build. The science was sound; she’d be medically sedated as the onboard AI, Kelex, infused her mind with the knowledge needed to survive on the distant star she was being sent to. Krypton’s telescopes and other astronomical tools were advanced enough to see across the stars, but not so advanced that they could make out any details on the planets.

    Since she was a young girl, Kara had always looked to the stars for answers, often standing on the white crystal balcony in her family’s spire. She knew, as did many of the noble houses, that Krypton was dying. They’d used technology and science to mine the core of their world, providing energy to fuel their civilization as Rao, the red star they orbited, slowly diminished. Without their energy mining, Kryptonian civilization would have simply frozen to death. But such a technique came at a cost, causing the planet to destabilize over time as well as make its people ill from radiation poisoning. Its destruction was inevitable.

    Her mother Alura stroked Kara’s forehead as she lay back in the pod. “The trip is long, but you’ll sleep most of the way, and we’ll be with you in your dreams,” she choked, a tear running down her cheek.

    An explosion sounded in the distance as the room shook. “Kara, you have to go now. We’ll be right behind you. When you wake, we’ll be at your side, all right?” He keyed in the final sequence as Alura hugged her daughter, then the pod door sealed over her. In moments, a cool blue gas flooded the pod as Kara felt the force of the pod racing down the launch tube… and then she remembered nothing else.

    Lena sighed, looking down at Kara’s trembling hands. “Your shuttle - your pod, as you call it, was launched just before your world exploded. Our ship weathered the explosion, and your pod was knocked off course and disabled. We were able to rescue you, and that’s how you got here. But there were no other pods besides yours, Kara. I’m so sorry. I’m afraid… you may be the last of your people, unless you have colonies elsewhere.”

    Kara shook her head, her throat closing as she suppressed her tears. “No, I- we- our ancestors traveled the stars somehow, but my people haven’t traveled much beyond Rao’s light.”

    “Rao?”

    “Our sun, the light that gives life and strength,” Kara murmured as she sniffed, tears forming despite her best efforts to hold them back. “He binds us to those we love, he- he gives us strength when we have none.”

    “I… I am so sorry, Kara. That we arrived too late to save anyone but you, that… that we couldn’t do more,” Lena said as she offered a cloth for Kara to wipe her eyes.

    “What will happen to me, Lena? I… I don’t have any place to go, no home left, no one to…”

    Lena impulsively took the alien’s hand in her own again, marveling at how smooth and flawless the skin was. If Alex hadn’t confirmed that Kara was alive, she’d swear the woman was a synthetic lifeform. “Well, we are headed back to my people’s homeworld, a place we call Earth.”

    Kara choked out a wet giggle despite her tears. “You named your homeworld after dirt?”

    “Well, what does Krypton mean in your language?” Lena huffed with a wry grin.

    “Our homeland! It’s much more noble than ‘dirt’!”

    “I’ll give you that, Kara,” Lena smiled. “We probably could have chosen better. Regardless, our homeworld is the center of the United Federation of Planets, an interstellar alliance of many different worlds. You’ll be welcome among our peoples, without question.”

    Kara looked up at the ceiling of the medical bay. “How long will it take to get there?”

    Lena did the math in her head. “Well, at Warp 5.5, which is the fastest this ship can go, about 34 days.”

    “THIRTY FOUR DAYS?” the Kryptonian shouted, sitting bolt upright in the bed. “That’s- that’s unbelievable!”

    “I know, it’s nowhere near as fast as the new Constitution-class cruisers. Those could make the same journey in about 6 days,” Lena sighed, briefly envying some of her compatriots as she virtually ignored Kara’s reaction. Starfleet’s priorities meant that for a pure science mission only, which is what she wanted to do with her career, the tradeoff was always slower, older ships.

    “Lena, you don’t understand. My people- our pods are the fastest ships we’ve ever made, and we calculated that it would take us close to 27 YEARS to make it to the nearest yellow star. That’s how long I was supposed to be asleep. That you can do it in so short a time is just… astonishing. How is it we have never met your people, your species yet?”

    Lena offered a soft smile and squeezed Kara’s hand. “Well, while I certainly wish it were under better circumstances, I’m pleased we have met now.”

    Kara lay back in the bed, pulling up the shiny metallic blanket over herself. “I am too. Thank you for rescuing me. If… if it’s not offensive to you, I’m going to rest a little more.”

    “Of course, Kara. Doctor Danvers will be here when you wake, and if you need anything, you need only ask,” Lena smiled, rose, and headed out of Sickbay.

    Chapter 3: Only Human

    “So, explain this to me again? Your technology revolves entirely around these crystals, and this little green crystal here stores the entirety of your civilization?” Winn Schott held up a slender green crystal, slightly glowing in contrast to the dull gunmetal grey of the shuttle bay walls.

    After several days of recovery, Kara was back on her feet and had spent most of her free time near her pod, checking over its systems. Every time he’d had a free moment outside of his shift, Winn hung around to ask her questions, like a puppy constantly begging for treats.

    “Exactly, Chief Engineer Winn Schott. We harnessed the power of light to create crystals that have knowledge built into it. They are far more information-dense than your duotronic enhancers you currently use,” Kara remarked as she carefully removed the crystal from Winn’s hands and put it back in her pod, which was now surrounded by equipment in the Da Vinci’s shuttle bay.

    Winn scrubbed his hands over his face. “That’s absolutely brilliant. We made fairly large strides in the 22nd century from transistors to duotronics, but optical storage media was always the dream. This… this could change so much for us. You can store more information on this crystal probably than we have stored in the main computer.” His brow wrinkled at a conflicting thought. “So, uh, I don’t know if this is rude or not, but if you have this much advanced technology around information storage, how is it you don’t have warp-capable starships?”

    Kara frowned. “Once, long ago, we did. My people walked among the stars as you do. But as Rao, our sun began to cool, we… we focused inwards. We turned our time and attention to surviving, to maximizing what we could of the resources we had left. We kept our civilization alive as long as we could until… well, until it wasn’t enough.”

    Winn nodded. “My ancestors faced similar problems. Two centuries ago, Earth was so unstable because we had superheated the atmosphere beyond what it could sustain.”

    Winn bowed his head. “We didn’t, not for more than a century. Given that the crew of the Enterprise used the transporter to restore/change the flavor of chewing gum, it would be straightforward to restore DNA damage.

    Kara thought back to Krypton, to the Science Guild and what she’d been taught about how her people survived on the harsh, radioactive world. Their bodies produced high amounts of resveratrol to absorb ambient radiation before it damaged their DNA. Kryptonian DNA had 5 phosphate backbones and 40 different base pairs; they’d been genetically engineered over the millennia to resist genetic damage from nearly all sources of radiation and contamination. The extra phosphate backbones and base pairs meant that any errors which occurred during cell division were more easily repaired because instead of having 1 copy of a broken base pair as a backup, they had 4 backups.

    She briefly summarized this to Lena, who nodded. “And the ion storm… must have had high-energy gamma rays within it somehow. Perhaps that’s what kickstarted your body and consciousness somehow? Maybe waking you to deal with a potential radiation threat?”

    “I’m honestly not sure, Lena. I… there’s still a lot I don’t understand about myself, about my people once we’re off our world. It was many generations ago that we explored space, and that was never taught to us in our schools except as cursory ancient history.” She looked out the shuttlecraft window for a moment, her gaze far in the distance. “And there’s no one left for me to ask.”

We’re all scientists. Surely we can decode all the mysteries of Krypton in your cells.”

    “I’d like that, Lena,” Kara grinned, pulling Lena closer. “Let’s explore me together. I’d like that a lot.”

    Just as their noses touched, Lena’s communicator burst into static before clearing. “…ca.. the…” it crackled. “Come in, Captain Luthor! Are you there?” Andrea’s voice finally came through.

    Lena squeezed her eyes closed, exhaled, and flipped her communicator open, still hovering close to the blonde. “Luthor to Da Vinci. Yes, I’m here and we’re unharmed, though I’m not sure what condition the shuttle is in. Two to beam directly to Sickbay, and let Alex know I’ll need some chelatrazine.”

    “Roger that, Captain. We’ll get you all squared away. Glad to hear you’re okay.”

    “I suppose that means it’s time to go,” Kara murmured, quietly mourning the lost moment they shared.

    “I suppose it does.” She felt the slight tingle of the transporter as the world around her began to glow. Just before everything vanished, Kara leaned forward and kissed Lena’s cheek, smiling devilishly.

    Author’s Notes

    Resveratrol - the chemical engineered into Kryptonians - isn’t fictional; it’s a polyphenol that naturally occurs in foods like soy, grapes, red wine, and peanuts. It occurs most in plants, which makes sense - they’d evolve chemical compounds to deal with constant exposure to ionizing radiation from the sun. Kara’s ancestors would have likely engineered it into their bodies to provide chemical defenses against their homeworld’s radiation in addition to having more robust DNA. It may or may not have other protective health effects, but multiple studies have shown that it does offer some protection against radiation.

I figured it would be a good choice based on the captain’s description of you.” The waitress left the bottle and wandered off to serve other patrons after giving Lena a knowing wink.

    Lena shook her head, confused, before turning her attention to Kara. “So…”

    “So…” Kara laughed as she finished the first glass, “wow, this stuff works fast. I’m a little lightheaded.”

    “Now that we’ve averted a war and my mother’s wrath, I find myself at a loss for what the future holds,” Lena muttered into her glass of wine, the sounds of the bar fading away. “For years… no, for my whole life, I’ve been measured against Lex, pushed by my mother. Now… they’re both gone. I’m honestly a little unsure of who I am.”

    Kara refilled and held up her shot glass, gently clinking it with Lena’s. “Here’s to not knowing who you are any more without parents pushing you every step of the way. I’m a woman without a people too!” she giggled, the whiskey’s effects becoming more pronounced as she poured a third shot into the glass.

    Lena pursed her lips. Who was she, really? A starship captain, to be sure. A scientist. An explorer. But she struggled to think of anything that defined her outside of work. She’d spent so long striving to be as good as Lex, striving to uphold the family name that she’d sacrificed everything else in her life. At Kara’s questioning gaze, she summarized her thoughts.

    “Girlfriend,” Kara exclaimed with a broad grin. “That’s what else you are, Lena. You’re my girlfriend, and I’m your girlfriend, and we may not have our families any more, but we have each other, right?” She took another shot as Lena carefully nudged the bottle out of reach. “We’ll have each other forever and ever, Lena. True love!” she slurred slightly.

    “No,” Lena mumbled, finishing the last dregs of wine in her glass and wishing she’d ordered something stronger. She briefly contemplated the Aldebaran whiskey for herself.

    “What?” Kara exclaimed, her hand grasping the shotglass so tightly that it shattered, reduced to a pile of fine glass powder on the table. “Wh-wh-what do you mean no, Lena? What did I do? Am I not good enough for you? Am I not pretty enough, am I-”

    Lena grabbed Kara’s hands and held them in her own. “No, Kara. You’re perfect. You’re literally perfect,” she started, wondering if this conversation would be better when Kara wasn’t tipsy. “And… that’s the problem. I love you, and I’m with you for as long as you’ll have me…”

    She exhaled, lowering her head for a moment. “But that may not be all that long in the grand scheme of things.”

    “What do you mean, Lena? I’ll love you forever! That’s what some of your Earth literature says, true love forever and ever,” she chanted in a singsong voice, bringing Lena’s hands to her lips.

    “For you, Kara. Alex was showing me more of her scans that she took after the Klingons rescued you. Your cells… your body… Kara, you’re genetically perfect… and with how you reacted to the star around Maranga IV, absorbing as much energy as you did, you’re staying that way. You’re not aging,” she said, turning her eyes up to stare into Kara’s.

    “Well, that’s great, right?”

    “Kara… you may not be aging. But… I am. That’s the problem,” she breathed.

    Kara’s eyes widened as Lena’s words sank in. Kryptonians were already a long-lived species because of their genetic engineering, but now with her being safely away from her homeworld’s radiation, she knew Lena spoke the truth. Tears began to pool in her eyes as she remembered humans lived for about 100 years; she would have lived well past 200 on Krypton and apparently would live for much longer away from it.

    Without a word, she pulled Lena into a tight embrace, carding her fingers through the brunette’s hair and clutching onto her as though the human might evaporate or fly away at any moment. “Oh Rao, Lena. We’ll fix this. We can fix this, I know we can. I’ll save you, I’ll keep you alive so we can be together.” She wrinkled her brow as Lena tapped her on the shoulder once, then several times, pulling back to kiss the brunette.

    And then she yelped. Lena’s face was bright red, her shoulders tense. “Kara! Whew, I- I couldn’t breathe, you were hugging me so tightly,” she gasped.

    “I’m so sorry Lena! Oh- I-” she laid her arms on the table and put her face down into them, her own shoulders beginning to heave as Kara cried.

    Lena rested her palm between Kara’s shoulder blades, rubbing small circles on her back. “Kara… listen, it’s okay. It really is. Nothing in life is certain anyway. How about- how about we enjoy the time that we do have together? Make the most of it? Statistically speaking, I’ve still got about 7 or 8 decades to look forward to,” she jested, nudging the blonde gently.

    “I just- I can’t bear the thought of losing you, Lena. I’ve lost so much, I’ve lost everything and everyone I’ve ever loved. I can’t lose you,” she cried, leaning into Lena’s shoulder.

    “Ar scáth a chéile a mhaireann na daoine”, Lena murmured into the crown of Kara’s head. She looked up at that expression, her cobalt eyes searching Lena’s emeralds for meaning. “It’s an expression from the country I was born in, Ireland. It means, ‘in the shadow of each other, people live’. We take shelter and refuge in each other. We’ve both lost so much, Kara, but we can be shelter to each other.”

    Kara searched Lena’s face with such intent that she might as well have been staring straight through it with her x-ray vision. “Promise?”

    “I promise, Kara. Love may not add years to my life, but your love will add life to my years,” she said as she kissed Kara, a hint of the whiskey on the blonde’s lips.

    “I like that a lot. Your love will definitely add life to my years, Lena. I’m yours for as long as I can be,” she breathed against Lena’s lips before seizing them between her own.

    Epilogue: Where No One Has Gone Before

    114 years later

    “Captain’s log, stardate 56706.25. The USS Da Vinci is on patrol duty just outside Bajor. Five years after the end of the Dominion War, we are still assisting in cleaning up the massive amounts of debris remaining from the war in systems all along the edge of the quadrant. Fortunately, our little ship is more than up to the task, which is good since it’s going to take years, if not decades, to clean up the mess left behind.”

    Kara tapped the display on her desk, turning off the recorder. She looked around at her ready room of the Akira-class starship. Though conceptually similar to the old NX class she’d been rescued by long ago, the Akira-class ship was more than double in size, and had all the modern amenities of a Federation starship. Built for the Borg Wars, the Akiras were now one of the most common heavy cruisers in Starfleet.

    She stepped over to the replicator. “Hot chocolate with whipped cream and Bailey’s Irish whiskey,” she asked, the machine materializing her favorite drink. She sat down at her desk, the black glass reflecting everything in the room. As she sipped her drink, her eye caught the photo on the corner of the desk, and she paused.

    Her and Lena, on their honeymoon on Risa, just a few years after they’d fallen in love. She reminisced about the magic of Risa; Lena kept the horga’hn ritual love idol they’d received as a hotel room gift for newlyweds first in her cabin, then in her office on Earth after her promotion to Admiral. Finally, Lena had put it on the mantle in their seaside home in Glandore, Ireland after her retirement from Starfleet.

    Kara kept a replica of the horga’hn in her cabin on the Da Vinci. None of what was aboard the ship was original - everything of sentimental value to her was back on Earth, in Ireland where it was safe. Precious possessions had no place on Starfleet vessels which seemed to suffer alarmingly high rates of mishaps and incidents. That was doubly true of the oil painting on the wall, the portrait she’d painted herself of Lena at her retirement party. Her raven hair had turned a beautiful, reflective silver in her later years, but her eyes never lost their emerald sparkle.

    “Captain Zor-El to the bridge,” her first officer’s voice interrupting her reverie.

    “On my way,” she exited her ready room and stepped onto the spacious bridge of the Da Vinci. Kara reached up and touched the bronze dedication plaque mounted on the wall, her fingers tracing the lettering on it.

    USS DA VINCI-A
    AKIRA CLASS STARSHIP
    SECOND STARSHIP TO BEAR THE NAME
    STARFLEET REGISTRY NCC-63297
    LAUNCHED STARDATE 47856

    Kara smiled at the plaque. Once Starfleet had somewhat relaxed the regulations around genetically-engineered lifeforms, she’d been able to apply to Starfleet Academy and worked her way up through the ranks. Though her status as a genetically-engineered person prohibited her from ever achieving a rank higher than captain, she still loved to roam the galaxy and explore, the way she and Lena had done together for years. Ordinarily, she’d probably be a two-star Admiral herself now were it not for the genetic prohibition.

    Commissioning a ship named after Lena’s old ship was one of the last acts Lena had done before her retirement , and Kara was the only captain ever considered for the chair once it was finally constructed. She smirked; it helped a little when your wife was both a Fleet Admiral and Chair of the Joint Chiefs. Her only regret is that Lena hadn’t been able to set eyes on the new Da Vinci.

    She turned and approached the captain’s chair, her first officer getting up and stepping aside. “Well, Commander, what have we got?”

    “Captain, you asked to be notified when the USS Aldrin was in range. We’ve hailed them as you instructed.”

    Kara sat down, putting her mug of hot chocolate on the chair’s armrest as the viewscreen sprang to life, the Sagan-class starship filling the view with its four glowing blue nacelles.

    “Captain Zor-El.”

    “Captain Luthor,” Kara grinned at the super-sized image of her daughter on the floor to ceiling viewscreen. “What brings you out this far to Deep Space 9?”

    The brunette on screen smiled, her blue eyes sparkling. She had Lena’s facial structure - high cheekbones, incredible jawline - as well as Lena’s hair, but Kara’s flawless complexion and sapphire irises. “Mom, I’m here for the same reason you are,” the younger woman laughed. “You know, cleanup duty. We’re like the responsible parents coming in to clean up after the kids threw a party and trashed the house.”

    Kara’s bridge crew chuckled at the analogy. Like Lena had once done, Kara cultivated a close, familial relationship with her crew, a welcome relief for many used to more strict, less personable captains.

    “All right, then. We’ll get started on grid 14-40 and we’ll leave you to grid 18-10. Want to pop over for dinner?”

    The brunette giggled. “Sure, Mom. Your holodeck or mine?”

    —-

    “You’re looking wonderful for a 100-year old, Alexa,” Kara smiled at her daughter as they sat down to dinner in the Da Vinci’s holodeck. Kara had replicated their family home in Ireland, and they sat in the rustic dining room at a rough-hewn wooden table Lena had commissioned when they moved in.

    “Same goes for you, Mom. 138 and still looking like a 24 year old,” Alexa laughed. She gazed fondly at the dining room, white plaster walls with exposed beams that reflected the morning sun. Lena’s favorite wooden chairs and an oak colored sideboard stuffed with knickknacks from their travels completed the simple room. “I can’t believe it’s been 20 years already.”

    Kara shook her head. “I can’t either. It feels like just yesterday she was rescuing me from Krypton.” Her eyes softened and tears welled up as she looked around the home they’d shared for 90 years together.

    “Have you been back?” Alexa asked softly, reaching her hand across the table.

    “Yeah, last time I was in Sector 001. I stopped by and cleaned her headstone. I try to make sure at least once a year, you know?” Despite the normal traditions of Starfleet, Lena had made it clear she wanted a traditional Irish burial, and her white granite headstone, carefully carved by Kara with her heat vision, sat on a grassy hill overlooking the ocean behind their home.

    “On her birthday or mine?”

    That brought a quiet laugh from Kara. Alexa was the product of a long and complicated procedure. After their marriage, when Lena had asked Kara if they could have a child together, Kara had initially said no. She didn’t want yet another family member she’d outlive. That’s when Lena had suggested the unthinkable, something that required them to leave Federation space entirely and break a few laws in the process.

    Illyria, one of the worlds outside the Federation and a planet that would never be admitted to it, had genetic engineering facilities. Using one of the embryos inside Kara and the data from her pod, they’d merged Kara’s and Lena’s DNA characteristics together as the Matricomp would have and infused it inside one of the embryos inside Kara. 9 months later, Lena gave birth to the second survivor of Krypton, and named her after Lena’s long departed brother. Because her child’s biology was fully Kryptonian, Kara would be spared the heartbreak of outliving her baby.

    “Hers, of course. You know I always call you on your birthday, Lexie,” the blonde smiled, serving up two plates of Lena’s favorite shepherd’s pie. When they’d first started living together, Lena had refused to tell Kara the secret technique that made it so delicious. So desperate was the Kryptonian that she used her x-ray vision to stare straight through Lena’s body and saw her stirring in an outlandish amount of butter in the mashed potatoes. Lena had called her on it a few months later when she watched Kara make the dish and use the correct amount of butter.

    “Mmm, just the way Mama used to make it,” Alexa sighed. “I’m glad you have a replicator pattern of one of her originals. No offense to you, Mom, but there’s no substitute for hers.”

    Kara nodded as she dug in, fully agreeing with her daughter. “She would still be offended that we eat it right away instead of waiting the hour she always told… told us…” she managed before covering her mouth with her hands, holding in her tears.

    Alexa got up and wrapped her arms around Kara’s shoulders. “I miss her too, Mom. Every day. I know you said not to, but I’ve looked into different technologies to see… well, you know. I haven’t found anything.”

    “I think about that every so often, Lexie, but she wouldn’t want that. Rao, she was so insistent about having a normal life, refusing everything we suggested. Remember when I suggested we harvest Borg nanoprobes to see if we could prolong her life?”

    Alexa nearly fell over as laughter seized her. “Oh Rao, yes. The look on her face!” She scrunched up her face and furrowed her brow, imitating Lena’s tone. “I’ll be having none of that inside my body, thank you very much,” she imitated to both their laughter before sitting back down.

    “We should do something special this year, you know?” Kara mentioned as she served herself another helping. “I’m trying to keep it positive, to remember that Lena’s worth celebrating more than anything else. That we were lucky to have her in our lives at all.” Her eyes darted out the window, the holographic re-creation of the headstone, hills, and ocean catching her eye.

    Alexa smiled at the thought, watching her mother’s line of sight. “Maybe we should take a month off and just go home, you know? The real thing,” she waved at the holodeck surroundings.

    “I think that’s a gr-”

    “Captain Zor-El to the bridge!” announced her first officer as Red Alert klaxons sounded, ending the holodeck simulation. As Kara got up, the floor of the holodeck shook, knocking her and Alexa off their feet momentarily before they both levitated above the floor, flying to the turbolift.

    “What the hell is going on?” Kara shouted as the turbolift shook around them. Something was tossing the Da Vinci around like a toy in a bathtub. As soon as the turbolift doors opened to the bridge, she saw what it was.

    A massive Borg Cube was off their starboard side, raking the Da Vinci with gravimetric torpedoes. Unlike its predecessor, the ablative armor on the Akira-class ships was easily able to handle the abuse, replicating new armor as quickly as Borg weapons tried to destroy it. The Aldrin hadn’t been quite as lucky but had gotten clear of the combat area. Kara zipped up her duty jacket and flew herself over the railing into the command chair.

    “Return fire!” she ordered, examining the tactical readouts on the armrests as Alexa sat down in the spare chair on the other side of Kara’s first officer.

    “Direct hit to section 5, Borg ship is losing power,” reported her tactical officer, the sound of the Akira’s heavy quantum torpedo launchers whooshing with each shot. The viewscreen flared blue as the quantum torpedoes hit their mark over and over again, gouts of fire erupting from the damaged alien vessel.

    “Captain, the Borg ship is making a run for the Bajoran wormhole!” her first officer announced.

    “Pursuit course. We can’t let it escape to the Gamma Quadrant. Keep firing!”

    Alexa looked at the console in front of her, then at the massive metal cube on screen emitting a bright yellow beam. “Mom, the Borg ship is emitting a crazy amount of chroniton particles in some kind of directed energy weapon.”

    Kara looked down at her display. “And it’s not at us. They’re- they’re firing whatever that thing is at the wormhole itself! Helm, get us between the wormhole and the Borg ship.” The Bajoran wormhole was one of the Federation’s most prized transit points, a stable wormhole connecting two quadrants of the galaxy. An attempt to destroy it would be catastrophic for much of the Federation.

    Behind the Da Vinci, the entrance to the wormhole swirled with chaotic new energies. Instead of its normal blue and yellow color, arcs of red and yellow lightning danced around the edges like a cosmic hurricane. The Da Vinci maintained its rate of fire as the ship shuddered, suddenly bearing the brunt of whatever new weapon the Borg had created.

    “We’re getting saturated with whatever energy this weapon is that they’re firing!” Alexa shouted over the din of both ships’ weapons. Gold light suffused the bridge as electricity arced across all the surfaces, as though Kara were standing in the middle of a thunderstorm. She looked down, her eyes wide, as her skin began to glow with the same golden light.

    Just then, nearly everyone was knocked off their feet as the Da Vinci lurched, the shockwave of the exploding Borg cube buffeting the ship. “Shields at 77% but inertial dampeners are compromised, captain!” the first officer shouted, smoke and sparks occluding her vision. “Captain, the shockwave has thrown us near the event horizon of the wormhole!”

    The young ensign piloting the Da Vinci was valiantly trying to correct course, even overriding the computer to try steering manually, but it was no use. Like a sailboat caught in a hurricane, the subspace eddy currents pulled the Da Vinci into the wormhole. As the ship fell in, everything turned bright white for Kara.

    Silence. Not just conventional quiet, where she could hear the gentle thrum of the warp core, or perhaps the breeze and birds planetside, but absolute silence is what Kara swam in. Silence, and white light. Nothing else.

    She had no idea whether she spent a second or a day like that, alone with her thoughts, wondering if somehow she’d managed to die. I would miss Lexie terribly if that were the case… but it might mean I could be with Lena again, she mused. Lena, my love.

    It was silent.

    For a brief moment, Kara felt as though she were being watched, and a mental image of a bald Black man with a goatee chucking popped into her head as the sound of a heartbeat echoed in her mind.

    And then everything went white again.

    “Hello?” Kara called out. She looked around the room where she’d appeared, as though someone had beamed her in, except without the telltale lights and sounds of a transporter. By habit, she brushed off her black uniform of nearly imaginary dust, straightened her jacket, and looked around.

    White. Everything in the room was white. Floor to ceiling windows overlooking a city skyline she didn’t recognize. The most unusually shaped desk sat in front of the windows, looking like a hollowed-out egg or perhaps a very uncomfortable shoe. Two light grey chairs on wheels sat in front of the desk, while a bookcase with several lights sat off to the side and a large white sofa opposite. On top of the desk rested a terminal computer of some kind, not too different from the one in her ready room, and a small potted plant with an orchid in it. The heavy shag rug under the desk - also white - completed the look of the room, some kind of office.

    Kara opened up her senses and heard dozens - no, hundreds of voices - talking in whatever building she was in, but didn’t recognize most of the conversations. The language that most of the occupants were speaking was close to Federation standard but not exactly the same.

    She turned her attention to the display on the wall and knew that something was terribly wrong. Wherever she’d been transported to, it was to some primitive location. The technology in the room was hundreds of years behind the Federation. Had she somehow ended up on a world in the Gamma Quadrant? Had the Da Vinci fallen through the wormhole from the explosion? She looked again carefully at the desk; a white plastic box that looked like an ancient communications handset sat on the desk, a tiny screen running across the top.

    Kara gasped at the display, recognizing the language. She wasn’t in the Gamma Quadrant. Somehow, she was on Earth - an Earth, though which Earth she wasn’t clear about.

    MARCH 27, 2017

    the display read. Somehow she’d ended up 363 years in the past.

    “Chroniton particles…” she murmured to herself. The Borg must have been discharging a weapon based on time travel somehow. But Bajor was 52 light-years from Earth, a distance that even an Akira-class ship would take 8 days to travel at maximum warp. There was no way she could have appeared on Earth both in the past and in mere seconds from where she’d been.

    She tapped her combadge on her chest, the idle clicking sound indicating she was nowhere near a subspace relay to make the device communicate. Sighing, she walked back over to the desk and looked out the window. Just as she started to catalog the skyline, she heard the door open to the room.

    And a voice she never thought she’d hear again.

    “Excuse me? What are you doing in here?”

    Kara whirled around.

    “Supergirl? That’s certainly a new look for you.”

    Kara’s jaw dropped.

    “L-Lena?”

    the end
