Kara sights Mike angling away from his defender, setting the defensive back on his heels.

There’s a small opening, hands are up, and Kara releases the ball quickly into the small pocket available.

She doesn’t see if Mike catches the ball. Instead she sees the ground rushing toward her face as the defensive lineman wraps her legs up, guiding her to the ground. She hears a whistle blow, signaling the end of the play, and rolls away to see Mike coming up with the ball safely in his hands.

A smile spreads across Kara’s face as she’s picked up off the ground again. She chances a glance at Coach Carr to see that his scowl is just a smidge smaller.

“Nice hands, Mike,” Kara calls out as he tosses the ball back to her.

Kara pulls her helmet off and puffs out her cheeks as she squeegees the sweat from her eyebrow. She slowly makes her way to the bench, helmet dangling from her fingers. There’s another man with graying hair and oversized sunglasses sitting next to him, enthusiastically talking into his ear. Kara’s throat immediately goes dry. She takes a moment to clear it and takes another sip of water, elbowing James, “Hey, there’s Winn.”

Barely.

Four yards.

Two yards.

Cornerback is up ahead.

It’s not much, but she loses her balance.

She pitches forward, out of bounds, cradling the ball to her chest.

She’s laid out on the turf. Sliding on her side with the momentum. Ball knocked out of her hands.

Then she’s colliding with something soft and decidedly not covered in football pads. She rolls to her back, hoping to cushion the fall of whoever she was barreling through. She feels the person drop and puts her arms out and around to guide them to the ground.

Something collides with her helmet. Something not at all soft and absolutely not protected by another helmet.

Oh shit.

“Oh god, I am so sorry! Are you ok?” Kara starts as she loosens her grip and her victim moves to sit up. Kara spits out her mouth guard, undoes her chin strap, and whips her helmet off as she follows.

She’s met with two watery eyes and a head of dark hair and suddenly Kara is aware two things all at once.

First, she just body tackled Lena the Cheerleader to the ground.

And B, Lena is still sitting in her lap, their legs still tangled together and wow was this not the time to fixate on that!

“Fuck - shit - god damn!” a stream of profanities are flowing out of Lena’s mouth as she covers her nose with both hands and curls inward on herself.

“Ok, ok, here let’s get you over to the bench!” Kara says as she scrambles to untangle herself from Lena. She moves to her knees behind the dark haired girl, hand placed lightly on her back. Kara gingerly places the other hand under her elbow and guides Lena back to her feet, “Wow, I really am so, so sorry.”

“You’ve said that,” Lena says, voice sounding delicate, like she was focusing entirely too hard on internalizing another slew of cuss words.

Kara guides her over to the bench as the athletic trainer rushes over with a first aid kit. Lena sits and pulls her hands down as the trainer approaches. Blood is streaming down her nose to her chin and, “Woah, holy shit is that blood,” Kara blurts out, taking a step back.

Lena, for her part, doesn’t take any notice as the trainer pulls on a pair of gloves and begins to hand her gauze and instruct her to lean her elbows on her knees.

Kara feels a weird twist in her stomach at the sight of a deposited gauze pad and moves toward the bench, “That’s… that’s a lot of blood. I, uhm... I’m gonna sit down.”

Lena and the trainer both throw her a quick glance, “You need to sit down?”

Kara just lets out a soft ‘Mmm’ and nods as she closes her eyes and counts back from ten, focusing on her breathing.

“I’m the one who just got plowed over by a football player,” Lena replies, sounding only half offended. Maybe joking? Definitely exaggerated.

In through the nose. Out the mouth. “Yeah, but at least I’m a girl football player.” Kara says once she’s reached zero. She peeks an eye open to see that most of the blood has been cleaned up and Lena is looking at her with a cocked eyebrow.

“Your head is still hard,” Lena settles on and looks forward again.

Heard that before, Kara thinks to herself. She looks back to the field where Coach Grant is approaching. Kara stands, “I really am very sorry. I promise not to plow into you again.”

Lena gives her a bit of an up-down at that and gives her head a little tilt, looking back up into Kara’s eyes as she does. Kara’s ears burn as she realizes how that could possibly be interpreted inappropriately, and she quickly turns away to head back to where her teammates are huddled.

Coach Grant passes her on the way, “Good legs, Danvers,” the tiny woman tells her, mouth turned down, “But you missed the first down by a yard. Five shuttle runs. You take the team through. To the twenty yard line.”

Kara’s shoulders drop in disappointment, but she starts her jog back. As she approaches her teammates, she sees James coming her way, “Not exactly how you imagined your first time under the cheerleader, I bet.”

Kara keeps jogging past him, “Shut up, James.”

As she approaches the touchdown line she calls out to her team, “Alright, shuttle runs to the twenty! On me!”

Afternoon practice is light in comparison to the full contact work from that morning. Coach Carr works the team in the weightroom before moving out to the field for special teams work in just helmets. Coach Grant dismisses the team with a quick reminder that the first game of the season is only a week away.

She walks to the bleachers to grab her gear bag and spots him quickly descending the metal stairs. He moves to join up with Kara at the stadium entrance that leads out to the parking lot.

“There’s the girl of steel!” Winn jokes, alluding to the nickname given to Kara by the local newspapers when she first started getting playing time last season.

“That is… the worst humble brag to ever come out of you,” Winn swats back at her hand that’s aimlessly flitting around his head.

“I said it was hard, didn’t I?” Kara throws a mischievous grin his way as they approach her truck.

Winn frowns, “Well, technically, you didn’t actually say that, no.”

She chucks her bag and helmet into the backseat and, sliding her seat back into place, clambers up into the old truck.

“Ugh. God Nel, you’re such a fucking boat,” Winn huffs out as he hoists himself into his seat.

“Hey! She can hear you!” Kara defends as she rubs the truck’s dashboard. “Shhhh, he didn’t mean it, babygirl.” Kara says to the vehicle itself, blindly grabbing for her seatbelt.

Nel rumbles to life at the twist of the key and Winn reaches to turn the radio from the local roots station before Kara slaps his hand away.

“Not today, kid,” she points her finger at him dramatically, as if to scold, “Hard day, remember? 165 pounds?”

“No. It sounds good,” Kara corrects as she locks eyes with him in a silent challenge. In a petty move, she reaches out and pushes the volume far past a reasonable level.

As Kara pulls from the parking lot onto the highway leading back to town, the opening chords of an upbeat bluesy song start to play. One that she’s definitely heard on the more popular stations.

Kara shoots Winn a triumphant look, “See?!”

Winn reaches out to turn the music back down to a reasonable volume with a resigned “Whatever.” Despite the complaints, though, Kara can hear him humming along to the words on the radio.

“Hey,” Kara breaks the comfortable silence that had fallen over the cab of the truck, “How’s Lena?” She had seen the other girl finish her check-up with the trainer and return back to the rest of her squad before the football team had finished their morning session.

“Oh, she’ll be fine,” Winn waves her worries off with his hand, “Trainer said she didn’t have any signs of a concussion and her bloody nose was done after a few minutes. She was able to join practice once, ya know, the pain in her face subsided.”

“That’s good. Did she seem, like, mad?” Kara pushes further, knowing that she’s showing far too much interest to be at all casual.

Her lack of subtlety is confirmed when Winn looks over at her, searching over Kara’s face. “No,” Winn finally settles on, “Not mad. It’s not like you ran her over on purpose.”

Kara nods and a beat of silence passes as Kara pulls into downtown Midvale.

“Kara,” Winn says softly.

“Hm?”

“Lena’s last name is Luthor.”

She doesn’t give him one.

“So?” She looks ahead at the intersection. Focusing on pushing in the clutch and shifting through the gears when it’s her turn to go.

“I just… I wasn’t sure if you knew. And thought maybe it’d be something you’d noticeably react to - once you found out. And, well, what if you found out from Lena and reacted in front of her? So I-”

“Kara-”

“Please stop trying to be my therapist, Winn,” Kara sighs out as she turns down Winn’s street.

“You’re right,” he starts, “I’m not your therapist.”

Kara thinks he’s going to leave it at that before he presses on, staring out the passenger window, “But I’m your best friend. And I wouldn’t have any way of knowing if ‘Luthor’ would mean anything to you because you’ve never talked to me about what happened.”

Winn pauses, “And that’s ok,” he continues, shifting to look at her, “I’ve never expected you to. But that doesn’t mean I’m not aware of what happened to your family or that I don’t still want to make sure you’re ok. In whatever way I can.”

Kara pulls to the curb in front of Winn’s house, shifting to first and pulling the parking brake. She mulls his words over, thinking back to when she first met Winn at a neighborhood block party shortly after the Danvers’ had taken her in.

His knobby knees had been grass-stained and scratched, the tail of his shirt untucked from his khaki shorts. He’d run up to her, as she stood shyly behind Jeremiah’s knee, and giggled while blowing bubbles at her face. He had run off back toward the field full of children, but Jeremiah had encouraged her to run after him.

And she had, after some time.

They’d been best friends ever since. Kara was fairly certain her friendship with Winn was one of the main reasons she’d been able to establish any semblance of a normal life. Why she hadn’t grown up to be a full-on Sidney Prescott.

Kara runs a hand through her ponytail. Grips the steering wheel. She’s probably being unfair.

“Look, Winn, I’m sorry. I know you’re just looking out for me. I don’t talk about it because I don’t-” she lets her hands slip from her hold on the wheel, “Because I can’t remember anything.”

She clears her throat and her gaze drops down to her hands, “I was there and I can’t remember anything.”

It’s a blessing and a curse. She’d spent hours in sterile rooms with children’s toys scattered about. Cops and child psychiatrists had tried to gently coax her into remembering something - anything - about the night of the murders. Any sort of clue that could help them figure out who the murderer had been. But the memories just hadn’t been there - still weren’t.

Winn reaches out and takes one of her hands, giving it a reassuring squeeze.

Kara swallows hard and blinks a few times. She turns to Winn and offers a small shrug, “After twelve years of not remembering, talking about it just feels like going around in circles.”

“I don’t need you to talk about it. But if you ever do, I hope you know that you can. To me.” Winn gives her a small smile.

Kara reaches over and ruffles his hair with a grin, “I know that, you big sap.” Winn laughs as he lets her pinch his cheek. With the mood a little lighter, she continues, “Now get the hell out of my car.”

“Yeah, yeah, yeah,” he ducks away from her assault and opens Nel’s door, “Hey! You coming to the beach party tomorrow? Last Saturday of the summer and all that.”

“Yeah for sure!” Kara grabs onto the subject change quickly, “I’m helping Ms. Donahue set up at the farmer’s market in the morning, then I’ll head over to Swan Beach. Meet you and James there?”

Winn moves to grab his backpack from the truck, nodding his head, “Sounds good. I heard a certain… cheerleader will be there.”

Kara rolls her eyes and tsks, “Don’t be weird.”

Winn smirks and turns up the walk toward his house.

Kara watches as Winn walks through his front door. She lifts the brake, pulling out into the road.

When Kara pulls into her driveway, the house is dark, save for the front hall light that’s usually left on when no one’s home. She sighs as she slowly ambles up the porch steps, keys jingling as she looks for the house key on her ring.

As she opens the door, she hears a loud thump and tap tap tap - telltale signs that the enormous ball of white fluff her family claims is a dog has heard her come home.

Krypto tears into the entryway as she’s trying to kick her shoes off. Kara tries to move around him to chuck her football gear out of the way, only to find herself stumbling over his many legs onto the low bench pressed against the wall.

“Hi boy! Oh you are just so excited that someone is finally home!” Kara smooshes his fuzzy face to hers as he jumps up to lick her hello.

Feeling her body relax for the very first time that night, Kara tucks herself into Lena’s side and pulls the fluffy throw blanket on the back of the couch across both of their laps. Krypto repositions himself across their feet.

 

Kara falls asleep.

____

 

Kara wakes to Lena gently running her fingers through her hair and softly whispering her name.

 

She looks up to the soft look on Lena’s face and feels the warmth in her chest spread to the tips of her fingers and toes. 

 

But, like a bucket of ice water being dumped over her head, she remembers.

 

Remembers that two more people are dead.

 

That Jeremiah is missing.

 

That he’s been kidnapped .

 

Kara blinks hard twice and slowly sits up. Krypto grumbles from where he’s been sleeping at the other end of the couch.

 

“What time is it?” Kara mumbles, trying to clear the sleep from her throat.

 

“Just after midnight. I thought you might want to move to your bed, and then I’d head home.”

 

Kara decidedly does not want Lena to leave, but she nods her head and moves to stand. When she stumbles, Lena reaches a hand out to steady her and stands up herself, the very faint ghost of a smile on her lips.

 

“Here,” Lena places a hand at the small of Kara’s back, her voice slightly amused at the clumsy way Kara sleepily makes her way toward the staircase, “I’ll tuck you in and everything.”

 

Kara makes a humming noise she hopes sounds like agreement as she trips up the staircase.

 

She toes her bedroom door open, not bothering to turn the light on, and heads for her bed. 

 

Lena turns on the light behind her and grabs an empty water bottle from Kara’s desk, “I’ll go get you some water.”

 

Kara nods. She feels her brow furrow when she notices a manila envelope propped against her pillow.

 

A small spike of anxiety wakes her up enough that she has the presence of mind to quickly survey her room as Lena’s footfalls sound down the staircase. Nothing seems to be out of place, but Kara is still cautious as she reaches from the unexplained envelope.

 

When she picks it up, she notices her name written in short, angry handwriting.

 

Her breaths come short and quick, and her heart is pounding hard in her chest. She notices, distantly, that her hands are shaking as she reaches up to open the flap.

 

The contents of the envelope is a photo. This much she can tell from the texture of paper before she even pulls it all of the way out. 

 

It’s the backside of the photo that’s facing her when she eases it all of the way out. In the same angry handwriting is a message ‘ The Boathouse. One hour. ’

 

There’s a smear of blood on the bottom corner of the message that has Kara’s stomach plummeting. And as she turns the photo over to look at its image, she’s sure she would have vomited if it weren’t for the fact that all of her internal machinations froze over instantly.

 

There are a thousand details to take in at once. The unnatural position of the body. The dark pool around the woman - Kara knows it’s blood despite the black and white photo. The puckering of flesh around the gash in the woman’s neck.

 

But these details all blur together because Kara’s focusing on the face. It’s the face of the woman that’s haunted Kara. Haunted her since she was a child. Haunted her dreams. Has been haunting her waking hours these last few months.

 

She’s looking at the evidence photo of her mother’s murder.

 

She feels like she’s on fire. Her chest burns as a scream works its way up her throat, but gets choked off by fear. She can’t move but her body trembles like it’s going to shake apart. 

 

And she can’t stop looking. Can’t force herself to look away from the odd angle of the neck. The way the hair sticks out at strange places where blood had dried. The eyes - the eyes that she sees in her own face when she looks in a mirror.

 

Kara doesn’t hear the footsteps coming up the steps. Doesn’t hear her bedroom door open or the confused murmur of her name as someone draws close to her.

 

Kara’s eyes flicker to where a hand clasps around her own. Reaches over to pry the photo from her hands. 

 

She blinks a few times and looks up to find Lena kneeling in front of her. Recognizes fear in Lena’s eyes.

 

“Kara.”

 

Kara shakes her head to clear it. Refocuses on the warmth of the hand - Lena’s hand - on her own. Notes her erratic breaths and tries to take a calming one - inhale four, exhale five .

 

“Kara,” Lena says, her voice a little more firm - grounding. She reaches up to place a hand at Kara’s shoulder. Slides it up to cradle Kara’s jaw, ducks down to catch her darting gaze.

 

“Jeremiah.” Kara whispers out.

 

Confusion mixes in with the fear on Lena’s face, “Jeremiah?”

 

As if suddenly snapping back into her body, Kara stands from where she had collapsed onto the edge of the bed. Lena leans back on her heels and looks up to her, confusion becoming more evident on her face.

 

“It’s a summons,” Kara explains as she paces away from the bed only to turn around and pace back.

 

Lena’s brow crinkles deeply, “A summons?”

 

Her mind is racing by her and she tries desperately to keep up. The missing evidence. Burning down the house. Kidnapping Jeremiah. It’s all been Intergang.

 

And they want to meet her.

 

They might not have signed the summons - she stoops to pick up the photo, examines the blood on the back - but Jeremiah did.

 

“They have Jeremiah,” Kara rushes out. She sounds winded, even to her own ears.

 

“Who does?” Lena stands up to look at where Kara is staring at the writing.

 

“Intergang,” Kara says definitively. “Agent Jones said they didn’t think the kidnappers had even left Midvale,” she points at the blood, “they have him and they’re using him to get to me.”

 

Lena shakes her head and looks disbelievingly up at Kara, “There’s no way to know this is Jeremiah’s blood.”

 

Kara runs a hand over the writing on the back of the photo, “Why else would they send me this now ?”

 

“Who?” Lena asks, “Who’s Intergang?”

 

“I don’t know who they are,” Kara chews her lip as she tries to puzzle through it in real-time, “just that they’re this big crime syndicate, or something. Maxwell Lord worked for them, and he was supposed to burn down the old house. And they probably kidnapped Jeremiah when they killed Lord.”

 

“Okay, but how does that have anything to do with…” Lena hesitates and then gestures at the photo in Kara’s hand, “A photo of a dead woman.”

 

Kara swallows, “It’s my mother.”

 

Lena’s eyes snap up to Kara’s.

 

“A while ago, the evidence to my parent’s murder was stolen from the police station,” Kara explains, piecing it together as she talks, “then a responding officer abruptly died shortly after. Then Maxwell Lord, who has known ties to Intergang, failed to burn down my parents’ house and was found dead a few days later. And Jeremiah was kidnapped by whoever killed him.”

 

Kara adds, “It can’t all be coincidence.”

 

Lena seems to be searching her eyes for something, then says, “So, what then? Are they trying to cover something up?”

 

“Or send a message.”

 

“A message.”

 

“A threat.” Kara clarifies.

 

There’s a beat of silence as they look at each other.

 

“I have to go,” Kara says, turning to grab the jacket hanging on the back of her desk chair.

 

“Kara,” Lena catches her arm and tugs her back around to face her, “you are not walking into-”

 

“The note says one hour .” Kara feels a surge of panic, “I have no idea when this was left here! I could already be too late, but I have to try.”

 

“You can’t-”

 

“Lena, If they have Jeremiah, he could be-” Kara cuts off, her voice cracking horribly, “he could be dead.”

 

Silence stretches as Lena looks over her face frantically. She seems to come to a decision that seemingly calms her down, “I’m coming with.”

 

“Absolutely n-”

 

“If this is something you have to do,” Lena interrupts her, “there’s no way you’re doing it alone.”

 

There isn’t time to argue about it. She considers Lena. Nods her head once.

 

“Okay?” Lena clarifies.

 

“Okay.”

 

With that, Kara turns to grab the flashlight she keeps in her bedside table and heads for the door.

They leave the house through the back door and cross through the neighbor’s yard to avoid being seen by the police detail positioned on the street outside of the house.

 

Kara turns on her flashlight and, despite Lena’s small protest, leads them down the trail through the forest preserve that she knows will spit them out two blocks away from the remains of her childhood home.

 

“This way,” Kara tugs Lena by the hand down an overgrown garden path that wraps around the edge of the property. She doesn’t spare a glance for the ruins of the house leftover from the fire. They make their way to the backyard that slopes down to the shore of the boating channel that eventually connects with the bay. There’s a gentle lapping in the distance as the moon illuminates the dilapidated boathouse through the light fog.

 

Kara pulls up short some ways from the entrance and clicks off her flashlight. She’s not sure why she does it, it’s not like whoever is waiting inside isn’t expecting her arrival. 

 

“Okay,” Kara turns to Lena as she takes a deep breath, “you stay here while I-”

 

“Kara I am not letting you go in there al-”

 

“Lena.” Kara stops her with a gentle hand squeeze. “It doesn’t make sense for us to both go in and possib- it makes more sense for someone to stay out here to watch if anyone else comes. Or to- ...to run and get help if something goes wrong inside.” 

 

She doesn’t want to say it out loud. That she can’t risk another person - can’t risk Lena - getting hurt because of whatever unresolved family trauma she may have. 

 

If she says it outloud, that means it’s real. The danger she’s about to put herself into. Put Lean into.

 

Lena just nods, scanning the trees near the boathouse, “Ok. I’ll wait just outside the door out of sight.” 

 

Kara hesitates, then takes a few tentative steps toward the dim structure near the water. When she looks back over her shoulder, Lena’s already out of sight. 

 

Nodding to herself, she sets her shoulders and strides toward the boathouse, eating up the distance before the erratic pounding in her chest can convince her otherwise. She notes the whitewashed door’s already cracked open, waiting for her arrival, beckoning her inside. Pausing for a moment, she peers through the door to see only darkness. 

 

With a forced exhale through her nose, Kara pulls the door open and slips into the boathouse.

 

The wholeness of the dark heightens her sensitivity to the sounds around her. Some small rodent in the corner of the room scurries away from her footfalls. The structure itself groans as the waves lap at the stilts and the sound of the water echoes hollowly around her. Wood creaks dryly, despite the cloying damp of the glorified shed. Whatever remains of a long forgotten boat bumps against the slip below with a soft thump, thump .

 

Her eyes adjust as she stands there in the rickety threshold until she can make out an electrical box to her left with a lightswitch. Instinctually, she reaches to flick it on and is unsurprised when it doesn’t work. A faint gust of wind sends the door behind her creaking and wafts the smell of mouse urine and seawater up into her face.

 

Thump. Thump .

 

Turning on her flashlight, Kara finds a work bench beneath the lightswitch, and a battery operated lantern sitting on top. It’s new and untouched by the dust that seems to cover everything else on the benchtop. 

 

Biting her lip as she considers her options, deciding the lantern is better than the limited light her flashlight offers, Kara reaches to flick it on.

 

Thump. 

 

She blinks in the harsh light as her surroundings come into view. Standing in the loft of the boat house, she can see the old workbench clearly - bottles of old engine oil and soiled rags litter the top, and dingy orange gas tanks are stored below. Oars and a mooring buoy lean against one wall. On the other side of the loft, hanging from their rusty hooks, are two adult-sized life jackets beside one smaller, child-sized life jacket. Kara reaches out to touch the stiff, rotted material.

 

There’s a railing that overlooks the sheltered dock below, and a short, steep staircase that leads down to the indoor slip. Another rusty hook hangs above the railing and Kara reaches out to hang the lantern from it, illuminating the lower level of the boathouse.

 

Thump, thump.

 

Below, in the dim light, she can make out the shape of an engine rack and long-corroded boat batteries. An old fishing pole leans haphazardly against the wall, the tackle box beside it on the dock. A yellow mackintosh, faded and ripped, rests where it was draped over a stool. The light reflects off the rippling water of the empty slip.

 

Thump. 

 

Kara’s brow furrows.

 

Thump. Thump. Thump.

 

If the the boat slip is empty, then what’s making the-

 

Thump.

 

Ice floods Kara’s veins as various worst-case scenarios flash through her mind - Jeremiah .

 

Slowly, Kara steps towards the stairs leading down to the dock. The wooden floor creaks beneath her and the blood pumping in her ears drowns out the sound of her boots clunking down each step.

 

She lets out a shallow breath of relief when a quick scan reveals nothing floating in the boat slip.

 

Thump.

 

Her breath catches as her eyes flick to the darkness that stretches below the loft - beyond the reach of the lamp light. Lifting her flashlight, she flicks it on, dimly aware of the way her hand is shaking, and finds nothing in the space beyond.

 

Thump, thump. Thump.

 

And the muffled sound of someone calling out.

 

“Dad!” Kara cries out, desperate to get him to keep making noise so she can find him. To let him know she’s there. 

 

“Dad, it’s me! I’m gonna - I’m gonna get you out of here.”

 

Another thump draws Kara’s attention to an oversized metal footlocker perched next to the boatslip. Rushing over, she drops the flashlight and fumbles with the locks on the trunk, hefting it open with enough force to make the hinges protest.

 

On her knees in the dim light, Kara comes face to face with Jeremiah - bound and gagged, and shoved into the cramped metal case.

 

Alive.

 

“Dad, I’m so sorry,” she watches through blurry eyes as her trembling hand reaches out to pull the cloth from between his teeth. Watches as his eyes go wide in warning. Watches his face contort in panic when a wet metal click sounds from behind her and something cold is pressed to the back of her skull.

 

“Tut, tut,” a deep, nasally voice sounds from behind her, “let’s not jump the gun - so to speak.” The self-satisfied voice wriggles something familiar in Kara’s mind that she can’t quite place.

 

“Why don’t you put your hands on your head and back away, hmm?”

 

Kara does as the voice asks, resting her hands behind her head and stepping back from the trunk.

 

“Turn around, Kara. There’s a good girl.”

 

Slowly, Kara turns around to a gun aimed precisely between her eyes. The fear goes flat as she stares down the metal barrel of a gun. Numb, as time seems to freeze around her. Suspended between this moment and whatever else these next few seconds will bring for her.

 

The numbness snaps from her like a rubberband. Surprise twists her face as she looks past the gun to the man that holds it.

 

“Dr. Nondas?”

 

The man she knew as her therapist lets out a throaty laugh that trickles down Kara’s spine and settles sourly in her stomach.

 

“Sure,” he says, “if that makes you feel better.”

 

“Wha- what are you…?” Kara shakes her head, trying to make any sense of it.

 

The smile tucked into the corner of his mouth looks more like a snarl as he replies, “Over a decade and that thick skull of yours is still as empty as the day they pulled you out of that closet. Pity you’ve got to die for it, but orders are orders.”

 

“I don’t understand.” Kara manages to get out. She can feel frustration stir in her chest at the thought of dying and not even knowing why.

 

“Clearly,” Nondas replies. “Listen. Kara. Here’s the deal. If you cooperate, you die and your dad gets to leave. You make this messy, I kill you and your dad gets tossed into the ocean locked in a box.”

 

Kara knows the truth behind that bargain. Jeremiah will die regardless.

 

The sensitive spot behind Kara’s ear starts to tingle. She clenches and unclenches her jaw. 

 

“And why do I have to die, Dr. Nondas?”

 

“Well, now, Kara,” his gun drops to aim somewhere at her chest. “You’re what would be called a liability. You’ve been allowed to run around with dangerous memories rattling around that head of yours.”

 

“And why would my therapist care about that?” she forces her jaw to relax as she says it.

 

The sharp bark he releases in response lodges something in her throat. Makes her want to reach out and shake him until he tells her everything he knows. But she swallows it down.

 

“Wow. You really don’t get it, do you?” he sounds exasperated and the gun drops lower, almost pointing at the floor, “I’m not a therapist, Kara! I’m your babysitter . I’ve been watching you . Since the day your parents died, I’ve been here to make sure those memories stay lost. And now? Well. Now you need to die.”

 

“Why not just kill me that night? Let me die with my parents?” Kara lets the frustration seep into her voice.

 

He sucks his teeth, admits, “You were meant to. But she didn’t want to kill you.” 

 

“She?” a bitter taste rises to Kara’s mouth. A trickle of sweat runs down her neck.

 

Dr. Nondas scoffs, “Of course - of course your bitch of a mother didn’t tell you about her. Didn’t want her near ‘precious little Kara’. Didn’t want to taint her perfect daughter with the stench of her own sins.”

 

Emotion flares in Kara’s chest, heating her face. She keeps quiet.

 

“The boss wanted you dead. She’s the only reason you’re alive.”

 

Kara blinks through her confusion. She needs to keep him talking. Needs to understand.

 

“Who do you work for?”

 

“There’s a worthwhile question,” he concedes. “But you’ve had me monologuing long enough.”

 

He levels the gun between her eyes once more. Something behind Kara creaks heavily and Dr. Nondas shifts his focus somewhere behind her.

 

“Oh, how fortuitous,” the glee seeps into his voice like oil, “you’ve done me the favor of bringing the Luthor brat with you. Two birds, one stone.”

 

The moment his gun trains on something over her shoulder, time slows for Kara. Or maybe it speeds forward - skipping ahead, leaving more blindspots in the timeline that is her life. She can’t be sure.

 

All she knows is that she’s moving. Hurtling forward. Spinning out of orbit until she collides with the solid mass that is the man in front of her.

 

A voice calls out desperately to her. Were she capable of logic, she’d know it was Lena’s voice. But all she hears is the last cry of a ghost that has haunted her since she was a child - the voice of a mother calling out for her child - before the ringing of a gunshot blots out any sound at all.

 

She feels his head bounce off the rotted wood as she collapses on top of him.

 

Gun . 

 

There’s a gun.

 

She gropes blindly in the darkness. Shoves her elbow into his jaw as she scrambles to untangle from his limbs. There’s a shuffling beyond her grasp, and she can barely make out the sound of metal sliding across wood - splashing into water.

 

She feels his hand on the back of her neck as it slams her face into the floor. Feels warmth flow from her face, down her throat. Her face slams into the floor again.

 

There’s more shuffling, and Kara is distantly aware that someone else is there - Lena. She clearly makes out the sharp sound of a slap and something heavy hitting the floor.

 

Then hands are on her. Rolling her over. Wrapping around her throat. 

 

Kara pries at the hands. Scrabbles, claws at them.

 

Darkness gathers at the edge of her vision. 

 

Kara thought she knew what darkness meant: the night sky far away from the city on a cloudy night. An ocean so deep that sunlight can’t reach.

 

But this darkness - it's different. It’s loud, and it’s angry, and it wants to tear her apart piece by piece.

 

“Kara!” she hears a sob, muffled. Not far from her.

 

Her mother’s last words. The last words she’ll hear. Who else will meet their end with her name on their lips?

 

No.

 

Something kicks in her chest. Recoils so violently at the idea of anyone else dying because of her. 

 

Who else will call her name in a cry?

 

This is not how I want to be remembered .

 

She sends her arms out wide, searching blindly around her. Until her hand finds something heavy. Her flashlight, heavy as a club.

 

Gripping tight, she swings the light up and smashes it into the side of his head, the beam of light flashing in the dark. His grip loosens on her neck as he grunts in pain. 

 

She plants her feet and swings again. This time he lets go to protect his head, and she arches her back and pushes through her feet with all her strength. She swings again, muscles aching, and he rolls away. 

 

His fist collides with her face as she rolls him off of her. It’s awkward and slow and glances off her brow.

 

She kneels over him, swings. The glass of the flashlight cracks. The light flickers. He reaches a hand out to grasp her.

 

She swings again. His hand falls away.

 

And again.

 

Again.

 

She realizes someone’s screaming.

 

Again.

 

“ Kara.”

 

Again.

 

A gentle hand on her shoulder. 

 

She’s surprised to find she’s the one screaming.

 

The hand squeezes, “Kara.”

 

She turns. It’s Lena, crouched beside her. A mark on her cheekbone, already bruising.

 

Kara drops the flashlight to the ground. It flickers out.

 

A broken sob escapes Kara as Lena pulls her into an embrace. Cradling her head - as if she were something precious.

 

She doesn’t know how long she stays there with Lena. Seconds. Minutes. 

 

But she startles to awareness and stiffly crawls from Lena toward the trunk, “ Jeremiah.”

 

She takes in the battered sight of him as she unbinds his hands, legs. Fully removes the gag from his mouth. 

 

“Kara,” he rasps, coughs. Runs his shaking hand through her blood-stained hair.

 

With Lena's help, they’re able to help Jeremiah slowly from the chest and guide him down to a collapsed position on the wooden planks of the floor. Kara falls into his arms as he strokes her back with soothing sounds. Never once letting her go. “It’s alright,” he says. But Kara doesn’t hear him over the ringing in her ears.

 

The police arrive with their bright lights and loud commands. Kara supposes Lena must have called them before entering the boathouse. They clear the scene, verify no threats are present, before making it to where Kara and Lena sit huddled with Jeremiah.

 

“It’s alright, you’re safe now,” an officer says as he approaches.

 

She looks away from the bloody body across the room and doesn’t say anything.

The police had taken their time investigating the scene and interviewing both Kara and Lena before sending them on their way. When she emerges into the kitchen, there are two figures seated at the kitchen table - Kara’s brain too foggy to process who they are. It isn’t until Lena is pushing forward and one of the figures stands up to meet her that Kara realizes it’s Lex opening his arms to tuck his sister beneath his chin.

 

Kara’s focus absently wanders to the second figure at the table making his way to standing. It takes a moment for her tired mind to process the messy black hair, sharp nose, and strong jaw before realization washes over her.

 

“Clark.”

Lex looks up from where he’d been quietly murmuring into Lena’s hair, “We met through work.”

 

Clark shifts in place, “It’s a long story.”

 

Kara’s chest tightens and her headache flares. She crosses her arms, “Can someone tell me what the hell is going on. All of it. Please.”

 

Clark shoves his hands deep into his pockets and shares a glance with Lex before looking over to Agent Jones who inclines his head slightly. “Alright then. We’ll start from the beginning.”

“That’s about right, yeah,” Clark sadly nodded his head.

 

The six of them, Kara, Lena, Clark, Lex, Alex and Agent Jones were sitting at the kitchen table clutching at their respective hot mugs in an attempt to stay awake.

Kara stirs, looking at Lex “You hired a Private Investigator to solve the murder of someone else’s parents?”

Her heart.

No.

She takes comfort in that.

Drawing the gun.

 

Thumbing the safety.

 

Sighting down the gun at-

Another step.

A step.

Two Years Later

Christmastime

 

Kara steps from her therapist's office onto the sidewalk of downtown Metropolis. Turning up the collar of her coat against the biting cold of winter, she raises her face to the sun and breathes deeply. Though it doesn’t provide any heat, the sun is bright and a comfort against the brisk air.

“Let’s go home,” Lena says softly, a soft smile on her lips.

 

And it’s with a content smile of her own that Kara realizes it.

 

She already feels like she’s home.
