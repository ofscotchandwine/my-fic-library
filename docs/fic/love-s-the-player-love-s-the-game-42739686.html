love's the player, love's the game

The thing is, Lena Luthor is competitive.

Like, yell at the referee, demand a rematch, practice in advance for game night competitive. It's a part of her, as surely as her quick wit and her Irish lilt and the freckles dotting her neck and shoulders.

Her competitiveness has reared its head in every relationship she's ever had. She'd competed with Jack to see who could recite Pi to more digits (her), with James over who could lap the Trivial Pursuit board quickest (also her), with Andrea over who could make more money (once again, shockingly, her). Hell, she'd been competing with her brother over everything from patents to hair-to-scalp density since the day she'd first met him.

So, Lena Luthor is competitive. The thing about it, though, is that she's not the only one.

 

When she'd met Kara Danvers, Lena's first thought had been not a threat.

Well. Actually, her very first thought had been something along the lines of eyes lips biceps hair shoulders oh God shoulders, but like, after that. After that, she'd sized up the preppy blonde in her demure little cardigan and decided that, next to the hulking mass of walking mistrust that was Clark Kent, Kara Danvers was not the danger she needed to fear.

The illusion had continued, for a while. Kara – sweet, hopeful, ever-lovely Kara – was nothing like the lipstick-armoured, Chanel-and-razorblade-clad women of the cutthroat circles Lena inhabited. There was never a sense of rivalry between them, never so much as a whiff of backstabbing or double-crossing. Only ever support and encouragement, triumphs shared and losses consoled.

But when Kara Danvers became Kara Zor-El, when the last of the pretence dropped and Supergirl's steel was allowed to shimmer through the sunshine, not a threat sublimated into thin air as surely as those lead-lined glasses.

Kara, Lena soon learns, has a competitive streak a mile wide, and will happily fight to the death for any chance at victory.

It starts small, as everything does. Chess games and Mariokart and who can fit the most marshmallows in their mouth at once. Then, Kara learns to fence under red sun lamps, and after fencing comes poker, then speed-reading and wood carving and rock climbing and ancient Greek and any number of other ill-advised skills they pick up just so there's a new playing field upon which she and Kara can duke it out.

“We can stop if you want,” Kara offers sweetly whenever Lena dares to express her misgivings about the relevance of learning to weave baskets just to see who can do it faster. “You know, if you're afraid to get beat.”

And Lena, who's always taken a challenge like that about as congenially as a red rag to a bull, clenches her jaw and grinds her teeth and sets to weaving her willow so vigorously she almost dislocates a shoulder.

It's fine. It is.  

With Lex gone, Lena’s been without a worthwhile opponent for far too long. She and Kara are evenly matched in everything from skill to dedication to utter unwillingness to back down, and if their newfound competition stokes the fire that's smouldered between them for longer than Lena can remember, well.

That, at least, is nothing new.

 

It's nothing new, until one unassuming Sunday morning, when suddenly it's something very new indeed.

Kara stays the night and they wake tangled up together in hazy sunlight, bleary-eyed and smiling. She orders brunch from the new French patisserie downtown and they eat it in bed, dropping pastry flakes onto the sheets.

"This,” Kara mumbles around a gargantuan mouthful of jam and fruit, “may be the best thing I have ever tasted. Lena, Rao, you gotta try this.”

Which, fine. Kara's desire to spread her culinary enjoyment around is nothing new. What is new, though, is the method of execution.

Without a second thought Kara swipes her thumb through the cream of her millefeuille, holding the offending appendage out in invitation. “Raspberry vanilla,” she prompts, thumb waggling between them. “Come on, try it.”

Lena almost sprays her mouthful of coffee all over the bed. She gapes, wide-eyed, from Kara's face to her cream-topped thumb and back again. Surely, surely she doesn't mean—

Under Lena's panicked gaze, Kara smirks. “What's wrong?” she asks, her voice low suddenly, almost husky. “Afraid to try something new?”

Well. That's a challenge if Lena's ever heard one. And what is she going to do? Back down?

Maintaining eye contact the entire time, Lena leans forward. Wraps her fingers around Kara's wrist, dips her head, and opens her mouth. Her tongue swipes the tip of Kara's thumb, lips sealing around it for just a second longer than strictly necessary.

Kara's breath hitches loudly enough to be audible without superhearing. Cheeks flaming scarlet, a high-pitched squeak escapes her throat.

Satisfied, Lena pulls back. The smirk is on the other foot now, or however the saying goes. If this is just another stage for their ongoing competition, Kara had better remember who she's up against. If she wants to step up their rivalry with a couple rounds of relationship chicken, well. Lena's not just going to let her win.

Plus, Kara’s right. The cream really is delicious.

 

So, at first, Lena assumes it means nothing. That it’s a coincidence; a slight and unintended infraction across the line drawn in the sand between her body and Kara's. A foot into non-strictly-platonic territory. An ankle, maybe. A toe.

Three things happen to make her question that assumption. Three things in as many days that make Lena think, actually. This is no accident. This is a dare.

First: on Friday, Kara's hand lands heavy on Lena’s hip at her foundation charity gala, thumb sweeping back and forth across the skin exposed by her backless dress, gaze roving her body with the rigid horizontal focus of an elevator.

“Lena. You look gorgeous .”

Blushing, fumbling, close to swallowing her own tongue, Lena deflects. “You're just sweet talking me because I had the caterers put potstickers on the menu.”

“I'm saying you look gorgeous because you're absolutely gorgeous.” Kara's tone is stubborn, her gaze never wavering for a moment. One brow arches, an unmistakable challenge. “Are you going to tell me I'm wrong?”

And, okay. That’s— that’s fine.

Second: on Saturday, beneath the corner table of a cosy Italian restaurant laden with linguine and gnocchi, something touches Lena's ankle. She moves her foot instinctively, breaking contact, but the intruder returns undeterred.

Paying no mind to propriety, the pressure – Kara's foot, Lena has expertly deduced, slipped free of her brown leather flats – skates over the ridge of Lena's ankle to her shin, stroking halfway up the length of her calf.

Lena promptly chokes on her own saliva.

The waiter pauses his rendition of the night's specials. “Are you alright, Miss?”

Lena's heart is beating so hard that if she opens her mouth the damn thing might make a break for it, clean out of her throat and out the door.

“She's fine,” Kara answers for her. Her foot strokes the back of Lena's knee, smoothing back down to her ankle in a gentle caress. Blue eyes flicker coyly to her face. Beneath the table, footsie continues. “There's nothing wrong, is there, Lena?”

So. That? That’s less fine.

Third: on Sunday, limited space at movie night finds Kara scrunched up in the corner of the couch, Alex and Nia's impassioned prodding scrunching Lena up practically on top of her.

Half in her best friend's lap, Lena hovers awkwardly, unsure how to best place her weight on the warm body below her, or even if she should at all.

Two hands make the decision for her. One lands heavy atop her thigh, the other snaking round to snag her by the waist, tugging her down solidly.

At Lena’s surprised squeak, a chuckle rumbles through Kara's chest. She's firm and soft and hot beneath her, breath a wildfire against Lena's skin as she whispers in her ear. “This isn't a problem, right?”

And— God. Not even in the ballpark of fine.

It's not that Kara has no respect for consent. On the contrary; Lena knows that if she were to show so much as a flicker of genuine discomfort her best friend would back off in a heartbeat, apologising profusely for the next millennium or so.

No, this is a game, and she and Kara experienced players. This is a challenge between two intimately acquainted competitors who can read the other's desire without it ever being articulated aloud. Kara has stepped up this new facet of competition between them, and so far the opposition has been woefully non-existent.

Well, Lena resolves. Not for much longer. No way is she going to be the one to call chicken first.

 

If beating Kara at this new game means crossing the line in the sand marked platonic propriety, Lena's already loaded herself into the catapult.

If there's one thing she knows, it's competition. So, when Kara goes in for a hug, Lena hooks her thumbs into the waistband of Kara's jeans. When Kara kisses her cheek, Lena kisses the corner of her mouth. When Kara suggests a sleepover for the third night straight, Lena suggests a fourth.

She just, she will not be beaten. Not in this.

And if Lena also happens to be enjoying this closeness, this frisson that's grown from a flickering spark to a veritable electrical storm, well. At least Kara seems to be, too.

The way Kara's cheeks pinken in the face of Lena's sudden boldness, the way her pupils dilate and her throat works and her teeth rake across the plump flesh of her bottom lip— it's not exactly an incentive for Lena to ease up.

She's matching Kara move for move. She's having her cake, and flirting with it too. Everything is going according to plan. And then, Kara asks her to dance.

It's nothing fancy; just a goofy quickstep around her apartment. But the quickstep soon becomes a waltz, which devolves into a languid Bolero, slowing and slowing until they're just swaying in each other's arms in the middle of the living room.

Though the tempo of their movement has eased, the chess game of their bodies continues. Moves, and countermoves.

Kara's arms encircle her waist. Lena's head tilts against her shoulder.

A pause. Two thundering hearts shatter the quiet.

Kara's fingers twine with her own. Lena's thumb strokes the hollow beneath her ear.

A beat of silence. Two. Three.

Kara's palm slips beneath the hem of her sweater. Lena's lips press against her collarbone.

The contact elicits a soft, breathy gasp. Lena lifts her cheek so that they're face to face, both breathing hard.

Somebody's hand cradles somebody's jaw. Someone's thumb smooths along someone else’s lips. Suddenly, nobody's breathing at all.

“We can stop,” Kara whispers, pupils blown wide. Fingertips trace Lena's earlobe. Only millimetres separate them now. “If you want.”

Lena licks her lips. Her mouth almost touches Kara's. “I don't.”

A silent gasp. A hard swallow. “Neither do I.”

Kara's nose brushes her cheek. Lena's eyelids flutter closed. “Because you want to win?”

“Because I want you.”

Oh. Oh. If this is chess, that was checkmate.

Lena bites at the inside of her cheek. Gazes into Kara's eyes as though she might climb inside them, might drown in the truth of her forever. “Is this a game to you? Just something else to win?”

“Of course not.”

Lena quirks a brow. Kara flushes. “Well, maybe the basket-weaving. But never this. Never us .”

The hands at Lena’s jaw cradle her face with tentative tenderness. “I want you, Lena,” Kara hushes, cautious and quiet and full of careful courage. “I guess I just— didn't know how else to show you. You know. In case you didn't feel the same.”

No more moves, no more countermoves. Honesty needs no strategy. Just this once, their game can have two winners.

“I do. God, I do.”

Kara's face breaks into a smile so brilliant it's almost blinding. “I love you, you know?”

Lena beams right back. Tightens her embrace around the woman in her arms; her sun, her world. Her Kara.

“I love you more .”

Kara laughs then, loud and bright and joyful beyond measure. “And here I was thinking that maybe just this once we could call it a draw. Split the prize, as it were.”

“Why?” Lena grins as she closes the final shred of distance at last, kissing her, holding her, loving her. “Afraid to get beat?”
