            She woke again from another bad dream, pacing her apartment through the early hours of the morning as the sun was slow to rise through the wide windows of the loft. The floorboards creaked beneath Kara’s feet and she could feel the restless tension coiled in her muscles, the itch to fly, to run, to exert herself as she pushed her powers to their limits to wear herself out so strong as she raked her fingers through her hair and over her face, rubbing her brow and her lips and pinching the bridge of her nose. 

 

            Still, the images burned into her mind like some horror show movie reel refused to fade, replaying behind her eyelids whenever she came to a stop in her anxious pacing, her heart pounding as her skin covered in a cold sweat and her mouth went dry. It was its own kind of torture, being back from the Phantom Zone, being back in National City, safe and home with her family, all the people she loved, but stuck so deep inside her head with the dreams that still plagued her, even though the Fear Visions had stopped tormenting her. The seeds had been sown though, and Kara relived everything that had been inflicted upon her in her own purgatory from the comfort of her own apartment.

 

            She didn’t tell anyone - they were all still living off the high of rescuing her, of having her back in their fold - and it was a private consequence of her sacrifice that she was slowly coming to realise she would have to bear the burden of for a while yet. Just another event to stifle and ignore as the trauma of it was masked with a bright smile and the responsibility of the world to concern herself with the trifling matter of delving deeper into the things that hurt. 

 

            The nights had now become empty hours of waiting. Waiting for sleep to come, her consciousness scared to fall asleep, knowing what awaited her once it dragged her mind down into the nightmares, waiting for the bad dreams to start once she reached that place, kicking off the blankets and murmuring to the stillness of the apartment, and then when she’d abruptly wake, gasping and drenched in a cold sweat, it became a matter of waiting for the night to end. It wasn’t quite so frightening in the day, the sunlight doing its best to recharge her, although, her lack of sleep was slowing but surely wearing away at Kara’s ability to keep plodding on as if everything was fine. 

 

            Once the sun started sinking and she was left alone, the waiting would start all over again. Nothing could stop them from coming, the thoughts burrowing through her mind to unveil images and scenes of the people she loved, broken and bloody at her feet as she was forced to look at their dead bodies. It wasn’t lost upon Kara that Lena appeared the most often, along with Alex. She refused to acknowledge what that might mean, turning her mind aside from that avenue of thought with the firm insistence that it was any malingering guilt that still nagged at the back of her mind.

 

            They’d barely even begun to patch things up before Kara left, and while Lena had brought her back from the Phantom Zone, holding onto her tightly the moment they came face to face, as if she was never going to let Kara go again, the memories of the Fear Visions, of her green eyes vacant at Kara’s failure to save her, Kara had avoided her. Not to the point where it had become abundantly clear that she didn’t have the strength to face her friends, those fears too fresh, too new, but she could scarcely bring herself to meet Lena’s eyes whenever they found themselves alone together for a few moments. She was oddly grateful for the lingering tension - it gave her an excuse.

 

            Dawn was only a couple of hours off that particular morning, and once she grew tired of pacing, her mind slowing as the adrenaline coursing through her body finally waned and guttered to nothing, Kara boiled some water for tea and turned on the TV. The sound was too loud in the quietness of the morning, the tea too hot, but she sipped it obediently and focused on the sound as she tried to blot out any other stimuli.

 

            By the time the sun was rising outside, the violet horizon lightening to amber as peach puffs of clouds streaked past, her appetite found her. She toasted an entire loaf of bread, wolfing down a few pieces dry as she waited for the next ones to finish, finally having the capacity to spread peanut butter over the rest before she finished off the jar with a spoon. Jittery and still on edge, she showered, the water scalding but not enough to work the kinks out of her tense shoulders, and then buttoned a shirt on and resignedly tucked it into her jeans. With a sigh, she put on the flimsy frames and let the weight of the day fall on her shoulders, wondering how she could keep herself busy for long enough to avoid the haunting artificial memories.

Taking a seat at her desk, Kara logged into the computer and took a sip of the coffee she’d picked up downstairs at Noonan’s, sifting through emails as she tried to get on top of all the things she’d missed. She’d been gone for weeks, and to say it was an adjustment to go back to her regular life, to work and game nights and karaoke at Al’s, was an understatement.

She flew up the stairs, bursting out onto the roof with her cape already unfurling, uneasiness making her stomach feel hollow and nauseous. Throwing herself into the air, senses sharpening as she focused, Kara streaked towards her old friend’s apartment and landed on the balcony.

It was very unlike Lena, who was meticulous and overly cautious with her work.

Crossing over to it, Kara touched the stain and found it dried into the rug, meaning it wasn’t recent. It sent a spike of panic through her, stealing the capacity to breathe as her heart pounded in her chest. How long had she been gone?

            “I’m … fine,” Alex haltingly replied, an anxious edge to the uncertainty in her voice. “Why? What’s wrong?”

            Alex was already agreeing with her before she could finish her thought. “I’ll get everyone to meet at J’onn’s. Just in case. We can all pool our resources to track her down if- if you can’t find her before then.”

Looking around the place, she strained her ears for that familiar sound of Lena’s heartbeat amidst the millions stuffed into the confines of the city, finding it achingly absent as her attention snagged on the laptop on the kitchen counter.

A site was already loaded, the video player waiting, and Kara felt dread thread through her as she pressed play. I should preface this by informing you that the moment you pressed play on this video, it went live on every news station in this city. Right now, this video is being aired on millions of TVs across California. Why, you might be wondering? Well, I wanted the world to bear witness to the failings of the Girl of Steel.”

A part of her wanted to melt the screen where it sat, but she knew whatever game he was playing was one she was hesitant to refuse. It was glaringly obvious that it would involve Lena’s safety, and that was something Kara wasn’t going to gamble with for the sake of her temper.

You’ve forgotten the limits of your powers, the faults of humanity, which you still subscribe to, even while you put yourselves up on these pedestals as a greater than thou act of morality and goodness. That is your hubris. Your confidence in your own abilities has made you believe that you can beat anything. So, I thought I’d test that.”

She hated the drawn-out act of it, and a part of her considered that she should have killed him a long time ago. Perhaps that would be her test, to see if she had the strength to kill Lex once and for all to save Lena’s life.

See, the Trolley Problem is a thought experiment in ethics and psychology that isn’t designed to have a solution. The traditional scenario has a tram or trolley on a set of tracks, leading to a fork. People looked at her with shock and stunned disbelief, mouths falling open as no one moved at the sight of her.             Nodding as another sob worked its way up her throat, Kara dug the heels of her palms into her eyes, a shuddering gasp hitching in her throat as her chest tightened painfully. “I got her. She- she’s safe. I got her.”

 

            Alex tightened her arms around Kara, hugging her fiercely. “It’s okay.”

 

            “No, no, no,” Kara cried into her shoulder. “They’re dead. They’re all dead, Alex. What am I- what did I do?   I let them die. I just-”

 

            “Listen to me,” Alex roughly replied, taking her face in her hands, fingers digging into her face as she shook her slightly, her face hardening. “You did what you had to. Okay? You saved her, you saved Lena .”

 

            “I killed them all,” Kara whispered, her face crumpling with anguish. 

 

            Shaking her again, sharply as her voice hardened, brittle and wavering, Alex gave her an earnest look through bright eyes. “No. That wasn’t you . You didn’t do that to them, do you hear me? That’s not on you - that’s on him.”

 

            Hot tears ran down Kara’s cheeks, slightly salty as they ran over her trembling lips and she made a soft keening sound, a quiet whimper as the breath hitched in her throat. “They were innocent . There were kids. ”

 

            “Stop it!” Alex snapped, “don’t do this to yourself. Please.”

 

            “I should’ve saved them. I should’ve- but it’s … it’s her . What kind of monster does that make me? That I- I would choose for myself- but I couldn’t- not her .”

 

            “I know,” Alex whispered, her voice softening as she swallowed thickly. “But … if you’re a monster, so am I. So is everyone . I would pick Kelly - pick you - over anyone. Without hesitation, okay? We all would pick the people we love.”

 

            Squeezing her eyes shut as she reached up to wipe her face, Kara let out a choked sound of pain. “Love. I love her. I love her, I couldn’t- it wasn’t even a choice.”

 

            With a quiet groan, Alex shifted off her knees, the wooden floor hard and painful beneath her, and sat beside Kara, resting her forehead on her sister’s shoulder as she rubbed her back. “I know. It’s why he did it; he knows what you mean to each other.”

 

            “She will hate me for it,” Kara choked out.

 

            “You saved her-”

 

            “It’s Lena. I just- I put a target on her.”

 

            “She loves you, you idiot,” Alex whispered, weary and resigned. “If it was you … she’d make the same call. She would forgive you for anything. You lied to her for years-”

 

            “This is more than that. People died . Because of me, because of her, because out of everyone on the planet … she’s one of two people I would save over the whole world.”

 

            Kissing her shoulder, Alex sighed, “it’s okay. It’s okay . You did what you had to - sometimes that’s all we can do.”

 

            Another sob worked its way past Kara’s lips as she rocked herself slightly, nursing the pain in her chest. “They’re going to hate me.”

 

            Wrapping her arms around her again, Alex softly exhaled, her sister’s words ringing true as she masked a wince, and she struggled for a moment to find the words without sounding callous. “Can you live with yourself? Can you live with yourself, knowing that Lena’s alive and safe, at the expense of ten lives? It’s harsh, I know it is, but I also know you wouldn’t be able to live with yourself if you’d picked the alternative. It would kill you to lose her, and I know that because it would kill me to lose Kelly. So, I know it’s- it’s going to be … hard, but … can you live with yourself?”

 

            “I- I don’t know,” Kara breathlessly replied, her eyes bloodshot as she clutched her chest tightly, still rocking slightly. “I just- the Fear Visions … I saw her die over and over. All of you. And I couldn’t- if it had been a hundred people, a thousand, more … I couldn’t let her die , Alex.”

 

            “I know, I know.”

 

            Wiping at her cheeks as she blew the air out of her lungs, Kara clapped both hands over her mouth and muffled the strangled whine building in her throat. It was a concentrated effort to get her breaths under control, the cold reality settling in as she numbly sat on the floor while Alex silently watched. Her sister would never know the burden of being a saviour, being an idolised being to the whole world, more than human, and she would never know what it was like to have failed at being more and shown herself as shockingly human and otherworldly callous. 

 

            They would have overlooked that failing, perhaps, if she hadn’t been an alien, but with her capabilities, it would frighten them to be confronted with the true depth of her flaws. It was why she spent so many hours rescuing kittens from trees and helping old ladies cross the street; Kara spent most of her time trying to come off as unthreatening as possible, as if the fact she could carry planes and fly could be smoothed over with a few good samaritan deeds.

 

            “I think … I want to be alone,” Kara hoarsely said after minutes of silence, wiping at her blotchy cheeks as she pressed her lips together.

 

            “No, I’ll stay-”

 

            “Alex, please.”

 

            Her whisper was fraying and desperate, beseeching her sister to afford her this one thing, the time to fall apart in privacy and let the weight of it all crush her until she could find a way to dig herself back out of it. She’d killed before, she’d been here before, learning how to come to terms with her compromised morals and reconcile it with the person she’d thought she was. Never had it been the result of such helplessness. 

 

            Pausing for a moment, Alex reluctantly agreed, nodding and kissing her sister’s forehead before she climbed to her feet. Kara wasn’t the reckless sort to do something stupid as she nursed her pain, and Alex slipped out quietly, leaving her to her all-consuming guilt.

 

            She didn’t move as the afternoon wore on, ignoring the usual sounds of sirens and disaster outside as she found herself unable to help, still sitting in that same spot. Red-eyed and unmoving, she stared blankly ahead, trying to process it all. It had happened so quickly, that choice thrust upon her with only the gut-wrenching panic of seeing Lena dead, of never seeing her again, save for old videos and photographs, consuming her mind. In reality, she’d barely given anyone else a second thought as she focused solely on the feeling of dread at Lena being put in harm’s way once more because of her. Now, it was all catching up with her as her mind slowed and her body reacted, volatile and suffocatingly opposing what she’d done as bile flooded her mouth with its sour taste and her whole body shook with the lingering adrenaline of a panic that just wouldn’t recede.

 

            Kara would have undoubtedly stayed there all night if it wasn’t for the knock on the door a few hours later that shook her from the mind-numbing slideshow of faces she hadn’t managed to save. The sound of an all-too-familiar heartbeat on the other side of the door made her stomach lurch, the urge to be sick so strong that she had to force herself to painfully swallow, trying to suppress it as fresh tears pricked her eyes.

 

            “Kara, I know you’re in there,” Lena called, her voice slightly muffled through the wood. “I- I need to talk to you. I just … I want to make sure you’re okay.”

 

            Rising to her feet, Kara was at the door in a flash, but she didn’t open it. Standing there, tears spilling down her cheeks, she rested her forehead against the wood with a gentle thud and pressed her hand, fingers splayed, against it as if she could feel Lena through it. Her heartbeat was strong and a little too fast, and very much alive. It was the only sliver of relief that Kara could feel amidst the sea of awfulness roiling in her chest and she pressed her lips together to suppress the building sob.

 

            “Kara,” Lena said, her voice softening as if she somehow knew there were scant inches separating them via cheap wood and a couple of flimsy locks. “Open the door.”

 

            Jerking back, Kara flashed back across the room, curling up on the sofa as she covered her face with her hands. Self-loathing was an awful thing and Kara wanted to lose herself in it, deserving of everything horrible thing her mind said to her, but that was bearable, at least. What she couldn’t bear was the thought of letting Lena in and seeing the accusation, the repulsion in her green eyes, every thought in Kara’s head written on her face and said in her voice as she lay the blame at her feet. Lena would think she deserved to die over the other ten, she was selfless enough to make that sacrifice, but Kara was the one to make the call, and Kara was selfish. She always had been; that selfishness had just always aligned with saving everyone else.

 

            She sat and listened to Lena’s heartbeat outside for the better part of an hour, the knocking getting louder and more frustrated as her voice hardened. Eventually, a neighbour opened their door down the hallway to engage in a colourful shouting match before Lena left and Kara could breathe through the loosening in her chest. 

 

            Still, keen loneliness knifed through her as Lena’s heartbeat retreated, leaving Kara to face the long night alone. Exhaustion from a lack of sleep and the emptiness of spent tears left her slipping away into nightmares as she stretched out on the sofa, deep blue bleeding into the black beyond the windows. But before long, the nightmares startled Kara awake, covered in a cold sweat and breathing quick and sharp as she looked around, still half in her dreams. 

 

            They’d been of Lena, of course, her face stiff and grey and her eyes vacant in the way that only death makes them so. Rubbing at her eyes, she was greeted by the early hours of the morning, the city as quiet as it ever was, Kara made the daring decision to turn on the TV and switch to a channel replaying the day’s news. It was with masochistic resignation that she sat and watched, flipping through the channels as she watched news anchors relay what had happened, making it past the local channels and then onto national ones and then international, French and Arabic and Spanish incomprehensible to her, but the meaning the same; the world had no place for a Supergirl who wouldn’t comply with what was expected of her.

 

            Once she’d flicked through every conceivable channel, she fetched her laptop and spent hours scrolling through the news, every online media outlet that dealt with celebrities, politics and general stories had thrown up articles about her. All of them offered scathing condemnations of her actions - save for a few that raised the difficult question of what others would have done with their loved one on the line, although it didn’t make Kara feel better to see - and the worst part were the comments. Within hours, she found petitions for the federal government to do something, for the world’s armies to contain her and her cousin, arguments for them to be imprisoned or shot back into outer space. They didn’t care that she’d just come back from there and saved the planet from Leviathan. All they cared about was the here and now and their expectations of her.

 

            Dawn was almost breaking by the time her laptop died, and she sent a text message to Andrea claiming to have mono, saying that she wouldn’t be in. It was an excuse that would buy her days, if not weeks, and Kara thought it might take even longer at that. She didn’t want to move ever again, didn’t want to leave the apartment, so ashamed and wrapped in her guilt as she was. Even as Kara, she was sure her actions would be written on her face, would have marred her soul and left a black mark on it, a burning sign for all to see.

 

            For the duration of that day, she stayed on the sofa, watching daytime TV and eating anything she stumbled upon in the cupboard. It became her process for the next few days, her front door locked to anyone who tried to coax her out as she stayed in the same pair of pyjamas, wallowing as she ate her way through pints of ice cream, a loaf of bread smothered with peanut butter and the wine and beer that did nothing to blunt the grief. And when the milk ran out, black coffee and handfuls of dry cereal from the box, until she’d eaten her way through the tins of peaches that had been waiting to be baked into a cobbler and the jars of pickles and stale cookies. Once the food was gone, her vast appetite leaving a bottomless hole of hunger in her, she was left to sit there, watching the news and her movies as her stomach growled and ached. And still, she didn’t move, didn’t even consider venturing outside, that world seeming out of bounds to her, a hostile place for her.

 

            Alex came around every day, bypassing the door with the spare key she had, barging in with an air of frustration as she tried to get Kara to talk to her. Aside from vague mutterings about how she was fine and she didn’t want to go outside, Kara didn’t offer much conversation. She wasn’t pushy and Kara loved her all the more for it, despite feeling undeserving of her empathy.

 

            Kara loved her sister, but it was always a relief when she finally left again, after sitting through some episode of a TV show - or two or three if Alex was especially concerned - keeping up a running commentary to fill the silence. Then she’d unwind and wolf down whatever Alex had brought her before returning to the dent she was slowly but surely wearing into the sofa from her long hours spent sitting there. Wallowing in her self-pity, she didn’t want to be disturbed.

 

            Her friends were patient though, and two weeks of isolation dragged around as Kara whittled away the hours, reading new articles and rereading old ones, watching as the news cycle picked up new stories and Supergirl’s absence was scornfully speculated. Two weeks was too short a time to nurse the wounded parts of her soul, but it made her feel somewhat better - perhaps vindicated as she indulged in a sort of penance - but as two weeks slipped into its third, the patience wore thin for one billionaire whose calls and messages had gone unanswered, and whose questions had been greeted with lies for years beforehand. The cold shoulder after such an event was more than Lena could take after so many years of being in the dark, and when she showed up at Kara’s apartment on a Wednesday evening, Kara’s stomach twisted with sickening panic at the sound of her heartbeat.

 

            “Kara,” Lena called out, banging her fist on the door, “I know you’re in there. And I’m not leaving until you open the door.”

 

            Kara’s throat constricted as she rose from the sofa, her hair lank and greasy, yesterday’s pyjamas rumpled and a pale hollowness to her face. She was caught in a place of sleeping to pass the day and staying awake to stave off the nightmares; it just left her permanently exhausted. Even if she’d wanted to see Lena, she didn’t have the strength for it, as slumped and tired as she was.

 

            “Kara!” Lena barked, her palm slapping against the wood as her frustration came to a boil. “Open the fucking door. You can’t just ignore me, and I’m not going anywhere until you talk to me, okay? I mean it. I’m just- I’m sick of you shutting me out! You lied to me for years, so you don’t get to be mad at me! You don’t get to freeze me out when I should be the angry one! I thought we were past all of that.”

 

            The pain in Lena’s voice, the doubting uncertainty that made her words grow quieter, gently cracking towards the end, made Kara wince as she closed her eyes. Wearily crossing the room at a human’s pace, she pressed her fingertips to the painted wood and licked her dry lips, voice hoarse from disuse when she spoke.

 

            “I’m sorry,” Kara softly replied.

 

            The door shuddered with the bang of Lena’s fist. “Sorry isn’t going to cut it. Open the door, damn it!”

 

            “I- I can’t. I’m sorry, but … I can’t see you.”

 

            “Kara, I swear to God-“

 

            “I’m not good for you, Lena,” Kara said, strangled as her throat tightened painfully and her eyes stung with tears. “You’re better off forgetting I even existed; I’m not a good person and you … you deserve better than that, so …”

 

            “Bullshit,” Lena hissed, although there was a defeated note to the word, a plea, a reluctance to accept what Kara was saying.

 

            Lena was too stubborn for her own good, which had put them at odds more times than Kara cares to admit, their egos clashing in a battle of pride. Perhaps she should have been more forgiving at times, instead of the contempt she’d saved for her, unable to bend her Kryptonian spine when she’d known that Lena was only doing what she’d thought was right. It was one of Kara’s many regrets, but it had never put Lena in harm’s way before. Not to this extreme. Kara would put a stop to it now.

 

            “I’m sorry.”

 

            With a huff of anger, Lena rattled the door handle, “just open the door. I’m not above breaking and entering and I’m not sure you want a busted door. I’ll … buy the whole building and walk right in with a key. Seriously, I will. I’ll-“

 

            Sighing as she suppressed a laugh, the feeling of warmth in her chest feeling so wrong as Lena’s ridiculousness made her face spasm with amusement, Kara yanked the door open before she could finish speaking.

 

            “I don’t doubt that you would,” she gravely replied, her expression blank and eyes shifting around as she refused to meet Lena’s gaze.

 

            Seizing her opportunity, Lena brushed past her and Kara quickly stepped back to avoid the feeling of her against her. Exhaling softly, Kara shut the door and rubbed at her forehead as she looked at the floor, socks scuffing along the floorboards as she waited for the inevitable. She’d tried putting it off, delaying this conversation, the confusion and anger and regret that would come with it, but now it was here and she wasn’t any more prepared for it than she’d been that first day when she’d dumped Lena at the hospital and left.

 

            “Well … this is uncharacteristically messy of you,” Lena noted as her eyes swept around the apartment, taking stock of beer and wine bottles, empty takeout containers and clothes and the gloominess of the closed curtains. 

 

            “I’ve been … busy.”

 

            Lena let out a snort of laughter, “busy avoiding me?”

 

            “Yes.”

 

            The truth seemed to surprise Lena, who paused, looking at her with confusion crumpling her face. Kara could see it from the corner of her eye as she looked past her, fiddling anxiously with her fingers as her stomach swooped and her heart pounded in her chest.

 

            “You didn’t have to do that,” Lena murmured, her voice faintly cracking. “You could’ve- you think that I- that I wouldn’t have understood?”

 

            Cheeks flushing pink as she ducked her head, it took Kara a moment to make her throat work, swallowing thickly as her tongue stuck to the roof of her dry mouth.

 

            “No, I don’t think you would have.”

 

            Scoffing, Lena’s lips creased as she drew them into a sour look, arms folded over her chest. “You know, you have a bad habit of just assuming what I can and can’t handle. He’s my brother . My brother. I know how it feels better than anyone to be manipulated by his little games. I wouldn’t have- you could’ve talked to me.”

 

            Squeezing her eyes shut, Kara screwed the hem of her shirt in her hands as she twisted the fabric, the panic building in her chest as she thought of how the events had played out. It was shameful and even now she couldn’t look at Lena.

 

            “No, I couldn’t have. You don’t know that I- if you knew why I- I did it … but I know you; I know you’d sacrifice yourself for anyone. So … maybe I’m just not as selfless or good as you.”

 

            “Why’d you do it?” Lena quietly asked as Kara evaded her eyes, unable to meet them. Afraid to. “Kara, look at me.”

 

            Reluctantly, fighting against every part of her that refused to do so, Kara raised her head. Her blue eyes were sad and guilty and hollow, her skin paling a few shades as shame and fear took root in her heart.

 

            “Why’d you do it?” Lena repeated.

 

            Face crumpling as her eyes silently pleaded, Kara exhaled sharply, her body sagging with the weight of her burden. “You have to know why.”

 

            “I don’t,” Lena said, her brow creasing as her expression clouded. “All those people who were doing good things; I lied to you, I hurt you.”

 

            Kara gave her a thin smile, rubbing the back of her neck as she averted her gaze, out the window where a streetlight gave the blue night an orange tint. “You do good things too - you’ve saved me as much as I’ve saved you. I think … the hurt was mutual, so …”

 

            “You-” Lena started before quickly cutting herself off, swallowing as she stared at her, mystified. “You can’t just- that’s not a good enough reason.”

 

            With a breathless laugh, choked and slightly strangled, Kara pinched the bridge of her nose. “It’s the only one I’ve got.”

 

            “You’re lying,” Lena tightly replied, her face stiffening as she clenched her hands into fists. “Stop lying all the time; just … tell me the truth. Please. For God’s sake, Kara, I deserve the truth.”

 

            With a rough sigh, Kara’s shoulders sagged, “you already know the truth.”

 

            Shaking her head, Lena gave her a yearning look. “I want to believe I do, but … I’m not sure that I do. I mean … maybe I’m wrong.”

 

            Lips twitching ruefully, Kara allowed a brief smile as she cracked the knuckles of her left hand, eyebrows rising and falling quickly in an amused manner. “You and I both know you’re never wrong.”

 

            “Well … then I want to hear it from you,” Lena stubbornly insisted.

 

            Softly groaning, Kara rubbed a hand over her face, tense and annoyed and wallowing in her misery and heartache. It could’ve been simple - it should’ve been - but she didn’t know how to explain her failings to Lena without exposing the horrific side of her that would turn her away. Could one truly love a monster? Someone capable of such selfish greed and egocentric callousness. It went against nature that one person should have been allowed to have the power to decide who lived and died, and Kara was still trying to come to terms with the consequences of her actions.

 

            “Because I-“ Kara started, the words petering out to silence as she bit the inside of her cheek, a rueful look of reluctance on her face. “Because I- it’s because I-“

 

            “Come on,” Lena murmured, coaxing and gentle but unrelenting. “Just say it. It’s just three words, Kara.”

 

            The fact that Lena knew was already mortifying enough, but to have her standing there, insisting Kara say it, was all the more awful. Kara could feel her cheeks redden, frustration and embarrassment clamouring inside her until she closed her eyes and let the words come pouring out of her.

 

            “Because I love you! I love you. I love you. I would kill any number of innocent people - hundreds, thousands - to save you. I’d kill them with my bare hands. I’d sacrifice anyone, anything, to keep you safe, okay? Is that what you want to hear? That there is no boundary on this earth, in this universe, that I would not and have not crossed to keep you safe, because I’ve seen the alternative, I’ve seen you die, over and over again, in dreams and nightmares and alternate realities, and I would do anything to stop it from happening. Because I love you, and I’ve always loved you. But what does that say about me? That I can be brought to my knees, brought to the brink of such destruction and monstrosity by the one person who makes me feel so strong? For you, I can do things I didn’t think I was capable of, and that’s … that’s not good, Lena. I’m not a good person; there’s so much wrong with what I can do, what I have and would do, for you.”

 

            Lena was silent, stunned and wide-eyed as Kara’s sudden outburst softened to a pleading murmur, her blue eyes full of tears as she swayed for a moment as if to reach out and touch her, before shrinking away. It was as if she couldn’t bear to touch Lena, as if she wasn’t worthy of it, was too dangerous for it. She didn’t know that Lena would do unspeakable things for the privilege of being touched by those hands, that she’d be willing to do just as much for the simple act of pressing her lips against Kara’s and calling her hers.

 

            With a weary sigh, Kara ran a hand over her face, looking haggard and haunted as grief bowed her shoulders and her eyes welled up with regret. “There is no world worth saving without you in it.”

 

            The breath hitched in Lena’s voice as her face softened, and she reached out across the space separating them, willing Kara into her arms, although the Kryptonian remained stubbornly rooted to the spot.

 

            “Then … come here.”

 

            “No, I can’t.”

 

            “Kara-”

 

            “How could I deserve you after doing that?” Kara cried, her head ducked down and her cheeks red with shame as she closed her eyes, fingernails gouging into her palms as she grit her teeth.

 

            “Because … because you and I are the same,” Lena blurted out, frustrated and desperate.

 

            It gave pause to Kara’s wallowing as she raised her head, meeting her eyes, confused and apprehensive. “No,” she rasped, “no, we’re not.”

 

            Quickly stepping forward, reaching out to grasp Kara’s hand, Lena gave her an insistent look, her fingertips lightly brushing Kara’s cheek as she swallowed thickly. Her green eyes were bright with fervour, a muscle twitching in her jaw, and there was a stubborn set to her shoulders as she softly replied.

 

            “Yes, we are. I would burn down the whole world for you,” Lena admitted, her fingers stilling on Kara’s cheek as she gently urged her to meet her eyes again. “If the world isn’t worth saving without me in it … then I don’t want to save it if you’re not here with me.”

 

            Kara’s eyes burned with tears as she turned her head aside, her cheek feeling cold with the absence of Lena’s fingers. Her lips trembled as she struggled to speak, her words a ragged whisper. “How can you even stand to look at me? How can you-”

 

            “Because I love you,” Lena fiercely whispered, grabbing her cheeks in her hands and urging her face up to meet her eyes.

 

            Kara’s lips trembled as the breath caught in her throat, gently pulling herself from Lena’s gentle grasp. “No,” Kara whispered, her fingers encircling Lena’s wrist to pull her hand away. “No, don’t say that. Don’t say that. You can’t- you’re not supposed to love me - not after everything- especially not now. ”

 

            A small laugh escaped Lena as she smiled, her brow furrowing as her eyes creased at the corners with bewildered amusement. “I’ve loved you for years. You think I’d stop now, after you saved my life again?”

 

            Dropping her wrist, Kara covered her eyes with a hand, shoulders tense as she backed away. “I killed those people. I killed them - me.”

 

            “That wasn’t you.”

 

            “It was. I could’ve stopped them but I didn’t. I picked you. And I can’t ever let myself be put in that situation again, do you understand me?”

 

            She opened her eyes as she dropped her hand, slowly backing away further, until she hit the door and grasped the knob, turning it and cracking the door an inch.

 

            “You need to leave. I can’t- I’m sorry, I just … can’t.”

 

            “Please don’t,” Lena blurted out, her face taut and a look of pain in her eyes as she looked at Kara. 

 

            It caught Kara off guard. This was Lena, who rarely asked for anything, who would settle for scraps of kindness, because it was more than she was used to. Asking for her friend - did that word still apply how? - not to shut her out, not to leave her. But how did they move forward from this now? Kara had only been as honest as she had been, under the assumption that she would have it in her to cut Lena off, and Lena would love herself enough to not plead her case. It was harsh to push her to that point, but Kara was more surprised that she hadn’t closed herself off already - perhaps she wasn’t the only one who had changed since being sent to the Phantom Zone. 

 

            Tears blurred Kara’s vision as she drew in a shuddering breath, angrily wiping at her eyes as frustration made her impatient.

 

            “Didn’t you hear what I said? There’s no world worth saving without you in it, and if- if I let you stay … you won’t survive me. I can’t protect everyone.”

            “What did you see?”

 

            Swallowing, Lena’s lips twisted ruefully, “I was drowning. That’s what I’m afraid of.”

            “Because you can save everyone if it means you don’t save me,” Lena whispered, “and that’s okay. I don’t want you to compromise yourself to save me. I didn’t- I thought you were going to save them.”

            “This is what I was talking about,” Kara murmured, slowly peeling her fingers off Lena’s shoulders and letting her hands hang back down at her sides. “You have more faith in me to make the selfless choice than I do. Turning away, she made for the door, and even though it’s what Kara had said she wanted, she found herself moving in a blur, blocking Lena’s path and holding her hands out before her to stop her in her tracks as she winced apologetically.

 

            “I didn’t mean it like that. I’m sorry. I just- I meant that … you’re so much more than just one girl. I would pick you over anyone, again and again. And I-“

 

            “I know,” Lena muttered, angry and annoyed as she rubbed at her forehead. “I get it, okay, and that’s- that’s fine. If that’s what you want then … well, we both know you’ll put that first.”

 

            She gave Kara a bitter smile, a wounded look in her eyes that sent a stabbing feeling through Kara’s chest. Sharply exhaling, Kara wiped tears off her cheeks as she swallowed thickly.

 

            “Don’t say it like that,” she whispered, her face crumpling. “I’m not- I’m not putting myself first. I’m putting you first, even if you can’t see it. That’s all I’ve ever done.”

 

            Blinking back her own tears, Lena’s eyes creased at the corners as she smiled thinly. “No offence, Kara, but I’m sick of you choosing for me. I don’t give a shit if you think you’re doing what’s best for me; that’s not how it feels, and that’s not good enough for me.”

 

            “I’m s-“

 

            “Stop it,” Lena snapped, anger brightening her tearful eyes as she pointed a finger at Kara in a warning. “Stop apologising for things fully in your control, okay? I don’t want to hear it. You can’t just … treat someone like this and think a sorry will make up for it, will … make it hurt less because it won’t. I love you and I get it because I would do anything to protect you too, but I also would never choose for you. So, maybe that’s the difference between you and me, but … stop making excuses for what you do. You either love me enough to trust my own mind, my own feelings, or you really don’t give a shit what I think and are fine with breaking my heart just so you can stroke your own ego for doing the right thing. ”

 

            “That’s not fair,” Kara choked out, “you know there’s a middle-ground there-“

 

            “Then find it!” Lena exclaimed, taking a step forward. “Find it and tell me where to meet you, because I can’t- I can’t lose you, any more than you can lose me. I would literally rather die than live with this- this loneliness. I don’t have anyone else, Kara, so you think you’d be saving me, but really I’d rather be loved by you for just a minute than live until eighty with all this … bitterness. It’s killing me; it would just take a hell of a lot longer than a couple of bombs.”

 

            Kara rubbed at her eyes and sighed heavily, her shoulders drooping as she ducked her head. “Why are you making this harder than it has to be? I just want to protect you.”

 

            “This isn’t protecting me, this is- this is making me miserable! And for what? I’m going to die, Kara! Eventually, I’m going to die, and I’d rather not spend my whole life just … heartbroken by someone I never even had the chance to love. So, I’m sorry that you’re grieving and that you’ve been hurt by my brother, but I don’t care what you did and I’m not leaving so …”

 

            “How can you not care? You said it yourself; you thought I was going to save them.”

 

            Blinking back tears as her voice turned gravelly, Lena exhaled sharply, “I’m alive because you didn’t and … it feels wrong, it feels like I’m not deserving of getting to live and just … move on when they were good people. But I’ve blamed myself for my brother’s actions for years and I refuse to blame myself - or you - for this one, okay. I can’t- I can’t do it by myself. I know you’re hurting because it hurts me too, because if- they would still be alive if I didn’t exist. So, if you’re going to blame yourself, you have to blame me for not giving in to one of the multiple assassination attempts before this, because it’s my fault too. So … that’s all there is. I can’t change it, and I can either hurt alone or we can do it together.”

 

            Kara swallowed, chewing on the inside of her cheek as she twisted the hem of her shirt in her hands. Her stomach twisted uncomfortably and her heart ached. Every inch of her fought against her better judgement as her mind prevailed against her heart, and she knew that there was only so long she could hold out for before caving. It wasn’t that she wanted to cut Lena off, only that it would be better, and after her recent mistake with selfishness, surely it was better to do this one selfless act that would save more than just one life. 

 

            “Baby, please,” Lena murmured, a quiet whine in her voice as she reached out and grabbed Kara’s wrist, shocking her into meeting her gaze. Kara’s lips parted as she took in the agonised plea in Lena’s eyes, on the verge of heartbreak. “I hate begging for anything,” Lena said as her voice broke and face spasmed, “but please don’t do this.”

 

            Reaching up, Kara cradled her face in her hands and touched her forehead to Lena’s. They stood like that for a moment, the tension thick as Lena braced herself for rejection and Kara delayed giving it, warring with herself. Pulling back, she sighed faintly and gently kissed Lena’s brow before dropping her hands and turning away.

 

            Lena let out a cold laugh, hollow and disbelieving. “No. I- I refuse to accept this.”

 

            Wincing as she rubbed her brow, Kara didn’t respond as she collapsed down onto the sofa, leaning against the pillows as she closed her eyes. She couldn’t bear to tell her to go, but she assumed Lena’s wounded pride would provide a quick exit that meant she’d never have to speak the words that would break her heart more. Perhaps a small part of Kara was convinced that if she didn’t deliver that final blow, Lena would forgive her someday. She already had enough reasons to hate Kara; one more would be the final nail in the coffin, and she was reluctant to hammer it in herself.

 

            Only, Lena surprised her with a fit of stubbornness, her teeth audibly grinding together as she angrily pulled her coat off and moved over to the rack. Hanging it up alongside Kara’s, she tutted and muttered incomprehensible things beneath her breath as she moved into the kitchen. Kara glanced up and watched as Lena started rolling the sleeves of her sweater up, nearing the piles of empty dishes, and embarrassed panic rippled through her.

 

            “Don’t- leave it!”

 

            “Well, I’m not leaving so I should at least make myself useful until you get your head out of your ass and realise you’re being ridiculous.”

 

            “Lena-“

 

            “Sit down.”

 

            Withering at the coldness of Lena’s glare thrown at her, Kara sank back down onto the sofa as her mouth fell open and eyes widened a fraction. She didn’t move or look away the entire time Lena washed her dishes, up to her elbows in the soapy water as she gingerly scrubbed plates that had been sitting there for a week. 

 

            Afterwards, she moved around and started picking up Kara’s dirty clothes from the floor, but that was too much. Flustered, Kara darted around and snatched up each thing before Lena could lay her hands on it, both of them finding themselves at a standoff as they scowled, Kara holding an armful of her clothes as her cheeks reddened. Dumping them in the laundry basket, she sighed heavily as Lena started lugging the basket towards the door, and her heart lurched in her chest as she watched her step outside and make her way down to the basement to put on a load of washing for her.

 

            Wincing with guilt, Kara locked the door behind her as her stomach plummeted, and she settled back down onto the sofa, hugging her knees to her chest as she brooded. It was petty and awful, but it was the only conceivable way she would get Lena to leave without forcibly removing her. When she heard her return, the guilt magnified tenfold and she closed her eyes as she listened to Lena try and turn the handle.

 

            A moment later the sound of a key scraping in the lock and the door opening made Kara blink in surprise, a stunned look on her face as she rose from the sofa with her lips parting. Lena gave her a pointed look as she shoved Kara’s keys back into her pocket, a look that said she would have to try harder to fool her, and Kara sullenly sank back down onto the sofa to sink back into her brooding.

 

            Even that she couldn’t do peacefully, Lena dragged out the old vacuum and noisily moved it around the place as it sucked up crumbs, before she changed the sheets on Kara’s bed and disappeared downstairs to put them on and put the other load in the dryer. Kara didn’t bother locking the door this time; she was smart enough to recognise when she’d been beaten by a Luthor’s stubborn determination.

 

            Still, she didn’t offer up any conversation or help as she pretended to be watching the TV while she tracked Lena’s movements back and forth in the apartment. She emptied the trash and scrubbed the bathroom and mopped the floors, tidied up the mess of empty liquor bottles and scattered notes from work that Kara had crumpled and tossed aside in anger. 

 

            It was dinner time when Lena opened the fridge and found it bare, and set about repurposing Kara’s work computer for grocery shopping, an overwhelming amount of food showing up an hour later. Kara’s stomach growled with hunger, the days sustained solely on Alex’s late-night takeout not enough to fill the hole of hunger within her. But she was stubborn too, and feigned indifference as she listened to Lena fill the fridge and the cupboards with more groceries than Kara had ever bought in her life - which was saying something.

 

            Following was the sound of a knife steadily chopping and the clanging of pots and pans and then sizzling onions and boiling water. Kara shifted uncomfortably as the weight of Lena’s presence started becoming unbearable, unable to ignore her as she turned on the radio and hummed quietly to herself. It felt too domestic, too normal, and Kara felt wrong to sit there amidst it.

 

            Leaving the food to simmer, Lena moved around and threw open all of the curtains and cracked the windows, the smell of cooking having already chased away the fetid smell of rotting garbage, but leaving the air still feeling close and stale. A cool breeze rushed in and cut through the stifling feeling brewing in the air, and Kara welcomed its cold touch as she found herself growing more and more flustered with Lena’s presence.

 

            “Dinner,” Lena eventually said, after hours of silence, placing a wide bowl in Kara’s lap.

 

            It was chicken carbonara, the heaped serving steaming as Kara reached out and took it. Her face spasmed slightly, guilt and shame and gratitude conflicted within her, and she swallowed.

 

            “Thanks.”

 

            Lena just nodded, opening a bottle of wine and fetching two glasses. She joined Kara, sitting on a different seat but nursing her own bowl of pasta while she filled both glasses and sipped her wine. The tension was awful, and Kara kept silently pleading for her to leave and just forget about her. She hadn’t planned on Lena sticking around to chew her out for trying to bail on their relationship - platonic or otherwise - again, and she was quiet and remorseful as she ate.

 

            “There’s more,” Lena murmured as Kara’s fork clattered against the rim of her empty bowl.

 

            Kara nodded but didn’t move, and with a sigh, Lena snatched her bowl from her and went to refill it before settling back down with the rest of hers. Blinking back stinging tears, Kara drew in a rattling breath.

 

            “Thank you.”

 

            She meant it for more than just the food but Lena didn’t show any signs of acknowledging the deeper meaning as she dipped her head slightly and drained her glass of wine. Refilling it quickly, she drained almost half of the new one and Kara blinked in surprise. Of course, she knew Lena had a bad habit of drowning herself in drink whenever things went awry with her family, but she didn’t ever think she was someone who would drive her to it. Kara wondered how many nights Lena had spent drowning her sorrows with wine and whisky when they weren’t talking and felt that all-consuming guilt flare back up.

 

            Afterwards, Kara made the effort to wash the dishes, a display of capability as Lena watched her from the corner of her eye and dried them before putting them away. She expected that to put an end to their night, with Lena giving up on her efforts and going home to her own apartment, but Kara was sorely mistaken. Taking her seat on the sofa again, Lena dutifully started finishing off the rest of the bottle, while Kara stared blankly at her for a minute, before Lena glanced up at her and she sank down onto the sofa, silently stewing.

 

            Ten o’clock rolled around as the tension built, Lena consuming a second bottle of red wine and the TV playing a movie they were both only pretending to watch. Eventually, Kara’s eyes started itching from a lack of sleep and her patient determination was giving way to miserable irritation.

 

            “Shouldn’t you be getting home?” Kara finally rasped, her voice gravelly from disuse. “You have work.”

 

            Scoffing, Lena gave her a contemptuous look, “my brother stole my business; now that you’re back, I’ve got too much free time on my hands. I’m good.”

 

            “Lena-”

 

            “Until you tell me you don’t love me, until you tell me that we will never have each other - not even the slightest chance of it happening - I am not leaving. Call the cops, tell them I’m trespassing, I don’t care. I’m not leaving you again; every time one of us leaves … it gets bad again.” Lena’s voice cracked as she swirled her glass of wine in hand, head ducked down with a torn look on her face. “Don’t you see that? We’re better together; everything just … works, and it doesn’t hurt as much.”

 

            Her eyes were glassy when she looked up at Kara, a crease forming between her brows as she gave Kara a mystified look. 

 

            “Why won’t you let me help you?”

 

            Squeezing her eyes shut, Kara swallowed, her mouth dry as her stomach clenched and her patience started wearing thin. She was tired and grieving and heartbroken, even as she did it to herself with her aloofness, and she shot to her feet, pinching the bridge of her nose.

 

            “I’m tired,” Kara wearily replied, deflating as her hand fell limply back to her side. “I’m tired of this; I’m going to bed. Goodbye, Lena.”

 

            Rounding the sofa, Kara walked through to the partitioned bedroom and yanked the covers back. She expected Lena to take the hint this time, but aside from grabbing the lights and plunging the apartment into mostly darkness, save for the flickering lights of the TV, Lena remained on the sofa, working her way through the rest of the bottle.

 

            Determined to freeze her out, if only for the grim relief of knowing that Lena would finally be safe from being a target because of Kara, she ignored her as she pulled a pillow over her head and shut her eyes. Drowning out the sound of the TV, Kara let her exhaustion lure her away to sleep without a fight this time, gratefully sinking into it with open arms if only to escape the train wreck of an evening with the woman she loved more than her own happiness.

 

            She traded one nightmare for another though, sinking into the place of dreams of death, of lifeless eyes and broken bodies and Lena so cold and still. It was infinitely worse than breaking the heart of the living version of her, and Kara was torn from her nightmares with a ragged breath. 

 

            Her shirt clung to her clammy skin and she looked around, wild-eyed and frantic for a moment, the hour late and disorienting as she was overwhelmed with the pounding of a heartbeat. Her favourite sound. Relief slammed into her so hard that she was weak at the knees as she tore from her bed, a lurching dash, quicker than a human could comprehend. And there was Lena, sprawled on the sofa, her cheeks blotchy as if she’d cried herself to sleep, the second bottle of wine empty and her jeans uncomfortable as she awkwardly slept.

 

            The breath was expelled from Kara’s lungs with such force that a small cry escaped her too, and some unconscious part of Lena’s mind was alerted to her presence. Her eyelashes fluttered and she jerked awake as she became aware of Kara’s shadowed form standing over her, kicking out reflexively and upsetting the empty wine glass. A few specks of red wine splattered across the rug and wooden floor and Lena’s heart pounded as she shrank back against the sofa, eyes wide and wild.

 

            Dropping to her knees before her, Kara clutched at the front of Lena’s sweater as she buried her head in her lap and shook with silent sobs. Gritting her teeth as she pressed her lips firmly together, refusing to let a sound escape her, Kara let the relief knife through her terror as she clutched at Lena, reassuring herself that she was there. She was alive. It was only a nightmare.

 

            “Look at me,” Lena whispered, her hands splayed over Kara’s back as she watched with unabashed surprise as the Kryptonian sobbed into her lap.

 

            Shaking her head, Kara made a pitiful sound. “I can’t.”

 

            “Look at me.”

 

            The urgency in her quiet voice made Kara raise her head, eyes swimming with tears and her bottom lip trembling as her lips parted to draw in a shuddering breath. “I don’t want to lose you.”

 

            “I’m right here,” Lena reassured her, leaning forward, so close as she hunched over, her hands moving to cup Kara’s cheeks, that their foreheads fell together. “I’m not going anywhere. I love you, okay? And I know you love me so … I’m not letting you get away again. Not that easily. I literally would have to be dead.”

 

            Kara choked on a breath, her eyes squeezing shut as tears rolled down her cheeks. “I’m scared.”

 

            “Me too. But I can help you; you don’t have to bear it alone. You don’t have to suffer alone, I’m here. I’ve got you.”

 

            Slowly, with her coaxing gentleness, Kara allowed Lena to guide her up off her knees and onto the sofa, her head buried into the side of Lena’s neck, wrapped up in her embrace in a shocking role reversal. And Kara dissolved into her arms, willingly sinking into the feeling of being held, the warmth of Lena against her, soothing the frayed edges of her nerves as her fears slowly abated with the lingering nightmares.

 

            “I love you,” Kara tearfully whispered, a tremor in her voice, “I love you but I’m so scared. Of me, of you, of the world.”

 

            “I won’t let anything happen to you,” Lena softly crooned, thumbing away her tears. “I know the guilt is eating away at you and you feel like you have to save everyone, but the weight of the world doesn’t have to rest only on your shoulders. Let me carry it with you.”

 

            “It’s too heavy; it’s not your burden.”

 

            Giving her a wavering smile as her eyes shone with tears, Lena leaned in and pressed a soft kiss to her lips. It was light and quick and Kara stared at her with wide, sad eyes, full of surprise and wonder as she drew in a shallow breath.

 

            “I’ll carry it so you don’t have to,” Lena firmly replied. “You save the world and I’ll help you do it. And if it comes to it, I’ll burn the whole thing down to bear that burden for you.             “Don’t worry about me. As long as we’re together, we’ll figure it out. The world is better off with a Super and a Luthor in love - let the boys handle the hate and let me love you. We can save the world together, I know we can. We can save more than ten lives.”

 

            “It’s going to be a long time before anyone will trust me to keep them safe again.”

 

            Stroking her hair, Lena gave her a sympathetic look, “not as long as you might think, and not everyone. I’d trust you to lead me to my death, Kara Danvers, because I’d know it was for the right reasons.”

 

            “You won’t die,” Kara stubbornly replied, her voice holding a sharp edge. “If- if I let you stay- if we carry this together … you can’t die. Promise me. If you promise to always put your own life first then maybe I can …”

 

            “That would be … incredibly selfish of me,” Lena hedged.

 

            “I don’t care. Be selfish, because I don’t want to have to be selfish again.”

 

            Drawing in a shuddering breath, Lena exhaled and cupped Kara’s cheek. “Okay. Okay, I promise. I promise but … there is a world worth saving without me in it. I am just one girl.”

 

            Touching their foreheads together, Kara sighed heavily, the knot in her chest unwinding as she swallowed and closed her eyes.

 

            “No, there isn’t. There’s no world worth saving without you in it.”
