The title, Heisszeit, is a German pun that literally means "hot time" in English. It was the country's word of the year in 2018, and refers to the coming age of climate change the world is facing.

It’s a quiet night on Magnolia Circle, the stately colonials lining the boulevard are dark, the streetlights dimmed. A pleasantly cool breeze wafts in off the Potomac, rustling the neatly trimmed hedges and manicured lawns of Senators and federal judges. The scarlet oaks stand tall along the sidewalks, buffering the old houses from the wind and casting their long, sleepy shadows over the street.

There’s not a soul about to see John Corben strolling down the boulevard, gloved hands in his pockets, as he whistles a tuneless melody.

Secret Service is supposed to patrol the house every hour, but Justice Alexander Slate has made such a stink about them disturbing his rest over the years that they settle, now, for posting a guard in a van on the street. It’s easy for Corben to sneak up on the dozing agent at the wheel and pop a bullet into his head, his silenced pistol going off with a click and a muted pop. The gun is back in his jacket before the agent has slumped forward onto the bloody steering wheel.

Corbin walks toward the house then as though he knows exactly how many steps to take, which he does. Fourteen from the sidewalk to the front door of number 86. Thirty more around the house and over the side gate, to the window Justice Slate keeps unlocked. The eighty-six year old Justice has a cigar habit that his wife hates, and so he’s forced to partake only in the easy chair in his den, puffing smoke out the open window and then forgetting to lock it when he goes to bed.

Corben climbs inside quietly, careful not to let the old pane squeak when he opens it. Inside the house it’s warm and dark, the streetlights casting nary a stray beam onto the dark wood floors. Only the kitchen counter gleams in Corben’s periphery, the quartz stark and white against all the shadowed sable of the rest of the space.

He steals up to the Justice’s bedroom, sitting right where the plans said it’d be, at the end of the second floor hallway. This door, too, is unlocked, and Corben opens it with the practiced caution of a cat burglar. The Justice is inside, asleep at this late hour with his wispy white hair tangled over the pillows and his covers half thrown off his body. He’s even wearing blue-and-white striped pajamas, the perfect picture of a doddering old man.

The Justice is snoring lightly, an empty snifter of brandy on his bedside table. Corben looks at him for not a moment before he unzips his own jacket, pulling from it his silenced pistol. He puts two bullets in the Justice’s temple, twin little pips of blood appearing next to each other, a spray of red across the white sheets on his other side.

Corben slips the gun back into his jacket, and leaves the way he came, back through the unlocked window. He pulls an iPod from his pocket, and heads down the alley on the other side of the house, breaking into a jog once he has his earbuds in. He runs out of the neighborhood at the leisurely pace of a hobbyist finishing his nightly circuit, and no one he encounters, thinks to give a well-groomed white man jogging after midnight a second glance.

But his work is not finished tonight, and he lets the directions to his next target filter into his ears from the iPod’s GPS as he runs. Three miles later see him standing in the late night market downtown, where his athletic gear fits in just as well. It’s packed tonight, the smells of fried food and sweat thick in the air. The raucous laughter and conversation of a weekend crowd make it easy for Corbin to slip into the throng of people unnoticed.

Justice John Hardy is a younger man at just forty-five. But his salt-and-pepper hair stand out among the even younger crowd at the market. Corben tails him for a while, until, like he has every week during the surveillance done on him, the Justice shakes off his Secret Service handler and ducks into a restroom at the back end of the street. Corben follows him, easily ducking inside the bathroom from the crowd without being noticed by the exasperated Secret Service agent.

Once inside, he makes quick work of the Justice. A bullet to the back of his head sprays his blood all over the wall in front of him. His body sinks down, lifeless, into the urinal he’d been using. Corbin tucks his gun back into his jacket and leaves the way he came.

He saunters out of the market and into the quiet Georgetown night, whistling as he goes.

2. Chapter 2

Lena Luthor wakes with a pounding headache and a dry mouth, cheek smooshed into a small puddle of her own drool. And that would be quite enough for one Monday morning, except she also wakes to the cold gray eyes of her brother looking at her as he crouches at her bedside.

“Get up, sis,” he says, mouth in a hard line. “Blood in the water this morning.”

Lena lets out a breath and turns onto her stomach as Lex leaves the room. Lets her long, black hair curtain her face against the sun streaming in through her bedroom window and lets out a muffled groan into her pillow. Then plants both palms below her chest to push herself out of bed.

It’s still only 5 AM, which is far, far too early to be up on a Monday. Especially when she’s hungover. And even more especially when she doesn’t have class until nine. She’s never letting Sam take her out on a school night ever again.

She does, however, get out of bed. Throws her hair back into a messy, wavy bun atop her head and pulls her silk pajama pants up from where they’d slipped low on her full hips in the night. Cinches them around her waist as she stumbles, yawning, down the hallway towards the kitchen.

Lex is already dressed when she gets there, in a crisp charcoal suit. A vibrant purple tie is notched at his throat with a smart four-in-hand knot. He’s sipping coffee and staring at the television at the far end of the attached living room. A news anchor drones on about some breaking story or other, but Lena has eyes and ears only for the coffee still bubbling in their percolator. She grabs a stout white mug from the cupboard with sleepy fingers and pours herself a shaky first cup.

Lena sits at the island and huddles over her steaming coffee like it’s the only thing keeping her upright, which is only half untrue. The aroma alone dulls her headache a bit, and the first sip is bitter and bracing. Lex always overbrews it, but it’ll do.

“Are you paying attention?” Lex says over his shoulder, not taking his eyes from the TV.

“Not for at least another hour,” Lena quips into another scalding sip of coffee. She’ll need food soon, if she doesn’t want the caffeine to give her the jitters. She takes another gulp and settles her elbows on the counter, too tired to follow that thought physically just yet.

“Well you’d better set your watch ahead, then,” Lex says, displeasure clear in his tone. It wakes Lena up a bit more, and she narrows her green eyes to critically observant slits at him. He’s a morning person, and that’s disgusting. But it also means he’s never out of sorts this early without reason.

“Who pissed in your cornflakes?” she grouses.

“Slate is dead.”

Oh.

“Shit,” Lena curses into her coffee mug. Downs the rest of the cup in two heaving swallows and goes to stand next to Lex in front of the television. “The leukemia?”

“No,” Lex says. “A bullet.”

“Shit,” Lena curses again.

“Well-put,” Lex says, not a hint of sarcasm in his subdued voice.

Lex had clerked for the Justice when Slate had been a federal appeals judge. Their own father had been an absentee alcoholic, and the sly liberal Slate had taken a very young Lex under his wing shortly before he’d graduated from law school. Lex had clerked for him for almost three years, quite a bit longer than most clerkships. But he’d emerged the best lawyer Lena knew, and ready to take the helm at the family firm. He’d been made partner just two years later, though he spent most of his time teaching now.

“He’s not the only one,” Lex says, finally turning to look at Lena where she stands next to him. They aren’t physically affectionate people, the Luthor siblings, but Lena feels the urge to hug her brother now. There’s a stony kind of sadness in his gray eyes that she doesn’t like. “Hardy got a bullet, too.”

Lena wrinkles her nose and brow in disbelief.

“Why kill the two of them together?” she asks. “They never agreed on much of anything, the way you tell it.”

Hardy had come up more recently, the product of a right-wing push to ensconce young, conservative judges at all levels of the court system. He had been the first to reach as high as the Supreme Court, where he was, putting it simply, everything Slate wasn’t. They’d written opposing opinions and asides to and about each other more than a dozen times in the three years since Hardy had been appointed. Killing them both doesn’t make sense, to Lena or, as it happens, to Lex.

“I don’t know,” Lex says. “General destabilization of the court? Trying to make the president look bad so close to re-election? Who cares? They’re dead, and now all kinds of shit is about to hit the fan.”

Lena doesn’t contradict him out loud. He’s grieving, and layering on trying to figure out who would have wanted his mentor dead would be unkind. She raises her eyebrows at him and nods just a bit, and Lex deflates, just a touch in his shoulders. It’s an old game between them, glances and body language offering comfort in place of physical affection.

I’m here for you, she says.

I appreciate it, he replies.

“Have you eaten breakfast?” Lena says next, changing the subject and letting Lex go back to brooding at the television. He says no, but that he’s not hungry, and so Lena makes a bowl of yogurt and honey for herself, retaking her place at the breakfast bar with a fresh pot of coffee brewing and her tablet nabbed from its charger in the living room.

Her first stop is the Supreme Court website, filtering recent decisions by subject matter and then by majority count.

If there’s a connection between the two murders that goes deeper than simply wanting to shake up the court in an election year, she’s going to find it.

 

Across town, in the seedy warehouse district just outside of DC proper, there’s a small office building wedged between two long-term self-storage businesses. The building is run down, shutters falling off the windows, paint chipped. The shiniest thing about the place is the brass handle on the door that at least seems to see regular use.

It’s quiet, this early in the morning, the sun not yet peeking over the fog rolling in from the river and the streetlights mostly out for the day. And Kara Danvers is enjoying that quiet in the best way: sleeping.

Her bed is a mattress on the second floor, her only blankets a comforter that she’s cast off in the humidity of the night. The old building doesn’t have central air conditioning, and she and her sister can’t afford to run the wall units all night. They save them for when there are customers to keep comfortable downstairs.

But there are none at the moment, and so Kara sleeps, mouth open, one arm splayed out to her side and the other hand fanned out on her bare stomach. She’d stripped down to her boxer briefs and sports bra before bed, determined at least not to soak her mattress completely through with sweat during another hot DC night. It mostly worked, though her long, blonde hair has come mostly free from its ponytail, too. And it’s sticking to her face and shoulders.

Three loud raps on the downstairs door, and Kara’s sweaty hair is the least of her worries, though.

Her sister comes into the room first, gun already in hand, short-cropped red hair sticking up at odd angles.

“’Oozat?” Kara demands, mouth thick with sleep. She scrambles out of her blanket and rolls to grab her own gun from its holster atop her desk.

“Shh,” Alex shushes her with a harsh sound and a pointed look. Gives Kara the hand signal to follow her downstairs. Visitors in the very early morning, in this part of town, are rarely good omens.

Kara takes up her position downstairs, padding barefoot and shirtless over to the side of the door. Alex stands behind their work desk in the center of the room, gun trained on the peep hole. Kara nods when she’s settled, her finger outside the trigger guard of her pistol.

“Who’s there?” Alex demands. Kara adjusts her grip on the gun.

“Is this the Danvers Agency?” comes a frazzled, scared voice from the other side of the door. A man, by the sound of it. “I heard you might be able to help me.”

Kara looks to Alex, who stares narrow-eyed at the peephole for a moment longer before nodding almost imperceptibly back. Kara takes a breath and reaches out with her off hand to open the door, slowly enough that Alex can keep her gun trained on their visitor should he try anything nasty while she’s letting him in.

“Oh!” he exclaims when he sees the weaponry pointed at him. “Please, I don’t want any trouble.”

The man is middle aged, perhaps forty or forty-five. The shock of black hair on his head has started to go gray at the sideburns, and he’s thick around his middle, no doubt from lack of movement. Because his suit looks expensive, definitely made of wool, and tailored to fit him. There’s a bead of sweat escaping from his hairline, falling down his forehead. But he seems too scared to wipe it away, holding his hands up in supplication and all but trembling in the doorway.

“Sorry!” Kara hastens to reassure him, dropping her gun hand to her hip. “Never can be too careful in this neighborhood, especially before the sun is up!”

She takes a step towards him, but Alex hisses her name from behind her and she stills. Looks down at her half-naked body, and colors sheepishly around the cheeks.

“Right. I’ll uh, just go get dressed real quick, and then we can take a look at that problem of yours, huh?”

The man nods a little dumbly, finally stepping into the space as Kara retreats. She hears Alex apologizing for her state of undress as she marches back up the stairs.

 

“I think I solved it,” Lena says, bursting into Lex’s office without knocking.

“My office hours aren’t until four this afternoon, Lee,” he says. He doesn’t look up from the file he’s reading, his readers perched delicately on the end of his nose. He looks so much like their father used to, wearing them. Before the drinking had taken him to darker places than his family could follow.

The similarity, unfortunately, doesn’t end there today.

“Are you drunk?” Lena accuses. The stench of scotch is in the air, as though someone’s spilled it on the rug. A half-full glass sits next to Lex’s nameplate on his desk, the amber liquid glinting as the afternoon sunlight is battered about by Lex’s office blinds moving with the wind.

“Lubricated, nothing more,” Lex hedges. But he’s still not looking at her, scribbling something down in the notebook off to his left. “I’m thinking of writing a book on Slate. I could take the rest of the semester off to get started on it.”

“You know better than to make decisions like that when you’ve been drinking, Lex,” Lena says.

“I have not,” Lex starts, slamming a fist down on the table hard enough to make Lena jump on the word, “been drinking. I have been relaxing in my office, in the building that bears my father’s name, on the campus of my alma mater, where I also happen to be a professor in good standing.”

“You’re obfuscating,” Lena says. Lex drops his glasses to his desk and pinches at the bridge of his nose.

“Did you come here just to badger me?”

“No,” Lena says. She digs in her shoulder bag for the brief she’d prepared that morning. “I came to tell you I think I know who killed your friend.”

Lex laughs, a little derisively.

“Give it here,” he says, holding his hand out for the file. “Let’s see what my baby sister has noticed that the FBI, Secret Service, and a dozen police departments have failed to yet grasp.”

Lena rolls her eyes but hands him the file. She slips into the chair across from his as he flips through the pages. Indulges in a sip of the scotch sitting on his desk. It’s still sharp with ethanol in her nose as she drinks. Clearly not his first drink, then.

“That’s it?” Lex says, staring at the last page of the brief and then at Lena. “We’ve got two SCOTUS Justices dead, a nation in a panic, and a political system that’s about to rend itself in two because the guy in charge is wildly unpopular and about to pick two new members of the highest court. And this is what you’re saying is the actual reason they were killed?”

This is a tact of Lex’s that Lena knows well. An aggressive sort of Socratic method, she could call it. He tears at her argument like a starving dog on a T-bone steak, forcing defense and evolution from her. It’s how he’s always made her better.

“Yes,” Lena says, legs crossed at the knee. She leans back in the chair, still clutching Lex’s scotch glass, and settles her elbows into a royal sort of pose. “The politics are negligible here, in the little scheme of things. I think the real catalyst is good, old-fashioned, all-American greed.”

3. Chapter 3

“Walk me through this again,” Alex says. She rubs her fingers over her forehead, pressing into the sinus there until she can feel it in the back of her skull. “You think you know who killed Slate and Henry?”

“I’m not positive,” the man says. He’s taken off his jacket, sits rigid and nervous in the chair across from the sisters’ one desk. His hands are balled into fists on his thighs, as though he’ll tear at his own clothes in anxiety if he relaxes them. “I found some stuff at work that I think. I think they might be up to something.”

“Does your work have business with the court regularly or something?”

The man fiddles with the cuffs on his dress shirt. It’s starched to high heaven, and he’s starting to sweat through it. Kara twists her mouth in sympathy, sitting just behind Alex. He’s clearly terrified, whatever the reason.

“I’d rather not say.”

Alex huffs, impatient. Kara taps her hip below the desk, a reminder for Alex to keep her cool. And Alex takes another, longer breath before she speaks.

“If you have evidence of a criminal action, the best thing for you to do is go to the police,” Alex says. “We can offer some protection, but we’re talking about the murder of two members of the highest court in the country. The FBI will protect you if you know something.”

“I’m not going to the government with this,” the man says. It’s the sharpest his voice has been since he arrived, the conviction there as stark as his fear. Kara slides her hand lower on her own leg, toward where her gun is holstered to the outside of her thigh.

“Why not?” Alex demands. “If you want to be protected, this is our advice. Before we talk about our services, you’ve gotta come forward with this information if it’s material.”

“Maybe it was a mistake, coming here at all,” the man says. He stands abruptly, then. And Alex panics a little.

“Wait!” she says, a little too loud. Her voice echoes in the small room, and both Kara and their potential customer jump at it. “Look, don’t go. We’re willing to help you out, I promise. Just –”

“I don’t think I can do this anyway,” he says. “I’ve got a wife and a kid, you know? I can’t get mixed up in this.”

“It sounds like you already are,” Kara says softly from behind Alex. She maintains soft eye contact with the man, like he’s a deer she’s trying to keep from spooking. “Let us help you.”

“Thank you,” he says. “But I have to get to work now. I’ll. I’ll call. If I decide to go through with this. Please, don’t tell anyone I was here.”

He leaves before he even puts his jacket back on, and once the door slams shut behind him and the silence of his departure settles in the room, Kara speaks into it.

“He didn’t even give his name.”

“I hate this fucking town,” Alex grouses. “I’m going back to bed.”

 

“I’m surprised you agreed to make time for me,” Eve Tessmacher says into a sip of her wine. “Thought you were busy molding fresh young legal minds in your image, or whatever it is you get up to up in DC.”

Lex laughs as he cuts into his steak. Mr. John’s is his favorite steakhouse in New Orleans, and, if he’s honest, Eve is his favorite kind of company. They’d gone through law school together, he and Eve. But her ambitions had run on the prosecutorial side of things, while Lex had eventually come home to roost as a corporate lawyer for the family business. Luthor Oil & Gas hasn’t had any run-ins with the federal prosecutor’s office since Lex passed the bar, and so Lex hasn’t had much word from Eve since after their respective clerkships.

“I may have an ulterior motive,” he admits. The ribeye is perfectly medium rare, charred to a pleasantly crunchy bitterness on the outside and silky smooth in the middle. Eve is stunning and sultry in a black cocktail dress, her lips painted a sinful red. He really needs to get down here more often.

Eve sits back in her chair, wine glass in hand. Her short, blonde curls bounce with the movement, and she sets her mouth in an indulgent sort of frown.

“Don’t you always?” she says. It’s not a question, despite the phrasing.

“I plead the fifth, counselor,” Lex says. And Eve smiles at him, shaking her head.

“Out with it, then,” she says. “So I can go back to enjoying my meal in peace.”

“Don’t know if you’ve heard,” Lex teases. “But there were a couple of Supreme Court Justices murdered a few days ago. Seems the FBI is at a loss as to whodunnit.”

Eve rolls her eyes, takes another indulgent sip of wine.

“You know I can’t talk about an ongoing investigation, even with an old friend.”

“You don’t have to talk,” Lex assures her. “I think I’ve got a solution for you.”

Eve laughs, her hair bouncing around her face. Lex likes the sound, high and tinkling like wind chimes.

“You think you solved it?” she chuckles. “The greatest crime this century, and Lex Luthor has solved it in three days? You’re a good lawyer, Lex, but you’d make a better fisherman. That’s quite a catch.”

“In a manner of speaking, anyway,” Lex says. He slides a blue folder across the table to Eve. She looks at it dubiously. “I had help.”

“Lex Luthor accepting help on a legal matter,” Eve says, opening the folder but still looking at Lex with mirth in her eyes. “Will wonders never cease?”

She scans the document inside the folder. Her smile fades the deeper she gets into the brief therein, and by the end, she’s leaned over the document with the sort of steadfast concentration Lex remembers in her from law school. It’s why she made editor in chief of their school’s law review over him in their third year.

“Lena came up with this on her own?” is what she says when she’s done reading. She’s closed the folder and placed a hand on the cover, as though she needs to physically keep its contents from escaping.

“She did,” Lex says, no small amount of pride in his voice. The brief really is a stroke of genius, beautiful in its simplicity. “What do you think?”

Eve responds by checking her watch, a thin gold affair that looks pretty and shiny on her small wrist.

“I think the director is probably still up in DC,” she says. “And I think he’s going to want to hear about this.”

Lex smiles, cuts another bite off the ribeye on his plate. Chews with the satisfaction of a man whose plans are all coming up aces.

“I also think that this won’t make your mother happy,” Eve continues. She levels Lex with an appraising sort of look, as though expecting him to have been playing an elaborate prank on her. Going against Lillian Luthor doesn’t often work out for people, and Eve can’t remember Lex ever giving her so much as pushback.

Lex’s fork and knife pause for the first time that evening, hovering over his plate. He meets Eve’s evaluative gaze and forces a smile that she sees right through.

“What mother dearest doesn’t know won’t hurt her.”

Eve smiles, and Lex knows she doesn’t believe him. Her face turns serious in the next heartbeat, and she finally leans forward to capture his full attention away from his plate.

“Can you get your sister down here?” she asks. “If this is for real, and you want to bring it to my bosses, I’m willing to do that. But I need to speak to Lena.”

Lex eyes her for a moment, and then resumes his eating script, cutting the last of his ribeye into bite-sized pieces. He does the motions almost by rote, a hard-gained imperative never to cease movement while under pressure that he can thank Lillian for.

Eve knows him well, though. She reaches across the table, clasps his wrist in a gentle hand. He stills under her touch.

“I can protect her,” she says, softly. Gentle enough that Lex doesn’t flinch at the implication of the promise. That their mother may very well turn to violence if Lena’s brief is on-point.

“Forgive me,” Lex says, voice suddenly hard. “But you couldn’t keep two Justices alive on the same night last week.”

“I will protect your sister,” Eve says, less soft this time. Lex doesn’t flinch, but neither does he back down from the point. “And you wouldn’t have flown down here to give this to me, instead of taking it to Quantico yourself, if you thought there was someone better to do it.”

Lex drops his fork, and upturns his hand into Eve’s. An awkward hold, but a big gesture coming from him: stillness and affection he rarely shows.

“I’ll see when she’s free,” he says.

 

Lena does not, under any circumstances, want to take a week out of her life to fly to Louisiana, of all places.

“Stay at the family suite at the Ritz,” Lex cajoles, his voice tinny and rough through the phone. “Make a vacation of it. You haven’t had one in years, Lee.”

“You’ve been on enough for the both of us,” Lena tells him.

Lena plops herself down on a couch in the living room. The television is on, an old movie queued up on the screen. She’d been prepared for a lazy sort of night in before Lex had called. She’s already had a very long, very hot bath, and wrapped herself in her softest pajamas. It’s the last quiet night she’ll get before reading week begins and the stress of finals fully engulfs her and everyone else on campus.

So of course Lex is trying to ruin it.

“It’s gorgeous down here, you know,” Lex continues, unimpeded by Lena’s snark. “Why make do with shit breezes off the Potomac when you could be down here in the gulf?”

“Because I have air conditioning, and the house to myself, for two,” Lena says. The calmly simmering good mood of the evening is quickly dissipating into a buzzing sort of annoyance. She can feel it in her teeth, like someone’s scraped their nails over a chalkboard. “And you may be a jet-setting corporate lawyer, but I, dear brother, still have to pass my classes.”

“As though you couldn’t pass those tests standing on your head,” he tries for flattery.

“I definitely won’t be passing them from New Orleans.”

“Lena.”

“Lex.”

There’s muffled conversation on the other end of the line, as though someone is speaking to Lex while he holds his hand over the receiver. A quick scuffle later, and a new voice is coming through the phone.

“Hey, Lena, it’s Eve, how are you?” comes a clipped-toned Eve Tessmacher on the other end. Lena doesn’t answer her question, but Eve isn’t waiting for one anyway. “Listen, your brother is asking you to come down here on my behalf.”

Lena lets out a suffering sort of sigh, switches her phone from ear to ear and gets up off the couch.

“Of course he is,” she says. Eve lets out a commiserating laugh. Lex has never been able to say no to Eve, ever since law school. They may have gone in different directions career-wise, but that much hasn’t changed.

“He showed me your brief.”

That stops her pacing. A cold tendril of something like dread winds its way around her heart.

“I hadn’t exactly intended that for public consumption,” she admits. No one’s around to see her nervousness on her face, but she still ducks her head and scratches hard at the back of her neck. “I certainly didn’t want it to get to a federal prosecutor.”

“Are you telling me the information and conclusion you reached is erroneous, or hyperbolic?”

“No,” Lena says. “It’s not that, it’s just –”

“You Luthor kids never want to actually take action against that mother of yours.”

Lena frowns harder in the absence of an audience to judge her for it.

“Something like that requires a little more preparation, is all,” she defends. “If I’m right, even more so.”

Eve hums, noncommittal but unwilling to start a true argument about it. Lena can be more obstinate than Lex when challenged, and in general, it’s rarely productive to take on a Luthor on their own terms.

“I want to pursue this line of investigation,” Eve says instead. “But I need your help. And honestly, Lena, if we go down this road, you’re going to need protection. I’m offering it.”

There’s a long, pregnant sort of pause after Eve finishes speaking. Lena can hear herself breathing, can feel her heart beating in her ears. Her throat has dried into a scratchy-scared feeling like she’s just run for her life and is trying to catch her breath. On the periphery of her mind, she recognizes the signs of an impending panic attack for what they are.

“Fine,” she says finally. It feels like moving the first dirt over her own gravestone, but making a decision at least calms her nerves a little bit. “I’ll fly down, if seeing me will help.”

“I expect not,” Not Kieran says. There’s an air of smug satisfaction in the set of her eyes, and she tracks the detectives as they leave through the ruined office door. When they’re gone, the flash of police lights fading around the corner, Alex is the first to speak.

“Who the fuck are you?”

 

The Danvers sisters are an odd couple of people.

The shorter of the two, a redhead named Alex, is immediately suspicious of Lena, despite that she’s just come to their rescue. Lena lets that slide, considering the circumstances. Once Lena explains why she’s there, Alex relaxes visibly.

“You drink whiskey?” Alex asks. She’s already reaching into her desk drawer as she speaks.

“I do,” Lena assents. A drink is certainly in order after the day the sisters have had. And Lena hasn’t exactly had the best week, either.

“Good,” Alex says. She gets three cups from the same drawer, setting them and the clear bottle of Old Forester on the desk between them. Two of them she fills with a healthy pour, the other with just a splash. She hands the kid’s portion to her sister and pushes one of the others across to Lena.

The scotch is surprisingly mellow for a bottle that costs less than one of Lena’s high heels.

The other sister, Kara, takes her portion and pours an entire can of Coke into it. Lena almost wants to laugh. With that ratio, Alex might as well not have wasted the whiskey.

“So tell us about this problem you have,” Kara says. Alex is busy downing her three fingers of scotch and then immediately pouring herself a refill. Lena is reminded of Lex, a stab of grief arcing under her ribs.

She turns to Kara to tear herself away from the feeling, and finds herself in the trap of the bluest pair of eyes she’s ever seen. Lena swallows before she begins, her own eyes flitting between Kara’s, searching in vain for a break in the earnest cobalt of them.

“I’ve stumbled across a plot at my family’s company that I think goes far deeper than I’d initially realized,” Lena says. “I need protection, and an investigator that they can’t reach. You two are the only outfit in the district that offers both, and hasn’t done any business with the company in the past.”

Alex is, inexplicably, angrier the longer Lena explains herself.

“Great, another one of these,” she says, rolling her eyes. She downs her second helping of whiskey and goes to pour a third.

“Alex,” her sister admonishes.

“Another?” Lena picks up. She’s uninterested in the anger – plenty of people are angry with the Luthors just on principle, so it’s nothing new. But what Alex had said – “Have you had another client come ask about something similar recently?”

“Are you at least going to tell us your name?” Alex asks, ignoring Lena’s question altogether.

Lena looks over to Kara, who she finds smirking back at her. It shouldn’t feel like an inside joke, having given a fake name to a stranger, but it does. And Kara looks at her with easy mirth on her face like she expects Lena to laugh right along with her.

Lena presses her lips together to keep from smiling.

“We got a walk-in last week,” Kara fills Lena in. “Nervous sort of guy, said he had information about those two SCOTUS Justices that were murdered. Wouldn’t give his name, and backed out once we suggested he go to the cops about it.”

He and Lena have that last bit in common, but she keeps that to herself for now.

“Funny coincidence, that,” she says instead. Kara perks up, Alex rolls her eyes, and Lena continues unabated: “I think I know who did it, and I need help finding proof.”

“We’re going to need more than our usual fee to get involved in all this political nonsense,” Alex tells her. Lena gets the impression that Alex would rather they boot her right back out the door, but unluckily for her, Lena is not short of cash.

“Whatever your fee, I’ll pay it,” she says. Alex narrows her eyes.

“Just who are you?” she asks.

“My name is Lena,” is the reply. Alex’s eyes go wide. Lena sees Kara mouthing her name in her periphery, testing it in her mouth. The way her lips pull back on the first syllable makes it look like she’s smiling, and Lena tries not to find that endearing.

“You’re Lena Luthor?” Alex asks, incredulous.

“I can provide identification, if you’re unsure of me,” Lena quips. She takes another sip of the whiskey, still on her first glass. Alex slams back her third like it’s the first water she’s had after months in the desert.

“A million,” Alex says then. Lena nods, tips her cup in acquiescence.

“Each,” Alex tacks on, seeing Lena agree so easily. “You hire me to help with the investigation, and my sister will make sure you stay out of trouble long enough to see the proof you’re after.”

There’s a tense moment that Lena lets fester a bit, wherein the Danvers sisters seem to hold their individual breaths. She takes another sip of scotch, pretends to mull the terms over. Sets her cup back on the desk and rises from her chair with two sets of eyes boring into her.

Lena smiles.

“You’ve got a deal.”

 

She and Kara do not get off to a good start.

Lena has had handlers almost all her life: drivers, personal bodyguards, governesses and the like were all a part of her upbringing.

Kara Danvers is something else entirely.

She has a list of rules that she insists Lena follow, and top of the page in big bold letters seems to be that credit cards of any kind are now off limits.

“You can’t expect me to fund either your employment or our investigation without my money,” Lena objects when Kara lays down this rule.

“Do you have any idea how much information is exchanged every time you swipe a little plastic?” Kara rebuts. “You’re giving away not just your consumer metadata, but a real-time log of where you’ve been, when, and even for how long. From here until this is settled, we’re a cash-only operation.”

“You must realize that’s not feasible,” Lena says.

“I realize that it’s the best way to keep your head on your shoulders,” Kara quips back.

A boiling sort of anger rises like bile in Lena’s throat, and she leans back in the chair across from Kara in the Podunk little office she and her sister keep, her arms crossed over her chest as she glowers at the younger Danvers. Alex is already out, something about wrapping up her last contract before she can devote all her time to Lena’s matter. And Lena had thought it best to go over the basics of Kara’s half of their deal.

She wishes now that she’d kept her big mouth shut.

Kara is self-assured and cocksure, and clearly used to being listened to. Her words brook absolutely no argument, and Lena, used to getting her way so long as she can put up a reasonable justification for it, finds that it sets her teeth on edge not to be in control this way.

“You have to at least allow me to get some cash, so we have any funds at all here,” Lena says. “And I’ll need to go back to my apartment in the city to collect my things.”

Kara mulls that over, her lips twitched off to one side as she thinks. Lena is still fuming, but not so much that the cute effect of the expression is lost on her.

It’s a shame she’s being so hard-headed, Lena thinks, because Kara is attractive. She’s tall, and blonde, of course, but besides that she has the sinewy strength of someone who trains for function rather than form. Her shirtsleeves are pushed up to her elbows, revealing the corded muscle of her forearms. Her neck, similarly, is a column of soft, supple muscle, ending in a strong jaw. In any other context, Lena might even flirt with her.

“I’ll take you to go to your apartment,” Kara decides. “We can hit a bank on the way, make sure we have everything we need. Then we’re off the grid.”

Grating faux-magnanimousness aside, it’s an amenable compromise for Lena. For the moment, at least. Kara gathers her weapon, tucks it into a shoulder holster beneath a leather jacket, and then holds a hand out to Lena.

“Keys?”

“You’re not driving my car,” Lena says.

“I’m driving you everywhere for the next few weeks,” Kara counters. She doesn’t drop her hand, and Lena crosses her arms over her chest. “Are you trained in tactical driving?”

“Have you ever driven anything with over four hundred horsepower?”

Kara doesn’t blanche like Lena had hoped. Instead, she grins. Lena practically sees her ears perk up. It’s adorable and infuriating.

“What kind of car do you have out there, exactly?” Kara asks, like she’s trying to get a friend to dish the latest gossip.

“One you’re never getting behind the wheel of,” Lena says. “That’s not negotiable.”

Kara’s lips twitch off to the side again as she mulls this over. Finally, she nods.

“Fine,” she says. “Let’s go then.”

Kara follows Lena out the door. She walks quietly, much to Lena’s surprise. She doesn’t hear Kara’s steps until she stops short: just as Lena’s car comes into view.

The Porsche had been a birthday present from Lex the year before: a sweet little 911 Carrera in metallic Adventurine Green. And Lena isn’t above rubbing it in, just a little, after Kara’s performance in the office. She pumps the unlock button on her key fob and doesn’t slow her stride towards the car. Throws a cheeky ‘keep up, darling’ over her shoulder at Kara.

Kara is still staring, gaping at the mouth, as Lena settles herself into the driver’s seat. But she isn’t looking at the car, and Lena flushes under her awed gaze. She rushes to rev the engine then, to break the spell of the moment, and that snaps Kara out of her daze.

“This thing is incredible,” Kara says when she’s in the car, running her hands over the fine leather finishes, fingering the shiny black knobs and interfaces on the console.

Lena watches Kara’s hands move from behind her aviators. She’s graceful with them: feather-light touches over surfaces she’s probably afraid to damage. Her fingers are long and slender. Lena swallows and looks out the windshield, preparing to pull off the curb.

“It was a gift from my brother last year.”

“Sounds like you hit the sibling jackpot,” Kara says, a big smile on her face as she fiddles with the climate controls on her seat.

“I like to think so,” Lena says quietly. If Kara hears the edge of sadness in her voice at the thought of Lex, she doesn’t show it.

 

Kara has never been in a Porsche before. The little coupe is surprisingly spacious inside, with plenty of room for her long legs. And Lena, she has to admit, is a good driver, and unafraid to let the zippy sports car carry them around what little district traffic is still about at this hour.

They hit up the bank first. Kara starts towards the tellers when they walk inside, but Lena clears her throat and leads her past the bank of windows and into the branch manager’s office. She doesn’t even knock, just opens the door and smiles at the man sitting at his desk like they’re old friends. And he greets Lena by name. Kara hides her surprise behind a strictly professional frown, and even agrees to stay behind when the manager insists on taking Lena into the back by herself to collect the substantial amount of cash she’s requested.

“Is he even allowed to give you this much money at one time?” Kara asks later, when they’re loading the two duffel bags of cash Lena had been given into the back of the Porsche.

“No,” Lena says, smiling coyly. “But there are benefits to being a Luthor.”

Kara stops dead, her hand on the passenger door handle.

The Luthors have a reputation, in Kara’s mind. Owners of one of the last family-run oil and gas companies in the country, they have a stranglehold on American oil drilling, especially in the south. They’re known for employing somewhat ruthless tactics in gaining new oil fields, and in keeping their employees in line.

“What exactly is your theory about the justices, then?” Kara asks. Her mouth sets in a hard line, and Lena lifts her chin in response to the challenge.

“I’ll let you in on everything once we have my computer and some other things from my apartment,” she says. She doesn’t give Kara a chance to respond, instead sliding right into the driver’s seat and starting the car. Kara follows after a moment, sitting and buckling herself in without a word.

They’re silent for the remainder of the drive to the downtown apartment Lena keeps.

Lena is mostly still as she drives, but every so often, she’ll flex her hands over the steering wheel in an unconscious fidget. Kara watches her long fingers curl around the wheel again and again, and wonders if her hands always betrayed her this way. Kara marks it down in her head as something to look out for.

 

Lena’s apartment building is predictably high-class, but they don’t go up as far as Kara had anticipated. Instead of the penthouse, Lena’s place is a small one-bedroom on the third floor.

It’s also clearly just been tossed.

Her furniture – a sleek white living room set with matching lamps and side tables – has been knocked over. The couch cushions have been split open, with their fluff strewn across the living room floor to search inside them. The drawers in the kitchen are open, their contents scattered across the counter and in the sink. Her towels and bath supplies have been thrown down the hallway leading to her bedroom.

Kara follows Lena silently from space to space, holding in a grimace at the damage.

Her bedroom is perhaps the worst. It’s a small space, and the only part of the apartment that feels lived in. The modest double bed is in disarray, the pillows slashed open and the mattress just the same. An antique-looking writing desk in the corner, just under the window, has been knocked over, its drawers open and turned over.

Lena looks about the space with critical eyes, but her hands twist together in front of her. She pulls on her fingers so hard the skin goes white. Kara puts a hand on her shoulder without thinking, trying to soothe.

“I’m sorry, Lena,” she says, lowly, so as not to scare her. She’s had clients who’ve been the victims of home invasions and robberies before. Knows how violating it feels, to have their private spaces invaded and demolished.

But Lena just shrugs to dislodge Kara’s hand. Shakes her head with something between a scoff and a sob. Her back stays ramrod straight, and she walks farther into the room to put some distance between herself and Kara.

“They took my laptop,” she says. “Not sure what else.”

Kara takes a breath, gauging whether to touch Lena again or not. She decides against it, opting instead for a bit of levity.

“At least now you know your theory’s gotta be dead-on.”

Lena gives her a baleful look, “Yeah. Lucky me.”

6. Chapter 6

Alex brings home Chinese takeout, and it’s still warm when Lena and Kara get back from the apartment with a few of Lena’s personal effects. They hide the cash in the safe upstairs, and Kara gives Lena the combination in case she needs to access it without either of the sisters present.

The three of them eat in silence for a while, then, and Lena contemplates what their next move should be while she watches Kara inhale three helpings of General Tso’s almost without taking a breath. There’s a small dot of sauce at the corner of her mouth. It’s fascinating and disconcerting.

“Where do you even put all that?” she finds herself asking. She picks at her own lo mein and then waves her chopsticks in Kara’s direction. Alex laughs from her seat beside them.

“I work out a lot,” Kara says, as though any workout could possibly burn the approximate billion calories she’s eating in just this one meal. She doesn’t even look up as she speaks, her mouth still full of chicken and her chopsticks digging for more in the container. Lena shakes her head.

“I was thinking about where we go from here,” Lena starts again. “I was supposed to meet with someone in the federal prosecutor’s office about a week ago, and I could reconnect with her.”

“You’ve already spoken to the authorities about this?” Alex interjects. She puts her food container down on the desk beside her, her attention fully on Lena now.

“Yes,” Lena says. “My brother had arranged for an old friend of his with the feds to look over my brief, and possibly send it on to Quantico.”

“Why didn’t you meet with her?” Kara asks. That dot of sauce is still at the edge of her mouth is still there, marring her serious expression a bit.

“I never got the chance,” Lena admits. There’s a lump in her throat when she thinks of why, and she has to swallow past it to tell the story. “I went down to New Orleans last week, but before I could meet with her, my brother—” she swallows again, “was killed in a car bombing. I thought it best to leave immediately after that.”

That admittance gets Kara to stop eating, too.

“I’m sorry,” Alex says. Lena waives her off. If she keeps dwelling on Lex, she’s liable to shut down completely. And that’s the last thing needed right now.

“You still want to pursue this?” Kara asks, so painfully gentle that Lena feels it like a blow beneath her ribs. “Your life is in danger.”

“That’s why I hired you,” Lena says, perhaps a bit more pointedly than is warranted. Kara purses her lips and picks up her food again.

“You got a death wish or something?” she says it with the cadence of a joke, but Lena wonders if that’s not just to throw her off from the real concern underneath. “After all that, you still want to play spy with us?”

Kara stares at her, blue eyes wide and clear. Appraising and a bit suspicious.

Lena doesn’t say what she feels: that Lex is dead because of her brief, because she showed it to him and should have known that he wouldn’t be able to leave it alone. That it’s her mother who’s likely behind all this, and that makes it Lena’s responsibility to stop.

That she’s paying Kara a million dollars to just do her damn job.

Instead, all she says is, “Yes.”

“So this prosecutor,” Alex interrupts their staring contest, but Kara’s gaze never leaves Lena even as she listens. “What’s her deal?”

“Like I said, an old friend of my brother’s. So we figured she would be trustworthy. And she’s something of a rising star in her office down there, so we figured that her opinion might carry some weight that ours wouldn’t have.”

Lena thinks about Eve, then. She hasn’t had any contact with Eve since Lex’s death. It’s possible she was hunted down as well, though there hasn’t been anything in the paper or online about a federal prosecutor dying.

“How well do you know this woman?” and that’s Kara again, still suspicious. Lena runs her tongue over her teeth before answering, picking her words carefully.

“She is my brother’s closest confidant. Why?”

Kara has no such compunctions about tact, it seems.

“Your brother shows her this brief, and he’s killed not a week later? It’s suspicious timing, is all I’m saying.”

“Eve wasn’t behind Lex’s death,” Lena says, a scoff following her words. The idea that Lex or Eve could ever harm each other is ludicrous to her. The only reason they weren’t married was their very equal level of ambition. Kara makes a displeased face but doesn’t argue the point further.

“Okay, so let’s start with her,” Alex says. “In the meantime, I’ll try to dig up something on that guy we met the other week. Might be he was onto something, too.”

“And we should get you,” Kara adds, pointing to Lena with her sticky chopsticks, “into a hotel where you won’t be found. Someone was bold enough to toss an apartment in one of the highest-end buildings in the city; no doubt they won’t mind going farther than that.”

That, at least, Lena and Kara can agree on. Lena nods to concede the point, and Kara, perhaps anticipating another round of pushback, deflates a bit in the shoulders. She squints a bit at Lena while Alex busies herself with getting some paperwork in order, as though Lena is a puzzle, but she can’t quite fit the pieces together.  

Given Kara’s current attitude towards most of what she does, Lena is in no hurry for her to complete the picture.

 

Needing a hotel that doesn’t require a credit card for deposit doesn’t exactly leave them with a lot of good choices. They end up in the last room of a clean but somewhat shady hourly-rate motel just outside of downtown. Lena brings enough clothing for an extended stay and a small bag of cash for incidentals, and pays for a month of lodging up front.

The room they end up with is not large, perhaps a dozen feet by two dozen including the bathroom. The carpet hasn’t been replaced since the 70s; it’s long and plush, but at least it doesn’t crunch underfoot. The bathroom is a riot of 90s gold fixtures and cheap, faux-linoleum tile, the tub stained but shining clean.

Lena sees only one thing when they enter the room, though: there’s only one bed.

“I’ll sleep on the floor,” is what Kara says when she enters behind Lena with her own bags. She says it softly, and only when Lena pauses in front of her to stare at the outdated comforter.

“Yeah,” is all Lena can think to offer. Kara sidles past her and into the room proper, and Lena gets a hint of her as she walks by. Orange blossom and cream, like a popsicle in summer. She closes her eyes behind Kara’s back and tries not to dwell on living with that scent for a month.

“We’ll need to hole up here as much as possible,” Kara says, placing her duffel on the end of the bed and unzipping it to pull out her toiletries. “Wi-Fi here is fairly reliable, so we can do research, and Alex knows how to get ahold of us if she needs help or has any updates. Given what happened at your place, I think it best if we lay low, though.”

“Right,” Lena says, apparently only capable of one-word answers at the moment. Kara doesn’t comment on it, and that earns her a positive mark in Lena’s book, though. So Lena takes a breath and follows her into the room, depositing her own duffel bag on the small desk in the corner.

“Do you want to take some time to get situated?” Kara asks.

“No,” Lena responds sharply, not looking back over her shoulder at Kara. “I need to find a new computer and get set up working on this. If I’m right, we don’t have a ton of time to spare here.”

She hears a rustling from behind her as she takes out her phone charger, and then Kara is beside her, slapping down a notepad and a black pen onto the desk next to her bag.

“Write down what you need. I’ll go and get it,” Kara says.

“Do you mean to keep me prisoner here for an entire month?” Lena quips, half-afraid the answer is going to be a resounding yes.

“I’m not –” Kara cuts herself off, sounding genuinely frustrated. Lena cranes her head to get a look at Kara’s face, and finds it screwed up rather adorably in consternation. As if she’s focusing very hard on something she’s finding unpleasant, all scrunched brow and pouting lips. “Ms. Luthor, it’s not my intention to insult you here. I’m just trying to keep you alive. And the best way to do that is to minimize your outside exposure. The longer we can keep your whereabouts a secret, the longer we have to plan and act before someone tries to stop us again.”

“Lena,” she says. A fit of something like guilt washes over her, seeing Kara struggling so hard not to insult her. Perhaps in her haste to bring Lex’s killers to justice she’s been unfair to someone just trying to help her.

“What?”

“You don’t have to call me Ms. Luthor,” Lena says. “And I understand. I’m sorry, this is all very new to me and it’s far from my forte.”

Kara’s shoulders sag, bringing her body closer to Lena’s. And there’s her scent again, calling like a cool spot of shade on a hot district day. She towers over Lena by at least four inches, and when she rolls her shoulders to stand up straight again, the fabric of her shirt catches tightly over the rounded muscle there. Lena watches it with a coolly detached look on her face, but her hands twist her phone’s charging cord hard enough for it to be audible.

“I really do just want to keep you safe and find whoever killed your brother,” Kara says. If she notices Lena’s death grip on the cord she doesn’t comment on it. “I promise.”

“Sure,” Lena says, giving up half a smile and a little nod. Kara’s brow smooths out at the gesture, and Lena can see her relief in the way she stands a little taller at Lena’s side.

“I’m on your side, Lena,” Kara says, in a whisper.

“I’ll let you know what I need and where to get it,” Lena says.

 

Kara is true to her word. She spends an hour or two outside the hotel room, picking up a new laptop and enough microwave dinners to sate an army. She munches her way through a loaded macaroni and cheese bowl while Lena sets up a workstation at the desk, Kara splayed out on the made bed behind her like a starfish after she finishes eating.

Lena spends the next week sequestered in the room. Kara forbids delivery, so they subsist on microwave meals and the occasional takeout that Kara brings back after her trips to the great outdoors. When she’s in the room, Kara mostly works out: calisthenics on the floor in nothing but a sports bra and sweatpants, usually. Lena catches furtive glimpses of her straining arms and glistening abs when she takes trips to the bathroom on the other side of the room, and pointedly does not remark on anything relating to Kara’s workouts, ever.

About a week and a half in, Lena is halfway through another round of background checks on potential leads and a quarter of the way through a truly terrible frozen rice bowl, when she snaps. Slams her plastic fork down on the desk. Kara jumps from her place on the edge of the bed, hitting the floor with a loud thud and a grunt of pain.

“If I don’t get some real food, I’m going to go insane,” Lena says, molars grinding through the words. She’s irritable, her stomach constantly roiling from all the greasy food she’s eaten. And she swears she’s going to start breaking out if she doesn’t eat an unsauced vegetable in the next fifteen minutes.

“Cabin fever is an unfortunate side effect of hiding out,” Kara says. Lena can hear the pain in her voice. Ignores it.

“I notice that I seem to be the only one at risk for it,” she says. Lena props her elbows on the desk to rub at both her temples. The headache has been raging for a couple of days now, no doubt the result of all the screen time she’s had and her newly-high-sodium diet.

Kara sighs, beleaguered and heavy. And Lena bristles. Spins on the stool that’s made do as her desk chair and points a finger at Kara aggressively.

“No, you know what?” she says, mad enough nearly to spit now. “You get to come and go as you please! I haven’t seen the sun in nearly a fortnight, and you’re here acting like I should be grateful to be cooped up like this.”

Kara is still sitting on the floor, and how her hair has managed to stay perfectly wavy and luscious despite her fall from the bed is perhaps more irritating than it would be, had Lena not already been teeming with anger.

“A fortnight?” she says, smiling a little condescendingly and picking herself up off the ground. She pats her own ass to get whatever was on the floor off of it. “What is this, regency England?”

“Go fuck yourself. Is that modern enough for you?”

“Hey,” Kara gripes. “I’m not the one who –”

But whatever she has or has not been the one to do, Lena never finds out. There’s a crash near the bathroom, and then two men are barreling into their tiny room, armed with shiny-sharp knives.

Lena freezes, then takes a step back to cover her laptop from view with her body. But Kara is quicker: she grabs the armed hand of the first man through the door and pushes it away from her chest. Lena’s heart jumps into her throat, but Kara has more than an upper hand now. She headbutts the man she’s grappling with, hard. Flips her head back to clear her vision, her hair billowing out behind her. There’s blood on her face, and Lena starts before she realizes that it’s the man’s – now also flowing from his nose and onto the shag carpeting. He slumps down in Kara’s embrace, and she leaves him on the floor to engage with his compatriot. She’s grinning, Lena realizes, face bloodied from battle and sky blue eyes alight with excitement. It sends a shiver down Lena’s spine that isn’t entirely unpleasant.

The next goon is quicker, getting his knife hand up before Kara can close the distance between them. He swipes down with it, and Kara moves to block at his wrist just a bit too late. The knife cuts into her forearm, and blood begins to run down her wrist and over her hand. She clenches her bloody fingers together against the pain with a grunt, then swings the bloody fist at the man’s side. It catches him just in the back, near his liver, and he whooshes out a breath.

Kara follows up with a right hook that lands under his jaw. There’s a sickening sort of crunch, and the man falls like a sack of bricks. Kara stands over him, shaking out her right hand, that savage sort of smile still on her face.

“Call the cops,” Kara says, more gently than Lena thought her capable after such a display.

“You’re bleeding,” Lena says, a little dumbly. She sinks back onto the stool, legs suddenly wobbly.

“You should see the other guys,” Kara quips. She takes a tentative step towards Lena. “Call the police, okay? I’m gonna find something to stop the flow here.”

She disappears into the little bathroom. One of the goons on the floor groans and rolls over, holding his head in a shaky palm but making no move to get up. There’s blood splattered over the carpet and up one of the walls from both the injured man and the slash on Kara’s wrist.

The sound in the room comes back to Lena then: vague groaning from the injured attackers, the water running in the bathroom. A soft ping sounds behind her, another of her checks being completed. That snaps her into action. She smooths out her hair and reaches for her phone, keeping her eyes on the men on the floor as she dials 911.

 

They’re in a part of town that attracts a lot of cop activity, but it takes over an hour for police to get there. Kara moves both goons into sitting positions against one of the blank walls in the room, hands ziptied behind them. She sits on the bed across from them with a towel wrapped around her still-bleeding wrist and her gun on the bed beside her.

The cops come and cart away the goons. At least a dozen cops and crime scene techs come and take all manner of samples: a swipe of blood from Kara’s cheek, a swatch of the carpet where Kara had beaten the first attacker bloody. Kara steadfastly refuses medical attention. They’re both instructed not to leave the district while the investigation is underway, and let alone for the night. The whole process takes maybe two hours.

Kara doesn’t disappoint: she chokes on her next breath, and her feet stumble out of rhythm. She catches herself in the next moment, admirably Lena thinks. And she’s smiling when she comes back into Lena’s embrace.

“I’m never going to get tired of being surprised by you,” she says. Kara’s laugh in her ear sends a pleasant tingle down her spine, the press of Kara’s hand on her lower back is a pleasant anchor for a moment that Lena never wants to end.

But it does have to end, at least for a while. Kara lets her go at the end of the song with a kiss to the back of her hand. Lena shakes her head at the faux chivalry, rolls her eyes at the low, “a pleasure, Ms. Luthor” that Kara parts with. But the skin of her hand tingles where Kara’s lips had touched, and she rubs the thumb of her other hand over the spot on her way back to her table.

Her step-mother is waiting for her there.

“She’s quite handsome,” Lillian says as Lena takes her seat. Lena doesn’t miss a beat.

“She’s here with Pamela Eaton,” Lena tells her, her eyes still on Kara. Her paramour takes her seat next to Mrs. Eaton, a big grin still on her face. Lena feels her own lips wanting to curl up at the corners in kind.

“I see,” Lillian says, approval in the two words. Bait taken. “I think it’s time to bring you fully into the fold, Lena. I have great plans for this law firm, and the Luthor companies it represents. With your brother sadly gone, I need someone I can trust by my side.”

 

Kara watches Lena get up with her step-mother, tracks them walking to the back of the room. They leave behind a guarded door, some goon stepping into place to block the exit.

“I think this is where I take my leave, Pam,” she says, turning to Mrs. Eaton beside her. Pam scrunches her nose up into a smile.

“Go get her, tiger,” she says, chucking Kara lightly on the chin with a soft set of knuckles. It’s almost motherly, and Kara takes the mock blow with a big smile.

She rises smoothly from the table, places her napkin on her seat and straightens her suit jacket. There’s a pulsing dance number playing from the DJ now, and her casual walk around the dance floor goes unnoticed. She’s beside the guard at the door without fuss, the two of them blocked off from the rest of the venue by a thick black curtain.

“Sorry ma’am,” he says, holding out a hand to her. “This area is off-limits to guests. I’m happy to point you wherever you’re looking to go.”

“My bad,” Kara says, holding up her hands. “And I don’t mean to be a pain, but I’ve got an appointment with Mrs. Luthor that I really can’t be late for.”

The guard’s face hardens at her words, but Kara doesn’t give him a chance to refute her again. She brings her hand down against his, trapping his thumb and pressing back until she feels the joint of it crack. The man yelps, and falls to one knee, his outcry swallowed by the pulsing beat of the music behind them. Kara brings her knee into his temple, and he crumples to the floor, out cold.

“Sorry, my guy,” she says, grabbing him more firmly by the wrist to drag him wholly behind the curtain. She fishes the key to the door out of his pocket. “I know you’re just doing your job, but I’ve got my own to complete.”

 

The key unlocks the door, and Kara makes sure to lock it behind herself. Hopefully, anyone who finds the goon out in the hall will find the locked door and look elsewhere first. She turns around then, and finds that the gesture is somewhat moot.

The shiny black barrel of a handgun presses into her forehead.

“You must be Danvers,” the goon holding the weapon says. He’s wearing a well-cut suit, and sunglasses even though he’s indoors. Kara flicks her eyes over his shoulder, and finds that he’s flanked by two more guards, their weapons trained on her as well. She could try to take the first guy as a shield, but it’s a fifty-fifty whether Lillian’s goons would just shoot through their companion to get to her.

“That’s me,” she says. “Any chance you gents would believe I was just looking for the bathroom?”

 

They lead her through a winding series of hallways, not unusual for an old opera house like this. Her hands aren’t bound, but there are three guys escorting her, each with his own gun. Kara had left her metal handguns back at the office, and she wishes now that she’d kept the plastic one that Lena had created. With that, three guys would be child’s play to escape from.

“Mrs. Luthor is waiting for you,” the guard at her right elbow says, stopping her at a closed door. He knocks twice, and the voice of Lena’s step-mother comes muffled through the wood for them to come in.

Inside is a luxuriously-decorated sitting room, with a wet bar at the back. Kara finds Lillian first, sitting in her glamorous champagne dress, her hair in a severe up-do. There’s a glass of sparkling wine in front of her on the chic coffee table, and she’s shooting an absolutely triumphant smile in Kara’s direction.

“Ms. Danvers,” she drawls, brown eyes sparkling in victory. She rises to her full height, as tall as Kara in her heels, and strides over to where her goons still have both of Kara’s arms pinned. “So lovely to meet you at last. My daughter has told me so much about you.”

“Never thought to meet her mother under such circumstances,” Kara quips. “If I’d known, I’d have sent flowers. Or maybe one of those edible arrangements, you know, the good ones with the chocolate-covered pineapple and –”

One of the goons launches a knee into her stomach, and Kara doubles over. A glass on the other side of the room breaks. Kara looks up through watery eyes to see Lillian lording over her prone form.

“Lena,” she croaks, looking past Lillian’s hip to where Lena Luthor is holding the remains of her shattered whiskey glass in her palm. “You never mentioned that your mother has immaculate Cruella de Vil vibes.”

Lena scoffs out a laugh that Kara knows is halfway genuine, and shakes her head. Puts her ruined glass on the side table next to her. And it might be that the wind was knocked out of her with that blow, but Kara thinks in that moment that Lena looks especially beautiful there in front of the window, the streetlight streaming in and backlighting her in a subtle gold shimmer. It tangles in her hair like sunlight, and Kara can’t help but smile.

Lillian scoffs, sounding so like her daughter that Kara has to look at her again. They look so little alike, but there it is: a little of the daughter reflected there in the mother.

“Are these theatrics really necessary?” Lena says, and Kara doesn’t need to look at her again to know that she’s rolling her eyes. “Just ask her what you wanted her here to ask her and let’s get it done.”

“You’ll have to excuse my daughter,” Lillian says to Kara, “but she has no sense of how to take care of a guest.”

Kara takes a subtle but deep breath, able to fill her lungs again. And when the air comes back, so does her bravado.

“Oh I wouldn’t say that,” she says, turning again to Lena and giving her the best smolder she can muster. Lena rolls her eyes again, but there’s a pretty pink dusting her cheeks as she does so.

She misses Lillian jerking her head in Lena’s direction, so it’s a surprise when her goons haul Kara to her feet and throw her in Lena’s direction. The room isn’t large; it’s just a few stumbling steps before she catches herself on the windowsill behind Lena.

“Vulgar, the both of you,” Lillian spits. She’s ranting now, hardly looking at the two of them. Kara straightens next to Lena and smiles ruefully at her.

“You’re nothing if not subtle,” Lena says, her voice nothing but a harsh whisper. She doesn’t take her eyes from her mother.

“You know how I like to make an entrance,” Kara says back. She brushes her hands down the front of her suit jacket, smoothing out any wrinkles the thugs had put in it while they manhandled her.

“Any ideas on how to make an exit?” Lena asks, finally looking at her and raising one shapely eyebrow. She’s calm, though. Brow otherwise smooth, breathing even. Kara feels a little twinge of pride at how collected she is, even with both of their lives on the line. In the foreground of the room, Lillian is waxing poetic about prodigal sons and untrustworthy daughters.

Kara only has eyes for Lena, though.

“We may a problem, Lena. There’s three of them, and I’m not armed.”

And to her surprise, Lena’s face breaks into a smile. Kara watches her dimples wink, one after the other as Lena’s crooked grin evens out into her full, heart-shaped smile. Feels her own shoulders sag at the sight, almost in relief. If Lena is smiling, well.

Nothing can go wrong from here.

Lena takes her hand, the one closest to the window, so her step-mother won’t see. She takes a half-step closer to Kara, until she can put her other palm on Kara’s chest. It feels almost like dancing again, Etta James resounding ghostlike in Kara’s head.

But then Lena pulls her hand lower, just under her hip, where her dress splits scandalously before wafting to the floor. Kara’s eyes widen at the feel of the soft skin of her thigh there.

“Easy, darling,” Lena says. And that’s when Kara feels it. Soft leather. A hard, round snap. The butt of a gun, kept warm so close to Lena’s skin. The 3D-printed Berretta. She smiles, shakes her head in awe, in overwhelmed adoration.

“I love you, Lena Luthor,” she says.

Kara slips the gun slowly from its holster, and is delighted to find that Lena isn’t as unaffected by their little encounter as she’d thought. Because Lena takes a big breath in, sinks her teeth into her lower lip. Her fingertips dig into Kara’s chest with just a bit more pressure.

“Mrs. Luthor?” Kara says, finally tearing her eyes away from the woman she loves. “So sorry to interrupt your villain monologue, but we have somewhere to be.”

She tosses the gun to her other hand, and raises it to chest level. Stands ready to face Lillian Luthor and however many goons she wants to send their way, so long as she has Lena by her side.
