You Could Be The One That I Love

Lena doesn’t realize it’s happening at first.

 

At first the day goes as normally as ever. 

 

She meets with investors for The Foundation in the morning where she argues with a CEO and refuses his investment because she’s fairly certain he’s just trying to use good intentions as money laundering. 

 

She has a nice lunch with Kara where Kara tells her about a story she’s working on, signs about three autographs, and offers to punch said CEO for Lena or investigate in an entirely reporter only fashion that has Lena smiling but refusing the offer all the same. 

 

And then she goes back to work for the afternoon before meeting with Brainy at The Tower to test her magical abilities as they’re still mapping that out and learning her limits. 

 

And then she finishes her day at Kara’s apartment where they rehash their afternoons over dinner and dessert and then she remembers she still has work she needs to get done  (she ignores the nervous energy around Kara and the disappointment at her leaving - she also ignores the something else in the air between them that sees to be getting heavier and heavier but neither one of them wants to address) and she goes home. 

 

She falls asleep looking at schematics for cybernetic prosthetics she’s trying to fund and get made en masse for hospitals across the world…

 

And she wakes up in her bed, in the pajamas she had on the night before, to the same announcement on the radio telling her, “It’s a beautiful Friday in National City, the skies are clear, the sun is shining, and Supergirl is smiling down on us all! So give her a wave if you see Miss Kara Zor-El and ask her to let us know if she sees any clouds in that sky.” 

 

And Lena wonders if she dreamed the entire day before. 

 

And then everything happens the same. Again. 

 

She’s a little off balance in the meeting, expectant for the CEO to make his big speech about her refusal and how she needs his money. 

 

She responds in the same way she did prior, “I have enough of my own, I don’t need dirty money to help me out,” but she finds her voice lacks the edge it had initially, that she’s only more confused when she says the same thing to the same reaction and not one person is asking if this has all happened before. 

 

When Lena sees the waitress drop the water glasses in the restaurant again and the same three kids rush up to Kara asking for autographs - she starts to wonder if maybe it wasn’t a dream. 

 

“Have we done this before?” She asks and Kara looks around. 

 

“Lunch? Yes?” 

 

“No, Kara, this . This exact lunch, this conversation.” 

 

“What conversation? Oh that reminds me so I’m working on this story about…”

 

“The library and its impact on the community.”

 

“Yeah. Wait, did I tell you about this already? They have…” 

 

“Kids read to dogs.” 

 

Kara blinks and stares at Lena, confusion in her expression as she fidgets for glasses that aren’t there. She’s still getting used to it, being without them, not needing to hide and Lena takes her hand to settle her as the worry starts to get to both of them. 

 

“Did I text you about it? I’m sorry if I’m repeating…”

 

“No, you didn’t, I think I’m just experiencing some magic premonition stuff.”

 

“Oh? Are you okay? Do you want me to call Brainy or Nia to help you out?” 

 

Lena shakes her head, “I’m seeing Brainy later I can ask him about it. I’m fine.” 

 

“Are you sure?”

 

“Yeah. Yes. Tell me about the dogs and the library.” 

 

“You said you already knew about it,” Kara replies, but Lena squeezes her hand in reassurance, holding it tighter and watching as Kara settles, as her eyes meet hers. 

 

“I’d still like to hear you tell me.” 

 

And Kara smiles, bright and shining, as she does and Lena never lets go of her hand the entire time she listens to the story. 

 

The day goes the same as before and when she meets Brainy she asks him outright, “Is it possible I could see the future?” 

 

And they spend an hour talking about it and ultimately come up with maybe visions but not an entire day and that leaves Lena unsettled, leaves her thinking and theorizing and wondering what exactly might be happening to her…

 

Until she gets to Kara’s apartment and it’s not like they haven’t seen each other in days, it’s literally been maybe five hours at most - but it feels like she can breathe again when she walks in. 

 

It feels like coming home when Kara hugs her like one of those people meeting their loved ones in an airport after a long trip - and it feels safe and warm when they sit on her couch and eat Friday night pizza and debate which movie they’re going to finally get to. 

 

“But you haven’t watched it, Lena, and you keep saying you will,” Kara argues waving around Love Actually. 

 

“I’m not in the mood for a rom-com, or a Christmas movie, it’s May.”

 

“Fine,” Kara relents, “What if we didn’t watch a movie, what if we just talked about our day and stuff instead?” She asks, her voice containing a nervous edge to it that Lena can discern even if she thinks she’s being smooth. 

 

“Is this your way of asking me if I figured out why it felt like we had the same conversation?” Lena offers and this conversation, this moment, hasn’t happened before. It had gone differently. They had decided to just let The Office play in the background as they ate quietly, somehow gravitating closer and closer until Lena was falling asleep, wrapped in the warmth of Kara until suddenly…

 

She’s not thinking about that part. 

 

She’s not thinking about Kara looking at her and that something, that tension, that thing between them that has been growing rapidly the past month or so, that truthfully has been there much longer whether or not they acknowledged it, but now, in the freedom of truth and open secrets and new beginnings has expanded without warning or hindrance - she’s not thinking of that buzzing in the apartment around them begging to be acknowledged. 

 

She’s not thinking of Kara’s hand in hers, the glance to her lips that Kara tried to hide, the glance that had her blushing as Lena did the same. She’s not thinking of them getting closer and closer until she could feel Kara’s shaky breath mix with her own. 

 

She’s not thinking of her heart in her throat and the way she jumped back and stood up and remembered she still had work to do. 

 

She’s not thinking of Kara’s disappointed expression and the way she practically ran for the door even if everything in her screamed at her to stay and talk this out and figure this out. 

 

She’s not thinking it would be a mistake to start this when they’re both just figuring out who they are and where they want to be and who they want to be - Kara especially. 



She can’t throw Kara into an entire new crisis, she can’t destroy their friendship again, just because she wants to kiss her and has thought about it - truthfully, a lot, since they met and thought about even when they weren’t exactly on the best terms and thought about a LOT more since Kara came back home, since the wedding, since…

 

Literally all the time. 

 

But she’s not thinking about that, because it didn’t happen. Not this Friday…

 

Because she’s sitting in Kara’s apartment and Kara is nervous and they aren’t watching TV and they aren’t getting closer. They’re just sitting on the couch and they’re talking. 

 

Except there’s something in the silences, in Kara’s nerves, in the air around the room that Lena knows…

 

A repeated experiment with the same result proves a hypothesis. 

 

And she doesn’t exactly have a hypothesis, doesn’t exactly know what this is that’s happening - but she knows the end result is going to be the same. 

 

So she stops it before it can start. 

 

“I think I’m just tired,” Lena states, “It’s not magic I just had a weird deja vu.” 

 

“Lena,” Kara begins, the worry there in her creased brow, in the way she leans closer without meaning to that has Lena holding her breath because…

 

She just wants her closer still.

 

“I’m fine, Kara,” Lena responds matter of fact and it shuts the other woman down. 

 

“Okay,” she accepts, “But…if you weren’t, you would tell me. Right?” 

 

“Yes. I would.” And Kara’s soft smile has her heart skipping a beat and getting stuck in her throat. It has Kara’s eyes meeting hers and softening still.

 

And there’s a long silence that only furthers that feeling in the air, that stillness, that pressure that feels like a storm is coming. 

 

It has Lena’s heart hammering in her chest, has Kara’s cheeks dusted pink as she fidgets with her hands until Lena takes one in hers, just as she did at lunch, and it settles the Super instantly. 

 

And Kara is looking at her again and Lena feels it just like she had the first time. Feels the world silence, feels and sees only Kara…

 

And she’s leaning, they both are…

 

And Lena freaks out once again. 

 

She runs for the door, leaves Kara there in the apartment with cold pizza and excuses and a mental note to put off whatever this is for another day when Kara realizes what she thinks she wants isn’t what she actually wants…

 

Or so she tells herself. 

 

She wakes up in her bed after leaving Kara’s apartment again and it’s the same day, again, and she knows something is wrong. 

 

The first repeat could be chalked up to a weird dream. 

 

The second - magical deja vu/not ready to accept what may or may not be happening. 

 

The third - Groundhog Day - no other explanation.

 

She cancels the meeting, tells the CEO she’s not interested in an investor who doesn’t actually care, and runs straight to The Tower. 

 

“I’m living the same day over and over again,” she says to Brainy who hmms and listens. 

 

“I admit it can feel like we are in a loop sometimes.”

 

“No I’m in an actual time loop, I’m actually living the same day.” 

 

“Like…”

 

“Groundhog Day, exactly like Groundhog Day.” 

 

Brainy considers, “How did this happen? Could you have done something magically, perhaps a spell gone wrong?” 

 

“I haven’t done any spells outside of our controlled ones, nothing that could do this, and out of everything I’ve read in my mother’s spell book not a single one had anything to do with time,” Lena argues as Brainy rubs his chin and they both try to work through the problem. 

 

“Perhaps it’s not about how it happened, but how to fix it,” he states. 

 

“How can I fix something when I don’t know the cause?” 

 

“How did they solve Groundhog Day?” 

 

“Bill Murray learned an important lesson. So what lesson do I need to learn?” Lena asks and Brainy shrugs. 

 

“What happens on the day?” 

 

They spend all morning trying to solve the problem, going through Lena’s routine, neither one of them finding anything unusual. 

 

“Maybe it’s not about what happens, but what doesn’t?” Brainy asks and Lena doesn’t really want to pull on that thread, doesn’t want to admit that maybe…maybe…

 

Kara texts Lena asking where she is for lunch and that breaks whatever train of thought she and Brainy were starting to let gather steam. 

 

Lena rushes to the restaurant with apologies and the memories of her running from Kara’s apartment hurtling through her mind.

 

“Are you okay?” Kara asks as Lena picks at her salad and watches the water glasses break and the kids rush Kara and she can’t help the soft smile at seeing Kara’s excitement, at the kids returning it with bright eyes and cheery expressions - as Kara can just live her life as herself - the peace and happiness in her that Lena can feel radiating like the sun. 

 

“I’m just tired,” she lies when Kara returns to giving her her full attention, “What’s going on with you?” 

 

And Lena listens to the library story for the third time, listens with the same reactions, the same smile and joy, like it’s the first time she’s hearing it. 

 

And to Kara it is, so Lena keeps it up. Let’s the day do what it’s going to do as she tries to figure out how to fix this. 

 

She ends up at Kara’s apartment that night, let’s The Office play and debates just telling her there’s something weird happening when Kara’s hand finds hers unbidden, her thoughts lost and the tv show paused as blue eyes stare into her soul begging to know that she’s okay. 

 

“What’s going on with you today?” Kara asks, thumb rubbing circles over Lena’s knuckles and Lena holds her breath, feels the air still around them all over again, feels that thing they don’t talk about burn between them. 

 

“I’m just tired,” Lena lies but Kara shakes her head. 

 

“No, you said that already, there’s more going on. Talk to me, please.” 

 

Lena shrugs, “I don’t know how to explain it. It’s just…” 

 

“What?” And Kara is turning to her, sitting up straighter and giving her her full attention, so concerned and so caring that Lena just wants to pull her in close and kiss her but she can’t. 

 

She can’t. 

 

Because it’s not the right time and there may never be a right time ever, because they’re just friends, they’ve always only been just friends, and even if there’s a weird something in the air it doesn’t mean anything. Because they are both pushing forward in their lives, in new uncharted waters, and Kara is getting to be herself, and live as herself, and Lena can not threaten that just because she wants to kiss her and keep kissing her forever. 

 

She pushes Kara’s hand away. 

 

“I just have a lot of stuff I’m working on. I should really get back to it. I’ll call you tomorrow.” 

 

But tomorrow never comes. 

 

Because she leaves Kara’s apartment, she goes home, she throws the schematics into the fireplace and lights them on fire and then she wakes up in her bed and it’s Friday all over again. 

 

She pulls a pillow to her face and she screams.

 

By the seventh repeated Friday Lena has a full on meltdown in the boardroom, laughing as she yells, “None of this matters, this doesn’t…it’s just going to be the same day…” 

 

An hour after that, Kara finds her in the park standing in the fountain without her shoes on and eating a pretzel she bought from one of the food carts that Kara has never seen her eat from.

 

“Hey,” Kara tries, voice careful and pitched in a way that Lena knows sounds placating, like she’s being careful not to startle her like she’s a rabid animal. Kara has her hands out as she approaches the fountain, trying and failing not to look alarmed at Lena’s crazed appearance, “Did you get a pretzel? Are you okay?” 

 

“It’s braided, it’s good. I’m going insane,” Lena says as she sits in the fountain and lets the cold water rush over her, holding out the rest of the pretzel to Kara, “Do you want the rest of this?” 

 

Kara looks around for any sign of some photographer looking to sell a photo of Lena Luthor having a breakdown and eating a braided pretzel while she sits in a fountain in the park and seeing none, she relaxes. 

 

“Okay,” she takes the offered remainder of pretzel and bites into it, asking, “You want to talk about it?” 

 

“There’s no point! Because it doesn’t matter, it’ll just be Friday again and no one will remember this, no one but me.” Lena splashes the water to reiterate her point, leaving a very worried Kara to stare at her.

 

“Why don’t we get out of the fountain and we can talk about this somewhere else?” Kara asks, finishing the pretzel as she does. 

 

“I don’t want to get out of the fountain, I like the fountain, I’ll just live here until I wake up again.” And Lena knows she sounds insane, feels like she’s going insane as she looks at Kara, as she looks and stares at the woman she knows she’s in love with, who can’t possibly love her back, but who looks at her with such care it makes her want to sink further into the fountain to avoid how it makes her heart flip. 

 

“Alright, well if this is where you want to live then I have no choice…” And Kara steps over the edge and sinks into the cold water next to her, to the alarmed stares of everyone around them pointing as Supergirl and Lena Luthor sit in a fountain in the park like two crazy people or two people who really like Friends but forgot the whole dancing part. 

 

“What are you doing?” Lena asks, looking at Kara’s drenched pants and blouse. 

 

“You said you’re living in the fountain so I’m living there too.” 

 

“Why?” 

 

“Because I can’t live somewhere you aren’t. You’re my best friend and if you live in the fountain, I live in the fountain, also I’m worried you’re going to get sick and I can’t so at least I’ll be able to keep you warm or fly you out of here when you fall asleep.” 

 

And those words, best friend, which should bring her comfort only somehow make this very sweet moment a little bitter. 

 

“People are staring at you,” Lena argues, “They’re going to think Supergirl has lost it.” 

 

“I don’t care what they think, as long as they think it about me and not you.” And that does bury the bitter in more sweet, that does make Lena forget the words ‘best friend’ and make her feel warm and hopeful as Kara looks at her while they both sit in a fountain in the park and all Lena wants to do is go home with Kara and stay there. 

 

All Lena wants to do is pull Kara close and kiss her in this fountain. 

 

But if she does she can’t exactly keep Kara as a best friend. 

 

And if she leaves then the day just starts over.

 

“Excuse me?! You can’t be in the fountain!” A park ranger yells as Kara jumps up and argues. 

 

“We’re dealing with something!” 

 

“Oh…Supergirl I…okay…just…you’re really not supposed to be in the fountain…but okay…” And the ranger leaves as Kara sinks back into the water and Lena can’t take her eyes off of her, off this woman who defends her wholeheartedly, who she loves... 

 

“Can you take me home?” Lena asks and wonders if changing the setting, if this might be the answer, if this might get the day to change. 

 

And Kara doesn’t need to be asked twice, just picks Lena up and flies her to her apartment - setting her down on the balcony with ease as Lena keeps two hands on her shoulders and stares, just stares as the sun shines behind her and Kara has never looked more beautiful than in this moment - with semi-wet hair and ruined clothes and the sun bright behind her while blue eyes swim with things unsaid. 

 

Lena feels her throat dry as her hands lower from Kara’s shoulders and stop where the ‘S’ of her suit would be - right where her heart is, where Lena can feel it pounding. 

 

“Thank you, for…today…for being there for me. You’re…you’re a good friend. The best friend I could ever have.” And Lena takes a breath and a step back and tries to ignore the way Kara breathes out, not relieved, disappointed. 

 

“I’m always going to be there for you. And you’re my best friend too, and I…” But Kara never finishes her sentence because Lena is turning to go inside. 

 

“I’m going to lie down, thank you again.” 

 

And she doesn’t know why she’s denying it, why she’s pushing Kara away, but she is, she knows she is, she knows she wants Kara, she’s starting to suspect Kara might feel more than friendship for her too, but why is it so hard to just say to Hell with it and go for it? 

 

She’s gone after what she’s wanted before, she’s faced down boardrooms filled with people who all hated her, she’s faced her own brother, but this…there’s something so terrifying and potentially world shattering in this. 

 

She could lose Kara if she’s reading into it all wrong. She could lose Kara if they try and it doesn’t work out. 

 

She’d rather not have Kara at all than lose her again in any capacity. 

 

So she sends Kara away and falls onto her bed and the day doesn’t actually end but she wakes and it’s Friday much faster than it’s been Friday before. 

 

She gets really drunk in The Tower while she explains to Brainy what’s happening once again with no solution in sight…

 

And the day starts over. 

 

After the tenth repeat where she pretends she’s home sick and Kara lands on her balcony anyway with soup and a smile and Lena runs from her own apartment to avoid Kara - she finally gets an answer as to who is behind it. 

 

“You’re supposed to be smart!” Mxy yells at her as she wakes up on the eleventh Friday, “This should not be taking you this long to figure out. I mean really? You have to be putting some of it together by now.” 

 

“Oh, you’re behind this…” Lena sneers as she sinks into her bed, “If I close my eyes will it start over and you’ll be gone.” 

 

“No, it won’t end until you get it together, Lena.” 

 

“Why?” She sits up and stares at the fifth dimensional imp and he throws his hands up. 

 

“Because you hate musicals and you’re so frustratingly in denial it’s killing me. I had to do something to get you to see.” 

 

“See what?”

 

“What do you know about Quantum Entanglement?” Mxy asks, a smile on his face. 

 

“What do you know about Quantum Entanglement?” She returns, an angry mimicking in her voice. 

 

“Okay that doesn’t suit you, and also…I’m giving you a hint, so take it, figure it out.” 

 

“You could just tell me the answer.” 

 

“You know the answer, you just have to accept it.” 

 

“How?” 

 

Mxy snaps his fingers and he’s gone and the day starts over before it even really began in the first place. 

 

“Mxy visited me, I’m repeating the same day over and over again. He stuck me in Groundhog Day,” Lena tells Kara at lunch on the twelfth Friday. 

 

“He did what?!” Kara asks as she looks around the restaurant. And Lena has tried changing where they eat, has tried not ending the day in Kara’s apartment, has even tried spending the entire day in the tower but nothing has changed, nothing has worked and she tells Kara as much after she proves that it’s all true when she points out the broken water glasses as they happen and the three kids coming over for autographs before they even ask. 

 

“Maybe that’s the problem,” Kara offers, “Nothing changed.” 

 

“What do you mean?” Lena asks, remembering Brainy saying something similar at the beginning of this whole mess, as Kara fidgets with her hands. 

 

“Mxy doesn’t do things without reason? I mean I know he can be…” 

 

“Annoying and irritating and confusing and cryptic and I can go on, I have many adjectives to describe that thing.” 

 

“But he also has a weird kind of purpose?” Kara takes a heavy breath, “He’s probably trying to get you to understand something or learn something or…did you call him a thing?” 

 

“He stuck me in Groundhog Day, Kara! Why are you defending him?” 

 

“He’s a friend, kind of and he also tried to help me fix things with you, so…” Kara seems to realize what she’s said because her mouth gapes and she snaps it shut.

 

“What do you mean?” Lena asks and Kara swallows and confesses. The tapes. The repeated chances to fix their broken friendship. And something settles in Lena then, something that feels like hope clawing its way to the surface, something that screams at her to examine it because if Kara did that when they were at their worst…

 

Then all those fears, all those reasons Kara might end things with her and they might lose each other, might be meaningless. That whatever they are, friends and more, it all means that much more, because… 

 

Because if Kara went through all of that to try to get Lena back in her life…

 

Because if Quantum Entanglement can exist between two people? If they were always meant to meet and the best reality was this one where they worked it out, where they were made stronger, where they are growing as two people who can see the other for who they are and who could be…

 

It hits her then. The answer is Kara. 

 

And it terrifies her. 

 

Because taking that chance, it could ruin them or it could be the best thing to ever happen to her, and if her leaving the apartment, leaving Kara resets the day, then…

 

Taking that chance is the only way to get it to stop. It’s the only way to get to Saturday.

 

“I have to go,” Lena says as she stands and rushes from the restaurant, from Kara, from the answer she’s afraid to face. 

 

The day starts over. 

 

Lena stares at the ceiling, lets her mind wander over the possibilities, over how to say it, over what to do. She could just live in the repeated day forever and never take the chance, never possibly lose Kara, never have to hold her heart out and hope it never breaks. 

 

She can just live the same day, have the same lunch, the same dinner, keep Kara as a friend forever. 

 

She doesn’t know which is worse. Giving Kara her heart with shaking hands and begging her to keep it safe, or never doing that and slowly going insane as she and Kara remain gal pals who have lunch and occasionally sit in fountains when Lena loses it. 

 

The day resets again. 

 

And again. 

 

And again. 

 

Before Lena finally gets out of bed and faces the day. 

 

She decides to use this day as research, to gauge whether or not Kara is in the same murky waters as she is or if she is just reading into looks and touches and silent moments between them. 

 

She ignores the two almost kisses because that could have just been her imagination, right? 

 

The day resets without warning. 

 

“Okay, I get it, denying just starts it over!” Lena yells, hoping Mxy can hear her, as she gets out of bed. 

 

“You look really pretty today,” Lena tells her at lunch and Kara blushes. 

 

“You look really pretty everyday,” Kara returns and that doesn’t really help Lena at all because Kara always compliments her and friends do that too. But Lena blushes all the same.

 

“Kara, if I was hit by a bus would you be upset?” Lena asks, at random, and Kara chokes on her water. 

 

“What?” 

 

“I mean like how would you feel if suddenly I was hit by a bus and was gone?” 

 

“Why are you asking me that?” 

 

“How would you feel?” And Lena knows it’s maybe not the smoothest or best course of questioning but she’s been through too many Fridays now and her brain is starting to break - she doesn’t really have the capacity for smooth or intelligent right now.

 

Kara tells her, “Don’t ask me that, I don’t want to think about that.” 

 

“Would you kill the bus driver?” Lena persists. 

 

“What?! I wouldn’t kill anyone, I mean not without reason, like maybe if they were some evil dictator or...” 

 

“But if they intentionally hit me with the bus, like on purpose, if it was Lex behind the wheel, would you kill him?” 

 

And Lena is speaking in hypotheticals, knows that Kara knows she’s speaking in hypotheticals, and yet, she catches a glimpse of something dark and angry in Kara’s eyes as her hand grips a fork too tight and it bends with ease. 

 

“I don’t want to talk about this. This is morbid and I don’t…I would be devastated, okay? Is that what you want to hear? I would be broken. And if it was…I probably would…” 

 

“You would?” 

 

“I wouldn’t hesitate,” Kara breathes out, swallowing down more water, “Now do you want to explain to me why we are talking about this horrifying scenario of you being hit by a bus and somehow me being unable to stop it?” 

 

“I was just curious.” 

 

“Why?” 

 

“Because…” 

 

But Kara won’t let her off the hook, still stares at her and waits for a proper answer that Lena can’t give. 

 

“I’m doing a survey.”

 

The day resets again.

 

Lena tries another line of questioning at lunch. 

 

“Do you think about the future and like where you want to be in ten years?” 

 

“Yes,” Kara answers, brow furrowed in confusion, “Sometimes. Why are you asking?” 

 

“Because I had a plan, or I thought I did, and I thought I wanted certain things and didn’t want other things and now…” 

 

She doesn’t miss the way Kara’s breathing seems to have stilled, the way her hand flexes unbidden, like it wants to reach across the table for her. 

 

“Now?” 

 

“It feels like it’s changed,” Lena states, taking a breath. 

 

“It does?” Kara asks and Lena doesn’t miss the hope shining in her eyes, the light that emerges unbidden. 

 

“Do you feel that way too? Like things have changed for you?”
