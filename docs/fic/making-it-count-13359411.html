May, 1912

Lena jolts awake, blinking blearily as a whistle sounds outside the rattling window. She takes in the landscape – snowy and barren, despite it being late spring – and Alex and Maggie sleeping in the seat across from her. Kara’s head shifts on her shoulder. Outside, a sign flashes by: ‘Welcome to Wisconsin’.

Right, Lena thinks groggily. The train.

They’re headed to Midvale to meet Eliza Danvers, and Lena is terrified.

Alex and Kara sent their mother a letter as soon as they got to New York telling her they had arrived in the States again, and Eliza had sent back an excited note with some money for train tickets asking that they visit as soon as they had time. Kara had bought their tickets the next day. Alex mentioned Lena and Maggie in the letter, but Lena is still nervous that Eliza will feel inconvenienced by her being there, or might not like her - or worst of all, might think that Kara deserves better. Lena is fresh from a life where she was discouraged from learning how to take care of herself, and Kara and Jess are still teaching her little things about living below the poverty line – how to cook and eat on a budget, how to clean and do laundry. How to buy things by herself, even. Simple things, things everyone else around her knows. Kara is sweet and endlessly patient and Jess is a great teacher, and Lena is trying her best to learn fast and figure things out by herself (and honestly, she actually enjoys doing these things herself instead of relying on servants), but she still feels distinctly useless. After growing up with a mother who criticized her every move, she can’t help but worry.

What will she think of me?

Lena stares out the window as the landscape becomes slightly less empty, scattered with farms, not noticing that she’s bouncing her leg incessantly.

“Nervous?”

Lena jumps. Alex is awake, and looking at her in amusement.

“No! No, I’m just…” Lena stammers, but Alex gives her a piercing stare, one eyebrow raised. Lena deflates.

“I’m nervous. I don’t have a great track record with mothers. Mine died when I was a child, and Lillian and Rhea both hated me.”

“My mother won’t hate you, Lena," Alex says firmly. "She’s a nurse, and as soon as she sees how smart you are she’s going to love you. Besides, she loves us, and so anyone we bring home is loved by association.”

It isn’t that Lena doesn’t believe Alex – it’s more that she has no idea what to expect.

Sensing Lena’s reticence, Alex keeps talking. “If it helps, Maggie is pretty nervous, too. I don’t think she’s ever met a girlfriend’s parents before. Especially this soon.”

“Is it too soon?” Lena mutters to herself, frowning. Maybe she has a different idea of the pace a relationship is supposed to go. Her trysts with women had always been secretive and rapid, and her arranged engagement with Mon-El had happened almost overnight – she was being paraded on his arm only a few days after they properly met for the first time. Now, with Kara, it just feels natural – there’s no doubt that they’re dedicated to each other. But for the first time, Lena thinks about how it must look on the outside. She’s meeting Kara’s mother after knowing her for just over three weeks.

As the thoughts crowd her head, she can’t help but ask.

“…Alex? How long is normal?”

“Well, Eliza has only ever met one person Kara has dated," Alex says thoughtfully. "And that was a long time ago.”

“Really?” Lena asks, her eyebrows raising. Kara must have dated a lot of people, or at least so Lena assumes. She’s sweet and charming and wonderful. Had she really only brought one person home?

Alex nods. “Yeah. I don’t think she’s ever really been serious about anyone before she met you. She’s had flings, and a few longer relationships, but she never really got invested. That’s why I was so surprised when she said she was in love with you after a day and a half.”

Lena blushes, looking down at Kara’s sleeping face with a smile. “She told you that?”

“Yeah. I thought she was crazy. You must be on a whole other level.” 

Lena laughs. “I don’t think so.”

“I see the way she looks at you, Lena," Alex counters. "And, I don’t think you’re so bad either.”

Lena gasps dramatically. “Alex Danvers, are you admitting that you like me?”

“Yeah, well. It’s a one-time thing, so enjoy it, Dawson.”

Lena feels warm at the use of her new chosen name. No more Luthor. Never again.

“Well, what about you and Maggie? You’ve known each other for, what, two days more than Kara and I?” Lena teases, feeling a sudden affection for Kara’s sister as she sputters indignantly.

“That…that’s none of your –” Alex sputters, her ears turning red. "You know what, I take it back. You’re the worst.”

Lena laughs as Alex kicks out at her from across the seat, jostling Maggie out of her nap. Maggie grumbles and hides her face in Alex’s shoulder until she stills again. Kara sleeps on, oblivious.

The conversation makes her feel better, but Lena is still unspeakably nervous. Alex and Kara talk with great affection about Eliza, but she can’t help but picture Lillian waiting for them on the train platform.

When Kara wakes up she's too excited for Lena to try to bother her with her nervous feelings – she’s pointing enthusiastically at things as they pull into the train station, talking to Alex about their childhood memories. She practically carries Lena off the train and onto the platform in her eagerness, tugging at her hand like an excitable dog. The similarity only gets more pronounced when she sees Eliza, and she runs bounding towards her to crash into her arms. Alex walks at a more moderate pace, shaking her head fondly.

Eliza – at least, what Lena can see of her that isn’t concealed by Kara – is a distinguished-looking blonde woman in her mid-fifties with a surprisingly kind face. Like Lillian, she’s still very beautiful, but instead of Lillian’s cold severity she actually looks human - Lena can see the similarity to Alex in her features but her smile is closer to Kara’s, despite the lack of blood relation. She has laugh lines and warm eyes and naturally grey-streaked hair. She holds Kara close, laughing at whatever is being rambled in her ear, and reaches towards Alex to beckon her to join the hug. Lena hangs behind with Maggie, listening.

“Oh, my beautiful girls, back from their world travels to see their little old mother!” Eliza kisses each of their cheeks, and Alex laughs.

“Mom, you aren’t that old.”

“I certainly feel old knowing that my daughters are bringing home girlfriends for me to meet!” Eliza says, peering between Alex and Kara's heads at Lena and Maggie. Lena starts to fidget, and Maggie sighs before grabbing her arm and pulling her forward. She leans close and mutters in Lena’s ear.

“Come on - if I have to do this, so do you.”

Kara finally releases Eliza and grabs Lena’s free hand, pulling her excitedly forward. “Eliza, this is Lena. Lena, this is my foster mother.”

Before Lena can so much as hold out a hand to shake, Eliza opens her arms and Lena is pulled into a gentle hug.

“It’s wonderful to meet you, Lena. Kara wasn’t exaggerating when she talked about how lovely you are,” Eliza says with a smile as she pulls back, and Lena blushes furiously, unsure how to respond.

“I – thank you, Mrs. Danvers.”

“Oh goodness, call me Eliza, please! Mrs. Danvers is much too formal for family.”

Lena gapes silently for a second at that – family? - but she’s saved by Alex pushing a nervous-looking Maggie forward.

“And Maggie, the girl who stole Alex’s heart," Eliza says with a wink. She hugs Maggie too, and Maggie seems only slightly more comfortable with it than Lena was. She, at least, got some warning. "You must be a real gem to have kept her around for long enough to meet me - you know, I thought she’d never settle down.”

As Eliza leads them to the car, Lena can hear Maggie hissing in Alex’s ear.

“You didn’t tell your mom when we met?”

“She doesn’t even know we were on the Titanic!" Alex whispers back. "I figured we’d drop one bomb at a time, here.”

“Alex – “

“Mags, it’s okay. She’s not going to care how long ago we met. I promise.”

Maggie sighs, nodding along despite her clear reservations. But Lena echoes Maggie’s worries. Eliza has been disconcertingly kind so far, but Lena is still waiting for the other shoe to drop.

The disarming niceness continues on the drive to the house, as Eliza tuts at the fact that none of them have any luggage. When they arrive Eliza insists on making tea for everyone, and they all settle in the sitting room with biscuits and steaming cups. As they sit down Lena can feel that even confident Kara is getting stiff and nervous at the prospect of telling Eliza the events of the last month.

As they sip their drinks, Eliza fusses over Alex – “You look so thin, have you been eating properly?” “I just…had to spend a few weeks taking it easy, after we got here. It’s fine, Mom.” – until she finally asks the question they’ve all been waiting for.

“I can’t believe you girls didn’t even bring any extra clothes with you. Where are all your things?”

Kara swallows hard. She starts to fidget, and Lena laces their fingers together and squeezes comfortingly.

“This is really all we have," Kara says, trying for a jaunty tone. "Half this stuff is borrowed from Clark and Lois.”

“Didn’t you bring anything onto the ship with you?”

Kara nods. “We did. But it’s…gone.”

“What do you mean?” Eliza looks confused, looking between Kara and Alex. Kara takes a deep breath.

“Um. Okay. Do you remember a few weeks ago, hearing about the Titanic?”

Eliza’s face turns serious. “Of course, the ship that sank coming over from England. All those people…” She trails off, pressing a hand to her chest in sympathetic grief. 

Lena’s hand twitches. Thoughts emerge unbidden, ones she's tried to fend off every night since they arrived in America - darkness, cold, screaming, panic. She shakes her head, focusing on Kara’s grip on her hand instead. 

Kara rubs her thumb over the back of Lena’s hand. “Yeah. That was, uh…we were…” Eliza is looking more and more alarmed as Kara fumbles with her words, and Kara has to swallow hard before she can finish. “That was our ride. Our stuff sank.”

There’s a moment of loaded silence. Eliza’s face goes from curiosity, to shock, to mild horror.

“You were on the Titanic?” Her voice is quiet, wavering. “You – over a thousand people died. I read in the Tribune, the lifeboats –”

Thanks to Cat Grant commandeering the Carpathia’s radio, Catco had been the first to report the accurate numbers of the disaster, Lena knows. Clearly, Eliza is a subscriber.

Kara nods, her leg bouncing nervously. “Yeah. 1500, actually. We…got lucky.”

Eliza stands, pulling Alex and Kara both closer with shaking hands. “You both got onto lifeboats?”

Kara shrugs. “Well, Alex and Maggie did.” Alex elbows her, and Kara winces. Eliza’s eyes widen in alarm.

“What? You didn’t – you were – how did you –"

Kara cuts in before Eliza can finish.

“Lena saved me.”

All eyes turn to Lena, who she shakes her head, alarmed at the sudden spotlight. “I didn’t, Kara. You were the one that found that piece of wood.”

“And I would have stayed in the water if you didn’t think of that thing with the lifebelt," Kara insists. "And you kept me conscious; you wouldn’t let me fall asleep. I’d be dead without you.”

“You pulled me back over in the first place, that first night. If you didn’t follow me, I would have –” Lena’s voice cracks, and she quickly hides her face in her hands to cover her loss of composure. The memories are coming back full force – the desolation, the cold, the fear. She feels them rising inside her like a tide. Kara takes Lena's face between her hands, and the warmth cuts through the bad thoughts.

“Lena, breathe. Look at me,” Kara murmurs. Lena does, her eyes blurry with tears. She feels a warm hand rubbing her back. Kara's voice is slow and calm, an even hum to banish the cold of her memories. “Lena, we’re okay. We’re both okay.”

Lena nods, and tries to breathe evenly. Kara leads her in taking deep breaths until she’s calmed down enough to feel the burn of embarrassment. The hand on her back turns out to belong to Eliza, who is looking down at her with all the warmth and motherly concern that Lillian never had.

“I’m okay. I’m sorry,” Lena rasps, taking the glass of water that Alex hands her and taking a shaky drink.

“Honey, don’t apologize. What you four have been through…” Eliza trails off, her own eyes wet. “I can’t imagine – how on earth did you two get separated?”

She turns back to Alex, who tenses.

“Um. Well, Kara was with Lena, and she…” Alex trails off, looking at Kara helplessly.

“See, this is why we wanted to tell you in person.” Kara takes a deep breath, squeezing Lena’s hand. “We…okay. I got framed and arrested and locked in the brig, and Lena had to come break me out. And Alex...was shot. Maggie had to get her to a lifeboat with a doctor as soon as she could,” Kara finishes in a rush, and her face scrunches up, bracing for the impact. 

“Alex was what?”

Eliza moves quickly towards Alex, patting her face and shoulders as if to make sure she’s actually there. Alex sighs, moving her shirt to reveal the bandage covering the still-healing bullet hole on her shoulder. Eliza lifts the gauze, examining the wound with the eyes of a medic.

“I’m fine, mom. I promise," Alex says, gently pushing Eliza's hands away and pulling her shirt back over the spot. "Maggie yelled at the guard until he let me onto a lifeboat.”

Maggie goes slightly red. “I mean, I had help.”

Eliza looks from Lena to Maggie and back a few times, both of whom are looking extremely uncomfortable.

“So, you’re telling me,” Eliza starts, her voice shaking with emotion. Lena braces herself, closing her eyes. Here it is. I separated them and she hates me and Kara is going to realize that I’m not worth it.

“You’re telling me that you two…saved my daughters.”

Blinking in surprise, Lena looks up at Eliza. The older woman is looking at her with an expression of such pure, genuine gratefulness that Lena has to look away.

“I…I wouldn’t…say that, exactly,” Lena stutters, looking at Kara for help. Kara just smiles at her, shaking her head.

“You did, Lena.”

“We wouldn’t be here without them,” Alex nods in agreement, her rubbing over the back of Maggie’s hand soothingly.

Before Lena can blink, she and Maggie are pulled from their respective seats and into a tight, comforting hug. Lena can feel Eliza’s tears on her shoulder, and it takes a few seconds, but she manages to relax marginally into it.

“Thank you,” Eliza says with quiet ferocity. “Thank you.”

Eliza insists on hearing the whole story after that, and Lena curls herself into Kara’s side as she relays what happened that night. She tries not to focus on the words, instead soothing herself with the way Kara’s voice buzzes evenly when Lena lays her head on her chest. Kara’s arm is tight around her, and when Kara finishes and Alex starts to tell her side, she pulls her even closer. Maggie, in her relaxed way, fills in the blanks where Alex was unconscious, but Lena stays quiet.

Eliza expresses surprise at Cat Grant’s intervention, but ultimately isn’t surprised that she seemed to like Kara.

“You’ve always been a charmer, Kara. As I’m sure Lena can attest.”

Lena smiles tightly, still trying to repress the swirling anxiety in her gut. Eliza loves Maggie already. She must think that Lena is a complete brat.

It takes a lot of explaining and tearful hugs, but finally Eliza bustles to the kitchen to start dinner, wiping at her eyes.

“She needs a minute alone, I think,” Kara whispers. “It’s a lot of news to process.” 

Sure enough, Eliza is in a more cheerful mood as everyone tucks into the food she made, although she reaches to touch Kara and Alex much more than strictly necessary. They both seem to actually lean into the touch – perhaps they need the comfort, too.

“So, Kara, tell me how you two met. I’ve never seen you so happy before – you’re practically glowing,” Eliza says, stroking Kara’s blushing face affectionately.

Kara laughs nervously, fidgeting with her glasses. “We, uh. We met on the Titanic, actually.”

Eliza looks a bit surprised. “You met…on the ship? How long have you known each other?” Coming from anyone else, Lena would probably take her words as a judgement or aspersion, but Eliza just seems to be amused.

“Um. Three weeks?” Kara says, blushing. Again Lena is bracing for impact, but again Eliza surprises her. She just chuckles, looking to Alex.

“And what did Alex and Maggie think of your whirlwind romance?” Eliza winks at Lena, and Lena relaxes a bit.

Maggie quickly shoves a large bite of food into her mouth, looking at Alex apologetically as she glares.

“Well," Alex says carefully, "I thought Kara was being crazy, to be honest – OW!” 

Alex glares at Kara, and Lena can assume that Kara has kicked her under the table.

“Fine," Alex grumbles, clearly reaching for Maggie's hand under the table. "Maggie and I met on the Titanic as well.”

There’s a moment of surprised silence.

“So, both of my daughters – who, before now, have been so wary of commitment as to spend their twenties traveling the world – brought people home to meet me after three weeks?” Eliza says, putting down her fork.

“Yes?” Alex says, the word turning up in intonation at the end into a nervous question.

After a moment, Eliza bursts into delighted laughter. She raises her wine glass, winking at Lena. “You two must be something very, very special.”

Lena lets out a relieved breath. Eliza is so genuine, so relaxed – she can see where Kara gets it. She didn’t think that mothers like this actually existed.

Eliza does get excited when she finds out that Lena is an aspiring scientist, and asks her excited questions about her interests and her old application to Oxford. She encourages Kara to pursue the job opportunity with Cat Grant, and when she asks Alex about her plans now that she’s back home, Alex perks up slightly.

“I think I’m going to go to medical school.”

Kara gasps. “Alex, you didn’t tell me you wanted to be a doctor!”

“I didn’t tell anyone, until Maggie. I didn’t think it was a realistic dream,” Alex shrugs, and Maggie smiles proudly. “But after everything…I don’t see the point in not trying, you know? I’d rather try and fail than not try at all.”

Eliza wipes at her eyes again, clasping Alex’s hand. “Oh, honey, I’m so proud of you. You’re going to be amazing. If you ever need any help -”

“I know. Thanks, Mom.”

 

 

After dinner Eliza, Alex, and Maggie settle in for more wine and some board games. Kara, however, asks Lena if she wants to go for a walk instead. Lena readily agrees, borrowing a coat from Eliza and looping her arm through Kara’s.

As they walk through Midvale, Kara leads them on a winding path and points out 'interesting' places that get more and more mundane until, finally, Lena asks.

“Kara, why did you really bring me out here?”

Kara is silent for a moment. She points to a dark arch across the road, with a wide, fenced-in field behind it. All at once, Lena understands.

The cemetery.

Kara pushes the gate open and leads her slowly through well-maintained paths until they reach two large headstones. Fresh flowers are sitting between the graves, the hedges on either side trimmed neatly. Kara is squeezing Lena’s hand so tightly that she’s lost all feeling in it, but she ignores the discomfort. When she speaks, Kara’s voice comes out thick and quiet.

“I wanted you to meet them. While we’re here.”

Lena keeps her voice hushed, as well. “Of course.” They stand silently for a few moments.

“Your mother’s name was Alura?” Lena asks, taking in the letters carved deep into the stone.

“Yeah.”

“It’s beautiful.”

Kara nods, reaching out and gently tracing the letters before letting her hand fall.

“We actually lived a few towns over, but Eliza arranged for them to be buried here so I could visit," Kara says softly, her eyes never leaving the headstone. "But I didn’t come here very often. I don’t – I’ve never connected with this place. It usually just reminds me that they’re gone.”

Lena nods, rubbing Kara’s arm gently, and Kara continues.

“I just…I thought that maybe if I brought you here…”

“I’m glad you brought me," Lena says, squeezing Kara's arm. And she is glad - as much as Kara doesn't connect with this place, it makes Lena feel trusted to be brought here. Like Kara wants Lena involved in every part of her life. Even the hard parts.

After a few more moments standing by the graves, Kara leads her to a nearby bench and they sit, huddled together against the spring cold.  

“People always told me that they’re in a better place," Kara says, rubbing her hands together. "That they’re watching over me. But I never felt that, not really. I sort of resigned myself to the idea that they were gone forever. But that night, when we were floating – " Kara cuts herself off, her eyes shiny. She sniffles, taking a deep breath and continuing. "I thought I was going to die. I think, maybe…I don’t know,” Kara finally trails off, huffing. Her breath fogs in the chilly air.

“Tell me.” Lena kisses Kara’s shoulder, shifting closer.

Kara shakes her head. “I don’t know. There was a moment where I felt like I was about to…let go.” Lena’s grip tightens as she considers that possibility. “But I thought I saw my mother. Or, heard her? I don’t know. It was all so hazy. And then I felt…something. And then I saw you, and you talked me through.” Kara sighs. “Does that sound crazy?”

“Not at all. A lot of people say that they saw things they couldn’t explain when they were near death," Lena reasons. 

“Well, what do you think?”

Lena purses her lips, thinking for a moment before she replies. “I think…that you saw what you saw. I don’t think there’s any way to know for sure. I just know that whether it was real or not, it brought you back to me, and I’m grateful.”

Kara nods, seeming satisfied with that answer. Eventually Kara feels Lena shiver, and pulls them to their feet.

“Let’s go home.”

As they walk hand in hand, Kara still seems thoughtful, and Lena can sense something below the surface. 

“Kara?” she asks quietly.

“Hmm?” Kara kicks at a stone on the road, and it skitters onto the grass.

“Would you like to tell me about them?”

Kara’s pace slows, and she smiles at Lena – a heartbreaking thing, sadness and surprise and relief all at once.

"I - you don't have to, if you don't want to," Lena says, squeezing her hand. "I just -"

Kara squeezes back. “No, I do. It's just...nobody has ever asked me that. Not even Alex.”

Lena frowns. “You’ve never talked to anyone about them?”

“Not really. I always thought that Alex and Eliza would think I wasn’t grateful to them if I did. And I think they thought I didn’t want to talk about them at all.”

The rest of the walk home is spent in conversation. Kara reveals more and more of herself with every story told, relaying all her good memories of her parents as well as some of the bad ones. Lena sees the picture of who Kara is filling in slowly as she talks about her mother’s sense of justice, her father’s sense of humour, how they loved to sing and encouraged her to take up drawing. By the time they get back, Kara is beaming, and Lena feels like she’s done something right.

When they get back to the house they join the others in a few games of Parcheesi before everyone retires to bed. Kara leads her up the stairs to her childhood bedroom, and as she turns the lamps on, the book of Kara’s life opens a little wider.

The walls are covered in paper. Most are drawings, of varying levels of skill. A few feature Alex and Eliza and a man with a kind face that she can only assume is Jerimiah. The ones closest to her bed are of two kind-looking middle-aged people, probably Kara’s parents - she can see Kara in her mother's eyes, in the shape of her father's jaw. Others feature young people that Lena doesn’t recognize in candid scenarios. Each drawing is unique – a pretty teenage girl leaning against a tree, a boy with a patchy beard and a nice smile throwing a baseball, an attractive woman in her late 20s with glasses and a firm stare sitting behind a desk.

“Your first affairs?” Lena says cheekily. None of the drawings are even remotely racy, but Kara flushes anyways, and snorts.

“My first crushes, maybe. Very much unrequited, all of them.”

“Was this one of your teachers?” Lena points to the stern older woman, noting her familiar features – intense eyes, strong jaw, dark hair. “I think you have a type, darling.”

Kara laughs, nodding, and then inexplicably starts to laugh harder.

“What?” Lena asks, bemused. It wasn’t that funny.

“It’s just – I hadn’t thought, but – she was my first real crush, when I was 14. She was my English teacher, and uh. She was Irish.”

After a pause Lena dissolves into giggles, and Kara follows suit.

“You’ve got to be kidding,” Lena gasps, her own accent coming through in her amusement.

“I guess I was just waiting for you,” Kara says, winking. Lena snorts, and goes back to investigating the walls.

An entire section of wall near the window features images of numerous national monuments and pages torn from travel books. A world map decorates the back of the door, with various cities circled in red – Lena examines it. Paris. London. Dublin. Amsterdam. Berlin. Rome.  

“You really wanted to travel, didn’t you?” Lena teases, gesturing to the mishmash of photographs, magazine clippings, news articles, and what look to be textbook pages that wallpaper the area.

“I did, yeah. I found a travel book at the library when I was 16. Jerimiah used to bring me magazines with pictures from overseas.”

“Clearly, you got your dream,” Lena murmurs. When she was 16, she had been in Finishing School. Her mother had caught her kissing a female classmate that year, and had her whipped like a servant. She had known so little about the world that she didn’t even know what to dream about, except that she wanted to get away.

Kara embraces her from behind, setting her chin on Lena’s shoulder. “Well, I always dreamed about meeting someone who made me feel the way my parents told me love should feel. I definitely got that dream.”

“And what did they tell you about love?” Lena asks blithely. Kara's answer is not what she expects.

“That it should be work, but work that you want to do. That it should exhilarate and sustain you, make you feel safe and warm but challenge you when you need it to. That your partner should be your friend as well as your lover. That love alone is strong, but you need to choose to nurture it and fight for it, and keep choosing, over and over, every day for the rest of your lives. And I choose you.”

Everyone scrambling so early in the morning isn’t too unusual right now, with the War creating constant headlines. Intelligence has been coming from Europe for years now, but since the United States had finally joined in the effort in April the reports have been getting more and more detailed. Reports of the Great War to end all wars, of millions dead from automatic gunfire and devastating new chemical weapons and terrible conditions in the trenches. Every day, it seems to get worse.

But something feels different, today. There’s an unease in the air, a heavy weight seeming to weigh on every shoulder, and every few paces Kara catches sight of a pale-faced co-worker staring into his hands or blinking, bewildered, at friends trying to give comfort.

Finally Kara catches sight of Clark, standing next to a junior reporter that started a few months ago. He’s handing the man a mug of tea, and Kara jogs over and taps his elbow as the reporter takes a shaky sip.

“What happened?” Kara whispers, looking around at the morose faces. “Did someone die?”

Clark sighs, shaking his head. His face is stubbly, like he forgot to shave this morning, and when he speaks she understands why.

“The first round of the draft was called this morning.”

Kara’s stomach sinks to the floor.

They had announced conscription months ago, just after the announcement about joining the war was made. Kara had known it was coming – but part of her had hoped, naively, that perhaps they would get enough volunteers that it wouldn’t be necessary. Clearly that was a pipe dream.

Clark looks less devastated than some of the other men in the office, but still Kara has to ask.

“Did you…?”

Clark had been forced to sign up for the draft last month, along with every other man between 21 and 30 after the announcement that the army would be starting conscription. James and Winn had as well, and Kara has been worried sick ever since.

“No,” Clark says, rubbing at his stubble. “No, not this time. But a few people here did. Have you talked to Jimmy or Winn?”

“I haven’t talked to anyone,” Kara says quietly.

All Kara wants to do from that moment on is leave – gather everyone and sprint to James and Winn’s place, and confirm that the worst can’t be true. Have James give her a big bear hug and tell her everything is fine, and they’re all going to stay here together. That none of them are going to be shipped off to hell to shoot at Germans over a conflict nobody really understands.

She works the rest of the day in quiet terror. By the end of it, strangely, her mood has flipped – rather than wanting to go home, she’s almost afraid to do so. She’s terrified of the truth she might find. And when she opens her apartment door to find Winn sitting at her kitchen table, his eyes red and Lena’s arm over him, she knows she was right. Lena’s school notes are abandoned on the kitchen counter, and Kara can feel the grief in the room like a fog.

“No,” Kara breathes, when Winn looks up and she can see the resignation in his eyes. “No. No, they can’t make you go. They can’t.”

“I have to,” Winn says hoarsely. A tear tracks down Lena’s cheek, and Kara can tell that both of them have shed countless more before she got here. “And James, too. We both got called up. He’s at Clark and Lois’ right now.”

“No,” Kara says again, her voice breaking. She sinks into a chair opposite them, and her work bag drops from boneless fingers. “Its not…it’s not fair!”

Winn laughs humourlessly. “It’s not. But that’s the lottery.”

“You shouldn’t have to go!” Kara rages. Her hands ball into fists, and Lena lays a soft hand over one that keeps her from doing what she wants to do and hitting the table out of sheer powerlessness. Some bureaucrat somewhere pulled her friends names from a hat and could very well be throwing them to their deaths, and they probably didn’t lose a bit of sleep over it.

“Better me than someone with a family, right?” Winn says, trying for a smile. Kara’s eyes start to burn.

“You have a family. You have us. We’re your family.”

Winn’s eyes water again, and Lena rubs his back with her free hand.

“Will you and James at least be stationed together?” Lena asks, her voice soft. It’s the same voice she uses when Kara has a bad night, waking from dreams where she’s lost everyone she loves. She gets the feeling that she’ll be having more of those in the near future.

“Probably not,” Winn sighs. “It seems to be random.”

“When do you leave?”

“Next week for basic training. Somewhere in Michigan, I think,” Winn says, wiping his nose. “I haven’t gotten a letter yet – just heard my number on the radio. Then we’re shipped to Europe.” His shoulders shake, and his face scrunches like he’s trying to stave off a sob. “We get off the worst maritime disaster in history, only for there to be a stupid war. I think someone up there has it out for us.”

Kara moves to the other side of the table, putting her arm over him just underneath Lena’s – and just like that, he breaks. He leans into Kara’s shoulder, and Kara’s eyes meet Lena’s over his head. Lena looks desperately sad and sympathetic; but for a split second before she catches Kara looking, she also looks terrified.

 

 

 

Kara finds out why when she visits Alex later.

“Part of me doesn’t want to believe it,” Kara says, slumping into an armchair. It’s covered in dog hair, and one of the many dogs in question comes and puts its head on Kara’s lap. It’s one of the younger ones named Reuben, a cheerful mutt with floppy ears, and Kara is instantly cheered a little by his sweet brown eyes. “Where’s Maggie?”

“She’s out,” Alex says vaguely, rubbing her eyes. “We had a disagreement.”

Kara frowns, patting Reuben’s head. “Over what?”

Alex takes a moment before uttering the words that shake Kara’s world to the core.

“About me enlisting as a medic.”

The silence following that statement rings in Kara’s ears like the aftermath of a church bell. Alex won’t look at her, instead looking stubbornly out the apartment window, and when Kara finds her voice again it’s hoarse with panic.

“No.”

“Kara…” Alex sighs, but Kara stands up, dislodging Reuben and ignoring his sound of indignation.

“Alex, you can’t. You can’t leave me.”

Kara’s protest is strangely weaker now than it was earlier with Winn. Part of her is terrified, but part of her is also hurt – that Alex would even consider this, consider throwing herself into danger where Kara can’t follow. It feels like a betrayal.

“It’s not about you, or about me,” Alex says tiredly, like she’s repeating an earlier argument. Probably the one that upset Maggie. “It’s about helping people who need it.”

“You help people here!” Kara insists. “You have patients!”

Alex shakes her head. “I haven’t made any decisions yet. But I can’t make any promises, either.”

The thought of Alex leaving is intolerable. Kara hasn’t been more than a half hour tram ride away from her since they were children, and the idea of Alex being in a war zone and having no idea how she’s doing from minute to minute makes Kara feel like she’s drowning. But to her horror, Alex’s resolve only seems to harden the closer the days inch towards Winn and James’ departure. Maggie gets less sad and seems to come to some sort of acceptance, and by evening before the ship-out they’re even more affectionate than usual.

With Winn and James both shipping out, they’re forced to give up their apartment, and the small farewell party (mostly spearheaded by Lucy) is held at Clark and Lois’ place. Everyone gathers naturally on the living room floor just like old times, perhaps needing some kind of comfort when everything is about to be upheaved; but after a few half-hearted rounds of cards, everyone puts aside their games settles on the hardwood together in one big cuddly pile instead. The radio fills in the quiet in the background, some jazz song that’s a little too merry for the mood playing over what very well could be their last night all together.

In the air is a bittersweet knowledge – that though they got through the Titanic together, each of them will be fighting on their own now.

“Promise you’ll write,” Lucy says, the first to speak up and the quietest Kara has ever heard her voice. “Promise you won’t forget us.”

“As much as we can,” James says. Kara’s head is half on his chest, Lena tucked into her side and Jess somewhere near her waist, and his low baritone is comforting even now.

“You guys need to promise you won’t forget us,” Winn says, his face buried in the crook of his lover’s neck. “We’re going to need as much comfort as possible, you know. I wasn’t made for damp European weather.”

“We’ll send care packages,” Maggie says, glancing at Alex just on the other side of Kara and Lena. “You’ll be the most spoiled soldiers on the front.”

Clark gets out the old pillows and sleeping mats that night.

The next morning is a brisk affair. Winn and James’ bags are loaded onto the train to head to training camp, and they have just a few moments on the platform to hug everyone before they have to jump on – and after a minute or so of waving, the train disappears into the distance and the platform crowd starts to disperse. Kara can see parents and children, siblings and spouses, all crying for loss of their family members.

When Alex quietly invites her over for lunch, just the two of them, Kara knows.

She sits silently, picking at a fingernail, as Alex fixes two sandwiches. She takes one small bite, but it turns her stomach – and Alex sighs, putting down her own lunch and handing Kara a box with a bow on top.

Kara opens it to find it full of oil paints and watercolours, and a set of brand new charcoal pencils.

“You’re giving me these so that I’m less upset that you’re leaving.” Kara states it as a fact, not a question, and Alex sighs.

“You’re going to need a distraction.”

Kara puts the box down.

“If you’re enlisting, so am I.”

Alex, clearly expecting this answer, fires back right away.

“You’re going to leave Lena here alone?”

“You’re leaving Maggie!” Kara says loudly, even as she knows Alex is right.

“Maggie understands,” Alex says patiently. “Kara, this war isn’t some valiant effort for freedom. It isn’t something you should put your life on the line for. It’s a political dispute played out with the lives of innocent people.”

“I know that, I’m a journalist,” Kara grumbles, pushing her plate away. “So why are you going?”

“Because those innocent people need doctors,” Alex says, firm and unshakeable. Kara knows that tone – it’s the tone that Alex can’t be talked out of.

Kara sighs, leaning forward onto the table. The fight is leaving her, being slowly replaced with cold dread.

“I need to know you’re here and you’re safe,” Alex says, taking Kara’s hand. “Taking care of everyone else. That’s what’s going to keep me going.”

“That’s emotional blackmail.”

“Whatever works,” Alex says with a small smile. Kara can’t find it in her to return it. “By the time I get back, you’ll be a regular Monet.”

“You don’t even know who that is.”

Alex shrugs. “You and Lena talk about him a lot.”

Alex tugs at Kara’s hand a little, sort of encouragingly; Kara pulls her into a hug, hard and fierce. She can feel Alex relax into it, probably relieved that Kara doesn’t hate her. Kara thinks she herself probably won’t be relaxed until Alex comes home.

“If you’re not home in a year, I’m enlisting and coming after you,” Kara mumbles into Alex’s hair. Alex laughs a little, her shoulders shuddering.

“Then let’s hope it’s over in a year.”

 

8. Chapter 8

December 1917

 

 

Kara’s first holiday without Alex around is shaping up to be a bleak affair.

It’s not for lack of trying. Lena is doing her best to make the apartment cheery, and they have weekly dinners with Lucy, Jess, Lois, and Maggie. But Clark had gotten the conscription call only a few months after Winn and James, shipping out at the end of last month; so by the time December rolls around, every gathering is haunted by the spectre of someone missing. With half of their little family gone and reachable only through letters that sometimes get caught up in the mail or cut up by censors and arrive weeks late, it’s hard to feel the spirit of the season.

“I should be over there, too,” Kara finally admits one day a few weeks before Christmas, drawing idle patterns on the frosted glass of their apartment window. “I should be doing something.”

She knows it hurts Lena to think about the possibility. But she can’t help it – Alex is overseas, suffering a harsh winter in the mud and muck with enemy shells constantly on the horizon, patching up horrific injuries in a shoddy tent. Alex won’t admit as much in her letters, but Kara knows that her sister is suffering.

“Kara, we’ve been through this,” Lena says softly, touching Kara’s elbow. Kara presses her forehead against the cold surface.

“I should! Alex is over there,” Kara says, her voice cracking. “What am I doing here? Writing about food shortages? I should -”

“What could you do over there, exactly?” Lena replies, her voice a little more firm. “You’re not a doctor, or a nurse. Do you really want to ship over just to do clerical work or shoot at people?”

“I might be able to -”

“What you’re doing here is just as important,” Lena insists, joining Kara on the windowsill and taking Kara’s hand from the window, lacing their fingers together. “You’re giving people news about their friends and family. You’re keeping people updated. You’re taking care of our friends. Of me.”

Lena is right. Kara knows she is, and they have been over this time and time again ever since Alex left – but it’s one thing to hear it, and another to accept it in her heart.

“I just feel so powerless,” Kara whispers. “Alex is over there, and Winn and James and Clark, and I just…every day all I can think about is what if they…what if we went through everything on the Titanic and they still -“ 

Kara’s voice seizes up before she can voice her real fear. The fear she’s had ever since they were all astronomically lucky enough to survive a catastrophe – that their collective luck will run out, and she’ll lose them all.

“You can’t save them by sheer force of will, here or over there,” Lena says, kissing each of Kara’s knuckles. Her lips are warm against cold skin, and Kara sighs at the sweet familiarity of it. “And what would I do without you? I didn’t jump onto a sinking ship for you just to watch you throw yourself into a war zone needlessly.”

Kara smiles, just a tiny bit. Kara might be the wordsmith usually, but Lena has a funny way of knowing exactly what to say sometimes.

“We all fight in our own ways,” Lena says finally, pressing Kara’s hand to her chest. “I need you here, Kara Danvers. We all do.” 

 

 

 

Even with Lena’s pep talks, Kara doesn’t realize exactly how much she could be needed until a few days later.

Her latest assignment is a hard one – an investigative piece on the city’s orphanages, which are starting to overflow and show their cracks with an influx of war orphans. She’s seen everything from harried but well-meaning proprietors working overtime to ensure their wards are fed, to cruel and detached wardens accepting money from the state when the kids in their care don’t even have a bed to sleep in. She’s seen lonely but cared-for kids, and she’s seen kids with their heads shaved being marched to a measly dinner like they’re heading to the war themselves. They’re all meant to educate the children, but few of them seem to actually make the effort. It makes her more grateful than ever for Alex and Eliza, and she’s been writing to Alex almost daily to vent about the conditions.

It’s on the fourth day of her on-site investigations that she finds Cora.

It’s not the worst orphanage she’s been in in the last few days, but it certainly isn’t the best. The building is old and drafty, the beds overcrowded, and the management seems to have given up trying to keep order. It’s chaos, every child for themselves, and Kara is studiously taking notes for her expose when a small voice calls out just to her left.

“Miss Kara?”

Kara whirls around, locking eyes with the girl who somehow knows her name. She’s small, probably 10 or 11, and slight – a wisp of a child sitting on a rickety bed, with chestnut hair and brown eyes sunk into dark bruise-coloured circles, and she’s looking at Kara like she’s seen a ghost.

She looks familiar, but it takes Kara a moment to place exactly where from. When she realizes, her heart lurches.

“…Cora?” Kara asks cautiously, taking a step closer. The girl’s eyes get ever wider.

“Are you real?” Cora asks in a whisper, her face stark white under her dark hair. “Are you really here?”

“I’m here,” Kara says quietly, close enough now to see what she knew to be true. She crouches in front of the girl, putting them on almost equal level. “You’re Cora. From the Titanic.”

Kara had almost forgotten about the little girl she danced with that night, in the chaos of everything that followed. But she remembers now. She had been tiny at the time, hardly more than 6 years old, but feisty. Spirited. This Cora looks like that spirit has been almost wrung out of her.

“You danced with me,” Cora whispers, her lip quivering. She presses her teeth into it, probably trying not to show weakness in this place, and Kara takes her hand in an attempt to let her know it’s okay.

“How did you end up here?” Kara asks, glancing around. Cora doesn’t seem to have any belongings of her own besides the dress she’s wearing.

“Someone put me on a lifeboat,” Cora says, sounding almost angry about it. Like she resents having been saved. “My dad…I lost him. I let go of his hand and someone took me away, and I never -”

Cora loses her battle with tears. She glances around her, wiping them harshly from her own face, worried the others will see, and Kara squeezes her hands.

“Can I hug you?” Kara asks, at a total loss for what else to do. “Is that –”

Cora launches herself into Kara’s arms, shaking with tightly-held emotion, and Kara wonders with a stab of empathy how long it’s been since Cora saw a friendly and familiar face. Years, maybe.

“How long have you been here?” Kara asks, and Cora pulls back slowly to wipe her face again.

“This place? A few months,” she shrugs with the practiced indifference Kara has seen in so many of these children. “I was at another one before this. They sent me to live with a family, but they…” Cora gnaws at her lip. “I ran away, and ended up here. That’s how it usually goes, for us.”

Kara stays as long as she can, but she’s shooed out before dinnertime by the warden. And as much as Kara promises she’ll come back, she can see in Cora’s face that she doesn’t quite believe her.

The moment Lena comes in the door that evening, Kara is talking.

“Do you remember that little girl I danced with that night on the Titanic?”

Lena frowns, putting her bag on the table and seeming to think back. “I think…Cora, right? She was sweet. I always wondered…why do you bring her up?”

“I found her,” Kara says quickly. Lena pauses, sitting slowly on the bed beside Kara.

“You found her?”

“I was doing interviews for my piece, you know, the one –” Kara says, her words tripping over each other in her haste to get the story out.

“I remember,” Lena says patiently. “The one on orphanage conditions.”

“Right,” Kara says with relief. “Well, she was in one. One of the bad ones. And she was so…she was so defeated, Lena. Not even sad, just resigned. Like she had no hope.”

“Those places can have that effect,” Lena says, her eyes getting a little far away. “When I was in one, back home –”

“You were in an orphanage?” Kara blurts. How she had spent a week writing a piece on the institutions and not known this she has no idea, but Lena just shrugs in exactly the same way Cora did.

“Not for long. My father came to get me after a few weeks. But for a time, yes. This horrid Catholic place,” Lena says with a shudder. “Although once I was with my new family, I almost missed the old kind of mistreatment I got there. It was simpler.”

Kara wants so badly to ask why didn’t you tell me? But it doesn’t seem like the right moment to make things about herself. She just laces her fingers with Lena’s, holding tight and tracing over the back of her hand with a thumb as Lena continues.

“We can’t just leave her there,” Lena says, getting more and more animated. “If we can help her – do you think we can help her somehow?”

“I don’t know of any way, besides trying to foster her ourselves,” Kara says, sighing. “From what I’ve seen –”

“Well, let’s do that.”

Kara’s thumb freezes mid-stroke.

“…really?” Kara says, a mounting sense of purpose building at Lena’s declaration. Neither of them have ever spoken about children, it being a biological impossibility, and for some reason Kara had assumed that Lena would be against it. Kara herself had felt only neutral on the subject, until today.

“I mean, not if you don’t want to,” Lena corrects quickly, looking worried. “I know we’ve never really…”

“No, I do!” Kara says, relief and love flooding her as she grabs Lena’s hands again. “God, I’m so glad – I mean, raising a 12-year-old isn’t something we can take lightly. But if you want to?”

“Yes,” Lena says, her face a little white but seeming resolved. “I mean, I’m terrified. But it’s the right thing to do. I know what it’s like to be shuttled into a family that doesn’t…treat you well. If we can get someone out of that?”

“Okay,” Kara says, laughing a little in disbelief. “Okay. We’ll go tomorrow.”

 

 

When Kara returns with Lena after work, Cora has a smile for her – you’re the one who stole Miss Kara away that night – but she seems confused by their presence.

“Why did you come back?” Cora asks, looking between them with a sort of caution. Kara clears her throat. It’s best not to get Cora’s hopes up too much, but she also doesn’t love the idea of offering to take Cora in if she doesn’t want to come live with them.

“We just wanted to visit,” Kara says, the lie a little clumsy on her tongue. “It seems lonely, here.”

Cora’s eyes narrow. She doesn’t look upset, but it reminds Kara a little of the look Lena gives her when she’s trying to figure out an equation. “They won’t let you adopt me.”

Kara blinks. “That’s not – we – wait, why not?”

“You’re two women.”

“Well, we weren’t going to tell them we’re a couple,” Kara says, looking at Lena. Lena is frowning in the same way Cora is.

“They don’t let single women do it, either,” Cora says. Once again she seems resigned, hopeless, even when Lena speaks up.

“What if they thought we were your family? Coming to claim you? Aunts, or something?”

Cora looks at Lena, and for the first time Kara can see a spark in her expression. “You’d…you’d do that? Lie to them? For me?”

“We can’t not try. If it works, would you…want to come live with us?”

Cora doesn’t seem capable of speaking – but she nods voraciously, offering Lena a hug that Lena seems unsure at first how to cope with. Her arms come up slowly, but when she finally returns it her grip is fierce.

The woman Kara ends up talking to in the office is even more rules-mongering than she feared.

“She’s my niece,” Kara argues, taking Lena’s suggestion and running with it. “I’ve finally found her, and you won’t even let me take her home?”

“Why were you not contacted as next of kin when her parents passed?” the woman asks, her vulture-like features tight with disapproval. She had looked Kara over the second she walked in the room, taking in her clothes and her demeanour, and the judgement had come off her in waves as she took Kara’s name and address. Kara was doomed from the first.

“The Titanic!” Kara says sarcastically. “Ever heard of it? I thought she was dead! If I hadn’t been coming here for work –”

“Do you have adequate space?” the woman interrupts, and Kara’s protests skid to a halt.

“What does that mean?”

“Will the child have space to sleep?” the woman says, condescension dripping from every word.

Kara thinks to her single-room apartment with Lena, their one bed halfway in the kitchen and the tiny bathroom. They could fit a trundle bed, maybe, but certainly not much more than that.

“She’d have more space than she does here,” Kara says truthfully. The woman’s lips purse.

“That is not saying much. We also only release children into married households. Are you married?”

“Why?” Kara asks, slipping her left hand into her pocket. “Why does that matter?”

“Financial and family reasons. Children should have a mother and father.”

Kara rolls her eyes, sitting up straight. “How many married mother-and-father homes have you released kids into and had them come back beaten?”

“That’s not the –”

“We’re at war, all the men are gone anyways!” Kara continues, but the woman is already shutting her record book.

“I’m sorry, madam. But you don’t fit our qualifications for foster care.”

There isn’t much Kara can do to dissuade her. It’s heartbreaking to have to sit and tell Cora the news, but Kara isn’t willing to give up just yet.

“We can find a new place with more space,” Kara says, and Lena nods in agreement. “And we have friends, male friends, who can pretend to be one of our husbands. Then we can –”

“Won’t they check the records?” Cora asks, perfectly reasonably.

“We’re going to do everything we can,” Kara says firmly. Cora nods, but the hopelessness has returned in full force, and it haunts Kara for the whole tram ride home.

They start their hunt for another apartment the very next day. Lena seems just as determined as Kara to find an adequate home, but they’re hindered by finances – while Kara makes a good wage and they’ve been able to put away a bit of money by living somewhere so cheap for several years, Lena is still in school. They’ll have two incomes eventually, but their new place needs to be affordable now.

They visit Cora, separately and together, as much as possible as December inches towards the new year, and with each visit she seems to finally fully believe that Lena and Kara are actually genuine. She’s still doubtful that their efforts will work, but at the very least she accepts that she won’t be immediately abandoned as soon as they hit a roadblock.

Perhaps it’s that new knowledge that leads to Kara and Lena coming home from a night out with Lucy and Jess a week before Christmas to find Cora sitting outside their building, shivering and definitely not wearing a coat.

“Cora!” Lena gasps, running forward as soon as the recognizes the huddled figure and draping her own jacket over her. “What are you – how did you find us?”

“They had your address in the office,” Cora says between chattering teeth. “Snuck upstairs to find it.”

“Jesus,” Lucy says quietly, as Lena rubs Cora’s chilled arms and Kara hurriedly unlocks the door into the lobby. “How long have you been here, kid?”

“Few hours,” Cora says, stumbling a little up the first flight of stairs. The four of them have formed a sort of protective ring around her, and Jess catches her before she can fall. “I couldn’t be there anymore. I’m sorry. Can I stay just for a night?”

“You can stay as long as you want,” Kara says, unlocking the apartment door and ushering her inside. “There’s some spare pyjamas in the drawer. Find something warm and go change in the bathroom, okay?”

Cora grabs a sweater and some of Kara’s pants – several sizes too big, Kara notes, they’ll have to buy her a few things tomorrow – and disappears into the bathroom. Kara turns to the group as she starts to throw together some hot chocolate.

“What are we supposed to do?” Kara asks in a whisper, warming powdered milk in a saucepan and mixing sugar and a little cocoa. It’s rationed, right now, but tonight seems like the time to use a little. “We can’t make her go back there.”

“She’s here now,” Jess says, entirely reasonable. “Why should she have to go back? She can just stay with you.”

“Isn’t that considered kidnapping?”

“Just say she’s Lena’s daughter,” Lucy suggests matter-of-factly.

Lena makes a strange sort of noise, and Kara pauses in her whisking.

“Lena’s daughter?” Kara says, frowning over the hot chocolate. “You really think that would work?”

“I mean, look at her,” Lucy says, gesturing at the bathroom door. “She could easily pass as Lena’s actual kid.”

Cora does have Lena’s colouring, Kara realizes now – dark hair, pale skin. Her eyes are brown unlike Lena’s, but that’s easily waved aside. As crazy as it seems, it could work.

“You’re suggesting we just…pretend she’s always been with us?” Lena says, sounding doubtful.

“Lena lost all her ID in the sinking and got new ones with a different name,” Jess says, and Lucy nods her agreement. “Cora could, too.”

“That was years ago,” Lena points out. “Right after the sinking. They aren’t exactly being that lenient anymore.”

“I’ll come with you,” Lucy says, shrugging. “I doubt they’ll want to fight you too hard if I threaten a lawsuit.”

Kara could kiss her. “You’d do that?”

“Of course. The kid has been through the same thing we have,” Lucy says, her tone uncharacteristically grave. “Worse, even. She deserves decent people to take care of her. We’re decent people.”

For a moment, Lucy reminds Kara so starkly of Alex when she first came to live with the Danvers that she’s almost overwhelmed by the urge to hug her. So she does, squeezing her friend tightly in lieu of her sister. Lucy seems to understand; she hugs back just as hard.

Cora is, as could be expected, completely on board with the lie if it means she doesn’t have to go back to her old life. She falls asleep almost as soon as she crawls into their bed, sprawling over the whole surface of it in a comfortable way that Kara is sure she hasn’t gotten to experience in a long while, and while Lena creates a nest of blankets on the floor for the two of them to sleep on until they can buy a trundle bed tomorrow Kara starts on another letter to Alex.

You won’t believe this, she writes, a disbelieving grin on her face as she scrawls, but you’re gonna be an aunt.

As Kara expected, Cora is still skittish for a while. She’s expecting the other shoe to drop, for this one good thing that’s happened to her to evaporate in front of her eyes. But she comes out of her shell a little more with each day that Kara comes home reliably from work, each day that Lena lets her help to make dinner or reads a bit of her novel out loud before bed. Things are shaky sometimes – neither of them have ever parented before, and Kara has no earthly clue what she’s doing. Lena has good instincts, but with her lifetime of mother-related issues she tends to overcompensate in fear of following the same patterns, and Cora has years worth of trauma to unlearn and move past.

But in the end, Lena was right all those weeks ago. This is exactly where Kara is needed.

9. Chapter 9

November, 1918

 

 

Dear Kara,

Things over here are about the same as they always are – cold and muddy. It’s amazing how condescending men can be even when you’re the one removing shrapnel from their legs. I’ve been hearing that the Armistice might go through, but it’s hard to tell on the front lines. People keep shooting until the higher ups tell them to stop. We don’t always have radio access, so if you hear anything else, send it along?

How is Cora? I can’t believe I have a niece now, and I still haven’t met her. Maggie told me she likes visiting the dogs. I love that she’s interested in science – I’d better get home before Lena corrupts her into liking engineering instead of the fine art of biology, huh? And thanks for the last package you sent – I shared the chocolate and the playing cards with some of the patients, and they’ve all asked that I send you their thanks as well. No medicine like distractions and a little treat now and then.

I know you said a year ago that if the war was still going by now, you’d enlist and come get me. I hope that adopting a kid has shaken you of that, but still – this isn’t where you want to be, Kara. Let me sleep well knowing my sister will never have to see this.

I’ll be home before you know it. Send my love to Lena and Cora.

Love,

Alex

The letter is on paper so thin that Kara worries if she breathes too hard on it, it’ll fall apart in her hands. She holds it delicately, reading the words over and over before she sets it in the shoebox where she’s kept everyone’s letters from the front for the last year, and leans against the frosty windowpanes. They’re in a bit of a cold snap, but Kara likes to sit in the window seat in their new apartment no matter the weather.

Footsteps approach behind her, and Kara knows it’s Lena before she even gets close enough to set her chin on Kara’s shoulder. Lena reaches a hand up and draws a heart in the frost with her fingernail, scraping their initials into it, and kisses Kara’s cheek.

“They’ll come home safe. They all will.”

Kara nods, but she doesn’t budge. Her real thoughts are too pervasive to brush away. “What if they don’t? What if this war just never ends?”

“We can’t deal with things until they happen,” Lena says, hugging her arms tight around Kara’s middle. The pressure is comforting. “Trying to predict the future never gets us anywhere.”

Somehow, the comfort of Lena’s arms loosens Kara’s tenuous hold on the tears he managed not to shed when the letter arrived. Her voice shakes when she finally speaks, almost too quiet to hear.

“Why did Alex have to go?”

“Oh, Kara,” Lena breathes sympathetically, pulling her arms tighter. The tears spill over, and Kara breathes out slowly through her nose.

“I know. I know, I’m just selfish. I want her home. I want them all home.”

“Kara,” Lena says firmly, moving a little until she can tilt Kara’s chin to meet her eyes, “you are the least selfish person I’ve ever met.”

Cora emerges from her room then, likely hearing Kara dissolving into tears, and joins the hug without saying a word.

It’s not an uncommon thing, lately, for Kara to get overwhelmed every time Alex or Clark sends a letter. She’s been doing her best to send them care packages whenever she can, and Cora has been interested since she first came to stay in helping put them together – she says it makes her feel like she’s connecting with them, even if they haven’t met yet. Cora has spent a lot of time with Maggie, but her having not met Alex yet feels strange to Kara. Cora only recently gotten brave enough to send a letter of her own to Alex, and when the reply had come and she had gotten real tangible evidence that her new aunt was excited about having her in the family she’d been giddy for weeks.

Cora’s suggestion of putting another care package together is a suitable distraction for Kara’s melancholy.

“What’s she actually like?” Cora asks an hour or so later, as they’re standing in the line at the market with a basket full of knickknacks, thick wool socks, tea, and a little bag of candies. Kara frowns.

“What do you mean? You’ve written to her.”

“I mean, I read her letters,” Cora says, twisting the hem of her dress. “But what’s she like in real life?”

Thinking about her sister again makes Kara’s throat a little thick, but it’s also strangely nice to talk about it with Cora. She clears it, and tries her best to describe one of the most important people in her life.

“She’s…she’s wonderful,” Kara says somewhat inarticulately. “She’s strong, so strong, and funny. She’s smart. She tries to be stoic, but she cares so much about everything. She’s always taken care of me.”

“Like you take care of me?” Cora asks, and it melts Kara’s heart completely.

“Yeah,” Kara says, clearing her throat again. “A lot like that. Remember how I told you she and Grandma took me in?”

“Like you and Lena took me in.”

Kara nods. “Exactly. But she didn’t just take me in – she accepted me. As her sister. We fought all the time, because we were teenagers – but she never let me forget that she loved me. She chose to love me.”

Cora’s next question is quiet, almost embarrassed, but completely genuine.

“Do you think she’ll choose to love me?”

Public place be damned, Kara’s eyes water at that. She puts an arm around Cora’s shoulders – at almost 13, now, she’s almost as tall as Lena and starting to gain on Kara in height – and presses a quick kiss to the top of her head.

“She already does.”

 

 

 

 

The day it all ends is eerily similar to the day it began.

They’re in a new and bigger apartment, of course, so when Kara wakes up at her usual time her shuffle to the kitchen is a bit longer. Lena gets up a half hour later, her internship starting a bit later than Kara’s shifts at CatCo. Kara makes tea and a quick breakfast for everyone, wakes Cora up, and heads to work while Lena walks Cora to school with no idea what’s happened in the world.

The sense of mania when she steps into the bullpen is similar, but the energy is entirely different. The floor is a whirlwind of activity, people talking excitedly over each other and scribbling furiously – Kara can almost hear the linotypes in the basement working overtime. There’s a story brewing, and when Lois jogs over holding a scrap of paper, her grin almost ear to ear, Kara’s heart lifts cautiously.

“What’s the hubbub?” Kara asks, and Lois thrusts the paper into her hands.

“Armistice,” Lois says breathlessly. “Look.”

Kara peers down at the paper. It’s in handwriting, but clearly a transcription of a telegram:

‘Armistice signed. Announced in congress. 11:00 hostilities cease. Media to spread word.’

“The armistice…was signed?” Kara says, blinking down at the words. They can’t be real. This has to be a dream.

“Hostilities stopped 11:00 Paris time, so three hours ago,” Lois says, taking the paper back. “The senior reporters are writing those articles – you’re on your regular beat. I don’t think anyone would miss you if you went to let Lena know.”

Lois leaves with a wink and a squeeze of her arm, practically skipping away. Clearly elated that Clark is, hopefully, going to be coming home safe.

Kara doesn’t need to be told twice. Only a few minutes after she entered she’s leaving the building again, sprinting to the nearest streetcar heading towards Cora’s school. She spots Lena before she even gets there, walking to the firm where she’s doing her internship as she usually does after she drops Cora off, and rather than wait to get to the next stop Kara just jumps out the open door and onto the street. The driver calls after her, alarmed and probably angry, but she doesn’t care.

Lena glances behind her at the commotion, and when she sees Kara there dodging between cars and bikes to get to the sidewalk she cries out.

“Kara! What on earth – why aren’t you at work? Did you just jump out of a moving tram?”

Kara almost bowls over three people to get to her, but finally she skids to a halt in front of Lena, panting.

“It’s over! It’s over, Lena. It’s done.”

“What’s over?” Lena asks, grabbing Kara’s hand and pressing her other one to Kara’s forehead. “Kara, you’re chalk white. What’s wrong?”

“Nothing, absolutely nothing!” Kara shouts, totally unable to keep her voice down. “For the first time in 2 years, Lena, nothing is wrong. The war is over! They’re coming home. Everyone is coming home.”

“Over?” Lena repeats, blinking. Some people around them have stopped to look at the commotion, and a few of them look to each other in shock.

“We just got the telegram. They’re going crazy at work, trying to be the first to break the news, but the radio stations have us beat,” Kara says, her breathing still heavy. “It was over hours ago.”

“So -”

“It’s over. It’s done, they’re all coming home,” Kara says, tears starting to choke her. Happy tears, for once. “Alex, Clark, James, Winn – everyone is coming back.”

Lena pulls Kara into a hug, then – one so hard that it rivals how they held each other when Lena jumped back aboard the Titanic. The crowd on the street seems unable to believe it’s true – but when a man leans out of a nearby window to shout down that the radio is announcing the same thing, everyone else follows suit. People Kara is pretty sure didn’t know each other before are now shaking hands, hugging, crying out in joy, and Kara pulls back from the hug to start dragging Lena back towards Cora’s school. Alex might not be coming home for months, but there’s still so much to do.

“We have to go get Cora. She shouldn’t be in school on a day like today. And we have to plan a party, a big coming-home party. And we have to –”

“Kara,” Lena says, pulling her arm taut. Kara stops, turning back.

“Yeah?”

“I love you,” Lena says, her smile lighting up her whole face. Kara lets out a half-laugh, half-sob, and in full view of the whole street she pulls her lover into a joyful kiss.

 

 

It takes months for everything to come into effect enough that people can actually be released from service. But Alex does come home, is actually among the first to do so because of the importance of her job, on a chilly morning in January just after the New Year. Kara has been tracking the progress of her ship through telegrams, and the morning of its arrival everyone bundles up and goes to wait with the rest of the crowd at the port.

“Okay, there’s a lot of people coming off. Look out for her – auburn hair, remember,” Kara says, shifting a little so Cora can have a better look. She’s perched up on Kara’s back so she has a foot or so of height above everyone else, and she’s scanning the soldiers marching single-file down the gangplank excitedly.

“I’m watching!”

“Do you have the extra coat?” Kara asks, nudging Lucy. “It’s cold out, I want to make sure –”

Lucy rolls her eyes, nudging back. “She’s been on the front for a year and a half, Kara. She’s not going to freeze to death from 30 minutes outdoors.”

Kara nudges her harder, glaring as much as she can with Cora resting her chin on the top of her head, and Lucy sighs.

“Yes, I have the extra coat.”

“Good,” Kara breathes, turning back to the ship. “And everything is ready at home for the party, right? We have everything -”

Before Lucy can make a snappy retort Cora shouts, pointing dead ahead.

“There! That looks like the picture you showed me - is that her?”

Kara, a jolt of excitement going through her, jumps to try and get a decent view. She can’t get far off the ground with Cora on her back, but it’s enough – Alex is coming towards them with her big canvas bag slung over her shoulder, looking exhausted and much thinner than she was when she left but with a grin that eclipses everything else.

Cora jumps off Kara’s back, and like a shot Kara is gone. She breaks through the crowd just in front of Alex with Maggie beside her, and as one they throw their arms around her neck.

“Woah, hey,” Alex says, laughing as they practically dogpile onto her in the middle of the marina. “One at a time, ladies. There’s enough of me for everyone.” She kisses Kara on the cheek, squeezing her tight, and then turns to Maggie for a reunion that makes Kara let go of the embrace with an ‘oh, yuck.’

“Miss me?” Alex says once she’s kissed Maggie soundly, her arm still around her beau.

“Clearly not,” Kara laughs, taking her sister’s other arm. Somehow it feels both like Alex never left, and like if she isn’t touching her sister she’s going to disappear again. “Come on, everyone’s waiting.”

Alex greets everyone with enthusiasm despite her clear exhaustion, and finally once everyone has been hugged she turns to the newest member of the group.

“And you must be Cora,” Alex says gently, her smile warm. “I hear you’ve been taking good care of my puppies.”

“Maggie says they like me,” Cora says, her eyes wide. Kara hasn’t seen her this nervous since she came to live with them, but she knows it won’t last long. This has always been one of Alex’s best skills.

“Of course they do! They know family when they see it.”

Cora smiles, her eyes shiny, and Alex holds her arms out.

“Are you a hugger? I’m generally a hugger.”

Cora’s shyness melts away on the way home. She chats with Alex about school and what it’s like to be a doctor, what Kara was like when she was younger, and how much Alex enjoyed her contributions to the care packages. Alex claps at the decorations strung all around her apartment when they arrive, and she unwraps everyone’s gifts graciously. But once the hubbub has died down a little she joins Kara on the couch, sitting heavily, and a cloud seems to hang over her head.

“You’re never allowed to leave again,” Kara says, patting her leg. Alex laughs, but in it Kara can see the ghost of her experiences of the last year. A haunted look in her eyes. She can’t even begin to imagine the things Alex has seen.

“Count on it. If I never step foot on another wretched boat for the rest of my days, I’ll be content.”

“Do you want to talk about it?” Kara asks hesitantly.

“Not yet,” Alex says, shaking her head. “Later, yes. But right now I think I just need to be with my family.”

“Of course,” Kara nods, shifting closer and letting Alex lay her head on her shoulder. “I love you, you know that?”

She can feel Alex smile against her arm.

“Love you too, Kara.”

They sit like that for a long while, just watching the commotion. Lois cutting cake and handing it out, Lucy putting a disc in the gramophone and pulling Cora and Maggie up to dance in the parlour, Lena idly petting two of Alex’s dozing dogs and meeting Kara’s eyes across the room with a contented smile. Alex hums along to the music, and Kara takes what feels like her first truly calming breath since her sister left.

There are still people missing from the picture. But they’ll be home soon – and after everything, after two disasters, they’ll all be together again.

10. Chapter 10

March, 1920

 

 

As soon as she gets home after work and finds all the lights turned down, Lena knows that something is off.

Kara is always home by now, and Cora is usually doing take-home assignments in the kitchen while she starts dinner. When Lena comes home she joins them, helping Kara finish up, and they clean up together and then retire to the parlour – Kara and Cora listen to the radio usually, while Lena reads or answers letters.

Tonight, the place is dim. It’s not quite dark, but the gas lamps are turned down and the only light Lena can see is the flicker of candles somewhere deeper in the apartment. She sets her bag down, calling out cautiously.

“Kara? Are you here, love?”

“In the parlour,” Kara calls back, and Lena relaxes a little. Now that she knows her family hasn’t been terribly murdered she slips out of her heels and sets her jacket on the hook, following the sound of Kara’s voice.

“Why are all the lights off? And where’s Cora?” Lena asks, throwing her keys into the jar by the door. Kara’s voice, when she answers, is high and a little nervous.

“Uh – why don’t you just come in here?”

Strange. Lena frowns, heading to the parlour in stockinged feet, and she can see that it’s the source of the flickering light.

“Kara, what on earth – oh!”

The parlour, when she enters, is aglow with more candles than Lena thought they owned. They make the walls dance with light, illuminating the contents of the room – most notably the wine on the coffee table, the rose petals scattered across the floor, and Kara in the middle of it all in her best shirt.

“Hi,” Kara says, a little breathless. Her eyes are wide and shiny in the candlelight, and she gestures around with a smile. “Welcome home.”

“What’s all this for?” Lena says, stepping fully into the room. Kara takes her hands, holding them tight – her hands are clammy, and Lena can’t for the life of her guess at why. “Did I forget an anniversary?”

“No, no, nothing like that,” Kara says, laughing a little. She wipes her hands on her trousers, and then takes Lena’s again. “I thought about doing this down at the harbour – where we arrived, you know? But it’s sort of dirty and crowded down there, and then I figured maybe near the Statue of Liberty but there’s so many tourists and you can’t get there at night, and so I figured why not here? This is our home, our safe place, and we built that together. We should build this here, too.”

“Kara, darling,” Lena says, confused but deeply endeared by Kara’s rambling as always. “Build what?”

“Oh!” Kara says with a start. “Right, um…” She fumbles around in her pocket, and what she produces sends a shockwave through Lena.

“Lena,” Kara says shakily, sinking down on one knee in the rose petals and holding up a tiny velvet box with a glittering ring, “will you marry me?”

The ring itself is simple, but perfect. A tiny rock set in silver – not an ostentatious show of ownership, but a small and affordable testament to their commitment. But Lena can’t quite find the words she wants. She’s as confused as she is delighted, and Kara is starting to look nervous.

“Will I –” Lena gasps, pressing a hand to her chest and feeling her own quick heartbeat thudding there. “I mean…how?”

“I know it can’t be a normal marriage,” Kara says quickly, still on her knee but talking just as earnestly as she always does. “Legal and all that. But that doesn’t really matter to me. 8 years ago, somehow you chose me. I want us to share another step together, even if it’s only symbolic. I want to be your wife. If you want that.”

If you want that. As if Lena has ever wanted anything as much as she wants to spend her life with Kara. Her confusion is wiped away by Kara’s explanation of the symbolism being the point, not the legality, and she’s left with only one word.

“Yes,” Lena whispers.

Kara, hilariously, looks surprised.

“…really?”

“Of course,” Lena says louder, laughing a little. It turns into a belly-laugh, and she reaches past the ring to grab Kara’s free hand and pull her to her feet. “Of course, yes. Did you think I would say no? Why would I not want to be your wife?”

Kara shrugs, but Lena notices her hands are shaking a little with relief. “Well, you were anchored to another person once before. I wasn’t sure if you would want that same symbolism in our relationship.”

Lena takes Kara’s face in her hands, pulling her close and kissing every precious inch. “Nothing you do could ever remind me of that.”

“Oh, good,” Kara breathes out all at once, and Lena laughs. “Do you want the ring now?”

Lena nods immediately, holding out her left hand, and Kara slips the ring on her finger. It’s a perfect fit – either Kara guessed correctly, or she’s done some reconnaissance and measured Lena’s other rings while she wasn’t wearing them to get the right size. Either way, it makes Lena’s chest feel warm. Where her first engagement ring felt like a two-ton weight dragging her down at every moment, this one feels entirely different. It buoys her.

Kara fidgets a little, playing with Lena’s fingers after the puts the ring on. “Um. Cora is with Alex and Maggie tonight, so. We have the place to our-”

Kara can’t even finish the sentence before Lena is dragging her to the bedroom.

“What kind of wedding do you think we’ll have?” Lena asks later, naked and tangled with Kara while her body cools down. It isn’t often that they have extended time alone, but Kara has more than made up for lost time tonight. “Not a normal one, surely.”

“I’m not really imagining a proper wedding, no. That’s not what’s important to me about it. I was thinking just family,” Kara says. “Just our people, maybe Eliza. Unless there’s anyone else you’d want to be there?”

“I have no other family to invite,” Lena says with a chuckle. She’s always been endlessly grateful and happy that their family is in her life. But thinking about how casually she was thrown aside still stings sometimes, and Kara gives her a squeeze.

“Let’s just throw tradition out the window. No aisle walking, no archaic rituals – we just stand together in front of our family and swear to love and cherish each other. That’s all I want.”

Lena nods happily, tucking herself into Kara’s side and laying her left hand on Kara’s chest. The little stone glitters there, and Kara brings it up to her mouth and kisses the knuckle just overtop as Lena starts to yawn.

“I still can’t quite believe you said yes,” Kara says, kissing down Lena’s arm as far as she can reach. “I’ve been trying to get up the courage to ask for months. Cora said I needed to just do it.”

“Cora was in on this?” Lena chuckles, picturing Cora giving Kara pep talks in preparation.

“She helped me pick the ring.”

It’s so fitting, all of it. Perfect. The night catches up with her all at once, and Lena’s eyelids get heavy with contented drowsiness. Kara kisses her forehead, murmuring her love into Lena’s hair, and Lena just manages to murmur a love you, too before she’s drifting into sleep.

 

 

 

 

 

The ceremony is exactly as Lena had envisioned it. They go to Midvale for it, everyone travelling together and starting the celebration in the train compartment and holding it in Eliza’s backyard. They dress simply, letting Lucy tie their hands together in a handfasting as they give their vows – she isn’t an officiant, but as a lawyer she volunteered as ‘the closest thing they have’ – and exchange rings. It’s a perfect weekend.

It’s only a month or so into their married life that Lena gets the news about Lillian.

It starts with Kara waiting at home for Lena once again, this time with a much less happy look on her face. Kara usually works a little late on Fridays to make sure the copy gets proofed properly before going to print for the Sunday edition, so it’s strange of her to be home before 7, and Lena’s stomach does a strange twist.

“What’s wrong?” Lena asks immediately, setting down her bag. “Who died?”

“Nobody!” Kara says quickly, standing up and gently grabbing Lena by her upper arms before she works herself into a panic. “Nothing like that, I promise. There was just…sort of a big news story, and it’s going to hit soon, and I wanted you to know before you read it in a paper.”

“Okay?” Lena says, her heart slowing down a little now that she knows there isn’t any immediate danger or tragedy. “What is it that’s so urgent?”

Kara sinks onto the couch, pulling Lena with her, and seems to brace herself.

“Lillian Luthor was just arrested.”

It takes a few seconds for the information to make its way through Lena’s brain. When it does, she can only think of one question to ask.

“…for what?”

“Quite a few things,” Kara says, fidgeting a little. “Apparently during the war, she was involved in some…well, she wasn’t a spy exactly, but she -”

“A spy?” Lena interrupts, laughing a little in sheer disbelief. “Lillian?”

“No, it was more…well, she sort of insinuated herself into Germany in the first few years, apparently,” Kara says, talking with her hands as she tries to explain. It’s one of her endearing traits, and it calms Lena a little. “Got a rich benefactor, became good friends with the Kaiser. And then…came back here, and used her old connections to get into high-ranking military circles and…send information back. She’s being charged with conspiracy. And espionage. And…treason.”

“Treason,” Lena says, shaking her head. “I never would have thought…she’s capable of it, of course, and desperate to maintain her lifestyle, but I always thought she was too smart to get caught up in something like that.”

“Clearly she thought the war was going to go a different way.” Kara takes Lena’s hands, holding them loosely, and Lena gives her an absent nod. She’d gotten off the Titanic happy in the knowledge that she would never need to see Lillian again – but now, she feels a strange urge to do exactly that. To show her that she’s alive and happy, thriving despite everything. That she never needed Lillian – Lillian needed her, and now their lives are finally reflecting it.

“I want to go to the trial,” Lena says, and Kara’s grip on her hands tightens a little.

“Are you sure? You’ve always said all she’s ever managed to do was hurt you. Do you really want her to know you’re alive?”

“You’re right,” Lena conceded, but now that she’s decided it her mind is made up. “But I think I need the closure. If she’s being charged with all that, she’s going to prison. I feel like I need to see it happen.”

Kara is reluctant but she agrees without much argument, just insisting on being beside Lena the whole time. As if Lena would want anything else. It’s going to be difficult, but Lena is confident – she can face Lillian one more time.

 

 

 

A few weeks later when they’re in a cab on the way to the courthouse, Lena starts to seriously doubt that conviction.

Kara is on one side of her and Cora is on the other, both having insisted on being present, and each of them are holding one of her hands as she starts to hyperventilate. She had been perfectly calm even last night, preparing for this day – but now that it’s happening, all she can think about is the vice grip Lillian always had on her willpower. She had almost forgotten it, but she can almost see Lillian in her mind’s eye now in the same way she used to – a towering figure, cold and cruel. Lena had been subject to her whims, and now at the idea of seeing her Lena is struck with a terror that takes her breath away.

What if somehow, some way, Lillian finds a way to destroy her life again?

“We can go home,” Kara says in a low tone, her hand strong and comforting. “You don’t owe her anything.”

“No,” Lena gasps, struggling to take deep breaths. “No, I want to do this. I want to. I’m only…god, what if she finds a way to take you away from me?”

“That will never happen,” Kara says firmly. Her conviction is unshakeable, and Lena wishes she shared it. “I won’t let it. None of us will.”

“She could find a way – she almost got you killed, she could –”

Cora squeezes her hand then, so hard that it almost hurts, and Lena looks to her instead.

“Mom,” she says clearly, intentionally. “You can do this. I know you can.”

Through the blood roaring past her ears Lena almost doesn’t hear her, but when she does it blots out everything else. All the panic, the worst-case scenarios whirling through her head – it all quiets.

“Did you just call me Mom?” Lena whispers. Kara, she can see in her periphery, is beaming.

“Is that okay?” Cora asks, a little bashful. Lena nods vigorously.

“Is that - of course it is! I just wasn’t expecting – are you sure?”

“I didn’t know if it would be weird,” Cora says, a little quickly. “My parents will always be my parents, but you guys have also been my family for a while now. Both of you. I think they’d be okay with it.”

“I think they would too,” Kara says, smiling through tears, and the matching tears in Lena’s eyes now aren’t those of fear. They’re happy tears, grateful tears, and Cora puts an arm around her. She’s getting so tall now that it’s easy for her; eventually she’s going to resemble Kara in stature as much as she resembles Lena in features.

“So, Mom,” Cora says, smiling. “You can do this.”

“Okay,” Lena says, the tightness in her chest easing off. “I can. She’s not my family anymore – you two are.”

When they arrive the courtroom is less empty than Lena thought it might be – there seem to be a fair number of reporters here, and Kara even nods at a few that she seems to recognize from work. Apparently a former New York sociality being tried for war crimes is newsworthy; it makes sense, Lena supposes, but it seems a little surreal. They take a seat in the back row, and Lena fidgets between Kara and Cora until Lillian makes her entrance.

Lena wasn’t sure what she was expecting – for Lillian to stride into the courthouse in her usual finery, maybe. She expects her to occupy the same space she does in her memory. But the Lillian she sees now isn’t in finery – she’s in a plain dress, made of a cheap material that almost looks like canvas. She looks thinner and older than she did last Lena saw her, and although she still stands tall and glares haughtily at everyone in the room, she looks so diminished from her old intimidating presence that Lena hardly recognizes her.

It doesn’t take long for her to be sentenced. The evidence is overwhelming, as is the fact that she fled back to Germany after the war, only returning to New York when she was given no other choice. The life sentence for treason is handed down, and despite everything, Lillian still has the audacity to look shocked by it.
