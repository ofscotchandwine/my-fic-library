for the light before and after.

It has been six weeks. 

Six weeks since Lena found out that Kara’s extraction from the Phantom Zone wouldn’t be as simple as they all thought.

Six weeks of relief efforts to find Kara and way too many failed attempts to count.

After trying several different coordinates within the zone and coming up empty, the team decides to take a break from trying to rescue Kara. Nobody would dare say it, but deep down, Lena knew that they were beginning to give up hope. She noticed it in the slump of each of their shoulders, frequent exhales of frustration and broken equipment; and they were increasing by the day.

That didn’t stop Lena from trying though. 

She took on shorter work days at LuthorCorp while utilizing most of her time in the lab, coming up with ways to reach Kara in the Phantom Zone without a precise location. To figure out a method to scan her Kryptonian DNA all the way from Earth.

Lena wouldn’t admit this to the Superfriends, not even to Sam, who always tried to get her to slow down a little bit, but the sixteen hour days were starting to weigh on her. 

So were the fights.

Lena and Alex would clash over methodologies and readings, and eventually one of them would storm off. Lena knows that Alex is as worried as she is, that the thought of National City never getting their hero back is as unthinkable as the blonde enjoying a kale salad. But she can’t help but feel like these altercations with Alex are building to something that they may not be able to come back from. 

The two of them haven’t spoken about much else other than Kara’s rescue, not since Crisis. The events that transpired between Lena and Kara is something that looms heavily on the group. Much to Lena’s dismay, it seems that Alex doesn’t have intentions of letting them go any time soon.

Lena wishes there was a way she could prove to Alex that she’s not the person she was last year, but there isn’t time for that. Not when the person they care about most is missing. 

Kara wouldn't stop looking for them if either of them disappeared. It’s that thought that makes her straighten her spine and expeditiously type code into her keyboard.

After a particularly long day in the lab--Lena nearly burned her eyebrows clean off--Alex shows up with a few scorch marks on her Sentinel suit, hair unkempt and bruises darkening her cheeks.

“Rough day?” Lena asks, tapping a few keys on the machine in front of her. Alex doesn’t respond and silently perches herself on the wall adjacent to Lena’s workstation.

Then the older woman lets out a weary sigh.

“I thought you could use an extra set of hands,” Alex replies, running a hand through her hair. Some ash falls onto the lab floor, but Lena finds herself too drained to draw any attention to it. “I think the team forgets I have a background in bio-engineering.”

Lena nods, thinking about how the Superfriends only contact her when they’re in dire straits. “I think you may be right.”

Alex’s gaze finds hers then. She takes note of the bags under Lena’s eyes, her expression adjusting into one of concern. Lena feels under scrutiny as Alex gives her a once over.

The same form fitting top and pencil skirt Lena wore the day prior. The wisps of hair poking out of her normally immaculate bun. The evident worry on the older Danvers’ face reminds her too much of Kara. “When was the last time you slept, Luthor?”

Lena’s shoulders instantly stiffen at the question, but she recalibrates, her focus back on the lab’s mainframe. “I was able to sneak in a few hours this morning.”

“Kara wouldn’t want you to kill yourself like this,” Alex tells her, shifting her weight from one foot to the other. Lena abandons the tech, turning around to face the other woman. She’s about to refute her words, but Alex cuts her off. “I know I’ve been giving you a lot of crap lately, but you care about Kara about as much as I do. She’d be pissed at me if I didn’t at least try to get you to take care of yourself.”

The words are concise and direct, making the corner of Lena’s mouth twitch. She has to lie her whole way through as she says, “I’m fine.”

Alex shoots her a look of disbelief. It’s so Kara, it’s uncanny.

“Fine.” Lena’s shoulders sink, and a hand grabs at each of her biceps. “You’re probably right, I could use some rest. But I can’t stop. I’m making headway with the tracking program. If I could just get the algorithm right—”

Lena turns back around, typing furiously into the mainframe. Alex moves behind her, watching over her shoulder as the program loads.

“Lena, we both know that the more tired you get, the less productive you’ll be,” Alex says softly. Lena can easily take her words as insult, but she’s surprised to find the older Danvers’ expression is one of genuine concern. “And that isn’t a comment on you, it’s science.”

Alex made a valid point, Lena would give her that. But if they were going to play this game, she wasn’t going down without a fight.

“What about you? You spend half of your time patrolling the city while the other is spent trying to save your sister. A bit hypocritical if you ask me.” 

“We aren’t talking about me right now,” Alex huffs in frustration, a hand finding its way to her hip. “Kelly already made the team promise we would work with Silas in shifts. When we aren’t needed there, Brainy and I are to help you at LuthorCorp a few times a week. That way you can catch a break and there won’t be a single hour of the day where someone isn’t actively looking for Kara.”

Deflating, Lena sighs with a furtive nod. It’s a good plan, but the exhaustedly irritated part of her can’t help herself.

“I never consented to you and Brainy working in the lab,” the CEO tiredly notes, slowly losing the edge to her voice. “This is my family’s company, as you well know.”

Alex just shakes her head, holding her chin up.

“If you refuse to rest, then the least you could do is help me look after Kara’s apartment. She’s been gone a few weeks already,” Alex offers, crossing her arms over her chest, her tone thick with something like heart ache. “Her fridge is bound to be rancid. So, could you do that for me? For her?”

There is something in Alex’s body language that finally makes her back down. Lena looks from another faulty algorithm back to Alex. Kara does deserve to have a tidy apartment when she gets back, it’s the least she can do. The super deserves clean sheets and her favorite snacks stocked while in recovery.

“Fine,” Lena decides. “But I won’t be long. And if you make any breakthroughs, any at all, please call me and I’ll portal straight back.”

“Scout’s honor,” Alex says, holding a few fingers up to her forehead. “Oh, the key to Kara’s apartment.”

Alex reaches for something in her pocket, but Lena waves a hand in dismissal.

“I have a copy,” she says, a red hue blossoming over her cheeks. The CEO slips off her lab coat and places it on its designated hook behind her.

“Right.” Alex’s eyebrows rise and she clears her throat. “Of course.”

It makes a small sheepish grin appear on the older Danvers’ face.

Lena remembers the day Kara gave her the key fondly. How much trust she must’ve had in Lena to allow her unlimited access into her home. Alex’s eyes linger on the keychain in Lena’s hand.

“I’ll be back in a few hours,” Lena warns explicitly, pressing the button on her portal watch. The swirl of purple energy pops up into the lab as Alex steps back.

“Take your time,” Alex smiles wearily, watching the younger woman walk through the purple abyss.

When Lena arrives at Kara’s apartment, it feels incredibly desolate, like she has stepped into a glum, parallel universe.

She takes in the space that was once so familiar to her. It used to be home to many game nights with their friends and movie nights where she and Kara were huddled under one blanket.

But the last time Lena stepped foot in Kara’s apartment was when she had tearfully expressed regret for working with Lex, and by proximity, Leviathan. Kara’s betrayal had gotten under her skin so viscerally that she lost sight of herself and chose to work with her maniacal brother. It makes something cold like remorse settle in her bones as she crosses the threshold and the door clicks shut.

There are takeout containers piled on top of the trash bin in the corner, clothes mounded at the foot of Kara’s bed, and used coffee mugs resting on multiple surfaces. Overall, the apartment is in disarray, seeing as saving the world took precedence in the weeks before Lex had banished Kara to the Phantom Zone.

“You are quite the mess, Kara,” Lena laughs mirthlessly at the four walls around her. The CEO opens the refrigerator door after shaking her head, and a rotten smell smacks her square in the face. Her hand flies to her nose reflexively.

She rolls out her stiff neck, pulls up her sleeves and like a light switch, Lena gears into work mode.

Lena grabs a trash bag and clears the racks of items that had spoiled over the last few weeks. Once the bag is full, she grabs the broom and dustpan from the side of Kara’s refrigerator and sweeps, then mops the floor clean. Lena uses the dust-buster on Kara’s furniture, fluffs the pillows, and folds the fluffy blanket she would normally steal over the back of the couch. She organizes Kara’s magazines, CatCo paperwork, and a few books into neat piles, then loads dirty plates and cups in the dishwasher.

Once she starts the dishwasher, Lena moves to Kara’s bedroom. She clears the floor of Kara’s clothes and places them into the laundry basket. She strips the mattress of old sheets and replaces them with new ones, breaking a sweat in the process.

When her tasks are complete, Lena is spent. She marvels at the hard work she's accomplished and rewards herself by settling on the bed with a groan. 

Her back muscles are in the middle of thanking her, when photos on Kara’s nightstand catch her eye. There is a framed moment of she and Kara at game night, their arms around one another and identical crinkly smiles.

A matching one sits in Lena’s drawer at her penthouse apartment and the wave of emotions arrive before she can stop them.

Lena holds the frame in her hands, longing for simpler times. A tear slides down her cheek and splatters onto the glass.

“I miss you,” Lena chokes out, speaking to Kara in the picture. She feels a little silly doing it, but more words spill out of her mouth, just like the cascade of tears flowing freely from her eyes. “ Please, come home.”

After a while, her back meets the mattress again. And she lays there, openly crying and heaving for the first time since Kara’s disappearance. 

Thinking about all of the things she didn’t have the chance to say to her, Lena drifts off to sleep. The photo never leaves her side, resulting in dreams of golden hair and smiling blue eyes.

Lena gives Sam the reins to LuthorCorp’s day to day operations. 

Most of it can be done remotely from Metropolis, unless it is absolutely pertinent that the CFO be present. In that case, Sam has been given access to Lena’s private jet which is set to land in National City as quickly as possible. 

Jess moves all of Lena’s meetings to Zoom so she can utilize all of her time working on the Phantom Zone tracker. If Lena is honest, the further away she is from tactless businessmen who think they know better than her, the better.

The last week really has been a lesson in multitasking, but Lena is a Luthor. She can handle anything that life throws at her, including this.

Alex is insistent that Lena take breaks regularly, much to her chagrin. Alex and Brainy sub in when Lena’s legs are about to give out, but not without having to literally pull Lena away from her work station. Kelly joins them sometimes, but her expertise usually lies at the Tower, where she is needed most.

When Lena does manage to clock out for the night, she doesn’t do so at her own apartment. She wouldn’t dare tell any of the Superfriends, but she has been spending all of her time that isn’t at LuthorCorp or the Tower at Kara’s place.

The CEO keeps up with cleaning and restocking on a weekly basis, making sure that everything is in tip top shape for the Kryptonian’s eventual return. She even orders from Kara’s favorite Chinese restaurant a few nights a week, despite the lack of kale on the menu. 

Lena finds herself watching some of Kara’s favorite movies and TV shows too. When immersed in them, she wishes her best friend was by her side, singing along and voicing the dialogue she had memorized animatedly. It always used to make Lena’s heart pound faster in her chest, but Lena never dared to explore why that was.

One day, while coming up the elevator, Lena notices Mrs. Jensen, a neighbor from a few units over, struggle with her bagged groceries. The CEO offers to help the woman put them in the appropriate cupboard. Once they finish, they chat about the weather and the woman’s grandchildren over a cup of tea. 

The interaction earns Lena a sincere smile from Mrs. Jensen, perhaps the only one she has seen in a while.

“I didn’t know Kara had such a lovely girlfriend,” Mrs. Jensen comments slyly, causing Lena’s blush to rise all the way to the tips of her ears. She doesn’t bother correcting the woman, just grins wordlessly, hoping it reaches her eyes. A deeper part of her feels an unnamed satisfaction at the sentiment.

Other days, Lena looks through some of Kara’s things as she’s handling upkeep. More often than not, she steals a sweater from Kara’s clothing rack to sleep in and naps on the comfortable couch in between working remotely.

If the garments she selects happen to smell a bit like Kara, she pretends not to revel in it.

One particularly stressful Friday night, Lena arrives at Kara’s at one in the morning. She’s exhausted after Brainy had to pry a beaker from her white knuckled fingers.

(“Go home, comrade,” he had insisted, taking over for Lena with a yawning, and frankly, adorable Nia at his side. “I can take it from here.”)

She doesn’t know why she had Frank bring her to Kara’s instead of driving her to her own place, except that she absolutely does know why. The only time she’s been to her penthouse suite in the past few weeks has been to retrieve her makeup kit and clothes that now have a home at Kara’s place. 

Lena has practically moved into Kara’s at this point. She’s even been paying her rent, Alex’s uncashed checks neatly placed in between magazines on the bookshelf. It’s only fair, since she’s been utilizing the space.

She unlocks the door, immediately slipping off her heels, the once comfortable skirt and the top she’s wearing. After scanning the bottom drawer, she changes into a pair of Kara’s sleep shorts and an old Midvale High School t-shirt. When she pulls her messy hair out from its ponytail, her tie falls to the floor and bounces underneath Kara’s bed.

Lena whines, falls to her knees and reaches underneath the bed frame blindly to retrieve the elastic band. Her hand finds a book instead.

She retrieves it and opens to the first page, finding that it isn’t just any book. It’s a journal. Kara’s journal.

I shouldn’t, Lena reasons with herself for a solid ten minutes. She boils herself water for tea, trying her best not to think about the book that sits heavily on top of the couch. But it calls to her, begging to be read.

After three sips of tea, she caves.

At first, she reads only one entry. One page dated years ago about how J’onn had to shape-shift into Kara, acting as Cat Grant’s assistant for the day. The thought makes Lena chuckle, wishing she had been there to witness that.

I feel guilty about lying to Ms. Grant. She deserves an employee that will be there for her whenever she needs them to be. Sometimes I wonder if I’ll ever stop falling short.

Lena shuts the journal, throwing it to the other end of the couch like it had burned her. The guilt runs through her like cold water and she shivers, feeling like she’s crossed a line.

She never knew that Kara had these insecurities about herself. It’s then that Lena realizes that these are things the Girl of Steel had bled onto the page. The fact that Kara feels guilty about deceiving her boss somehow makes Lena feel the slightest bit better about how the super had lied to her.

She sighs, because Lena can’t help but feel closer to her best friend after reading through that entry. There are so many things about Kara that she never got to know, because of her secret identity. And Lena, being the realist that she is, knows that she may never get that chance now.

So she crosses the room to retrieve the book, opening up to a page closer to the middle. She reads a few more entries, ones about Winn having confessed his feelings for her, her kiss with James, and how happy Kara was that Alex had finally accepted herself as a lesbian.

Lena learns a lot about the Superfriends, old and new. It completes the picture that she had only the outline of, now filled in with vibrant color. Winn is in the future with a wife and child, James has found love working at his hometown’s newspaper, and Alex is, as far as Lena knows, the happiest she’s ever been with Kelly. 

Everything has changed so much for all of them, and Lena can’t help but feel like she’s at a standstill, desperate to move forward but without the means to.

It’s then that Lena sees her own name, and is drawn to it like a moth engulfed in flames. Her eyes consume the prose like she’s starving.

Lena doesn't realize how much potential she has. I hope now at least the world will.

Lena sucks in a deep breath and reads on.

I wish people would see past the tough exterior she has. She’s powerful, strong, and a certified genius, but she’s also more than that. Lena wants to help people - really help people. Her heart is so full, sometimes I wonder if it’ll ever burst from it the way mine does. 

She is so incredibly kind, despite having been betrayed by those closest to her. I wish people took the time to know her the way that I do. See her the way that I do. Understand the beauty that lies both on the outside and within her heart. 

A tear trickles down Lena’s face and onto the page, alerting her to the fact that she’s crying. Despite the level of hurt Lena felt, she knew that her friendship with Kara was genuine, that Kara wasn’t really so deceptive as to use their friendship for Supergirl’s ends.

That Kara didn’t just see her as another Luthor.

But still, the omission hurt. Since she had found out the truth, Lena had mulled over all of her interactions with Kara. Every time she abruptly left because a source for one of my articles is returning my call or I have extremely bad cramps, I have to go, sorry ! 

Every time Lena had spoken badly about Supergirl to Kara’s face. Every lie and every save. Every laugh with Kara and every altercation with Supergirl. 

Lena thinks about all of the times Kara had saved her life and she hadn’t known. The helicopter, the chemicals. Mercy Graves. It was all her.

It’s been so hard to grapple with, merging two different people into one identity. Rectifying everything Lena thought she knew about Kara Danvers.

Or is it Kara Zor-El?

Did Lena really know Kara at all? Did Kara even want Lena to know her? The real her?

It’s those questions that push Lena to read on, despite the invasion of privacy. She’ll apologize profusely for it later, but for now, she needs to know.

A few hours into reading the diary like a novel, and not the inner musings of her best friend, the sound of a key turning into the lock startles Lena. She hurls the journal across the room like a football, it lands on Kara’s bed while she rearranges her features to look as natural as possible.

It's Kelly who walks in, eyes shooting up with surprise as her hand drops from the door. “Sorry, I didn’t think anyone would be here,” the therapist says, fumbling with her keys. “I’ll go—”

“Please,” Lena interrupts, before Kelly can leave the way she came. “Don’t be silly. Stay.”

The younger Luthor signals towards the couch, having made space for Kelly already. Lena pretends not to notice the redness of the other woman’s eyes and her smudged makeup.

“I came here to escape, actually,” Kelly starts, pulling some of her hair behind her ear. Lena momentarily wonders if it’s a nervous tick, seeing as they’ve hardly had conversations outside of their friend group. Well, former friend group. 

“Join the club,” Lena jokes with a small smile. Kelly mirrors the action slightly, her features brightening slightly. “Do you want to talk about it?”

Kelly honest to god laughs at that. “Usually I’m the one asking that question.”

The unfamiliarity of the encounter paired with the new insight that Kara’s journal has brought her makes Lena want to help Kelly. Give the other woman a shoulder to lean on, like her best friend frequently had given her.

“More of a reason to share then, isn’t it?,” she offers with a kind smile. “I swear I won't bite.”

Kelly steps further into the apartment, closing the door behind her. She carefully places her purse on the coffee table and joins Lena on the couch. She wipes at her jeans with clammy hands before meeting Lena’s soft eyes.

“Alex told me that Kara is Supergirl,” she starts, maintaining eye contact with her. Lena nods, taking the information in, glad that Alex had taken her earlier advice. Nobody should have to deal with Kara’s absence alone, least of all her own sister. “With the way Alex has been acting since Supergirl vanished, I should’ve known. In a logical sense, I understand why she kept her sister’s identity from me...” Kelly trails off, gazing out the window at the city lights. Her expression turns sour as the seconds pass.

“But?”

Kelly turns her body back towards Lena.

“I’m so angry with her,” Kelly sighs, her fists balling in her lap. “I wish Alex would’ve told me sooner. Now it isn’t just National City’s hero who is gone. It’s my friend Kara, and she’s gone.” Kelly swipes the few tears that had fallen from her cheeks with shaky hands. Her voice lowers a bit when she breathes, “It’s Kara.”

The empathy Lena feels hits her like an avalanche. She’s aware of her own tears hot on her cheeks at Kelly’s admission, the presence of heartbreak always at the forefront these days. 

She wants to tell Kelly that she understands her hurt, but, ultimately, Lena decides against it. She doesn’t have the right to compare their feelings. While Alex and Kelly are in a committed relationship, she and Kara are not .

Instead, Lena does what she can, as someone who isn’t quite used to affection. She places a hand over Kelly’s, telling her what she wishes someone had told her when she found out Kara’s identity.

“Your feelings are valid, Kelly,” Lena says softly, the words dry in her throat. “The woman you love didn’t tell you the truth about something important and that’s unnerving. It makes you wonder what else she could have lied about. It’s the what ifs that send you reeling.”

Kelly nods, cognizant of Lena’s far off expression. There’s something in it that she can’t quite pinpoint. “It’s strange talking about her in her apartment.”

Lena agrees, wiping her tear stricken face. She hears the unspoken without her here , and it makes her heart swoop and drop unpleasantly in her chest. She shakes it off as best she can, crossing her legs on the couch.

“I brought over a thirty year old bottle of scotch. We could crack it open if you’d like.”

Kelly pulls her own bottle of liquor out of her purse. “I thought I’d be drinking alone.”

“Listen, if you ever want to drink away your sorrows, you call me. Maybe we could order some food too?” Lena asks from the kitchen, grabbing two glasses.

“There’s this new Mediterranian place down the street,” Kelly says, pulling up a menu on her phone. “How do you feel about kale?”

Lena beams for the first time in weeks. 

Her night with Kelly allows Lena to decompress for a little while, conversing well into the night. Kelly shares how she wishes there was more she could do for Alex and how useless she feels when it comes to the team. Lena explains her sordid history with Lex while the other woman encourages her to get out from under his toxic thumb.

She can’t talk about Kara though. She won’t.

Aside from that specific tidbit, for once, Lena feels like she has a friend. One who isn’t Sam, and one she knows hadn’t lied to her about Kara’s identity. 

A week and a half later, they’re sitting at the kitchen island, in the middle of their dinner. Kelly had suggested an Ethiopian restaurant that she’d been dying to try, and without her weekly lunch dates with Kara, Lena was more than willing to oblige. They chat about an array of topics; from their shared unemployment status to which Harry Potter film was the worst ( Hands down, Order of the Phoenix. Why on Earth would the largest book have the shortest film?)

Lena takes a large bite of her steamed cabbage when Kelly asks, “That’s why you were so distant from the group last year? Kara didn’t tell you she was Supergirl?”

Lena barely nods, lamenting the things she did in the name of despair. Kelly scratches her nose and tilts her chin down into her takeaway container.

“What?”

“Nothing,” Kelly squeaks. Lena’s eyes narrow. “Gosh, these spices are incredible.”

Lena, the aficionado of sidestepping her emotions, doesn’t allow the subject change.

“It’s clearly not nothing, you can’t even look me in the eye,” Lena chuckles uneasily. “I know I’ve made mistakes. Unforgivable ones, but—”

Kelly cuts her off with a palm held out. “Lena, it’s not that. We’ve all made mistakes. How could I possibly judge you for yours?”

Lena droops her shoulders in relief. “Then what is it?”

Kelly bites her lip before putting her fork down. 

“When the two of you had fallen out, I had no idea Kara was Supergirl. Alex obviously couldn’t tell me why her sister had drifted apart from her best friend,” Kelly starts apprehensively, “So I drew my own conclusions.”

“That makes sense,” Lena nods. “What conclusion did you come to?”

Kelly rubs at her shoulder, but eventually rips off the proverbial band-aid.  “I thought you two had broken up.”

“Broken up?” Lena furrows her brow. “That sounds like you thought Kara and I were in a relationship.”

Kelly nods.

Oh. 

Oh.

Lena sits there speechless, her mouth agape for a solid minute. She keeps playing Kelly’s words over and over in her head, but all coherent thought is null and void.

Lena hasn’t allowed herself to think about Kara in this way, and has always shut off any feelings that would bubble to the surface before she could put any stock into it. But with one, simple misunderstanding, Kelly has lit the fuse that has been itching to burn Lena for the last five years of her life.

The other woman ultimately pities her, the panic evident on Lena’s face. 

“Lena?” The CEO could hardly say anything other than a disjointed hm. Lena hasn’t felt unmoored like this in a long time. “I know we haven’t been close for very long, but you can talk to me about anything.”

Lena turns to her, eyes glistening and lips trembling. The only thing she can manage to get out is a whispered, “She’s my best friend.”

All Kelly can do is smile tautly.

Lena realizes that despite the advice she gave to Brainy about little boxes, she was still keeping things locked away in them. 

Lex had her keycard access revoked after he’d found out she was sneaking into LuthorCorp’s sublevel lab. Luckily, with a little finesse, Alex, Nia and M’gann were able to steal her program back after regular business hours. It was just like Lex to restrict Lena from her own technology. Finally, she had friends who could give her the win she so desperately needed after being forced out of a company that used to be hers. Well, in another universe anyway.

It’s been two months since Kara was exiled to the Phantom Zone. Of course, Lena was spending every gruelling minute she could at the Tower, trying to save her.  In fact, she was so sure that the simulation she was running was going to be the one to bring Kara home. Even Brainy had said there was an 87.9% chance she would be successful.

But when the system says “error found,” Lena loses it.

She tears the monitor off of the wall and slams it heavily to the ground. Shards of glass shatter, each piece ricocheting in different directions. A guttural scream tears through her throat and tears fall freely from her eyes, blurring her vision. 

Lena doesn’t see Brainy approaching her, her view obstructed like a windshield during a thunderstorm. Lena flinches when she feels him settle behind her apprehensively. Her breaths are clipped while her chest rises and falls in quick succession. 

“I can’t do this anymore, Brainy,” Lena cries, running a shaky hand through her wavy hair. “I can't keep pretending.”

Brainy’s brow furrows as he observes Lena. His head then cocks to the side, the realization dawning on him. Lena carefully steps around the glass shards towards the wall and slips down onto the floor. Brainy crouches down next to her.

“I-I don’t know when it happened,” Lena starts with a shuddery breath. She then shakes her head and laughs through her tears. “Actually, that’s a lie. It happened the moment she and I met.”

Brainy doesn’t utter a word, yet he knows exactly who Lena is referring to.

“Remember when you asked me what love felt like?” He nods. “When you asked me that, my mind went to her straight away. To Kara.” Lena’s eyes flutter closed, this being the first time she’s said the other woman’s name in this context. “Then I began thinking about Dreamer’s speech, her opening up to the entire world about who she is and why that hit me so hard. I thought it was about working with Lex, but maybe, just maybe , I saw something in Dreamer. Something I wanted for myself. To be honest about who I am and who I l--”

Lena’s hand shoots to her mouth. When she looks up at Brainy, part of her expects to find judgment, considering her experiences with her family. But what she finds there is openness and care. Not shock or confusion, only understanding .

He grabs Lena’s hand in both of his.

“I have been across the galaxy, have spent my days in many timelines and countless universes. Despite all of that, Kara Danvers is still one of the most inspirational people I’ve had the pleasure of knowing,” he tells her fondly. “It is understandable why you would fall for her.”

The words hit Lena square in the chest like a sucker punch. All of the air is sucked out of her lungs, and a sob escapes her throat. Hearing those words out loud makes her have to face her feelings, the ones she’s been shoving to the side the entire time she’s known Kara.

It brings something dangerous to the surface. 

“What if she’s gone, Brainy?” Lena whispers so quietly that she’s sure he didn’t hear her. Brainy violently shakes his head.

“No. We can’t go down that path, Lena. We will find her,” he assures with vigor. “And when we do, I think you owe it to yourself to be honest with her.”

The thought of admitting these things out loud makes Lena want to flee to Ireland. Settle down in a cottage, never to surface in National City again.

“I don’t know if I can,” Lena sniffles. Her eyes are bloodshot from weeping, her energy on a downswing.

“Hey, no more boxes, remember?” 

“I regret telling you that now,” she laughs, attempting to regain her composure. “I’ll think about it.”

Brainy stands at full height and offers his hand for Lena to take. Once she’s on her feet again, Lena hesitantly steps in front of him.

“You look like you could use a hug, Lena Luthor.”

Lena doesn’t say anything before melting into his awkward arms. 

Later that night, Lena is exhausted from the thoughts that have taken residence in her mind. The phantoms. Lex. Losing L-Corp after Crisis, and now LuthorCorp. Losing Kara. Her not so platonic feelings for her. It’s all so loud when she steps into Kara’s loft for the fifth night in a row.

Looking around the space, anyone would think she’s moved in. The amount of surfaces her belongings are littered over is exorbitant and frankly, out of control. There wasn’t much time to organize the space the last few nights with the knowledge that the more time Kara spent in the Phantom Zone, the less likely their recovery mission would work.

Lena shrugs out of her coat, placing it on the coat rack and throws her bag onto the couch. She then goes through her usual routine. She sifts through Kara’s dresser and slips into a pair of her gray sweats and finally, she throws on the usual NCU sweater she’s been commandeering for years and slips into bed.

She waits for Kara’s scent to wrap around her like a blanket. Unfortunately for Lena, it never comes.

“No,” Lena breathes in a tizzy. She pulls the garment off, feeling like a mad woman trying to smell the remnants of her best friend on it. She can’t, so she slams it down on the mattress, a piece of her breaking in the process. 

She pulls her knees up to her chest, sobbing openly while clutching the sweater. “I’m sorry, Kara. I’m so fucking sorry.”

She’s lying on her side when Alex enters the apartment a little later. “Lena?” The sound of her footsteps come closer, and Alex’s fire-red hair comes into her peripheral. The other woman takes note of the tear tracks on Lena’s face and the jumper she’s clutching in her hands. “Oh, Lena.”

Lena hiccups, her eyes fluttering closed. She tries to will the other Danvers away so she can grieve in solitude, but no such luck. Alex perches herself at the edge of the bed, placing a cautious but comforting hand on Lena’s back.

After about ten minutes of silence, other than Lena’s occasional sniffle and the vibration of Alex’s phone, Lena whispers, “It doesn’t smell like her anymore.”

Alex nods, a bit startled after the long stretch of time without a peep. “For me, it was her Midvale High gym tee. She’d leave it in my bottom drawer for when she stayed the night.”

Lena sits up, pushing the comforter back with her socked feet. She realizes that she’s in a state of semi-undress, so she holds Kara’s sweater to her chest. 

Alex turns to her, sighing. “Some days I wake up and it’s difficult to put one foot in front of the other. If I didn’t have Kelly, I don’t know,” she trails off, placing her hands over her face. She shakes her head, and holds her head up high. “What I’m trying to say is, you don’t have to do this alone.”

“Even after everything I've done?”

“Lena, we both know that you weren’t the only one in the wrong.”

“You did threaten to blow me up,” Lena supplies casually. More casual than she actually feels.

Alex has the decency to look remorseful. “I never would have.”

“For me or for Kara?” Alex looks at her with honest eyes, and Lena has her answer. The other woman focuses her attention on the loft’s ceiling.

“For as long as I can remember, I’ve always had this sense of duty,” Alex starts, playing with her fingers in her lap. “When Kara came to live with us and my life became about protecting her. When I followed in my parents’ footsteps in getting my bio-engineering degree. When I was recruited as an agent for the DEO. Following orders is all I’ve known for the better part of my life.

“Occasionally, when it came to protecting my family, I’d bend the rules. To save their lives. When I became Director of the DEO, all of that changed. I couldn’t break any of the rules, because they became my rules. My code. One that I needed to enforce so that my agents would too.”

Lena shifts, hanging onto every word. She nods, because she isn’t mad at Alex, not really. “I understand. I was acting like a villain—”

Alex politely cuts her off. “No, Lena. You were hurt and I contributed to that hurt. I used Kara to track your location to Mount Norquay when she just wanted to get you back. I crossed a line by pointing that missile at you, and it’s one of the reasons I’m glad that the DEO is no more.”

Lena doesn’t know what to say, so she just waits for Alex to continue.

“I haven’t told anyone this, not even Kara, but that night really changed things for me. Because if I was able to point Claymore at someone I consider a friend, what else am I capable of? What else would I have done in the name of justice?” she asks, wiping a stray tear from the corner of her eye. 

“Alex, I was on the verge of doing something terrible. You did what you had to do.”

“Maybe, but I could’ve gone about it in another way. You deserved at least that,” Alex says, choked up, but without room for Lena to disagree. “I am so sorry that I chose not to believe in you the way Kara did back then. But I believe in you now, and I know that you are the best chance we will ever have to bring her home.”

Lena hugs Alex, hard. So tightly that she was probably bruising a few ribs, but she didn’t care. She never thought that she and Alex would be on good terms again after the bad blood they had last year. She now believed they could overcome things, in their own atypical way.

“Thank you,” Lena says, glassy eyed against Alex’s shoulder.

They sit like that for a few moments before Alex gently pulls them apart. Lena is still clutching onto Kara’s sweater, holding it close, when a scrunched look appears on Alex’s face.

She takes in Lena’s outfits hanging on the clothing rack, her books that rest on Kara’s nightstand, the photo of the two of them next to the pile. The way Lena had so easily integrated herself into Kara’s space. Alex had hardly noticed until now, but it’s hard to unsee something that had initially seemed so seamless.

“I don’t say this enough, but thank you,” Alex says, squeezing Lena’s hand. “You’re always there for us when it counts.”

“Of course,” Lena smiles uncomfortably. “National City needs Supergirl.”

“Just National City?”

Lena’s cheeks flush, but then she grows serious. She unlinks their hands before clearing her throat. “Despite my efforts that day, she still got hurt. Maybe worse.”

“That wasn't you,” Alex assures. “That was Lex.”

“It’s my fault that my brother got his hands on my project in the first place. I am culpable, whether I like it or not.”

“Nobody is holding that against you, you know,” Alex promises, and Lena nearly believes her. “Kara told me you wanted to take away people’s pain. I can’t blame you for that.”

Lena smiles thinly, crossing one leg over the other. “I suppose my intention was good, but the methodology left much to be desired.”

Alex chuckles lightly, which shocked Lena. Never did she think they’d be in a place to joke about her misgivings.

“I have to say, it’s kind of a relief. My sister was so torn up over your friendship. Even Mxy was rooting for the two of you.”

Lena scrunches her face. “I’m sorry, who?” 

“Mxyzptlk? The imp from the 5th dimension?”

“Doesn’t ring a bell, I’m afraid,” Lena says with an inquisitive eye. 

“Kara never told you about him? About what she did?”

“Told me what?” 

Before Alex can say anything further, the front door swings open and Kelly pops into the bedroom area. She gives Alex a quick kiss hello. “Are you ready?”

“Yes,” Alex says, redirecting her attention to her girlfriend. She gets up from the bed, grabs her belongings and is about to leave before turning back around. “You coming, Luthor? I’m starving.”

“Coming where?” Lena quirks her eyebrow.

“To dinner,” Kelly smiles softly. Off of Lena’s confused reaction, she turns to Alex. “Did you not tell her?”

“I forgot,” Alex reveals with an apologetic expression on her face.

Kelly shakes her head with a grin.

“Please. Don’t let me interrupt your date,” Lena sighs, laying back into the bed. “I’m fine here.”

“Clutching my sister’s sweater, half naked? Right. And don’t think I haven’t seen you hiding Kara’s journals.”

Lena’s cheeks turn a rosy shade of pink, her pride clearly wounded. “I’ll have you know—”

Alex rolls her eyes. “Lena, please come out with us.” 

Lena is about to protest when Kelly cuts her off with some kind, but tough love. “Don’t even bother arguing. We’re getting you out of this apartment.”

Lena grumbles, but eventually succumbs to their demands. It’s probably for her own good anyway—after the emotional night she’s had, Lena could use some comfort food.

“Fine. But we need to go somewhere that won’t alert the press. They don’t need to know that the black sheep of the Luthor family wears sweats.”

“Lena, your algorithm,” Brainy runs over to Lena like a kid in a candy shop. “It’s working.”

Lena looks at Brainy crookedly, like he isn’t speaking a language she understands. It’s been a rough week—well, a rough two and a half months. “Huh?”

He places both of his arms on each of Lena’s deltoids, steadying her. But no amount of physical touch seems to be helping her process what he’s telling her.

“Lena, your tracker is scanning the Phantom Zone as we speak. We’re one step closer to finding Kara,” he grins widely. “You did it!”

At that, Alex, Kelly, and Nia quickly join them by the mainframe.

“Did I hear that right?” Nia asks, the anticipation making her buzz. The older Danvers sister bites her lip, the conflict evident on her face. Kelly slips her hand into her girlfriend’s, giving her the support that Lena wishes she had right now. She fixates on the little squeeze Alex gives her and it somehow brings her back to reality.

Lena steps over to the large monitor and sees that Brainy is right. The system is scanning the Phantom Zone for Kryptonian DNA. Her mouth is agape, in a seemingly permanent state of shock. 

For the first time in nearly three months, she has hope again. The foreign feeling is so strong, she may burst from it.

Brainy explains the ins and outs of what Lena’s tech has done, but she hears none of it. All she can think of is her best friend and how her chances of seeing her again has just doubled. 

Before, the goal was simply getting the signal to work. But now, Lena is faced with more what if’s. 

What if the tracker doesn’t pick up her DNA? What if we’re too late? 

The questions speed through her mind like a freight train. The thought of getting any answers to them, good or bad, makes Lena’s head spin.

“Lena,” Kelly’s hand lay softly on her shoulder, Lena still not used to the level of affection she’s been on the receiving end of lately. She gives the woman a simple hmm in response. “You did it. You’re going to be the reason we get Kara back.”

Alex is watch on beside her, teary eyed, nodding a small thank you to Lena. She cannot take the pressure that’s been thrust upon her, it all starts to get too much.

“I wouldn’t jump the gun just yet,” J’onn interrupts sternly with a small grin on his face. M’gann’s arms are tangled up in his. 

Lena silently thanks him for being the fatherly voice of reason, bringing her back from the edge of the proverbial cliff she’s been trying hard not to jump off. The one she’s been looking out at since her brother sealed Kara’s fate. “We still have a lot of ground to cover. From what we know about the Phantom Zone, it is very vast and even more unpredictable. Especially considering everything we knew about it before Crisis is null and void.”

The team all nod, coming back down to reality.

“If we track her exact location,” Lena chimes in, but quickly notices her mistake. “ When we track her location, we’ll then need to get her exact coordinates. It’s only then that we can send her back through the transmat portal.”

“So you’re saying this can take hours? Days?” Alex asks.

“It could be weeks,” Brainy tells them dejectedly. 

They all turn to the screen, willing it to move faster. They stand around wordlessly, waiting for something to happen. When it doesn’t, four shoulders slump.

“You all need to go,” M’gann scolds gently. “Staring at this screen won’t do you any good. Go out for drinks, something . J’onn and I will stay here and alert you all if anything changes.”

They all start speaking in unison:

“Space Mom, please—”

“I’d rather not, if I’m honest—”

“Kara will need me, if she—”

“Kara wouldn’t want you to stop living your lives for her,” J’onn smiles sadly, without leaving room for disagreement. “M’gann’s right. There’s nothing for you to do here that she and I can’t handle. Now go.”

It’s said with the utmost love that tears pool in Lena’s eyes. M’gann grins up at J’onn, pressing a palm to his chest. 

They all mutter their thank yous as they exit the control room. Lena is the last one out, lagging behind until she’s alone with J’onn.

“Thank you,” Lena says, and the words are heavy on her tongue, laced with so many different meanings.

“It’s me who should be thanking you , Lena,” he returns, placing a warm hand by the collar of her shirt. Lena looks to the warm hand resting there, not used to the feeling that has taken up residence in her chest. “You’ve given everyone something that we haven’t had since Kara’s been gone.”

Lena swallows back her emotions to the best of her ability. “What’s that?”

“Hope.”

They forgo the bar and end up at Kara’s place. 

There’s more alcohol covering the kitchen table than any human, alien, or hybrid could possibly drink. They had ordered Chinese food from Kara’s favorite restaurant, with empty containers now littering the coffee table while Nia, Brainy, Alex, Kelly and Lena sit comfortably around it.

The strangest part about it is that Lena feels light around these people. Her friends. 

Maybe it’s the fact that they’re sharing their favorite war stories about her best friend while Nia drunkenly records it for posterity . Or maybe she just finally feels like she is beginning to belong somewhere. 

The fact that her place seems to be with most of Kara’s favorite people (minus James and Winn, who wish they could be there to help, but are unable to), doesn’t escape her notice.

“Remember the night Kara inhaled twenty potstickers so quickly that she got soy sauce up her nose?” Nia asks, holding her glittery phone with a trans rights sticker out in front of her.

“What about the time that Dominator was wreaking havoc on the city and Supergirl stopped fighting him to pet a bunch of puppies,” Alex supplies, kicking back some of her whiskey. “Kept me and the agents up all night.” Alex’s brow crinkles, and Kelly rubs her arm as she tries not to giggle and fails.

“Oh, how about when she was two hours late to her own game night, because she promised her neighbor she’d jazzercise with him?” Nia laughs, alcohol nearly shooting out of her nose. “He was like 80.”

“Does Supergirl need to exercise?” Kelly asks, genuinely intrigued.

“Any human form of exercise wouldn’t do much for her Kryptonian physique,” Brainy offers, swinging his beer back and forth in his hands.

Lena thinks back to the times she invited Kara to her spin classes, and bites back the residual hurt there. 

Tonight isn’t about that. Tonight is about celebrating Kara—wonderful, selfless, brave Kara who is reliving the biggest trauma of her life right now.

Lena excuses herself to the kitchen so she can refill her wine glasses, swallowing back the guilt she feels.

Nia follows, grabbing another wine cooler from the refrigerator. Lena’s nerves kick in, her hand shaking lightly as she pours from the three year old bottle of red, trying to keep it as steady as possible. She is quite aware that she and Nia have only had a handful of conversations throughout their acquaintanceship. It’s the awareness of that unfamiliarity that has her pace quickening.

Nia places her hand on Lena’s shaky one. “I’m glad you’re back, you know,” the younger woman says with a hazy, drunk smile. “We really missed you at game night.”

“You did?”

“Of course, dude,” Nia smirks from the side of her mouth, grabbing the cap of her drink. She twists it between her thumb and pointer finger, and it slides onto the floor with a clatter. Nia picks up the cap and places it onto the counter ungracefully. “You’re amazing at like...every game. And you’re drop dead gorgeous too, it’s kind of unfair? Don’t know if I ever told you that, but I am telling you now, and that’s what counts.”

Lena blushes hard, taking a long sip of her wine. She nearly chokes on it. “Thank you, Nia. You’re not so bad yourself.”

“Are you hitting on me?” Nia deadpans, making Lena nearly jump out of her own skin, because what . Nia places a hand over her chest and animatedly continues, “I am very much in a committed relationship with that twelfth level intellect over there.”

“Nia, I’m not—”

“Eh, if I wasn’t, I’d probably consider it. Although, I think Kara would be a bit mad if I went after her woman.” 

Nia winks, and rejoins Brainy who is in a heated argument with Kelly about a military grade something or other. Lena is left stupefied, not able to process a single thing Nia has said to her in the last five minutes. So much for unfamiliar territory.

Something dawns on Lena then. 

“Wait. Kara’s what ?”

After Alex has assured Lena for the nth time that J’onn hasn’t made contact yet, they decide to play a few games. Ultimately, they don’t last very long; it feels too weird without their Kryptonian host. 

The two couples doze off sometime around midnight. Nobody is in any shape to drive, and Alex grumbles at talk of a Lyft back to her and Kelly’s place, so they decide to all crash at Kara’s.

Lena was planning on spending the night there anyway. At least she wasn’t doing so alone tonight.

Nia’s face is nestled in the crook of Brainy’s neck on the floor while Alex and Kelly are snuggled close on the couch. A wave of protectiveness settles over Lena then, thinking about how cold they must be. Lena grabs some blankets out of Kara’s closet and drapes them over her friends. 

She steps back, taking a look at her handiwork and wonders how she got here. Caring about people who cared for her in return.

While the others rest, sleep doesn’t come for Lena. With the fate of Kara’s return in the hands of her program, the possibilities don’t allow her mind to settle. The apartment is eerily quiet as usual, with the occasional thump of the upstairs neighbors and city chatter outside of the large windows.

After tossing and turning for a while, Lena picks up her favorite night time book, accompanied with the rest of her wine, and eases back into the sea of pillows on Kara’s bed.

She reads through some of her favorite passages, ones about Kara’s job at CatCo as well as some of the metahumans and aliens she’s fought over the years. 

There’s one particular entry that Lena reads every night before she sleeps.

In an unforeseen turn of events, Lena Luthor helped me realize I want to be a reporter. I don’t know if she realizes the gift she’s given me, but her words have stayed with me since the day Kal and I met her. 

I don’t know why, but I have this gut feeling that she is important.

After reading through the passage a few times, Lena eventually succumbs to slumber.

After Nia snores for the fifth time in twenty minutes, Alex wakes with a groan.

She scans the apartment and it’s still pitch black, only a few hours having passed since they all dozed off. She pulls her wrist up to her face, checking her watch and sees that it’s three in the morning. 

Her phone has no new messages, so Alex rubs at her eyes and huffs, trying her best not to wake Kelly. “Guess I’m up now.”

She slips out from underneath her girlfriend and the blanket covering them and notices that Lena is absent from the group. She’s seconds away from angry calling her, for not being able to stay away from the Tower for one night, when Alex finds her sprawled out on her sister’s bed.

Alex smiles tautly, pulling the open book off of Lena’s chest when she realizes what it is. Alex is momentarily angry at Lena for reading through her sister’s private musings. Then she grabs it, reading the entry Lena had the book open to.

Alex only reads a few lines before shutting the journal and placing it on the nightstand next to Lena’s head. The movement stirs the sleeping body in the bed and Lena and cloudy green eyes flutter open.

“Alex?”

“Go back to sleep,” Alex whispers, placing a hand on Lena’s shoulder. “Nothing to report yet.”

Lena nods, sleepily rubbing at her face. “Must’ve dozed off.”

Alex shuffles on either foot for a moment before glancing at the journal. “All that late night reading, I would’ve too.”

Lena sleepily follows Alex’s eye line. Emerald eyes go comically wide as she sits up hurriedly in bed. “Look, Alex. I wasn’t—”

“I get it.” Alex sits at the edge of the bed next to Lena’s feet. She doesn’t say anything, just simply waits for Lena to speak.

“I needed to know if she...” Lena starts quietly, playing with her fingers. “If there was any indication that we could go back to the way things were.”

“You and both know that isn't what you want.”

“What? Of course I do,” Lena says with a furrowed brow. “I want Kara and I to be okay again.”

“But there’s more to it than that, isn’t there? You want more, don’t you?” It isn’t really a question that falls off Alex’s tongue. Lena looks thoroughly scrutinized, like Alex was inside of her mind. “It’s okay, Lena.”

“Yes,” she says plainly. “But as long as she’s in my life, that’s more than enough for me.”

“Kara wouldn’t want you to do that,” Alex assures. “No matter what the outcome, I think she’d want to know the truth.”

“I’ve lost Kara twice now,” Lena slips out in between her falling tears. “I can’t do it again.”

Alex is about to say more when her phone buzzes incessantly in her pocket. She and Lena share a look as she pulls it out. “It’s J’onn.”

Lena is already on her feet. “I’ll wake everyone.”

It all happens very quickly. 

Lena’s tracker program has matched the genome from Kara’s hair to one in the Phantom Zone, which then flagged the coordinates to the Tower.

Lena gets the transmat portal ready while the team suits up, preparing to fight off more phantoms than they had ever faced before.

J’onn gives a speech, saying things like We’ve been training for this moment for nearly three months and Stick together, leave no person behind . Finally, he ends it with, “Let’s bring our girl home.”

Lena tries to ignore all of the mixed feelings she has swirling in her stomach as she enters the exact coordinates into the portal’s central processor.

“Portal is ready to go,” she tells Alex, J’onn, M’gann, Brainy and Nia. “I’ll be out of range once you enter the Phantom Zone, but you will be able to communicate with one another.”

Kelly is at Lena’s side, a hand around her waist. Bless the woman, because Lena would probably collapse to the floor from the sheer weight of this moment alone.

“Once you walk through the portal, you won’t be able to get back without this device,” Lena tells them, handing it to J’onn. “The red button will allow you to get the portal back up and running so as to not let anything or anyone else through.” 

“The last thing we need is a bunch of rogue phantoms loose in National City,” Kelly jokes sardonically, lightening the mood.

Lena is acutely aware that if they fail, it’ll be because of her. They may not get another chance like this.

Lena channels all of the strength she’s getting from Kelly’s small, “You can do this,” and the portal comes alive, purple light hitting every inch of the Tower. As they disappear into the Phantom Zone, the beam wanes and everything goes quiet.

Lena shudders, the gravity of the situation hitting her.

“They’re going to bring her home. I know it,” Kelly whispers.

“I certainly hope you’re right.”

When twelve hours come and go, Lena begins to panic.

“You’re going to burn a hole through the floor with those heels,” Kelly quips, swirling around in the computer chair. “Which, by the way, how the hell are you wearing those right now?”

Lena doesn’t hear her though, pacing and mumbling different formulas and protocols under her breath. 

Where did she go wrong? Did she just get all of her friends killed? It was too dangerous, she would’ve gone in there herself if she could’ve—

Suddenly Kelly is in front of her, closer than Lena remembers her being before. “Come here.”

Kelly guides Lena to the chair she was previously occupying. 

“I’m fine.”

“Clearly.”

Lena huffs, her shoulders slumping in her suit. “They’ve been gone a long time.”

“Didn’t Brainy say that time moves differently in the Phantom Zone?”

“Well, yes, but—”

Kelly sighs. “I know you’re worried, but they will bring Kara home. Have faith in them.”

Lena stands, arms crossed over her stomach. “The last time I had faith in someone, I found out she had been lying to me about who she was for three years, so forgive me if I’m a little out of practice.” The words come out harsher than she intends. “I’m sorry.”

Kelly nods, understanding the displaced aggression. “Listen, I’m worried about them too, okay? But I know Alex. She wouldn’t do this if she didn’t believe it was worth the risk.”

“I just hate this. The waiting, the worrying.” Lena pinches the bridge of her nose. “It isn’t just Kara I’m worried about. They could all be in danger.”

“Hey,” Kelly says, kneeling in front of Lena. “They all knew what they were signing up for, okay? They all were willing to put their lives on the line. That’s what it means to be a hero.”

Lena’s mind races, her anxiety peaking, when the transmat portal whirrs into life.

Kelly grabs her Guardian shield and pulls Lena behind her. “Get back.”

Lena has the Prototrap at the ready. “Don’t worry about me.”

Nia and M’gann come through the portal with an injured Brainy hanging on each of their shoulders. He groans in pain, his leg bleeding. “We need help!”

“Where are the others?” Kelly asks, holding onto the other side of Brainy. He’s groggy and barely conscious, his body limp like a rubber band.

“They were right behind us,” M’gann says hurriedly. “A horde of phantoms ambushed the team. J’onn and Alex were still fighting them off, but we had to get Brainy to safety.”

“And Supergirl?”

Their eyes shift toward a desperate Lena. Nia shakes her head regretfully.

“Go,” Lena swallows, trying to subdue her tears. “I’ll man the portal.”

Kelly abandons her shield to guide M’gann and Nia to the medical bay. Lena clenches her fingers around the Prototrap, knuckles turning white from the harsh grip she has on it. If her tracker couldn’t locate Kara, they were out of options, and have potentially lost her in the Phantom Zone forever.

J’onn falls through the portal next with a phantom in his clutches. He has it in a headlock, but the phantom gets a few hits in. “A little help here!”

He shape shifts out of the phantom’s hands and grabs him by the neck, giving Lena the opening she needs to zap him. She’s fixed to the spot where she’s stood, overwhelmed by everything that has gone wrong already.

“Lena, now!”

She kicks into gear at the sound of her name. The yellow beam of her invention sucks the phantom in like a vacuum. J’onn relaxes, turning back into his human form. They both breathe a sigh of relief. 

“Thanks.”

“Don’t mention it.”

As Lena helps J’onn off the floor, two more bodies walk through the portal. Alex looks worse for wear, a few scrapes and cuts on her, but ultimately she has all of her limbs intact. 

Beside her though, is a true sight for sore eyes.

Kara.

Her Super suit is charred and dirty and her lip is busted, but Lena couldn’t care less. Kara is here, in the flesh. Alive. They didn’t fail. 

She didn’t fail.

Her name slips between Lena’s lips without permission. An invisible electricity passes through the room when sapphire eyes lock onto emerald green.  

Kara throws Lena a small smile and it says everything that had previously gone unsaid between them. 

I’m so glad to see you. I forgive you.

Lena returns it, trying to convey the things she never got to say to her best friend before she got the chance to.

They don’t get the chance to reconcile properly, between Kara needing to recover under yellow sun lamps and everyone getting private time with her. 

Alex and Kelly tend to Brainy, making sure he’s stable and medicated. Nia stays at his bedside while Lena stays by Kara’s, even if the Super is asleep most of the night and the following day.

Lena slips out mid-afternoon, not knowing if her face would be one Kara wanted to see upon first waking up.

Lena spends the night at her own place.

As much as Kara wanted to spend time reuniting with the team, her exhaustion won out. Even though the sun lamps had allowed her to stabilize after returning to Earth, the Kryptonian was mentally exhausted. Alex escorted her sister back to her apartment and advised everyone to get some much needed rest. They would have plenty of time to reconnect once Kara was feeling more herself again.

Lena boils herself water for tea, orders in and catches up on emails that she had been neglecting. Since leaving LuthorCorp, the businesswoman had begun moving money from offshore accounts and cashing in on several investments. Lena needed the capital to start a new company, one not tainted with the Luthor name. One that evil could not touch. 

All of that was put on hold when rescuing Kara had been her primary focus. Now that that’s happened, she can bring her attention back to her career. After the emotional toll that losing Supergirl took on Lena, she had to push the woman to the back of her mind where she usually dwelled.

Lena calls it a night at around 10pm, the fatigue from the last three months seeping through her bones. She brushes her teeth and removes her makeup, uncovering the dark bags that have formed her eyes. Lena glances at her reflection in the mirror and doesn’t know who the person staring back at her is. Now that there isn’t anything or anyone holding her back, she can explore who she is.

Once she slips into sleep shorts and Kara’s NCU hoodie that she’s commandeered, she slips under the covers and flicks off the lamp at her bedside.

It’s no surprise that slumber doesn’t find her.

It’s nearing 12am when she hears a faint noise on the balcony outside of her bedroom window. She doesn’t reach for the gun in her top drawer underneath her underwear, because logically there’s only one person it can be.

She opens the glass door, a chill hitting her as it enters the space. Outside is Kara wrapped in a blanket and a pillow in hand. She isn’t wearing her glasses and her golden locks are flowing a bit haphazardly in the midnight wind.

She’s more beautiful than Lena remembers, even if she does appear a bit haggard.

“I didn’t mean to wake you,” she voices, cheeks flushed.

“You didn't.” Lena looks down to the item in Kara’s hands. “Were you planning on sleeping out here?”

“Yes,” Kara says plainly. “I faked being asleep so Alex would go home. She looked so drained and she was in hovering mode, so.”

“I can’t sleep either,” Lena supplies. “Would you like to come in? It’s awfully cold out here.”

Kara smiles faintly. “I actually don’t feel the cold. But I'd like to come in anyway, if that’s alright with you.”

Lena doesn’t say anything, only moves aside as Kara enters the bedroom. The Kryptonian takes in the room as if she’s seeing it for the first time. 

“When I was in the Phantom Zone, my memories were foggy,” she starts in a near whisper. “I didn’t know which ones were real and which were the phantoms messing with my head. Then I’d remember being here, with you, and...It was one memory that I was sure was real.”

Lena tries to keep her tears at bay, but they pool by her eyelashes. She doesn’t know what she’s supposed to say, so she waits for Kara to continue. But Kara doesn’t look like she wants to discuss it any further.

It pains Lena to say, “I can take the guest room tonight if you’d feel more comfortable here.”

“Lena.”

“Kara.”

“I’m not going to put you out of your own room,” she smiles camly.

“It’s fine. The bed in there is just as satisfactory as—”

“Stay? Please.” Kara looks so broken in that moment that Lena could hardly say no. Not that she’s denied Kara anything in the time that she’s known her.

Lena slips back under the covers she had previously abandoned, taking her usual right side when Kara had normally stayed over. Their bodies don’t touch, which isn’t difficult considering the king size of the bed, but still Lena can feel the energy radiating off of Kara.

It’s so surreal having Kara here with her, she has to pinch herself to be sure she hadn’t drifted off.

“Goodnight, Lena.”

“Goodnight, Kara.”

They both sleep well into the night for the first time in months.

Lena wakes to the mid-morning sun spilling through her curtains. When she opens her eyes, she registers that there are strong arms wrapped around her.

Kara.

Sometime during the night, the other woman must’ve drifted to her side. Her eyes crinkle when an all encompassing smile takes over her face. Not wanting to let go of that feeling, Lena snuggles back into Kara once more and succumbs to slumber once more.

When she awakens a second time, there are no strong arms surrounding her torso. She hears a voice coming from the living room, so she pulls some slippers on and follows it.

“I’m fine. Yes, I’m at Lena’s, I’m—I’m sorry I didn’t send a text. I just forgot, I—Hey, Kelly. Yes, thank you.” Lena can hear the smile in Kara’s voice. “I’ll come by later. Yes, I swear. No, I will not swear on Streaky...Okay, okay. Bye.”

Kara presses the end button with a huff when Lena finally makes her presence known. 

“Coffee?” 

Kara turns around and smiles at Lena. “Please.”

Lena moves toward the Keurig machine, setting a pod into the contraption and places a mug underneath the spout. Coffee begins to brew while Kara settles into her usual seat at the kitchen island. 

If Lena didn’t know better, she’d think a year and three months hadn’t passed. She’d think that they were fine, that there wasn’t any tension between them.

But she does know better.

“How’d you sleep?”

“Better than I have in months. You?”

“The same,” Lena says, twirling her fingers. “I’m not really missing the lump in your mattress.”

“My apartment looks immaculate by the way,” Kara smiles, turning to Lena. “I appreciate everything you’ve done for me, Lena.”

Lena inhales through her nose and nods. “Alex told you.”

“She told me a lot of things.”

There’s something underlying Kara’s tone that unnerves Lena to her core. “Like what?”

“That it was a tracking device you created that got me out of the Phantom Zone,” Kara says. The sunlight shining through the glass windows illuminates Kara’s face. Lena can’t look away, doesn’t want to. “That you never stopped looking for me.”

Lena laughs without mirth. “It was a team effort.”

“But it was different for you, wasn’t it?”

“Kara—”

Kara steps into Lena’s space, coffee long forgotten about. “I need you to know that your brother banishing me to the Phantom Zone was not your fault. I don’t blame you for what happened.”

The tears at the precipice now free fall from Lena’s eyes. It was one thing to hear Alex say it, but it’s another thing completely to hear it from Kara herself. The weight of her guilt slowly lifts from her shoulders. Still, she can’t help but ask. “How? How could you not blame me for all of it?”

“Because I know you would never hurt me in that way,” Kara says, strong and sure. “I meant what I said back in the Tower that day. I know what’s in your heart.”

“Not everything,” Lena whispers. She gasps, her hand flying to her mouth, shocked at the words that had involuntarily come out like a reflex. She remembers the conversations she had with Kelly, Brainy, and Alex. No more boxes, remember? With her hands shaking and her heart rattling wildly against her ribcage, Lena says, “You don’t know everything that’s in my heart, Kara.”

Kara looks surprised, and a bit scared. Lena then puts it together that Kara has just been through quite the ordeal. She doesn’t need Lena dumping her emotions onto her as well.

“I’m sorry. We don’t need to talk about this right now. I want to be here for you.”

“Lena, if there’s something you need to tell me, I’m ready to hear it, no matter what it is,” Kara mutters softly, placing a comforting hand on top of Lena’s.

Lena takes in a harsh breath and gestures towards the living room. They settle on the couch, and Lena turns toward Kara.

"I’ve decided to stop showing up to Lena’s apartment and LuthorCorp, even if staying away is the last thing I want to do. But today I couldn’t help myself. No matter what I did, I couldn’t concentrate. Not on work, not on saving the people of National City, not on my friends. No matter what corner of the Earth I go to, I can still hear her heartbeat as clear as day.

I’ll keep my distance, but I can’t help needing to be in close proximity to Lena. I don’t think I could ever truly stay away from her." 

Lena finishes reciting the passage from memory. Confusion passes through Kara’s features, but once she reaches the final lines, recognition covers her face. There isn’t anger there, but something else. In the presence of it, Kara goes blank.

“You read my journal.”

“I’m sorry,” Lena says regretfully. “I know I crossed so many boundaries by doing so. I didn’t know if I—we had lost you.” 

Lena swallows back the thought, it being completely unthinkable.

“It’s okay. I just don’t understand...”

“That passage stuck with me, out of all of the others. Why couldn’t you stay away from me, Kara? Even when I begged you to?”

Kara looks pained by her question and crosses her arms. “I wanted to make sure you were safe.”

“When you first told me you were Supergirl, you’d said you lied to me because you didn’t want to lose me,” Lena says calmly, without hurt lacing her voice, probably for the first time. “Then you told me you’d done it to keep me safe, just like you are now. So which is it?”

“Both? I don’t get where you’re going with this,” Kara replies somewhat defensively.

“I convinced myself by keeping my own secret, that I was protecting you. I was so upset with you for omitting the truth from me, yet here I was doing the exact same thing. I haven’t been honest with you, but I owe it to you to do so now.”

Kara looks frightened, like Lena is about to shatter her entire world. Which, in all fairness, she may. But Lena needs to tell Kara the truth. So she does.

“I was incredibly hurt when I found out you were Supergirl. I convinced myself that you didn’t trust me, that you saw me as just another Luthor. That our entire friendship was a pretense, because all of that was easier than facing the real catalyst behind my heartbreak.” Kara’s eyes shoot to hers, and it makes Lena’s heart swoop. “Kara, I am in love with you. I am so in love with you, and have been for longer than I’d care to admit. 

“I have been betrayed by so many people in my life, but to feel like the person I would go to the ends of the Earth for had done the same to me...well, I just couldn’t bear it.”

Kara doesn’t say anything. Lena tries not to completely lose her nerve.

“Just to be clear, I don’t expect anything from you. I know you probably don’t see me that way, and that’s so alright. I am perfectly fine with us working our way back to the friendship we had before, just with everything out in the open now, i-if that’s something you want. If you’re not, I absolutely understand. If I’ve lost you completely, I can’t say I blame you. I just thought you deserved to know the truth.”

Kara sits with her hands on her knees, her face devoid of any emotion. She stays reticent for a few minutes, and with each tick of the clock, Lena grows more and more worried that she had said the wrong thing. That after spending all of her time getting Kara back from the Phantom Zone, her feelings are how she loses her for good.

“Since you found my journals, I’m surprised you didn’t come across the tapes,” Kara croaks out after a while.

“Tapes?”

“The night I told you that if you continued to work with Lex, that I’d treat you like a villain,” Kara swallows, the taste of regret acrid on her tongue. “A friend of mine had offered to help me alter the timeline for any reason I desired.”

Lena remembers her conversation with Alex. “Mxy, right? Your sister mentioned him.”

“Yes, Mxy. When he offered this chance to me, it was a no brainer. I could’ve changed so many things, but I chose to alter our timeline so that I could tell you I was Supergirl sooner. So that maybe you wouldn’t hate me and I wouldn’t have lost you,” Kara admits, turning her head away from Lena’s expressive face. “We tried several different timelines, and Mxy let me keep the tapes of each one.”

“Did anything change? Did I react well in any of them?”

“There was one where you did. We were partners from the very beginning,” Kara smiled, recalling the particular memory. Her features turn grim recalling what happened next. She explains it to Lena, who is stunned by the outcome. 

“We all died, because you revealed your identity to the world?”

“Yes,” Kara confirms.

”And you revealed your identity to the world to save my life?”

”I did.”

Lena takes this information in, it being incredibly difficult to wrap her head around. She takes a few moments to let Kara’s words seep in, like moisture on skin. To simply inhale and exhale before compiling her thoughts. They still come out more jumbled than she’d prefer.

“There’s one thing I don’t understand. You wanted to change the timeline for our friendship? Kara, you could’ve made it so Krypton didn’t explode! Your parents...I don’t—why change things with me?

Kara gives Lena a look, and it’s like the last piece of an elaborate puzzle falls into place.

Oh.

“With all the time I had to think in the Phantom Zone, it all clicked for me,” Kara says with confidence. She interlaces her fingers with Lena’s. “I love you too, Lena.”

Lena doesn’t realize she’s crying until Kara wipes away the wetness from her cheeks. “You do?”

“You really didn’t know?” 

Lena shakes her head with a smile. “I can't say I did.”

Kara pulls Lena’s hand to her chest. Her heart is beating wildly underneath her palm, much to Lena’s surprise.

“How could I not be in love with you?”

Lena laughs, shaking her head. Kara’s eyes though, fixate on plump lips. “Can I kiss you?”

Lena doesn’t answer, instead flings herself into Kara’s lap and slides her lips between the blonde’s. The kiss is a bit rushed, both heaving ragged breaths, but they both pour their entire selves into it. It’s like time is moving quickly, but at a stand still. The imperfectness of it doesn’t matter to Lena, because she can’t imagine being anywhere else in the galaxy than being here right now.

It hits Lena then, that Kara really does love her. That even though she doesn’t know where the two of them stand, that they will get to a good place, a better place, in due time. They have moved mountains to get here, and they will push forward beyond the mistakes and misgivings of the last year.

Lena doesn’t know a lot of things. But the one thing she is certain of, is as long as they have each other, they will make it through.

“I love you,” Lena whispers when they break apart, foreheads touching and arms around one another like they are one body. One soul.

“I love you too.”
