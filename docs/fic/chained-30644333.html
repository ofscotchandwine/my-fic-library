Lena is hers, she wears the crest, a weak little creature, a pet that she had claimed. Astra watches her niece through the one-way glass, her eyes burning with the tears she won’t allow to fall. She’d hoped, prayed to Rao, that filtering the poison from her blood would bring the little one back to her, full of remorse and crying, begging for forgiveness. “I will not accept this, Alura. This is not what we planned, this is the opposite of what we came here to do.” Astra can’t even bring herself to look in the direction of the half-undressed human, her nostrils flaring with the anger that she can no longer hold back.

The human is fragile, her heart fluttering within her chest, a trapped little bird with nowhere to run.

For a moment, a brief, hopeful moment, she’d convinced herself that guilt had flashed across those young features, that Kara had at least realized she was wrong. She chews her lip, unsure of how the humans will choose to punish Kara, but willing to step aside and allow them to dispense their own judgement. Perhaps she’ll be sent to the cages alongside Reign until the Argo prison is ready, or maybe they’ll kill her— exterminate the poison once and for all. Either way, Astra will accept their decision.

With another sigh, she listens as Lena demands to see Alex, wishing she was surprised by the human’s stubborn side. The young Luthor had welcomed them all with open arms, offering her kind a chance to prove themselves, hoping for the best while preparing for the worst.

Her research, alongside Andrea’s tenacity, had saved them all from the royal household's disdain for the human race. Or, at least, has given them the power to save themselves— given the human race a fighting chance against ones who seek to control and kill them.

There’s nothing Alex can say to her, nothing that Lena wants to hear, that will make any of this better. There’s a part of Lena that understands why she did what she did, but the other, much more dominant part of her, hates Alex for it. Lena almost died, and she almost wishes she did if it meant she didn’t have to live with this. With the trauma and anguish, with the knowledge that she somehow has to move on from this.

It’s impossible, cruel, for anyone to expect Lena to work with Alex after this, to even expect her to join the resistance at all, and yet Lena knows she won’t walk away from this— can’t walk away from this. Not when so many more people are enduring everything she was put through, not when she has a chance to help them escape their own personal hell.

“I want to see her.” Lena stares down at her lap, unable to meet Alex’s gaze any longer. She feels nothing but anger for the fellow human who stood back and allowed this to happen, who participated in the abuse to save her own skin. “I don’t care if you disagree, I want to see the person who did this to me and I’m not asking.”

“Lena, you ca—”

“It’s Miss Luthor,” Lena coldly cuts her off. “That’s what everyone here chooses to call me, right? You can take me to her or I’ll find her myself.”

She watches as Alex bows her head, easily spotting the way her throat bobs with a thick swallow. The healing cream slowly works its magic beneath the bandage around Lena’s head, but the nausea that stirs within her is caused by something else entirely. After all of this, after everything she has been through, Kara has survived, and Lena can barely make sense of her warring emotions.

Her hatred for the one who enslaved her bubbles close to the surface as Lena follows Alex to the other wing of the medical bay, and yet, infuriatingly–disgustingly– the familiar tinglings of relief lifts an invisible weight from Lena’s shoulders. 

She’d been so enamored, obsessed, with her Mistress, had craved her approval and love, and, even now, Lena longs to see those blue eyes soften. It terrifies her that, despite knowing better, Lena thinks she’d struggle against the urge to submit if Kara welcomed her visit with open arms. If they were still in the mansion, Lena would wrap her arms around that waist and never let go.

Lena swallows dryly, curling her hands into fists to hide how much they shake. Astra is outside the room when they arrive, staring morosely at the niece she no longer seems to recognize, and if she’s irritated by Lena’s presence here, she doesn’t show it. The General merely nods, unable to meet emerald eyes for more than a few seconds.

“I’d like a moment alone with her.” Lena is polite, cordial, to the one she knows saved her life, but her eyes betray her true feelings. This is a demand, an expectation, and Lena doesn’t plan to allow either of them to stay even once she spots the hesitation in Astra’s gaze. “She’s restrained, yes? I’ll be safe, but I need this. You may not understand, but I—”

Lena nods and waits for them to leave, a lump forming in her throat as she catches sight of her former owner through the glass. She looks furious, a sight that Lena should be used to by now, and, even though Lena is now safe, it causes heavy dread to form a tight knot in the pit of her stomach.
