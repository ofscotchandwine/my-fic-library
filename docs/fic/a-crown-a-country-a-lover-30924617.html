1. coming home

The smell of freshly cut flowers tightened the air in the private airplane. She knew that J’onn would never admit it to her face, but it was starting to get nauseating. Still, Lena was grateful that she got to keep them – maybe she could dry them and remember her graduation day, and the last day in Ireland.

Next month would mark the sixth-year anniversary since Lex announced his abdication. The news that the Kryptonian Crown Prince would step down and pursue his passion as a clergy member shook the world and thus the focus on fifteen-year-old Lena increased. Drastically.

In Ireland she had had luck – plenty of the girls in her boarding school were also from different royal families, but when Lena became heir apparent, she was the only one that would actually become Queen Regnant, not consort of some sort. That had caused her fame and some jealousy, but thankfully she’d managed to overcome that quite easily and now, at 21, she felt hopeful to start her permanent stay in Krypton. And in a few months, she’d be crowned Queen.

It was weird thinking those words, Lena had never pictured herself as queen. She knew she would have to dedicate her life to public service as Princess, but at least then she would have been able to choose which charities and organizations to support. As queen she had to oversee everything, and all the weight and responsibility would fall onto her. And now, as the only young member of the Royal Family, it wasn’t like people had other royals to gossip about.

It didn’t really help that she had decided to go to college in Ireland instead of returning to Krypton. The Kryptonian royal family traditionally went to Ireland or England for college, but the Kryptonians seemed to think that the new heir had favoritism for that country, and some rumors that she wasn’t ‘Kryptonian enough’ were circulating. Lena sighed and rearranged herself in the small but comfortable seat. It would take a lot of work, but she had her mind set: she would be crowned. She would be queen. And she’d prove herself to her people.

Lena tried to quiet her fears growing inside her head and instead focus on the sight that was starting to appear through the small window of the airplane: Krypton’s capital, Kandor. 

“Welcome home, your Highness” whispered J’onn from across the airplane. Lena looked at him and smiled.  It sure is now.

As the airplane door opened, she heard flashes going off. At least the paparazzi weren’t screaming at her, which she compensated with a small smile and a wave. Taking a deep breath, she climbed down the stairs as gracefully her 5-inch stilettos allowed her, hand on the rail. 

“Your Highness,” James bowed at her at the end of the stairs. “I hope you had a pleasant flight; everyone is eager for your arrival”. He smiled with kind eyes. Lena had always liked James, they were close in age and he always covered for her when she escaped any State dinners. 

Lena would be lying if she said she hadn’t missed him.  He was hired as part of her security detail when Lex abdicated and had flown a week beforehand, so to get everything in order.

“Thank you, James. It’s nice being back. I hope everything went well?” She followed him towards the black Volvo SUV that was waiting for her. Lena heard J’onn’s voice still inside the airplane, making sure that all her belongings arrived at the Palace and to her room.

“Everything went well, your Highness. The palace is finishing touches for tomorrow’s party, the guests have arrived, but you don’t need to see them until tomorrow night.” He quickly added, knowing full well that the princess would want to get settled first before interacting with anyone. She nodded as he spoke, grateful not for the first time, for having him on her side. James was diligent, smart, quick on his feet, but mostly, he had an excellent memory for what Lena preferred, which was indeed a lifesaver.

He opened the back door for her. “We really do hope everything is to your liking”.

“I have no doubt,” Lena quickly turned to the paparazzi and waved to them. “Thank you for coming, it’s really nice to finally be back home.” That made the flashes go faster. Lena could picture tomorrow’s headlines ‘Crown Princess: back ‘home’?’. With that thought in mind she gave one last nod towards the cameramen and turned to James. 

As she climbed in the SUV she heard some shouts from the paparazzi towards James. Since he had appeared walking behind the princess six years ago, several articles had come out about them fooling around. Why did they write about a 21-year-old security detail with a 15-year-old princess, she had no clue, Lena never saw the appeal in that. Especially when she had only seen James as an older brother, with a quick-witted sense of humor and protectiveness that she assumed most brothers had. Not that Lex was anything like that to compare, but Lena didn’t blame him. He was conceived with a job in their parents’ minds, a duty to fulfil for their country and people. That could easily take away any sense of youth from anyone. Even if, at the end, Lex had given his back to it.

Lena looked from the SUV’s window as it drove through the city. It was close to seven in the afternoon, streets were starting to buzz with activities and people. Lena saw it all, from couples walking hand in hand, groups of friends going into pubs, families entering cinemas. Something in her stomach pulled, grief for a life not lived. She was young, at 21 you’re supposed to venture in the workforce, rent a flat with your mates, drink and dance. Now she had a nation to reign, a responsibility she never, not even in her weirdest dreams, thought would fall to her. 

Lex was what any parent prayed in a son: intelligent, brave, responsible. The world’s biggest and most important mandataries always complimented Lionel and Lillian on such a wonderful prince. They would talk about his ability to make conversation, to find solutions in friendly arguments, to command a room with a simple stare. Lex was the king Krypton deserved; Lena had never doubted that. Just as she didn’t doubt her brother would make an excellent priest. 

Six years ago, Lex had called her. It was a warm Saturday, Lena was just appointed as head girl of her floor in the boarding school, and she was finishing some history homework. The call didn’t surprise her, Lex sometimes called just to chat about their mother or about Parliament shenanigans. 

“I – there’s something I need to tell you, Lena.” She had him in speaker, not really paying too much attention as she skimmed through a paper with a small font about Latin America. 

“Mm-Hm. Go on,” Lena put her tongue between her teeth and squinted at her laptop’s screen.

“Lena, I’m stepping down. I can’t do this anymore. I can’t be the head of a church when what I want is to really be part of it. I – I’m sorry, I can’t”. Lex’s voice was hushed and rushed, letting out a secret that probably no one in Krypton thought existed.

Lena’s heart skipped a beat. The connection went quiet, only rapid breaths going through.

“Please tell me something. I know this changes everything, and I’m –” his voice cracked. Lena could picture him in his chamber, sitting next to the big window that she had always envied. She could see his brother, shoulders hunched, and exhausted from pretending. “I’m so sorry I’m putting you in this position. But I can’t, I know I can’t. It’s been a year since Father’s passing, I’m coming of age and soon Mother will talk about coronation and I can’t. It can’t be me.”

“So it has to be me, then” Lena heard herself saying, as well as anger coming from her stomach.

“I’m so sorry, truly. My mind is set and I just hope you can forgive me. One day, maybe” Lex’s voice let Lena know he was in fact crying. But she couldn’t move. The words in front of her stopped making sense, the life she had imagined for herself disappeared completely in her head. 

“One day, maybe” she repeated. Anger came and went quickly, leaving Lena cold inside and out. “When will you tell Mother?” 

Lex sighed on the other side of the line. “Tomorrow morning, I’m guessing by Monday there will be something in the news.” There was no need to tell her what she knew, ‘be ready for it’ was what Lex was truly saying.

“Thank you for calling,” was all that Lena was able to mutter before ending the call. 

Lena sighed heavily and stood up from her desk. She walked towards the opposite wall with the mirror hanging by the door. Lena was raised with a big sense of duty and service; she loved her country and her people. But Lena was always meant to be Princess Lena, maybe Queen if she decided to marry a royal, which she seriously hoped she wouldn’t. 

She stared at herself in the mirror. Dark black hair in a braid with big, squared glasses, and her too pale of a face. She looked directly into her green eyes, feeling herself age years just in seconds. She lifted her chin proudly. And there she was:

Queen Lena.

Then, on Sunday afternoon Mother called. 

“Lena dear, there’s no easy way to say this.” Lena heard Lillian take a deep breath, as if preparing for a punch. “You know your brother has always had an affinity for Church,” Lillian chuckled, probably remembering all the years she had to drag Lex out of meetings with the highest clergy members in order to attend big State dinners. “He… he has decided that he will abdicate, Lena. Which means…” 

“Which means I have to step up”, Lena finished her mother’s sentence. It was strange, Lena didn’t really feel anything as she said it. It was a fact, nothing to do or change.

“You are brilliant Lena. The family, the Crown, it needs you to step up. It’s a big responsibility, but you will have time to be prepared.” 

Lillian went on about specifics and the logistics of the announcement to the press, about the new security members that would arrive at the boarding school and prepare to keep her safe. Lena would receive weekly State papers –nothing too important, but enough for her to know what was happening in Krypton. Lillian would talk to the Headmistress, as Lena would take extra lessons from a private tutor sent from Krypton. As a child her parents didn’t see it necessary for her to know about those types of things. Now, she needed to catch up.

“Mother, would you release a statement from my behalf?” Asked Lena shyly, interrupting the queen mid sentence.

“A statement? Why?” Lillian decided to ignore the misconduct in favor of the shock of Lena wanting to release something publicly.

“I just want it to say that I am honored to step up for the people of Krypton, for the Crown. That I dutifully accept my place as Crown Princess”.

A sharp intake of breath was all Lena heard, without a doubt taking the queen by surprise. “I’ll see it to happen.”

When news broke on Monday morning Lena was already in second period, hoping that by some miracle her classmates wouldn’t find out until dinner time. Murmurs followed her, fingers pointed at her more than she had anticipated, girls started to take interest in the nerdy Kryptonian princess. 

It was a hard time, but Lena was grateful to have gone through it and she had learnt a big deal. She supposed it served as training, now back at Krypton the paparazzi and everyone would be like those teenage girls, but worse. And Lena, thankfully, had grown thick skin.

The Volvo SUV went through the city at a fast pace, it didn’t take long for them to be slowing down at the front gates of the Palace, where more paparazzi waited. Lena smiled and did small waves through the window. Truthfully, she felt her body grow tired. She had woken up before the sun, she went to her graduation alone, only J’onn with her and her friends, and then hopped on the airplane and flew for two hours back to Krypton. She needed the day to be over and finally rest. 

As the car passed and the gates closed behind them, Lena allowed herself to look at the end of the road. Finally, home, she thought. 

Luthor Palace sat in a 15-acre land, it was big and formal, everything you would expect from one of the oldest royal families in the world. For Lena it only brought memories of her childhood – chasing Lex around the gardens with their nannies at toll, riding lessons in which she’d always giggle too much for the horse’s liking and disappearing for the afternoon with a book and a couple of snacks in the maze that Lillian had insisted on making. At first Lena had protested against it; too big, too bush-y, too much trouble to upkeep. But she had to give it to Mother: it was useful for when she needed alone time. 

Then she thought about the holidays of the last six years, when she had come home for summer and winter, in and out in a couple of weeks. Aside from the obligatory family time and different dinners with the Prime Minister and the Parliament, she was always busy: meetings, lessons, charity events, appearances at the Opera or Ballet, and other public outings.

However, people always seemed to complain that her visits were too brief, that they couldn’t get a glimpse of the real Lena. Now that she was there to stay, she hoped to give her people what they needed of her: a worthy Queen that represented all of them.

A guard dressed nicely in the formal green uniform opened her door as soon as the Volvo came to a halt outside the entrance of the Palace. She thanked him and got out, grateful once more that this particular entrance was too far away from any paparazzi. 

In front of her appeared Jess, her mother’s private secretary. “Your Royal Highness, welcome home”, she smiled and curtsied. Jess had taken her role just last year, but Lena appreciated her deeply. She was nice and young, something that set Lena at ease. “If you follow me, I’ll take you to the Queen” Lena nodded and followed along. 

She knew the Palace perfectly, all the secret hideaways and stairs, but she allowed Jess to direct her to wherever her mother could be. As they walked through the Palace servants stepped to the side and bowed or curtsied to her. Lena smiled at all of them, nodded or said thank you whenever it was necessary. 

Jess led her to her mother’s tearoom, where she let Lena walk first into the room. Guards opened the door for them, and there she was. Queen Lillian in a smart dark purple dress. She was talking to a man that looked too small next to the Queen. He was dressed in a dark grey suit with a green tie that matched the Luthor emblem.

“Finally! You’ve arrived!” Lena walked to her mother, gave a quick curtsy and gave her two kisses, one in each cheek. 

Lillian grabbed her by the shoulders, studying her over. “Oh dear, you look every inch of a Crown Princess”. Lena smiled at her mother and blushed. Lillian had never been big on compliments, especially towards Lena, but Lex’s abdication had shifted their relationship to the better. Two women were at the front of the Royal Luthor Family, they needed to have each other’s back.

“You look good too, Mother. I’m so happy to be back and finally be able to stay” Lillian smiled at that and turned them towards the man that had taken a few steps back out of respect.

“Yes, yes. We all are happy that you’re here for good now. This fine gentleman here,” Lillian extended her arm towards him, inviting him over, “is Winslow Schott. He will be your private secretary, from here on.”

“You Royal Highness,” Winslow bowed, “it’s a privilege and an honor to be your private secretary. You can call me Winn if you prefer.”

Lena smiled at him, “Winn, it’s nice to meet you. I do hope we can get to understand each other and work together nicely.” He smiled at that. Lena immediately liked him, there was something in his eyes that made it clear he was eager to begin.

“I hope so too, your Highness. The Queen and I have gone through the itinerary of tomorrow, if you’d like to go over it?”

Lillian smiled, looking particularly pleased with herself for choosing someone so diligent. “I’ll leave you two to talk about the party. Lena, dear, when you’re done we’ll meet for dinner. I want to hear about your graduation.” 

With a bow from Winn, and an agreement from Lena, Lillian departed, leaving them alone.

“So, Winn, what have you planned for my birthday?” Lena asked with a playful smile. 

“Well, your Highness, let’s see.” He looked at her with a mischievous little smile. Oh, Lena definitely liked him.

Even if Lena’s real birthday was in January, it was a custom that royals celebrated their birthdays in spring or summer. This allowed a better season for country-wide celebrations to happen, and the atmosphere of nice weather made for good parties.

When the plans for her arrival were made months ago, they agreed that the following day – a Saturday – would be the celebrations of Lena’s birthday. As a Crown Princess this day could shift from date to date, they chose it depending on Lena’s availability and presence in Krypton. This would change once she was crowned, and her birthday, as all of the Regnants’, would be celebrated on May 23 rd , as it was once ruled.

Lena knew as a fun fact, that her great-great-great grandfather had chosen that date because it was when he came back after a three-months stay in Daxam, the neighboring country. As an excuse of wanting to celebrate, the King had ruled that they would celebrate his birthday that day. And so, the tradition stayed.

Winn went over the plans for the party with Lena. Every lord and lady would attend, as well as some other royals of neighboring countries. Prime Minister Cat Grant would give a speech at dinner and the Queen would give one as well during the reception. Lex sent his apologies and a gift, as he was overseeing some business for the Church at north. Lena’s dress was already hanging in her closet and the Queen had requested a tiara to be polished for her to use. 

“It seems you are very thorough, Winn. Everything looks fine to me, thank you for setting this up.” Lena kept her eyes in the papers in front of her, each detailing hours, places, food, music. 

“No need to thank me, your Highness. I enjoy this.” Winn moved in his seat, Lena saw his hands open and close, clearly concealing something. 

“Yes, Winn?” She offered, raising a quizzical eyebrow to him.

“Right now, there are twenty-three bachelors with whom you must dance tomorrow, your Highness. It is a tradition, given that you are unmarried and are not yet engaged.” Lena sighed, massaging between her eyebrows with her left hand. She had forgotten completely about that tradition. Lex never danced with any bachelor, but he was a man and thus didn’t need to be courted.

“Do you have a list of their names? At least so I can prepare” Winn quickly took out a piece of paper with names, titles, countries and some more information that Lena could use for small talk.

“These are the ones that signed up, but perhaps more seem inclined tomorrow night.”

Lena studied the list, some of them she knew from infancy, others because she knew their sisters from boarding school. Twenty-three were a lot of dances, Lena could feel her feet getting tired just from the thought of it. They just want to see the Crown Princess, that’s all. They aren’t offering their hands in marriage.

“Thank you, Winn. I think that if we are all done here, I’ll go have dinner with the Queen.” They started to get up, Winn quickly grabbing the papers and starting to put them in a certain order in his briefcase.

“Sure, your Highness. I’ll see you tomorrow at breakfast? We can go over it again.” 

“Perfect, we’ll talk tomorrow. Goodnight,” Lena waited for him to bow and she exited the room, list too heavy in her hand.

Just dance and smile, Lena. You just have to dance and smile. 

After dinner with the Queen Lena was finally allowed to go to her chambers. 

When Lex left, they offered her to move to his, but she declined. This was her room, even if the sight from Lex’s was better, this was what she could hold on to. At least until coronation, when she would take her late Father’s room and her Mother would take what was once her grandmother’s. 

Lena shook her head. The coronation wouldn’t be for another three months. She didn’t need to worry about that. Guards opened the double doors for her, opening to a nice waiting room with a couple of couches. Lena walked through it, where a maid opened the door towards her room.

It was big, with green, grey and dark blue accents here and there. The right wall was occupied by bookshelves, interrupted only by the window and opening to the balcony. In the corner closer to the door was a small sitting area, soft grey sofas in which Lena had stayed up reading every other night.

Opposite of that, at the left of the room, was Lena’s desk in which were her flowers from graduation. Farther into the room at the left side was the door towards her dressing room and the bathroom, with which the bedroom shared a fireplace. It was nice and efficient, that way both rooms were always warm during winter. Her bed, the thing she missed the most right now, stood at the back of the room, a green duvet already open and waiting for her.

Lena dismissed the maid, asking her to wake her up for breakfast with her mother. She walked into her dressing room and changed into pajamas. It had been a long day, and tomorrow would be even longer.

Once in her bed, Lena felt her eyes close and her body rest. She slept, dreams filled with dances and dinner parties.

Lena woke up to a still dark room, but some shimmers of light coming through the curtains. She could faintly hear people outside, probably starting on all the last minute preparations for the party. 

She dwelled on the thought of it. Growing up she had gotten attention, but Lex was always the spotlight. Lena knew from boarding school and different balls that most second or third or even fourth children from royal families always acted out, wanting a bit of attention from their parents or the media. Lena wasn’t really able to bond with them over that. She knew that acting out, getting in trouble for being disrespectful would only cause trouble. For her, standing out meant doing good in school, being an overachiever the Kings could be proud to show-off.

The last six years she had the ability to practice being in the spotlight, but all those affairs were short and filled with activities. Tonight, she was meant to talk and mingle and just be. It was an opportunity for people to meet her, for Lena to be charming and convince nobles and royals that the Royal Family of Krypton was indeed in good hands.

Lena would have to eat, make small talk, joke and… dance.

Twenty-three dances, how are that many people interested in dancing with me? Lena closed her eyes momentarily, raising a small prayer to Rao for strength. What if one of them tries to court me? What if one has already spoken to Mother and tonight are just formalities?

Lena knew she had to marry, but she hoped that by being Queen Regnant she could do it later, maybe at 27 or 28. She felt all too young, too naïve, to even consider marriage. But it was there, at the back of her mind and certainly in the minds of every Kryptonian out there. 

Maids entered the room, already debriefing her on how the whole Palace was working tonight, and how they were excited to see everyone. Lena nodded, smiled, and provided information as the maids walked her towards the shower. It would be a long day.

After a relaxed morning of writing thank-you notes for all attendees and for people who sent gifts for her, Lena found herself being fitted into a royal, opal green sleeveless dress. It was queenly, was all that Lena could think. She looked regal and young, it sat nicely in her hips and the neckline dipped in a respectful manner. 

“It’s wonderful, you look magnificent” Lillian approached her from behind. The seamstress walked around Lena, making sure everything was in its place. 

“It’s a lovely dress. Thank you,” Lena directed towards the seamstress who smiled and blushed. She was young, Lena had never met her, but she could feel her trembling hands as she touched the princess.

“Thank you, your Highness. The color really suits you” the seamstress managed to answer back.

Lillian seemed unaffected by this exchange, but Lena swallowed maybe too harshly. She had never paid too much attention to people around her. She knew that she was allowed to fool around if she so desired, she knew very well her brother had. But relationships were different, too formal, and even if it were just a couple of 12-year-olds calling each other ‘girlfriend’ or ‘boyfriend’, expectations could arise. And no noble child or teenager wanted that kind of pressure from their parents. A young affection could easily be an alliance or status to uphold.

She locked eyes with Lillian in the mirror in front of them, the Queen moved her head slightly to the right, as if she was wondering about Lena’s thoughts just then. Lena had never spoken to her mother about… well, romance. Or marriage, for that matter. Lena wasn’t even sure if she could distinguish love among her feelings. She had never felt anything of the sorts, for anyone. But lust? She had felt it all around. 

Veronica Sinclair at boarding school, with her long legs and infectious smile. Lena had stayed plenty of nights up, head filled with Veronica and the small, seemingly polite touches they’d share in the dining hall. It messed with Lena’s head.

In college she was attracted to this British boy, with dimples and glasses from her Physics lab. However, Lena never acted on those feelings, too afraid it would end up on the news. She was too cautious, too nervous people in Krypton would criticize her, call her promiscuous for something as silly as dating. And so, Lena decided to just omit that part in her life. 

Now, with the seamstress's hands in her, all Lena could think about was all the hands of men and women with whom she had to dance tonight. She let herself wonder what if?

The thought quickly dissipated as Lillian instructed her to get out of the dress so the seamstress could work on the finishing touches. 

“We want you to look the most beautiful you’ve ever been tonight. And I also need to go and get ready. I’ll see you at dinner.” The seamstress and Queen Lillian abandoned Lena’s room. 

She headed for a bath, hoping to drown her fears with the warm water.

Friendly chatter could be heard from outside the formal dining room. Lena stood in front of the guards, waiting for James to receive the go-call and she would be presented in front of all the guests. Lena could feel her heart beating too fast, but she fixed her face on a nice smile trying to ease herself.

“Roger that, Pluto on the way” James said and signaled to the guards. Lena tried not to roll her eyes at her nickname. When her father was crowned it was instructed that the royal family would be referred to as planets. Lionel was Jupiter, Lillian Saturn, Lex Mercury. Lena liked them, but it reminded her too much of her childhood, when none of this was even in her mind.

Lena had to push those thoughts aside, the guards were beginning to open the doors for her and the trumpets announcing her arrival shook her core.

“Presenting, Her Royal Highness, Princess Lena of Krypton.” A deep voice called. 

The room went silent, people in all types of robes, tunics, suits and dresses bowed to her. Lillian stood a couple of feet in front, smiling at her. Lena walked with ease, offering a wide smile to everyone. 

Just as that, chatter began again, and Lena was introduced to several nobles she hadn’t met. Lords and ladies from all over Krypton. Some Lena recognized from the list of bachelors she had to dance later in the night. She tried not to dwell  too much on it, but it was getting to her nerves.

Some minutes later they were called to take their seats as dinner would begin shortly. Lena sat in the middle of the long dining table and in between some of the older gentlemen of Parliament. In front of her sat Cat Grant, the Prime Minister with her oldest son, Adam. The Queen sat right next to Cat and with some other parliament members. 

Lena chatted with the gentlemen who were too eager to speak with the young princess. They spoke about their sons, daughters or even their grandchildren, and their accomplishments. Lena listened to them attentively, commenting here and there, but with zero interest in pursuing any of them.

After Cat Grant’s polite speech, everyone was instructed to continue the celebration in the ballroom. Lena led the way with some parliament members that caught up with her and were trying to offer their own children or grandchildren. Lena had a small and personal chuckle, they reminded her to horse breeders: all of them swore their kind was the best in the market.

Queen Lillian’s speech was nice. She talked about being grateful for her daughter and how she had become a nice, young lady. She prayed to Rao for a long life for her daughter and everyone cheered along. 

With that, the dance floor was opened, and Lena was almost ambushed by several different lords and ladies, all wanting to dance with her. Lena smiled and chatted and let herself be spinned around by different men and women. They all talked about their accomplishments in the army, at hunting or some other sport nobles were inclined to. They complimented Lena on her looks, most of them looking at her body with lust and interest. 

It didn’t bother her. She had heard people talk about her like that all her life. But she had let herself hope that at least some of the people that had signed up to dance with her wanted to actually know her, not just feel her up with the vague excuse of a dance.

Lena was beginning to feel nauseous by the twentieth dance and opted for a break. Finding Winn in the crowd, she approached her private secretary and whispered to him “next time, please, no more than ten dances. This is getting unbearable.” Winn nodded.

“Duly noted, your Highness. I’m sorry it got too long this time,” he offered a sympathetic smile.

“No worries, it’s the first time.” He bowed at her and Lena walked through the crowd.

She found Andrea, an old friend from boarding school, whose family had a title in England. They weren’t really close, but Lena needed the company of someone who wasn’t interested in feeling her up, or brag about anything.

They were walking towards the open bar, chatting about college and common friends. Suddenly, Lena felt the air from her lungs being punched out, as she impacted against something strong. After what seemed minutes, a Lord stood up. Gasps following him. “I second the motion.” He looked at the other members. “It’s time we have new traditions.” With a shrug, he added, “I like change. And Princess Lena seems brave enough to lead us into it,”

Lena pretended not to hear Cat’s worried sigh. “All those in favor of abolishing the marriage rule, say ‘Aye’,” the Primer Minister nodded to Parliament.

From the benches, several arms were raised with their respective ‘aye’. Lena’s grin grew more and more with each member that agreed. Cat had her hands raised, folding her fingers as new votes were casted. After some, she nodded.

“All right, the ayes have it.” She turned to Lena, “congratulations Princess. The rule has been abolished.”

Lena felt her chest fill with pride, excitement and happiness. She had done it. There would be no wedding, no arranged engagement. She would be crowned without a marriage bracelet in her wrist. 

Queen Lena.

 

 

Nine days later, Lena tensed the ceremonial bow and released a flaming arrow into the ring, which lit on fire with ease. Parliament had agreed to move the coronation day, as there was no real need to wait any longer. 

The Princess couldn’t believe how easy everything seemed now. She recalled her speech, and with help of Lex, Sam, and her Mother, she had different perspectives from what had happened. Truly, if it weren’t for them, most of it would be a blur in Lena’s memory by now. 

In those nine days she had had several meetings with Parliament and her Mother, working on a swift transition of Queen. Lena had been also busy with the orphanage and several other charities and organizations she wanted to prioritize. To say the least, the soon-to-be-Queen had her plate full with work. And she couldn’t be happier.

On the tenth day, the morning of her coronation, she had asked to be left alone as long as possible. Lena decided to visit the throne room. She sat down in what would be her own sit in a few hours and looked at the paintings around the room. Finding the one of her Father, she smiled at him.

I did it, Father. I’m going to be Queen, I’ll make you proud.

A creak from the door captured Lena’s attention. There, in a simple grey suit, stood Kara. They hadn’t talked since the almost-wedding day. The Princess wasn’t even sure what she’d say to the Lady, but now there she was.

“I know this might be too bold, but I would like an audience with your Highness,” Kara walked until she was in front of the Princess, and she dropped to one of her knees. 

Lena grinned, falling into the game as well. “What is your dilemma, young lady?”

Daring to look up at her, Kara smiled. “You are, in fact. I am in love with the Queen-to-be,” Lena’s mouth opened. “And I’m inquiring if she loves me, too.” Kara held the Princess’ stare.

Lena grinned, but tried to hide it behind her right hand, resting her head on it. “Do you have a chicken for my table?” Kara giggled freely, happily.

“No. My kitchen was out of chickens,”

“Ah,” they held each other’s stare. Lena felt herself blush and an eagerness come from her belly up to her chest. The Princess vaulted up and Kara, with fast reflexes, mimicked her in time to catch her in her arms.

“Lena,” Kara whispered before they sealed their lips together.

The Princess had missed her so much feeling Kara against her was like satiating a hunger she wasn’t aware she had. The kiss was reassuring, soft, loving. Both of them let their feelings flow through it, serving as communications between them. Lena felt Kara’s grin against her and then, she was lifted and twirled around. The Princess broke the kiss to let out a big, hearty laugh.

They separated shortly after that, as Lena had to get ready for her big day. Kara laid a kiss on her hand, promising to be there in the front row and to meet her after. The Princess felt giddy, anxious but so full of happiness. 

This time the seamstresses had gone all out. Lena wore a regal long dress. It was light green, the color of the Royal Family. Her makeup was light, and her hair was brushed down, prepared for the crown that would be set down on top of her. 

Lena walked at the end of the hallway, seeing in front of her but on the other side of the room her Mother. Lillian wore a simpler dress, a big velvet crown on top of her head. They shared a smile before they turned and walked towards the ceremonial room.

They each had a clear path in which they should walk. In the middle of the room was the big, fancy throne that was only used for coronation and official portraits. The Archbishop stood in front of it, with some other people behind him that held the scepter that would be placed on Lena’s hand. 

The Princess reached the throne from the right side and sat down. Her Mother stood next to her. The Archbishop reached for the crown on Lillian’s head and began the ceremony.

“Will you solemnly promise and swear to govern the people of Krypton, according to the statutes in Parliament agreed on, and the respective laws and customs of the same?” He placed the crown on top of Lena’s head. She realized that the weight did not bother her one bit.

“Will you, in your power, cause law and justice and mercy to be executed in all judgments?” The Archbishop offered Lena the scepter, which she took without hesitation.

“I solemnly promise to do so,” her voice was clear, calm, no one would suspect her heart was beating way too fast.

The Archbishop bowed in front of Lena and then stepped back. She stood, careful and graceful enough to carry the crown and the scepter in her hand. Lena walked in a couple of steps, enough to stand in a center of light.

“Presenting,” the deep voice of the Royal announcer resounded in the room. “Lena Kieran Luthor, Queen of Krypton.”

The guests bowed and curtsied for the first time to their new Queen. A woman that was never meant to rule, but found herself having to fulfill the place that was passed to her. A woman that hadn’t been afraid to accept a marriage if it meant protecting her people. Someone that put Krypton first, above her own feelings and wants. 

There she stood, in all her glory and youth. Queen Lena.

 

 

“You really thought I had sold us out?” Lena groaned, letting the book fall on her face. Kara’s timid fingers traced her hair. “I’m just curious why would you think that?”

After three months and a half since her coronation, Lena had asked Winn for a weekend off. She loved her job, she woke up every day ready to face anything and everything. But it had also meant seeing Kara briefly every day, maybe sharing meals if they were lucky, or late night phone conversations, which Lena always regretted the following morning when she woke tired.

They were on the beach at North, a small place that James had assured them was private for them to go to. Lena was lying on Kara’s lap, reading about ships and their inner workings.

“I was scared, Kara. I thought you had been careful with the planning. It was the logical conclusion,” Lena removed the book from her hace and squinted at Kara.

The blonde bit her lip, a furrow in her brow only deepened the scar that Lena had learned she had earned in her first rugby game as a child. The Queen smiled and reached for Kara’s chin.

“I understand you, Lena. I still feel guilty,” a pout accompanied the feeling.

“You don’t have to, darling. It wasn’t your fault. I know that, you know that. It’s fine, don’t worry about it,” that appeased Kara. She leaned into Lena’s hand and turned to place a small kiss on her palm.

“I’m glad to be here with you,” it was a whisper so full of tenderness, Lena had to pull her down for a kiss.

“And I’m glad to be here with you, Kara.” They smiled at each other. “It’s a shame you’re no longer part of the navy, I would’ve loved to see you in your uniform sailing the boat,” Lena smirked playfully and Kara rolled her eyes.

“I still have some back at home. Maybe one day I’ll give you a show, Queen Lena,” Kara nuzzled in her neck.

“Oh, if you don’t, I’ll have to give you an honorary position or something. That way you’ll be in uniform every day.” Kara chuckled in her ear. “Hey, being Queen has some perks.”

Lena let herself be engulfed by Kara.

It was just the beginning of her reign and of her relationship with Kara, but Lena had faith in herself that she could become a great Queen that Krypton deserved, and she had faith in Kara and herself. 

There would be hard times, tough decisions and missteps, Lena was aware of that. But it didn’t scare her. She was ready for whatever was to come. She just had to take it one day at a time.

After all, she had all her life ahead of her. 

 

 

The end.
