December 1st, 2022

On days when Sam works late, Lena picks Ruby up.

Already the other kids are buzzing about winter break—Ruby talks a mile a minute about the plans she’s already made, most of which involve snow days spent in the park. She is a sweet kid, with few but fiercely loyal friends, and Lena agrees to chaperone these snow adventures right away. Of course, it’s not like there ever was a doubt about that; Lena adores Ruby, and she would do anything for her.

(Even endure the carolers on Main Street.)

“What’s your favorite part of Christmas, Aunt Lena?” Ruby asks as they wade through the fresh snow, cups of hot chocolate in hand. “My favorite is the music. Wait, and ice skating. Wait—maybe hot chocolate…”

“I don’t know,” Lena says, taking a slow sip of her lukewarm drink. “Maybe I’m like you, with a little bit of love for everything.”

That makes Ruby snort with laughter. “No way,” she says. “You always make a face when I say Christmas. Like…that! The one you’re making right now!”

“I’m not making a face,” Lena denies. “What face do you think I’m making?”

“A face like you’re grumpy,” Ruby explains, which Lena takes great offense to. “Mom says you’re not a Christmas person, but isn’t there anything you like?”

“I like being here with you,” Lena says. “That’s something.”

“Yeah, but…that’s not Christmas.”

Lena would beg to differ, there—every time she thinks of Christmas, she thinks of Sam and Ruby. Her company closes for pretty much all of December to give her employees plenty of holiday time, and that means Lena spends half of her time with Ruby when she gets off school, and the other half of her time crashing at Sam’s for cozy winter nights. Lena may not be a big fan of Christmas, but this is an okay time of the year; it’s the time where she gets to have a family with absolutely no strings attached. She wouldn’t change it for the world.

“I’ll pick something else, then,” Lena says. “Something you will approve of.”

“You can’t just pick something ‘cause I asked! You have to love it,” Ruby says. “That’s going to be my Christmas wish: for you to fall in love with Christmas.”

“Well, you’ve ruined your chances by telling me your wish. Everyone knows it won’t work now.”

“That’s only for birthdays,” Ruby scoffs, like she has all the wisdom in the world. Lena decides to leave it at that—who is she to argue a teenager’s logic?

“Okay, wish whisperer,” Lena says. “Let’s get home before your mom accuses me of spoiling your dinner.”

Ruby pouts. “Fine,” she says dramatically. Then, “Can I drive?”

“You’re hilarious,” Lena deadpans, taking her keys out of her jacket and lightly pressing her elbow against Ruby’s side to dissuade her niece from reaching for them.

“Hey, it was worth a shot,” Ruby says, practically skipping to the car in newfound excitement. “Ooh, can we watch a Christmas movie tonight? I promise I’ll finish my homework after!”

“Nice try. First homework, then a movie,” Lena says. They wait in the car for a minute to let it warm up, shivering as the heat fires up, and then they go on their way; Main Street isn’t a far drive from Sam’s, but it still takes them a while to inch through the snowy roads.

This, too, is also what Lena can appreciate about Christmas. Driving in the snow is a nightmare and the constant stream of Christmas music playing is tiring, but right now Mariah Carey’s “All I Want For Christmas is You” is playing, Ruby is yelling the lyrics, and snow is falling around them—nothing can really compare to a nice night like this. Lena can’t even bring herself to be mad that she was swindled into making a ten-minute drive just for hot chocolate.

Lena is even beginning to get into the song herself on this rare occasion when suddenly everything goes wrong.

She doesn’t see the figure on the road until she’s crashing; it’s sudden, abrupt, and the jarring stop of the car sends both her and Ruby’s heads sharply forward. Luckily, it’s not bad—they’d only been traveling at about twenty-five, and the most Lena is concerned about is whether or not Ruby’s frightened.

“Ruby? Are you okay?”

At first the only answer is a dramatic fit of coughing, but eventually Ruby presses a thumbs-up on Lena’s shoulder. “Just kidding, I’m fine,” she says, and that makes Lena burst out in tight, relieved laughter.

“You’re certainly your mother’s daughter,” Lena mutters. “Stay here, okay? I’m going to see what we hit.”

“That thing came out of nowhere!” Ruby exclaims, already unbuckling her seatbelt (to Lena’s absolute disapproval). “Was it a reindeer? Did we kill Rudolph?”

“You are pushing it, young lady,” Lena says, but she’s never been able to pull off intimidating when it comes to Ruby. To rooms full of snobby rich people? Sure. But Ruby…well. Lena’s a bit of a pushover for that little girl.

And so she lets Ruby follow her outside to assess the damage, even though if Sam were here she’d never let her tag along. Lena does pull up Sam’s number first to call her, anyway—she’ll deal with Sam’s judgement in person later.

But she is unprepared when, as she steps foot onto the street, she realizes she’s hit a woman.

“Oh my God,” Lena says, watching with growing horror as the woman carefully untangles what looks like a cape from Lena’s mangled bumper. “Oh my God. Are you alright?!”

The woman looks up, blinks, then raises her hands like she’s been caught red-handed. “I’m sorry,” she says. “I got in the way of your ship.”

“…what?”

“Your ship,” this, the stranger says with a frown, flexing her hands and squinting back at Lena’s car. “I feel so…odd. What kind of sun do you have?” Before Lena can even reply, she cups her hands around her mouth and shouts towards the sky, “Hey! Someone messed up here!” She looks at Lena again, then adds, “Don’t worry, help is on the way.”

Lena casts a worried glance at Ruby. “Um…” she says. “Ruby, honey, why don’t you go wait in the car while I call 911.” And keep you away from drunkards, she thinks.

Ruby, however, is watching the exchange with wide, entranced eyes. “You’re dressed funny,” she says. “Did you come from a costume party?”

“No—from a different ship.” The woman begins to wander, frowning at her surroundings, and she ultimately glances back at Lena. “What planet is this?”

“Earth?” Lena is beginning to feel bewildered; clearly, this woman has been hit much harder than she thought. “Can you stay here? I’ll call for help.”

“I already have,” the woman argues, but Lena places a finger up in the air and starts dialing anyway.

While they wait for someone to come, Lena manages to coax out a few details from the stranger. Her name is Kara Zor-El, she’s not sure what her zodiac sign is, and she is apparently not cold at all despite wearing a small skirt in this weather—she also is very apologetic, and promises she won’t fly away until she helps them sort their ship out.

(She actually says that she won’t fly away until her friends come and help. Like that’s something that comes out of her mouth.)

It takes fifteen minutes for a police car to show up, and the officer who emerges—Maggie Sawyer, someone who Lena’s never been the biggest fan of—gives Kara an immediate once-over. She casts a skeptical glance at Lena next, and Lena merely shrugs her shoulders.

“Hello, Officer Sawyer,” Lena says politely.

“Ms. Luthor.” Maggie nods at her briefly, eyes quickly falling back on Kara. “The chief said you radioed in about a woman who’s been mumbling to the sky for the last half hour after you hit her with your car?”

“Yes, she’s been very out of it,” Lena says. “I haven’t been able to get her to tell me if she’s hurt or not.”

“Judging by the clothes, she’s probably an actor from the drama house,” Maggie says. She places her hands on her belt and asks, “How hard did you hit her, exactly?”

“She came out of nowhere,” Lena says, but Maggie looks unconvinced. They’ve had a quiet rivalry ever since Lena moved here—Lena isn’t over the fact that Maggie has arrested her twice (and only once had Lena merited the arrest), and Maggie isn’t over the fact that Lena’s company poached several of their best officers (allegedly, because Lena isn’t positive any of the employees she has would’ve won any police awards).

“Hi there, ma’am,” Maggie calls. “I’m Officer Sawyer. Can you come talk to us, so we can piece together what happened?”

Kara’s head is craned up towards the sky, but she sheepishly glances in their direction when she’s spoken to. “Sure, of course,” she says, blinks, and she fiddles with the ends of her cape. “I’m really sorry about hitting—hitting—” She stops, then frowns. “Hey, I never got your name!”

“It’s Lena,” Lena says.

Before she can add anything else, Ruby interjects, “And I’m Ruby!”

“Where are my manners, huh?” Kara laughs. “It’s nice to meet you both. Especially you, Ruby.” She gives Ruby a high-five, and Ruby happily returns it with a beaming smile. Kara also takes Lena’s hand and—for some reason—presses a kiss to the back of it. “And again, I am really sorry about your ship.”

Ship? Maggie mouths. Lena motions to her car with a quick jerk of her chin, trying not to let on that she is unreasonably flustered over the fact that her hand has just been kissed.

Ruby has definitely not caught on to the subtlety. “Kara, how come you keep talking about ships?” she asks. “Do you have a pirate ship? Or a spaceship?”

“Spaceship is right,” Kara says, crinkling her brow, “because we do use it in space?” She looks at them like they’re the crazy ones, then flexes her hands all over again. “Do you happen to have a yellow sun, by any chance? This feels weird. I’ve been to planets with yellow suns before, and I’ve never felt this…weak.”

“Our sun is a star,” Ruby pipes up, unhelpfully. “We learned that in second grade. What kind of planets have you gone to? Like Mars?”

Lena coughs as conspicuously as possible. “Ruby, let’s not bombard Kara with questions,” she says. “Officer Sawyer is going to talk to Kara, and you and I are going to call your mom.”

Ruby sighs. “Okay,” she says glumly. Then, “Hey Aunt Lena, since you hit Kara with your car, are you going to go to jail?”

It is a testament to how fragile Lena’s relationship is with the police in this town that, frankly, she isn’t quite sure whether it would be worse to say yes or no.

“I can’t believe you hit someone with your car,” is the first thing out of Sam’s mouth. She sounds downright amused at the fact that she’s come to pick Lena up from the police station again—nevermind the fact that this might be potentially traumatic for Ruby, or anything.

Lena scowls. “Shut up,” she mutters, and that makes Sam laugh.

“Come on, Lena, you have to admit it’s pretty funny,” Sam says. “And hey, I think Maggie’s warming up to you! At least all the conference rooms aren’t conveniently filled this time around.”

“It’s a Christmas miracle—your friend didn’t stick me in a holding cell for questioning,” Lena deadpans. “Can we leave now, please?”

Ruby, who’s been hanging off her mom’s arm, giggles at what must be the sour expression on Lena’s face. “Don’t worry, Aunt Lena,” she says. “I don’t think Kara is pressing charges.”

“Pressing charges? Since when do you know legal terminology?” Lena asks.

“I watch Law and Order,” Ruby explains. When Lena raises an eyebrow at Sam, Sam merely shrugs and says (rather unapologetically),

“We’re addicted.”

Lena decides not to question the matter further. All she cares about is getting a good night’s rest and avoiding all things police-related from here on out. That, and maybe a burger. The greasier the better.

They stop by a McDonald’s on the way to Sam’s. Ruby requests a full meal, but is fast asleep within minutes; Lena suspects she’s had a lot more excitement than she’s used to for a day, and not even a milkshake can keep her attention.

Lena and Sam trade off on finishing Ruby’s fries until, finally, the question that’s been bothering Lena spills out: “Did Maggie tell you what was going to happen to Kara?”

“Kara?” Sam echoes.

“Yes—the girl I hit with my car,” Lena says. She tries to justify her curiosity somewhat by adding, “I just hope she’s alright.”

“Oh, I see,” Sam hums. “You think you knocked the sense out of her head.”

“Well…she did think she arrived on this planet via spaceship,” Lena says. “I’m afraid to be sued.”

Sam gives her a critical side-eye. “And because you have common human decency, right? And you want to make sure she’s not dead?”

“Yes, oh my God,” Lena says. “That goes without saying!”

“Hey, don’t blame me. You did kind of sound like Ebeneezer Scrooge there,” Sam teases. “Don’t worry, Maggie left Kara with competent hands at the hospital. Apparently Kara passed every breathalyzer test under the sun, so, surprisingly she wasn’t drunk.”

“Then what do they think happened?” Lena asks.

Sam shrugs. “I don’t know exactly. I heard they’re not ruling out some kind of head injury, but that’s as much as Kelly told me,” she says. They arrive at her house then, and Sam reaches over to fondly pat Lena’s cheek. “Relax, Scrooge. The girl you hit already swears up-and-down that you didn’t hurt a hair on her head.”

“You didn’t see her, Sam. She was…hardly coherent.”

“Hardly coherent is better than not coherent,” Sam points out, “so there you go. At the very most, you inflicted a tiny bit of brain damage.”

“…that doesn’t make me feel better.”

“Yeah, I thought it wouldn’t.”

When they pull into the driveway, it’s already midnight. Lena helps Sam coax Ruby out of the car, and then they help her sleepily shuffle to her room; once Ruby’s passed out for good, Lena gravitates to the kitchen for a much-needed bottle of wine.

“What a way for the holidays to begin,” Lena muses once she’s two glasses in. Sam, who is still nursing her first, raises her head in alarm.

“Don’t let this get to your head, Lena,” she says. “If it will ease your conscience, we can go visit the hospital first thing tomorrow.”

“It’s not just that,” Lena sighs. “Of course it’s mostly that, but…I feel like the past few Christmases haven’t been the very best. God knows I’ve tried to make this time of year as painless as possible, but trouble seems to follow me everywhere.”

“That’s not true,” Sam negates. “Last year—” She stops entirely at that. “Shit. Last year.”

Lena gives a dark chuckle against the rim of her glass. “Exactly,” she mutters. “Just one good year. That’s all I’m asking for.”

“Well, this year isn’t over by a long shot. Don’t get too comfortable in your misery just yet,” Sam says, and while her words could be teasing, her voice is far softer than Lena expected.

Lena feels Sam reach for her hand; she obliges her, and gives it a squeeze in thanks. It’s the most she can do right now, she thinks, in order for Sam to think she’s okay.

She has two more glasses of wine that night, and isn’t even sure at what point she passes out on Sam’s couch.

December 2nd, 2022

Kelly Olsen takes one look at Lena that morning and, in a split-second (and notably pity-driven) decision, allows her to see Kara.

Lena hadn’t arrived with any kind of plan, or even an explanation, so when Kelly ushers her down the hallway she’s struck with a panic over what to do. Honestly, she wishes she’d brought some kind of peace offering right about now—flowers, maybe. People in the hospital like to receive flowers.

“Your friend’s been awake for a while,” Kelly says as they walk. She has a clipboard in hand, and when Lena gets a glimpse of it, sees Kelly has been working on a crossword puzzle.

“Oh, she’s actually not my friend,” Lena tries to explain, but Kelly shakes her head before she can continue.

“Your friend,” Kelly stresses, “could do with a little cheering up, I think.”

Lena’s not stupid—she gets the picture. “Thank you,” she says once they reach Kara’s room. She gives Kelly a small, grateful nod as Kelly props open the door, but then she is left alone—left alone with the girl who she hit with her car. (She’s beginning to wonder why Kelly let her in in the first place.)

Kara is gazing out of the hospital window when Lena walks in, completely mesmerized by something out there. Lena’s not sure what, exactly, because it seems like Kara’s room only affords a grand view of the hospital parking lot.

“Hello?” Lena calls out cautiously. “Kara?”

When Kara turns away from her spot, Lena is surprised to see there is a rather tight, pained expression on her face. As Kara realizes she has company, however, that look melts away entirely. “Hi!” she says. “Lena, right? We met yesterday.”

“That’s an understatement,” Lena says slowly. “Um, I—I hope it’s alright that I came to see you.”

“Of course! I’ve been dying I’m so bored,” Kara says. “The doctor won’t let me leave, can you believe it?”

Lena isn’t sure what to say to that. “Well are you…doing okay?” she asks. “Why won’t they let you leave?”

“I don’t know,” Kara says, frowning. “They can stick the IVs in my arms, isn’t that funny? Usually my skin is unbreakable under a yellow sun. Maybe there is something different about your planet.”

“I see,” says Lena, even when she does not. She decides not to unpack the whole “unbreakable skin” thing, and instead opts for, “So you are…from a different planet.”

Kara squints at her. “Judging by the way everyone reacts to that, I’m guessing Earth doesn’t get many foreign visitors,” she says.

“You’d be right,” Lena says, crossing her arms uncomfortably when Kara frowns at that. “At least, none the government will tell us about.”

“Huh.” Kara tilts her head, considers this for a moment. “Maybe our worlds aren’t so different in that sense.” She gestures towards her empty hospital bed and says, “Do you want to sit? Sorry I didn’t ask earlier.”

“That’s…fine.” Lena tentatively sits down on the edge of the bed. Kara, she notices, remains standing. “Have the doctors said when you’ll be released?”

“When they’re done running tests, I think,” Kara says. “No one will tell me yet.” She fiddles with the edge of her hospital bracelet as if it itches, then goes, “Not that I’m not glad you’re here, but you’re looking at me funny. Did I do something?”

“What? No, of course not.” Lena realizes her hands are clasped together tightly—so tightly her knuckles are white. “I was just worried about you.”

“How come?” Kara asks, truly curious and confused. “I’m okay.”

“Are you?” Lena presses. “Because I hit you with my car, Kara. And now you’re talking about how you’re from another planet, and everyone thinks you’re out of your mind, and maybe I’m to blame for all of this so—” She has to stop, inhale, try to relax. It doesn’t work. “I can pay for anything you need. Your hospital stay, any therapy afterwards—”

Kara’s expression, once naive and empty, twists into growing concern. “Whoa, whoa, slow down,” she says. “Whatever you think you did to me—I promise you didn’t.”

“That’s what your concussion thinks,” Lena argues, but Kara shakes her head.

“I heal fast,” she explains (at least, what must be an attempt at an explanation). “Yellow suns always have that effect on me. Can I share a theory with you?” Kara appears so earnest that all Lena can do is slowly nod, and then she is immediately rambling on: “The Legends took me on a trip to an Earth once, I think. I was helping them fight a particularly nasty group of Adaptoids, and afterward the battle left me so fatigued I was completely powerless for a whole day! I’m sure that’s why I feel so weird right now. I even tried to fly earlier and it didn’t work.”

Oh, this is so much worse than she thought. “Kara,” Lena says gently, “I would really like to help you. Is there anything I can do? Anything at all, no matter how big.”

“Unless you have a way to get me home, I really don’t think so,” Kara says, and she deflates slightly just at the mention of home. “Man, everyone must be so worried. I wish I had some way to communicate with them.”

“I have a cell phone, if you’d like to use it,” Lena offers.

Kara smiles at her faintly. “Thanks, but that’s okay,” she says. “I don’t think cell service exists between worlds here.”

“Right,” Lena says, like one might say my mistake, and she suddenly has an idea. An idea that Sam would raise her eyebrows at, an idea that Maggie Sawyer would categorize as insane. “Do you know anyone in this town? Anyone you might be able to stay with once you’re released from the hospital?”

Kara scrunches her nose like she has to think about that question. “Well, no?” she says. “You’re pretty much the only person I’ve met on this Earth. Except for Ruby, and Dr. Olsen, and her brother James, he’s super nice. Oh, and Eve, she’s the nurse that comes through here on her rounds. Plus there’s Officer Sawyer—”

“Just, just the no is enough,” Lena hurriedly cuts in before Kara starts naming the hospital cats or something. “Kara. Um. I know this is going to sound crazy, but…I have a guest house on my property. And if you have nowhere else to stay while you recover, I would like you to stay with me.”

By the way Kara blinks, it’s quite clear she does not expect the offer. “That’s…really nice,” she says, almost as if she’s surprised. “Really? I wouldn’t be in your way?”

“It is the least I can do,” Lena says, and Kara breaks into a wide smile.

“I will definitely take you up on that, then,” she says. “And don’t worry! I will be the best roommate.”

Lena sincerely doubts it, but she feigns a polite smile in return. Lord help her; this might be the most disastrous Christmas season of all time.

December 5th, 2022

“So are you letting the concussed girl stay in your guest house out of goodwill, or is this to avoid being sued again?”

“I’m not sure I appreciate your tone, Sam,” Lena says plainly. “Or your accusations.”

Even over the phone, it is impossible to miss the fact that Sam is inevitably grinning. “Babe, I love you,” she says, “but this is a new height of rich bitch weird—even for you.”

“I know what you’re going to say: I’m crazy for letting a stranger into my house,” Lena says, “and I will inevitably be robbed of all my valuables.”

“That…sounds more like something Jess would say.”

“Oh, she did. Verbatim,” Lena informs her, and Sam laughs.

“It’s a good thing, what you’re doing,” Sam adds afterwards. “I’m proud of you. And your constant need to help people.”

“I wouldn’t go so far as to say that.” Just as she speaks, Lena watches a squad car pull up to her front door. Kara’s ride from the hospital is going to be a showy one, apparently; Maggie nods at Lena briefly through the window, and Lena attempts a smile in return.

“Face it, you’re in a true Christmas-giving-spirit. Ruby is convinced this is your Grinch awakening.”

“I hate you both so much,” Lena declares, and she can’t resist smiling at her phone. “I have to let you go, Sam. She’s here.”

“Good luck with your journey of self-discovery. Call me later!”

As Lena echoes the goodbye (albeit not as dramatically as Sam’s), Kara approaches the door. She has a wide, sunny grin on her face and a trash bag slung over her shoulder, and Lena briefly entertains the idea of greeting her with a smile of her own.

But she doesn’t, because scowling Maggie Sawyer is there. “Ms. Luthor,” said officer acknowledges her. “I’m obligated to inform you that we were unable to obtain a background check on Ms. Zor-El.”

Lena isn’t quite sure how to respond. “Okay,” she says. “And do you have reason to believe she’s dangerous?”

They both unconsciously twist around to look at the woman in question. Kara Zor-El, dressed in sweatpants and a questionably stained men’s jacket (likely from the hospital’s life and found), has already dropped to her knees to gently lift a snail off Lena’s driveway and onto the grass.

“There you go, little guy,” Kara coos. “Safe and sound.”

Maggie hums lowly. “Yeah. Terrifying,” she says, and there’s a hint of a smile on her face all of a sudden. As quickly as it comes, however, it goes; she eyes Lena distrustfully once more, then sticks out a card. “Call me if she tries to murder you.”

Lena accepts it a beat slower than expected. “Thank you,” she says. “I think?”

“You’re welcome,” Maggie says, and with that she heads back to her vehicle without so much as a goodbye.

Kara Zor-El, potential murderer, waves at her departing car. “Thanks for the ride, Maggie!” she shouts, and the fact that she is on a first name basis with Officer Sawyer is the scariest thing about her by far.

That is about as far as Lena can assess, anyway; once her driveway is sufficiently cleared of bugs, Kara allows herself to be ushered into the guest house, and she treats every single piece of furniture as if it’s marvelous. When Lena tells Kara she is allowed to use Hope—Lena’s personal version of Alexa—Kara spends three straight minutes squinting at Lena’s speaker and inquiring about its inner workings.

“It’s like every AI,” Lena explains. “You have Siri on your phone, don’t you?”

“Siri…” Kara echoes seriously. “Does she also work at the hospital?”

So. Of all the threats Lena has dealt with in her lifetime, she doesn’t believe Kara is one of them. She leaves Kara to her own devices, and tells her she’s welcome to anything in the guest fridge until they can go shopping.

Before Lena takes her leave, however, Kara stops her with a gentle hand to her shoulder. “Thank you for this,” she says. “Really. Without you I would be so lost, and it’s…nice to make a friend on this Earth.”

“You’re welcome,” Lena replies awkwardly. She’s not really a friendly person, nor does she have many friends, and it’s strange to be branded a potential friend by a complete stranger. “Anyone would do the same.”

And Kara smiles at that, an odd half-smile that is warm but undeniably curious. “I’ve been to many planets,” she says, “and I’ve met many people. Trust me, you're in a league all of your own.”

“No, Kara, I don’t need you to carry me.”

December 25th, 2022

When the clock strikes midnight, the best gift Lena could ever ask for is the ensuing chaos of an Arias reunion.

Sam is throwing her arms around her mom, people are kissing each other's cheeks and yelling Feliz Navidad, and Ruby and the other kids are shaking presents around the tree; it might not be like the Christmases of Lena's past, but it feels like home. More than any other home she's actually lived in.

That’s when it really hits her. This holiday might be hit or miss, but Lena does have a favorite part of Christmas: her family. Her chosen family.

“Merry Christmas, Aunt Lena!” Ruby comes barreling over once she’s satisfied presents will be opening soon, and Lena hugs her back, unexpectedly emotional.

“Merry Christmas, Ruby,” Lena whispers into her hair, and doesn’t let go until Sam interrupts to give her a hug, too.

The kids start lining up vying for the opportunity to rip into gifts, and that’s where Lena finds Kara; she’s holding someone’s baby in one hand and gently using her other hand to sort through presents for the kids too young to read their names on the tags. When Kara sees Lena staring, she mouths Merry Christmas so as to not disturb the baby on the verge of dozing off on her chest.

Lena smiles in response and hopes that, despite being slightly teary, her eyes return the sentiment. The only other person missing—at least, for Lena—is Jess, so Lena takes out her phone in order to text her a quick merry Christmas. Knowing Jess, she will inevitably respond to it with some iteration of leave me alone, do you know what time it is. It's one of their longest-standing Christmas traditions at this point.

“Aunt Lena,” Ruby says, nudging her way into Lena’s personal space to squint at her phone, “it’s Christmas, no screen time.”

“There’s an exception for adults,” Lena reminds her. “Who else is going to take pictures of you and your presents?”

Ruby scowls; she’s at that age where absolutely no pictures taken by family are acceptable (or allowed). Luckily for the rest of the Arias family, the younger kids have no such qualms, and the grand spectacle of gift unwrapping is already underway. One kid goes at a time, so Kara hands off the baby in her arms to his mother—baby Mateo is blinking dazedly and drooling, but every aunt, uncle, and grandma in the vicinity begins to coo over him as soon as he’s inches away from his first gift.

“Remember when you were that cute?” Sam sighs dramatically, dragging her daughter under her arm. “That seems like a million years ago.”

“Hey!”

Lena laughs without meaning to, and Ruby glares at her in mock betrayal, but immediately hangs onto her mom’s arm and otherwise stays quiet as the other kids yell and shriek over the toys they unwrap. The sight is undeniably adorable. Actually, it fills Lena with an indescribable joy, which isn’t a feeling she ever thought she was able to have. There's just something about that innocence of children who have never known a bad Christmas that makes her weirdly emotional.

“Aw, look, the real baby Jesus.” Kara has somehow found her way back to their little group, and she says this to Lena as Sam's four-year-old nephew tears into his first present. “Wait. Is that offensive to the other baby Jesus? Ruby was trying to explain all of it but I'm…not sure I get it.”

“I haven't the slightest clue either,” Lena says, amused by the visual alone, and Kara bites her lip sheepishly.

“Well, I’m sorry baby Jesus,” she says to the general space of the air, because that’s the kind of person Kara is—the one who will apologize to omniscient deities she doesn’t even believe in, just because almost everyone else in the house does.

It drives Lena absolutely crazy. The ensuing warm rush of affection over something this trivial shouldn’t feel this electrifying, but it does. It just reminds Lena how much of a good person Kara is, and she feels a pang of sadness when she remembers no one has tried to find Kara ever since the accident.

How isn’t anyone missing Kara around this time of year? It makes no sense. Her picture has been plastered in the police station, the hospital, the fire station—anywhere where someone searching for a missing person might look, but nothing. No calls, no alerts. Maggie has taken to texting Lena no update every day before Lena can even ask for one.

Even then, the strangest thing is that Kara doesn’t seem too bothered by the lack of search for her. Lena, truthfully, had expected that this might be rough for Kara to be surrounded by Sam’s family; the last thing she would ever want is a painful reaction. But Kara seems…fine. “Hey, Kara,” Lena says quietly, to get her attention.

“Mh-hm?” Kara's muffled voice comes from the corner of her mouth, the other stretched out with the widest part of her candy cane. There is something about the way she gazes at Lena every time—with slightly wide, curiously attentive eyes, as if everything Lena will tell her might be life changing news.

“Are you alright with staying, or do you want to go home? I haven’t checked to see if you’re, I don’t know, uncomfortable or not.”

Kara’s brow crinkles, but her serious expression is somewhat hindered by the candy cane sticking out between her lips. “Of course not,” she says. “I love this, it’s fascinating. Are all Christmases this fun? We should have another one tomorrow.”

“Yeah!” At once, Kara sombers. “Wait, are you not?”

“No, I…” Lena shakes her head to herself, absolutely lost. “I always like Sam’s parties. I was just worried that maybe you might feel overwhelmed, since…” It feels silly now to say it out loud when Kara is staring at her with such unbridled confusion, but she does: “Since you’ve been without your family.”

No, rather, she smiles—smiles just with the corner of her lips, small and fond. “It’s okay, Lena,” she says. “Honestly, I’ve…been away from home a long time. And Sam’s family is awesome, they’ve made me feel so welcomed.”

“Yeah, everyone’s been so great. You have been…” There is a smear of red from the candy cane on Kara’s upper lip, and it’s mesmerizing. “I don’t know how I’ll ever pay you back, Lena. You changed my life.”

“You don’t have to pay me back for anything,” Lena says, and it comes out like a whisper, half-startled by the sudden proximity of Kara, who has shifted intoxicatingly closer. This hasto be a sign. The universe is screaming that there could possibly be no better time than to confess her feelings—no better time than to finally, finally throw her inhibitions out the window and let hope take over.

(That is, until the whole room is flooded by red, crackling electricity, and someone comes tumbling out of Sam’s chimney. Someone dressed in what looks like a red tracksuit and…a wrestling mask.)

Four-year-old Jesus Arias’s jaw drops. “Santa?”

The Santa imposter yanks the mask off his head, gasping, as he fruitlessly tries to shake off any ashes of last night’s fire from his jaw. “That was not intentional,” he says to no one in particular. “Cisco did that on purpose. Kara nods like this all makes perfect sense, and Lena has never been more lost in her life. “So this is all part of my training, then,” she confirms. “For the Legends.”

Barry coughs, and sheepishly tugs at his collar. “About that. Uh, Sara kind of dropped you off here as a joke. And then she…lost you.”

Ruby gasps, directing every eye to her instead of the world’s strangest tennis match of storytelling. “Then Kara really was dropped from a spaceship?”

“We call it the Waverider, actually,” Kara says absentmindedly, gnawing on her bottom lip anxiously like Lena has never seen her do. No longer is the elation clear on her face; it’s been replaced by glum disappointment. “So this whole time this was a mistake?”

“Of course.” Barry pulls his mask back on, and it’s truly even stranger once Lena gets a good glimpse at it. “I’ll wait outside.” He gives a small wave to the eyes still gawking at him sheepishly and says, “Merry Christmas,” before—in the literal blink of an eye—he vanishes, and all that remains is the same crackle of red lightning.

What the fuck. It’s official: Lena needs to stop drinking.

Despite her stupor, that prompts Kara to slowly break into a grin. “Am I not exciting enough of a superhero for you?”

Ruby shrugs. “I dunno. You’re not as mysterious now that I’ve seen you lick a rock.”

Even if it turns out there wasn’t a mission after all…you helped me work on everything I was missing, like the secret identity and my reasons for doing this. But here and now all she feels is disbelief, thick and cloying in the pit of her stomach. And silence, too. Complete and utter silence.

December 31st, 2022

“What are your New Year’s resolutions, Aunt Lena?”

This question comes from behind a literal wall of white and gold balloons, only half-audible; everything in Lena’s living room is a complete and utter mess, thanks in part to Ruby’s glitter-happy fingers, and also Sam’s insistence on every New Year decoration that was gold and shiny. “I’m going to have boundaries when it comes to you two throwing parties without consulting me first.”

Ruby considers this. “No, I don’t think you will,” she says, and then continues to gleefully rip into her millionth bag of confetti.

“Wha—I don’t think it’s very nice to ridicule my resolution,” Lena says, affronted.

Jess laughs from somewhere, also lost among balloons and whatever decor Ruby has her organizing (which could involve banners, or maybe some unfortunate photo booth cutouts). “Christmas is over, Ruby. I think you’re a little late for hot tubs.”

Lena merely rolls her eyes. “They are not haunted. Kara only thinks that because the pipes are—” Her breath catches, just as it always does, when she speaks about Kara as if she’s still here. She still can't shake the habit. “Well, she only thought that because the pipes make weird sounds when warming up.”

No one speaks for a moment, until Ruby sighs. “I wish Kara was here for the party. She would love it.”

“Ruby,” Sam says—warns—and Ruby pouts.

Lena slowly sets her glass down. “I’m going to change,” she says. “I’ve become infected with glitter too, thanks to you all.”

Moping is an unfamiliar feeling for her. Hell, Lena hasn't felt this heartbroken since Veronica Sinclair dumped her their senior year at boarding school, which had felt like the whole world had fucking splintered in pieces. But she was a kid back then who didn't know any better; she has common sense now that dictates pining over someone she never dated is just plain stupid. Unfortunately, her head and her heart are entirely out of touch with each other in this regard.

It's stupid. So stupid. Maggie Sawyer sent you a happy new year text, so you’ll be comforted to know the world must be ending.”

Lena remains at her vanity. The stranger gazing back in the mirror remains unrecognizable, even though the bags beneath her eyes are hidden and her scabbed bottom lip has been painted over with lipstick; there are some things that makeup just cannot hide.

So fucking stupid, she thinks, to let someone into her heart so effortlessly. Really, she is the only one to blame for this poor bout of judgement.

When Jess sees Lena’s smudged handiwork she raises her eyebrows, but otherwise leaves it alone—the clock reads 10:15 pm and the invitation Kelly mailed to their door had said 8, so Lena likely ranks low on the list of priorities right now.

“Sure,” Lena agrees, craning her neck back in time to catch Ruby beam, and they officially make their first social appearance sans Kara for the first time in a month.

Everyone already knows, of course, that Kara is gone. It had stumped everyone at the fire station that she’d left without saying goodbye—Alex Danvers especially—but as with all news, the surprise died quickly. Even the rumors of the UFO sighting the night Kara disappeared have become an afterthought, replaced with whispers about Winn and James’s engagement and Cat Grant’s estranged son showing up for the holidays.

“Alright everyone, ground rules,” Sam announces as they reach the door. This is more of a formality than anything, because everyone knows she’s only addressing Ruby. “No boys, no alcohol, and absolutely no touching anything that looks expensive.”

“No boys?” Jess repeats, feigning betrayal. “Then how am I supposed to kiss someone at midnight?”

“You’ll have to kiss Lena, sorry,” Sam says unapologetically. “Or me. Those are your only options.”

Ruby, meanwhile, is turning a shade of red Lena has never seen her reach. “Mom, seriously? There won’t even be any boys here. Ugh.”

“Then why am I here?” Jess mimes like she’s about to turn around and leave, and Ruby thumps her head against the door.

“You guys are so embarrassing,” she groans against the wood. “I’m never going to get to date until I’m fifty years old.”

“That sounds good to me,” Sam says brightly, and she rings the doorbell while Ruby groans louder.

Kelly answers the door. “You guys made it!” She hugs every single one of them, lingering just a bit longer with Lena; there is an unspoken understanding of a shared loss, for Kara. “Thank you so much for coming.”

“Sorry we’re so late,” Sam apologizes. “You know how it is, with kids.”

Ruby gapes at her. “I wasn’t even the late one!”

Kelly just laughs, warmly ushering them inside. “Well, we’re glad you managed,” she says. “We have drinks over by the kitchen, some food on the tables…and Ruby, the kids are mostly hanging out upstairs, if you want to head up. Don’t worry, there are teenagers here, too.”

“Are any of these teenagers boys?” Sam cuts in, eyeing her daughter closely mostly just to mess with her, and Ruby glowers.

“I’m leaving,” Ruby huffs, stomping her way up the stairs for approximately two seconds before she immediately stops (likely remembering this is not, in fact, her house). Lena has to give her points for self-restraint there.

“Drinks are exactly what we need right now,” Jess says. “Lena, a drink?”

“Sure,” Lena allows, and for a moment, everything is kind of normal. They take off their coats and join the familiar faces in the living room; a glass of wine is pressed in Lena’s hand; they watch some New Year’s countdown full of celebrity performances that no one pays attention to.

Alex eventually—inevitably—makes her way over to Lena. She offers her a half-smile and a refill, but doesn’t really talk. Not at first. Until: “So, uh…I think I owe you fifteen dollars.”

Lena lowers her glass, which she’d been raising to her lips. “For what?”

“Spotify,” Alex grimaces. “It was a habit, I’m sorry. I kind of kept on using Kara’s account and forgot you were the one paying for it.”

“Only because she has the attention span of a bird,” Alex says. “And also, she’s a weirdo who listens to practically every genre of music on Earth but thinks audiobooks aren’t fun.”

There is that tell-tale tug on her heart, simultaneously inconvenient and welcome. And Lena smiles even when it aches. “Because she thinks books need battle scars,” she recalls. “And you can only get that with physical copies.”

“Yeah. What a nerd.” Alex is drinking what seems like straight whiskey, and she practically chugs the remainder of her glass. “I tried calling her, but…”

Lena nods before Alex even finishes her sentence. Any calls or texts to Kara’s number come back undeliverable—no matter how many times Lena has fought with the phone company. Somehow, even though it’s paid and completely usable, they just can’t seem to locate Kara’s phone to fix the issue. “Me too,” she confesses. “Usually when I’ve been drinking.”

Alex clears her throat. “Well, anyway,” she says. “I just wanted to say, if you ever do see her again…tell her she owes me a proper game of pool. That moron always makes up her own rules and I need to win my money back.”

“Sure thing,” Lena says, gripping onto her glass so tightly her fingers turn white, and Alex quickly stands up to seek a refill of her own drink.

The countdown is reaching minutes. Sam is wearing the ridiculous 2023 glasses Lena had refused to bring along and making kissy faces at Jess; Jess is pointedly ignoring her; Alex is holding Kelly in her arms as they whisper about something; Winn is taking pictures of his engagement ring against the decor; Andrea Rojas is holding an entire bottle of wine and eyeing Jess with, frankly, disturbing intent.

Her skin is buzzing, her mind slightly foggy, and she stumbles into the front yard with the immediate need to sober up. It’s not that she’s drunk—she’s not even tipsy—but she feels the need for a hit of reality nonetheless, and the frigid air helps. She breathes in through her nose, practiced and careful, and relaxes.

Two doors down, the neighbors are lighting fireworks, leaving lingering scents of sulfur and smoke behind. A car whizzes past blaring a song that sounds like it's from Back to the Future. All of these are facts—cold and clear reality. Even then, Lena ends up looking up at the sky like always, just waiting for the impossible. Because if this is a world where aliens and superspeed and UFOs exist, then surely the world can spare just a smidgen of hope once in a while.

January 1st, 2023

The clock strikes midnight; Lena knows it does, because she can hear the loud cheering even from outside. Her mood almost lifts, imagining the chaos of who got to kiss Jess tonight. She'll be sure to press for those details later.

Another car goes by, slower this time, but also blasting music: this time something Lena can't pinpoint. More neighbors have begun to light fireworks and it's now every few seconds that they go off. The lights from the house across the street flicker on briefly, then back off again.

“You know,” a voice says from behind her, “I've heard you shouldn't be outside without a sweater.”

Every nerve in Lena's body seems to freeze up, the kind of blank numbness one might get from paresthesia. She almost can't turn around, but—she manages.

Kara Zor-El is waiting for her when she does.

Lena blinks once, then twice, and a third time for good measure. Kara is dressed in what looks like a weird blue tracksuit with a giant red “S” on the chest, along with a matching red cape to boot, and she looks remarkably different when she's not wearing an old flannel and jeans. Somehow knowing she's an alien…superhero or other…just makes her seem more serious.

“Kara?” All at once, the numbness vanishes. In that instant Lena realizes she's freezing, and she has to pause to wrap her arms around herself instead of stepping closer.

“Oh, here.” Kara hurriedly unclasps her cape, rushing to drape it over Lena's shoulders. This also means she has bridged the gap between them, and she lingers there for a moment, and swallows noticeably before she steps back.

The cape is actually quite thick and warm, and Lena grasps onto it like a lifeline. She has a million questions running through her mind, but all that comes out is, “Did your…suit always have pants?”

Kara glances down, likely caught off guard at the comment, but to her credit she recovers admirably fast. “Er, no,” she says. “It was a skirt, before. But Cisco made the change for me when I asked him to.”

“And Cisco…”

“He's a friend,” Kara explains. “He likes to design suits. Plus he's also an awesome superhero: Vibe.” She pauses to frown apologetically. “Well, not that you'd know him.”

“Right,” Lena says, and they just stare at each other. A million emotions are fighting to escape right now, all wound up in the tight confines of her throat; confusion, and elation, and so much horrible pessimism that it burdens her to speak at all. “Do you want to go inside? Alex—um, I know Alex would love to see you.”

“In a bit, definitely,” Kara breathes out. She’s nervous, fidgety, which is odd for Lena to witness when she has only ever seen Kara move with purpose. “But if it’s alright, I wanted to apologize first. To you.” Before Lena can even begin to unpack what she could possibly be apologizing for, Kara goes on: “When I said goodbye, I kind of…left out a few things. It was kind of a lot of pressure in front of everyone.”

The pessimism begins to win all at once, and it causes Lena’s shoulders to slump. “Oh,” she says quietly. “So you came to finish your goodbye?”

“Kind of? But not really.” Kara places her hands on her hips like she has no idea where to put them. Almost instantly they fall back at her sides. “No, wait. One hundred percent not really. This isn’t a goodbye, it’s…” She expels a frustrated sigh. “I gave you a really dumb speech when I left. Obviously you made me a better hero and obviously you taught me so much, but you were more than that. You were my friend.” Softer now: “I wanted us to be more than friends.”

“You did?” There’s that hope she had been repressing; it blooms right in the center of her chest, as uncontrollable and parasitic as a patch of weeds in a new garden. Lena’s voice cracks from the sheer weight of it, and Kara seems mesmerized, as if that break of emotion has ignited an entirely new hope for her as well.

“Yes,” Kara says. “Rao, Lena, you made me feel things I never even knew I was capable of.” She takes one step closer. “I was always going to be fine just being your friend. Lena draws the cape even tighter around herself and laughs, wetly. “Siri isn’t a real person, Kara,” she says—sniffles—because suddenly a lot of things make more sense. “It’s an AI programmed into the phone.”

“Huh.” Kara digs into her pocket and frowns at her phone, which somehow now has a cracked screen and a mysterious green stain on the case. “No wonder she wouldn’t give me updates on you guys when I asked. Now I feel bad for calling her some really mean names.”

“I don’t think you have the ability to be mean at all,” Lena says, wiping at her eyes before remembering her makeup. “Oh fuck, my mascara. God, I must look like a total mess right now.”

A firework cracks in the sky right above Alex and Kelly’s house, and for a moment Kara is suspended in reddish-pink light—and so is the way she looks at Lena, with absolute, unbridled devotion heavy in her eyes.

“You look beautiful,” Kara says, and she traces the underside of Lena’s jaw with her fingertips, almost hesitant to move closer. “Like always.”

“It’s actually not an S, it’s my family crest,” Kara says, covering Lena’s hand with her own. “I wanted to honor them. Even if we don’t always see eye to eye, you know?”

Lena thinks of the latest letter to her mother, already sealed and waiting for the mailman to pick up tomorrow morning, and she does know. Better than most, she’d wager.

“That’s a very nice idea,” Lena says. “I’m sure they will be really proud when you become a…hero. With the Legends.” She still has no clue who the Legends are or what the fuck they do, but if Kara wants to join them, then clearly they must be an honorable bunch. She can give them the benefit of the doubt.

“I don’t know, actually.” Kara—unflappable, unfazed Kara—has begun to blush, tips of her ears going pink. “They have plenty of members already. I was thinking maybe…National City could use another hero. If you’re fine with having some help, anyway.”

“That would imply I’m already National City’s hero,” Lena points out, quirking an eyebrow.

Kara smiles, and squeezes Lena’s hand fleetingly, barely a hint of pressure. “Aren’t you?”

Dammit, there are now tears stinging at the corner of Lena’s eyes. “Is this all a really weird way of telling me you’re staying?”

“It is,” Kara promises. Her hand falls to Lena’s waist now, gently bringing her in closer, and then she pauses. “Wait, is my confession weird? But I researched! Ruby said it would be romantic.”

Lena can’t help but let a short laugh escape. “Is that why we overheard you two repeating every cheesy pick-up line off the internet weeks ago?"

“Hey, don’t laugh, it was going to be mind blowing. I was going to show up and saying something clever like ‘funny seeing you here’ and ‘are you from tennessee because you fell from heaven,’ but then I saw you didn’t have a coat on and my brain kind of…made my choice for me,” Kara admits. Hastily she adds, “We should not tell Ruby that part.”

“Oh my God.” Lena lets go of the cape entirely just so she can grasp the face of this horribly moronic idiot with both her hands. “Kara, no. We have to tell Ruby those pick-up lines are awful.”

“They are?”

“They’re so, so bad. I cannot believe you went to the teenager who’s never even been on a date for romantic advice.” Lena is overcome with hiccupping, unattractive laughter that’s still kind of teary all at once; this just does not feel real. Kara doesn’t feel real. Not the smooth lines of her face, not the heavy, reassuring hands on Lena’s waist, not even the inhuman body heat against her dress.

“We also watched movies,” Kara interjects, but her bottom lip is jutted out in disappointment; Lena immediately reaches out to soothe with her thumb.

“Well if it’s any consolation,” she says, “I think you’re very romantic.”

Kara brightens. “Really?”

“Really,” Lena affirms. “It’s not every day that a girl abandons her secret superhero league for me. This must mean 2023 is off to a great start.”

“Shoot, the new year!” Kara’s eyes widen. “Did I get here on time? Ruby says it’s very important for me to kiss you right at midnight for the movie magic. Siri! What time is it?”

A beep, and then: “It is 12:27 A.M.”

“Aw,” Kara sighs, pitifully pressing her cheek deeper into Lena’s palm, “it would’ve been more romantic if I came here at midnight. I swear Cisco did this on purpose.”

Lena traces the edge of Kara’s warm mouth, teases along the curve of her pout, and memorizes the feeling; if this is a dream she never wants to wake up. “Well, I haven’t been kissed at all this year,” she says pointedly. “Maybe you could help me out with that.”

Slowly, Kara begins to smile. And in that instant Lena realizes Kara’s smile might be the most familiar sight in the world, as well as her favorite. Especially when her smile brushes against Lena’s cheek with a faint but promising kiss which just about sparks every nerve in Lena’s body on fire, lingering from the gentle pressure on her waist to the softness of Kara’s lips against her skin.

Lena’s eyes flutter shut without meaning to; she has every intention of teasing Kara for not kissing her properly, but then Kara pulls her closer and her words die on her tongue. Kara’s lips hover just above hers for a second—one pause to give Lena time to pull away—and Lena grasps onto the back of Kara’s neck desperately until Kara finally, feverishly, closes the gap and kisses her until they’re both left breathless. Then Kara kisses her again—and again—and at one point even swings Lena up into her arms to kiss her once more, while Lena yelps against her mouth and says,

“Kara! Put me down, I’m not even injured anymore!”

But Kara only gazes at her, twists a tendril of Lena’s hair between her fingertips tenderly, and Lena holds onto her neck tighter and forgets what she even said. Across the street, the lights flicker back on.

This time, they stay lit.
