In Kara's defense, she's not really thinking when it happens.

She's three miles into her run, eight songs into her favorite playlist, and fucking tired from fighting off a three-night long phantom infestation. So when she turns left instead of right and steps off the running path, she barely notices; then her foot snags on something, and she goes flying to the ground, and when her eyes readjust to the dark: she sees she tripped over a body.

As in, a human body.

Needless to say, she's a little bit surprised (read: outright fucking startled), so maybe she does accidentally bring a corpse back to life.

"Anyway," Kara says, a few minutes later, trying and failing to think of a conversation starter. "I think the blood really accentuates your skin tone."

The woman standing across from her just keeps staring. Silently. Her eyes are mostly black, face pale and halfway shielded by the dark, and the fact that she won't talk is giving Kara goosebumps. This is just her luck, though: she's always had a knack for getting herself into fucked up situations, probably why she's on the shit-list with the Magic Council as it is, but this is big even for her.

She's been a licensed screw-up of a necromancer for six years, but she's never accidentally reanimated a human. 

"I was gonna finish my run," she keeps going, "but maybe I'll head home instead. You should probably follow me there. I mean, unless you want to wander around the park as a corpse. No judgment if you do. But people might find that a little weird, considering."

For a second, it seems like the woman's eyes are shifting, flickering in the moonlight, and Kara hopes to all hell this is a simple reanimation and she didn't just awaken some next level super-freaking-natural shit, ugh. She should really start running when it's light out like a normal person. 

"Just a suggestion, feel free to think about it," she says, pressing her hands together. "But maybe think a little faster."

The woman opens her mouth, then a long second later she just says, "Lena."

"Excuse me?"

"My name." Her voice is low and raspy, which is somehow creepier than the silence. "At least it was, but now I'm dead. Apparently."

"Being dead doesn't change your name, I don't think," Kara says, then she remembers her manners. "Thanks for telling me, though. I know your name holds a lot of power once you enter the other side."

"The other side?"

"Yeah, the—oh," Kara cuts off, squinting at Lena. She isn't an expert when it comes to human bodies, but Lena can't have been dead that long. Maybe she's not acquainted yet. Which is good. They don't need to bring the other side into this. She switches the subject back. "It's not important," she waves it off, "but my offer was real. To go home with me. If you're interested in getting out of the cold."

Silence again. 

Awesome, they're back to where they started. Ugh.

Fuck.

She can do this. It's just small talk with a dead person. It's objectively in her job description. "We can circle back to that, though," she says slowly, trying her best to stay patient, calm, cool. She thinks about her training, thinks about the consequences if she doesn't manage to get Lena home with her; then she thinks god I have absolutely nothing to talk about, before she opens her mouth and asks, "So, what's it like being dead?"

Okay, so it's not her best attempt at socializing, but Lena at least reacts to it: she tilts her head, and for a second Kara thinks that's all she might get, but surprisingly Lena... responds.

"It was cold," she says. "Then it was just black for a long time." She pauses, taking a second to look at Kara. "And then I saw you."

"Oh. I'm sorry, then," Kara says, reflexively.

"Why?"

She shrugs. "Not sure. That I'm not God?"

Lena snorts, face shifting into something that looks close to a smile. "It's okay, I don't think I expected to see him, anyway."

"Yeah, he's an elusive one, isn't he?" Kara jokes. Lena actually laughs, just a little bit, so Kara smiles, letting her shoulders relax, watching Lena loosen up, too. "I imagine dying puts a lot of things into perspective. Do you have any wisdom to impart now that you've crossed over? I've always wanted to ask, just never had the chance to, I suppose. But since you're here."

"Well, I haven't had time to think about it, but um," Lena stretches out, and then she doesn't speak for a while, the silence in the park growing thick again. Finally, she shrugs. "I guess Shakespeare was right."

Kara quirks a brow. "About what?"

"A lot of things," Lena says, "but mostly when Henry says when this body did contain a spirit, a kingdom for it was too small a bound; but now, two paces of the vilest earth is room enough." 

Kara's too tired for this. She has no idea what that means. "I have no idea what that means."

Lena shrugs. "I suppose you will at some point. As you said, you get perspective."

"Hopefully not soon," Kara says, shivering for effect.

Lena smiles again, but there's something too weird about it for this time of night and them being on the subject of Kara dying. "It happens when you least expect it, I'd say, from personal experience. But what do I know?"

"Shakespeare quotes, apparently."

Lena laughs, this time more openly. Kara likes the sound of it, the way it fills up the still of the park. She actually doesn't mind talking to Lena, but they also shouldn't be standing here. She's tired and cold, and they should really just go back to her apartment. "Seriously, though, we should go back to my apartment. The longer we stand here, the more likely it is someone will see us."

"I suppose that's true," Lena says warily.

It's a good enough opening for Kara. "So, you'll come?" she asks.

Lena sighs. It's not exactly the best sign. Kara expects a little opposition to follow or maybe more questions, but she doesn't expect Lena to lift her shirt, poke a finger in one of her stab wounds, then ask, more curious than anything, "Is this some sort of sexual fetish for you?"

Which... what?

"No," Kara blurts. "What?"

"Are you a psychopath, then? Who invites dead girls home?"

"Someone who's trying to help," Kara defends. "I'm harmless, like a box of kittens."

"With just as many claws," Lena comments. She pulls her finger out and lets her shirt drop. "Seriously, why not just run away and pretend you never saw me? It's weird that you're still standing here."

"I just feel... responsible," Kara admits. "And I ethically, and also legally cannot leave you standing here."

"Why not?"

"Because I'm the reason you're not dead anymore," Kara says fast, "and there are a lot of rules attached to that, and it'd be a little—okay, not a little, but very, very—bad for me if I just let you walk around, so—"

"So, you're into some creepy shit?"

"No," Kara frowns. "That's not - what - you're terrible at finishing sentences, okay? I was going to say so come home with me. Again. Because you're also terrible at listening. We should really go, though, like, now."

"I wasn't finishing your sentence, I was making an observation," Lena says.

God, they never make this easy, do they? "I'm trying to be nice. You're being difficult for no reason."

"Well, I'm dead, so—"

"So, you don't have anywhere else to be," Kara cuts in. "I'm better at finishing sentences than you are. Anyway, lovely conversation, but there's somewhere that's not here that we can be having it."

"You sound like you're getting a little worked up," Lena says calmly.

Kara rolls her eyes. "And you sound like  - ugh, okay, you know what? Fine. Good luck wandering the park, then. I'm going home. Where it's warm, and I don't have to look at dead things."

"You're not going home," Lena points out. "You're here as long as I'm here, that much is obvious."

Kara used to consider herself patient, but Lena's really shitting on that. "Does it make it more enticing if I say please?"

"It doesn't," Lena says, clenching her jaw. Kara doesn't want to have to make her move, but when it's half-past eleven and pitch black out she's not exactly opposed. "Tell me how I'm alive, and I'll consider it," Lena decides. "I remember dying. Distinctly. I shouldn't be here."

Right. Kara does owe her that. "Yeah, so, funny story," Kara prefaces, then realizes there isn't really a punchline. She rudely ripped Lena out of the afterlife, and she's probably going to be in a lot of trouble for it. "I'm sort of a necromancer?"

"Really?" Lena blinks at her, skeptically.

"Well, you're dead and you're talking," Kara says. "You don't have a lot of room for skepticism."

"Fair point," Lena concedes. "So, you're just going around raising an undead army?"

"No, I uh, tripped over you while running and your body scared the crap and the magic out of me, which. Isn't my best work, but I'm trying to make up for it."

Lena smiles. This time it's pretty. For a dead girl, Kara supposes. "Smooth," she teases. "So, we go to your apartment, and then what?"

Kara smiles back. She really has to appreciate the humor in this situation, because outside of that she just... royally fucked up. "Then we get yelled at by my sister."

"Okay," Lena says. Then, after a whole minute of nothing, she shrugs. "Fine."

One awkward walk to Kara's apartment, a phone call, and twenty minutes of yelling later, Kara hangs up and looks over at Lena. She's been slumped in Kara's recliner staring at a wine stain on the carpet the entire time.

"You know, it looks a little like blood," she says, "I can't stop staring at it."

"Everything looks like blood, I hear. After you've been dead."

Lena's eyes light up when she looks at Kara, jaw clenched like she wants to smile but she's holding it back. "You weren't joking about the yelling."

Kara shrugs. "Alex is predictable like that. Kelly is sending recipes, though. She's like the Martha Stewart of witchcraft. She can make anything that does anything in an edible form."

"Are you going to poison me?"

"The opposite, actually. I want to make you feel a little better."

"Why?"

Kara shrugs again. "I don't know, but I at least want you comfortable while we figure everything out."

"Right, because you really messed up this time," Lena says, something light about her voice. "You should just kill me now while you're ahead. Sounds like you have a long, long history of very bad magical decisions."

Kara's being teased again. By a dead girl. Her life is brilliant. "I did mess up, yes. But I'll fix it. I just - okay, it's hard. And not that it's any of your business, but being able to bring things to life accidentally hasn't been super easy to control."

"Don't I know it," Lena says. This time she does smile.

Kara rolls her eyes. "You have like three living days left on this planet and you're going to use them all messing with me, aren't you?"

Lena snorts. "I didn't know I was on a timer; I'd better turn it up a notch."

"No, you - I - fine," Kara huffs. "But you have to stay here, alright? With me. As fun as that sounds."

"I heard Alex yell don't let her out of your sight all four times. I'll stay. There's no one else I can visit, anyway."

It seems sad, but Kara can tell Lena doesn't want to talk about it. So, she goes with, "Sometimes these things can take a few days to fizzle out. Just waiting for the magic to run its course. So, I'll do the best I can to accommodate you in the meantime."

Lena looks like she's considering that, then, "Do I get to eat?"

"Yes, kind of, but nothing solid, and only what I make you," Kara says, then gestures at the bloodied-up holes littering Lena's shirt. "But I think I'll have to sew you up first, make sure nothing leaks out."

"Sexy. That's always what a girl loves to hear," Lena smiles.

Kara just blinks at her.

This is going to be a long three days.

"You're just going to stare at it and not eat it?" Kara asks.

Lena wrinkles up her nose and takes another whiff of the mug of soup Kara handed her. She pulls back again, dissatisfied again. "It smells funny. I don't think you did the recipe correctly."

Kara frowns, setting her own mug on the table. Black coffee—no cream no sugar—to snap her out of this nightmare. "I had to cut you open and sew up your insides, then close you up and sew up your outsides. And then, because m'nice, I spent four hours making you a special, healing soup and all you have to say is that it smells funny?"

"Someone's grumpy," Lena mumbles, taking a sip and curling her lip in disgust afterward. "Why healing?"

"It'll help you feel a little more human, not aching all the time."

"What are you going to do with the body?" Lena asks, taking another sip. "You know, when I just drop dead again in your living room."

"Put it back in the park?" Kara shrugs, then decides that's a distasteful joke. "I haven't thought that far ahead, but I'm hoping I can do something discrete, leave an anonymous tip so your family can find you and get closure."

"My memory feels likes swiss cheese, but I'm fairly certain my family killed me," Lena says, blinking like that's not even a little bit shocking. "Lena Luthor. You might have heard of my brother, Lex."

"The billionaire sociopath?" Kara asks. "I see where you get your humor."

Lena snorts. "Stop that, m'nothing like him," she slurs, mouth full of soup. She swallows, then sighs. "He hated me, and now he's done what he's always wanted to do. I expect we'll hear about it on the news in a day or two."

"That he killed you?"

"No," Lena laughs. "That I'm missing. Then there'll be a search, and that's why I'm wondering: what'll you do when it's suddenly you that has the body of a dead billionaire with an evil brother in her apartment."

"Oh," Kara says.

"Yes, oh," Lena says. "We should think of something. You're too cute to take the fall for this."

Kara blushes. She doesn't mean to. Lena's pale and her body cracks when she moves and Kara's seen her large intestine, but still. Here she is. "I don't think the way I look determines if I deserve to wrongfully go down for a crime or not."

"Not the way you look," Lena rolls her eyes. "You're like, I don't know, nice. You go around feeding dead girls. Someone like you should be on the streets. Not someone like Lex. Or anyone else that would usually kidnap a corpse."

"I did not kidnap a corpse."

"Potayto potahto," Lena waves her hand. "Bigger point, you've just made this easy for him."

"Maybe," Kara says, but she doesn't want to talk about this. It's not her first time hiding a body, and she also doesn't want to talk about that. "Anywho, I have to run to the store and grab some things, so could you try not to rip my apartment apart while I'm gone? I didn't get any sleep last night like one of us did."

"Barely a few hours before you woke me up," Lena quirks a brow. "Hardly counts as sleep."

"Well, you've been out cold for a day or two already. How much could you need?"

Lena laughs. The sound is really starting to grow on Kara. "I'll try not to go through your porn collection while you're gone."

Kara takes a deep, centering breath and shakes her head. "You know, that's actually fine. Just make sure you clean up afterward."

"Now you're just baiting me."

"Whatever," Kara laughs. "Do you want me to grab you a puzzle or something? Rubik's cube?"

"Sure," Lena says. "Surprise me."

She makes it through the door of the store and the full eight steps it takes to get to the produce section before Alex is calling her cellphone. "Please tell me I was dreaming last night when you said you brought a corpse home."

Kara drops the mushrooms in her basket. "Nope."

"Fuck, that's not good. It's not authorized and I doubt the Council will take kindly to that."

"You know, I hadn’t thought about that, but you're probably right." She drops in rosemary and oranges and looks around for dried lavender. "I have no experience with messing up bad enough to warrant a disciplinary hearing."

"Don't be an ass."

Kara turns and heads for the tea section, smiling to herself.

"I can cover for the phantoms, but not for this, Kara. You have to get rid of her like, yesterday."

Ah, there's the lavender. She grabs a couple boxes of tea, too. "Well, I didn't get rid of her yesterday, so I guess you're just back to not covering for me."

"Do you even care?"

Kara closes her eyes briefly. Alex cares. That's why she's like this. She has no idea how hard it is being born with magic that no one teaches you to control but keeps punishing you for misusing. "Alex, just drop it, okay? I know I messed up, and I know I'm in trouble, and I really don't expect you to cover for me. Right now, I'm more focused on getting her back okay, though. I don't want this to be an experience that turns her into something malevolent, alright?"

"Fine," Alex says. "Kelly made more recipes, I'll text them."

"Awesome, thanks." Kara clicks out of her phone and heads toward the cereals.

She has several minutes of peace on the bus ride back to her apartment, then she walks through the door, sees Lena on the couch, and – "You have ghosts in your apartment," Lena says.

Kara toes her shoes off, trying her best to balance the paper bags. "Who, George and Saffron? They're harmless."

"They're afraid Amy will come back."

"She won't," Kara says. Lena doesn't say anything, but she still looks genuinely concerned. Kara sighs, then she sets the bags down and walks over to kneel on the carpet in front of Lena's knees. "She won't come back because I got rid of her when I got rid of the infestation. They're just nervous little boogers, and she terrorized them, alright? You shouldn't be talking to them, anyway."

Lena frowns, rubbing her palms over her thighs. She's wearing Kara's joggers. "Well, you weren't home, so they latched on to me. It's not my fault."

"I shouldn't have to go out again while you're here, okay?"

Lena clenches her jaw, but her shield cracks as fast as she put it up. "Promise?"

"Of course," Kara says, giving her knee a squeeze. "I'm here for you, no matter what."

Lena leans forward and cups Kara's face in her palms, like she's looking her over. Kara sits up straighter, taller. "What's it like?" she asks. "Being surrounded by so much death all the time."

Kara shrugs. "It's always been this way. I don't know what it's not like."

"It's new for me," Lena comments, letting Kara's face go. "It wasn't all black when I died, like I said. I saw the other side."

"Just go through it," Kara says.

"Easier said than done."

"I won't pull you out this time, so it'll be better."

"No, this is better," Lena mumbles, "but I know I can't stay here. I don't want to end up like them, stuck haunting you."

Kara can't guarantee her she won't end up stuck, or make dying seem better, so she just reiterates, "I'm here."

"Awesome, the world's worst necromancer at my service, just what I need."

Kara smiles because Lena's smiling. "Stay out of my shower. You smell like my soap."

"Does that turn you on?"

"No," Kara laughs. "I'm gonna go make you more soup."

"Why not show the world?"

Kara lets her hand go. "Because the world can't handle it. I'm all done for now."

Lena goes back to frowning. "With the conversation or with the body rub?"

"It's um—" she wants to say complicated, but she knows better. "If you're here long enough your uh - spirit, if you will, recognizes it's not supposed to be here and it sort of leaves your body. Which creates this whole earthly tether-spectral realm thingy that's just. Not good. So, we should keep you all in one spot."

Lena just laughs. "That sounds made up."

"It's actually terrifying, and one of the most foolproof ways to make a poltergeist."

"Are you afraid I'll cross over and still poke you at night?"

Kara laughs, almost too loud for her small apartment. "Actually, yes. Greatly, deeply, massively afraid of that."

"I'd never hurt you."

"I know. You don't seem like the type."

"What type do I seem like?" Lena smiles, sitting up on the couch.

Kara rolls her eyes. She feels like she's been doing a lot of that lately. "Is this taking it up a notch? You're flirting with me?"

"Not necessarily, but do you want me to be?"

"Not necessarily," Kara mimics, "but you can keep going if it amuses you. It's not going to work."

Kara laughs. "Just make sure you keep it on, alright? It should keep your head clear, and hopefully keep the ghosts away."

Lena stands up straight again and holds out her wrist. Kara puts the bracelet on, then Lena takes a deep breath, makes a face, and says, "I don't feel anything."

"You're not supposed to," Kara says.

"I thought it was supposed to do something..." Lena trails off, looking past Kara's head. "Ooh, who's that girl? She's cute."

Kara whirls around to where Lena's staring, to where she has her iPad propped up on the counter with the news cycling, and – fuck. There's a picture of Lena on the screen. "It's starting, isn't it?"

"You sure?"

"Yeah," Kara whispers. She's not sure, though. She's kind of freaking the fuck out. "You're very beautiful, by the way."

Lena chuckles. "Now or then?"

Kara just smiles. "I'll make us something to eat."

Against her better judgment, she lies down on the couch while she's making more soup, and then she wakes up to the lights off in the apartment and a weight on top of her.

A couple seconds of heavy breathing later, she realizes it's Lena. Then she feels a chill. "She stopped the apartment from burning down," Saffron says.

"Yeah, she's nice," George chimes in, "can we keep her?"

"She's not a pet," Kara says. "And can you two stop talking to her? Let her live her last few days in peace."

"Seriously, we should keep her," George goes on. "I know a guy on the other side that can help with a more… permanent spirit placing. You just have to find a gullible human and transfer her to a living—"

"That was cold," Saffron says.

"Sorry, I'm just definitely, probably going to get my magic license banned."

"No, you're not," Saffron says, and George says no shh, petulantly.

Kara rubs her eyes and shifts underneath Lena. "Tell me why not, or maybe Amy will come back."

"She's unsettled," George says fast.

"She probably reached out to you at the park, and you just didn't notice because you're – well, you're objectively terrible at your job. Sorry," Saffron chimes in.

"I hate both of you," Kara huffs. "Why would she be unsettled?"

"There's probably something about the circumstances surrounding her death that she doesn't remember," George tells her, "but the feeling is strong enough to keep her tethered here."

"Her brother killed her, she said," Kara recalls, blinking away her sleep haze, trying to focus her brain.

"Yeah, but why did he kill her?" Saffron asks. "Why now?"

Kara can hear clapping. She knows it's George even before he sing-songs, "She knows something."

"Yep," Saffron says, "and if you figure out what that is, it can be your ticket to getting out of trouble with the Council. Just say you brought her back to investigate. If it has to do with Lex Luthor, it'll definitely be juicy."

"God," Kara groans.

"You know, he doesn't really exist," Saffron says.

Kara doesn't know the answer to that. "What are you saying?"

"I'm saying the more you like her, the more she lives."

"I know I'm objectively keeping her alive, but I'm not like, keeping her..." Kara trails off, then, "Saffron, I'm not keeping her alive, am I? Please tell me I'm not that much of a fuck up."

"You're not a fuck up," George says. "You just like her. It happens."

"No," Kara whispers, compeltely in denial. That can't be true, because if she's keeping Lena alive, then that means in order for Lena to die - Kara will either have to want her to die, or the council will have to expel her. "Are you saying I have to will her back dead or report this to the Council for an expulsion?"

"One of those is worse than the other," Saffron says. "It'll be terrible for her soul."

"But the other one just isn't going to happen," George adds in. "I hate to say it, but you have to tell the Council."

"I need another day," Kara says, and she almost feels like crying.

"It'll just make it worse," Saffron tells her, and every single piece of Kara knows she's telling the truth. "If you let it go on any longer than it has already, she'll definitely be tethered."

Kara's heart sinks low enough her chest hurts. Tethering isn't just a fuck up that leads to a disciplinary hearing, it's outright illegal in the magic community. If she tethered Lena, they wouldn't just use a binding spell on Kara, they'd take her magic away for good and make sure she never interacted with the community again. And if they take her magic away, Lena would die anyway. It'd be even worse than an expulsion. They probably wouldn't even let Kara talk her through it. She just might hate that fact the most. At least Kara can argue to be there with her if she's expelled.

"You're right," she decides. "I'll tell the Council."

*

It takes her twenty minutes to breathe herself into finally making the phone call. She knows each second she wastes is another second too long, and she's no stranger to being in this position, but it feels different this time. She has her own little world when she has her powers - George and Saffron, Johanna at the bakery, Phil at the laundromat and Lucy at the car wash - and she's just as sad about giving that up as she is about Lena.

But she has to. She knows it's the right thing to do.

Kara takes another deep breath, and then she dials the number. "Hey, Kara," Janice on the other end says. "It's not a good thing that I recognize your number. Do you need J'onn?"

Kara chews on her lip for a long moment, then says, "No, I need Cat."

There's a pause. "Kara," Janice says, then, "Sweetheart. What did you do this time?"

"I messed up," Kara admits, small and almost too soft. "I think I'll need Cat for this one. It'll probably be more than just a slap on the wrist and getting yelled at by Alex."

There's another long pause, before, "Alright, then. I'll route you to Cat. Good luck, Kara."

"Thanks," Kara says, then she nervously waits the minute before Cat finally answers.

"Kara Danvers," Cat starts with, and she already sounds exhausted. "Considering I nearly died today but didn't - I'm in a good mood. But that only extends so far. What did you do?"

"You nearly died?" Kara asks, mostly out of concern, and maybe partly to avert the topic.

"Yes, we all nearly did," Cat says. "Have you not seen the news?"

"No," Kara admits. She's mostly been freaking out about the phone call the whole morning, and avoiding anything to remind her that Lena's missing and that fact is adding to her fuck up. "I haven't turned on the TV today."

"You should," Cat tells her, "it's the only thing being broadcasted. There was nearly a catastrophic meltdown with the Obsidian energy project. They planned a public demonstration for today, but canceled last minute to do a safety inspection and actually found a malfunction in the system. If they'd gone through with the demonstration it would have led to an explosion that leveled the entire city."

"You have to be kidding me," Kara whispers, mostly to herself. "Obsidian? Do you mean like, Andrea Rojas?"

"Yes," Cat says. "It's her company. She called off the demonstration. She said she received an anonymous tip that suggested there was malicious tampering with the system."

Kara feels like she's frozen in time. Lena said they would save a lot of lives, but she didn't think she meant the entire city, or that their own lives were potentially in danger. She's not even sure if Lena remembered the specifics herself other than she needed to warn Andrea. If she did, she was remarkably fucking calm about it.

"Anyway," Cat says, grabbing Kara's attention again. "Tell me what you did."

"I think I did that," Kara whispers, then louder says, "I think that was me. The anonymous tip to Obsidian."

Cat doesn't respond for a second, and Kara can hear her own heart thumping in her ears in the silence. "Can you be a little more specific about that? And why that warrants a call to me."

Kara swallows, then says, "Lena Luthor," and has to take a deep breath so her hand stops shaking while holding the phone to her ear. "She's been on the news as a missing person, you might have seen it. Except, she's not missing, she's dead. And she's been in my living room reanimated for the last week."

"Kara Danvers," Cat says, firmer than the first time she said it.

Before she can get another word in, Kara gets out, "Yesterday, she texted Andrea Rojas to call off the demonstration. They were friends, I think. She even knew where she got lunch on Wednesdays and what her sandwich order would be. I kept her around because she was unsettled, and—"

"You didn't tell the Council because..."

Kara doesn't have a good answer for that. She goes with, "I just thought we wouldn't agree on whether or not I was doing the right thing. But, I think this proves it. I think it was good to keep her around."

"Bring her in," Cat says, "Immediately."

Then, she hangs up the phone.

*

"I finally pooped," Lena announces happily as she exits the bathroom to join Kara in the living room. Kara's concern about her having a bowel movement is dampened by the smile on her face. "I feel like I've been bloated for ages. If I had any feeling, it would have probably been painful. Good thing I'm a pin cushion," she laughs, then frowns when she actually focuses on Kara's face. "What's up? Your eyebrows are doing the frowny thing."

"It doesn't matter if my mouth isn't," Kara says, widening her smile.

Lena rolls her eyes. "I wouldn't be a good emotional support corpse if I didn't notice all of your body language."

"That's just an excuse to stare at my butt."

"I don't need an excuse for that," Lena says flatly. "Tell me what's wrong."

"What's your favorite meal?" Kara deflects. "We can try grabbing it for lunch today."

"So, it's happening then." There's a long bit of silence between them after Lena says it, and Kara looks at her hands too long before she lets them make eye contact again. "When do you have to turn me in?"

"Right now, technically," Kara says, "but I've already messed up this much, figured I could buy us a few hours."

"You don't have to do that."

"I know," Kara whispers, "but you saved my life, so. Consider it a thank you."

Lena laughs. "I might have spiced it up and annoyed you for a few days, but you weren't so far off the ledge that you needed -" she pauses. "Wait, were you? That's -"

"No, no," Kara jumps in fast. "I meant with the tip you sent to Andrea. You saved the whole city."

She picks up her iPad off the couch beside her and pulls up an article, and Lena walks over to plop down right next to her, taking the iPad as she leans against Kara's shoulder. It feels familiar - too familiar - like Kara won't just miss this, but it'll actually hurt to lose Lena. "Holy fuck, this would have been catastrophic," Lena gasps, like she's just now herself figuring out the scale of what she's done. "I knew I was saving people, but I didn't realize - fuck."

"Do you know who was behind this?" Kara asks. "Who was behind the hit?"

Lena shakes her head. "I think I did at some point," she says, clicking off the screen of the iPad. "But it's like it's still repressed somehow, you know?" She lights up a little bit after she says that, turning to face Kara. "Maybe we can tell the Council that, maybe even argue I should stay longer. That person is still out there."

"That's not the issue," Kara says gently. She just might hate this part the most. "There's a thing called tethering that becomes possible if you're a strong enough necromancer. It would link your life to my magic, and - essentially," Kara hesitates, then finishes, "you'd live as long as I'm alive, or as long as I have magic."

"So, I'm a risk because you kept me alive too long?" Lena asks slowly, like she's piecing together if her life becomes permanent, something a little more drastic needs to be done to end it. "You could still argue it was for a good purpose," she says. "That you shouldn't be punished."

"I'm really bad at being a necromancer," Kara sighs, looking down at her hands again.

"Hey," Lena starts, squeezing Kara's thigh, but Kara looks up to meet her eyes and she stops short.

"You wouldn't be tethered because you were alive for too long. You'd be tethered because I wanted you to be. It would be accidental, of course," Kara clarifies. "I think I just - I got used to having you around."

There's a long moment where they just stare at each other, then Lena lets her mouth hang open for a few seconds before her breath catches and she says, "Oh my god, you're into dead girls."

Kara's so caught off guard that she has to physically shake her head to reorient herself. And even then she's still flabbergasted that's the first thing Lena would say. "I'm not into dead girls," she defends. "I'm just into... you."

It comes out faster than she can catch herself, and all Lena says is, "Oh," in response.

Kara thinks about clarifying with not physically, or not romantically, or any other thing to suggest she meant that in a purely platonic way, but she's not so sure that she did mean that in a purely platonic way. She'll have nightmares about this moment, probably.  "Yeah, well," she mumbles, looking at her hands again, twisting them together hard enough her palms are going white where her thumbs are digging in. "You're funny and easy to talk to, and you're smart and don't judge me. So, it's not like it's hard to like you, but. You're just a corpse, and I know that, okay? I know that."

"Is that all I am to you?" Lena asks, and she sounds serious, her voice dropping low enough to make Kara's heart sink. She never meant to hurt Lena's feelings. "Am I just a corpse?"

The thing is, Kara knows she should say yes to that question. She knows Saffron talked to her about intentionality, and she knows she's trying to avoid tethering, but she still opens her mouth and says, "No." She knows what the right decision would be in this situation, but that never actually stopped her from making the wrong decision. "You're alive to me," she confesses. "And I think that's what the problem is. But I can't help it."

Lena actually smiles at that, then she shakes her head, and then she leans in and kisses Kara. It's soft and quick, and Kara doesn't even get a chance to close her eyes, but she still feels like her head is spinning.

"I like you, too," Lena decides, still smiling. "I'm glad you tripped over my body in the park."

"Me too," Kara admits, smiling herself, too big and too happy. "I mean, both because I got to know you, and because we saved all of those people. The kiss was just like, a bonus."

"Yeah?" Lena laughs. "The cherry on top of your corpse crush sundae?"

Kara giggles, too. "Shut up, don't ruin it. I'm pretty sure I'll have to talk to my therapist about this as it is."

"Just come here," Lena says, grabbing Kara by the shirt, and this time she pulls Kara into something deeper. Kara actually manages to close her eyes, and Lena opens her mouth a little, and they kiss long enough for Kara to loosen up and finally let herself melt into it. She's out of breath when she finally pulls away. "Don't worry, I won't tell anyone," Lena teases her. "Well, no one living, can't say I won't leak this to some of my friends on the other side."

"Please don't," Kara frowns. "I can't have some ghost possessing my next girlfriend just to get some tongue action."

"I don't know which part of that sentence I hate the most," Lena says, "but it's definitely a tie between tongue action and next girlfriend. You're already moving on from me," she huffs, slapping Kara's shoulder.

Kara laughs as she grins at her. "'Til death do us part, babe. You know how it is."

"Babe? Now I'm just another notch on your bedpost."

"Oh good," Kara chirps. "You can keep my other notch company. He was starting to get lonely."

"Oh my god," Lena says, like she's actually shocked at that. "Please tell me you're joking. You're too cute for a single notch. You should at least have three from college."

"Who wants to have sex with someone who talks to dead people? It's a harder sell than you'd think."

"Don't tell me you led with that."

"No, I usually whip out the crystal collection," Kara says, "but somewhere around the tarot cards and the candle-lit pentacle on my dorm room floor, they sort of pieced it all together."

Lena laughs again. "Oh, you were that type of weird, then? Sounds like we would've been in the same circles."

"Maybe not," Kara giggles. "I was mostly just in the library. There was a spirit there named Janae that would recommend me all of the good books. Being a necro isn't always so bad."

"Seriously? You're fucking adorable," Lena smiles, and then she pulls Kara in for another kiss.

Kara maybe forgets to remind her they're on a schedule.

*

They arrive at the Council headquarters two hours later, and Kara doesn't even get a chance for them to linger in her jeep. Cat is already waiting for them when they get there.

"Can I at least come with her?" she asks, and surprisingly, she doesn't get yelled at.

"We have to do the initial assessment alone," Cat tells her. "But we will send for you if we need your guidance through an expulsion." Lena reaches over and squeezes Kara's thigh, and Cat sighs as she looks through the window of the jeep. "We've already discussed the significance of saving the city, and it will be considered in the direction we take moving forward. We're not looking to set an example with you, Kara," she says. "We just want to handle this correctly."

"I understand," Kara says. "I just want you to know that I really tried to do this one right."

"We know you're never being malicious," Cat says, "but that's also what scares us."

Right. Kara's had this talk with Alex before. If she doesn't work on controlling her magic, she's just as dangerous as someone who actively uses it in a bad way. "I know," she says to Cat, then to Lena, "I'm sorry I let us get to this super dramatic ending, but I want—"

"It's alright," Lena cuts her off, shifting across the armrest to kiss Kara on the cheek. "It was fun while it lasted, and a lot more than most people get after they die. Tell George and Saffron I said goodbye."

"Yeah, I'll do that," Kara says, cherishing the warmth on her cheek where Lena's lips just were. "I'll see you in there, maybe? But if not, and they go another route, just remember what I said."

"Going straight through, no detours," Lena says. "I mean, maybe one if I see—"

"Lena," Kara laughs. "Straight through the other side. No detours."

"Straight through," Lena promises. "Even if a part of me does want to haunt you. I'll be good."

"I better not feel you when I get home," Kara warns.

"Don't make me make a sex joke in front of your boss," Lena smiles, then she nudges Kara's arm and gets out of the jeep to walk inside the headquarters with Cat.

Kara waits exactly sixty seconds before she's dialing Alex. "So, I turned her in," she starts with. "And before you give me shit, I just want you to know that—"

"It was Lena, right?" Alex asks. "The Andrea thing?"

"Yeah," Kara whispers. "How'd you know?"

"Kelly put it together. They're apparently very public best friends, and she felt super strong energy coming from Lena. She said she wouldn't be surprised if her spirit was even the reason you decided to go for a run."

"Yeah, I did have a lot of random motivation to run that night," Kara reminisces. "Anyway—" she thinks about making a joke about her kissing Lena, and decides she doesn't want to give Alex enough ammo to make fun of her for the next 67 Halloween gatherings. "I sort of miss you, you know? We should hang out."

"Is this weighing on you?" Alex asks. "I figured it might, seemed like you were getting attached."

"Yeah, she was just like, a breath of fresh air," Kara admits. "I think I needed that, as weird as it sounds."

"You needing fresh air?" Alex asks. "That's not weird at all. When's the last time you didn't have nightmares or coop yourself inside all the time? Seriously, I'm asking that."

"Before or after Lena?"

"Did she -" Alex cuts off, and then she's silent on the other end for longer than Kara's comfortable with. "I'm sorry I gave you so much shit," she goes with. "I know powerful spectral energy wards off other energy, and that probably felt nice after being stressed for so long, but I really just wanted to look out for you."

"I know," Kara says, sinking down in her seat and looking out at the parking lot, trying her best not to focus so hard on the door and if it's opening. "I'm not mad at you or anything. I get how I can be frustrating."

"Let me make it up to you?"

"With Kelly's cooking?" Kara asks, and actually manages a smile thinking about that. "There are very few things that her cooking can't fix, to be honest. Even losing what felt like a great friend."

Alex laughs. "It's funny because Kelly said she was sort of an asshole."

"She was a total sociopath, classic corpse energy. But she also looked out for me, and that was nice."

"Are they letting you in with her?"

"Nah," Kara sighs. "Not yet, at least. Said they'd call me in for the expulsion."

"Need company?"

"Please," Kara smiles, and then she puts her phone on speaker and tells Alex about taking a corpse on a rollercoaster.

*

She sits outside the headquarters until late into the evening, chatting and playing phone games with Alex, until Alex has to go eat dinner and she's stuck waiting alone. She's just about to nod off to sleep when someone taps on the passenger side window. She's so excited to hear from someone that she doesn't even check to see if it's a burglar, just slides it down. To her surprise - it's Lena. "Hey, let me in, it's freezing," she says, and then she climbs into the passenger seat after Kara clicks the locks on the doors.

Kara turns the car on and waits a second for Lena to warm her fingers up, before she says, very slowly, "So, am I being invited in to help with expulsion, or? What's happening?"

"They said I can stay," Lena says simply.

Kara's confused. "You can't just - Lena, you can't just stay. That's against the rules."

"I'm tethered," she informs Kara. "They had me go over an extremely detailed recounting of all of our time together, and they figured it happened somewhere between the amusement park and the kiss."

"You did not," Kara groans.

"Hey, they were very intimidating," Lena defends. "If it helps, I told them I started it."

"It doesn't help," Kara says. "Also, what the heck? They're just letting you stay tethered? They're not going to kill you and take away my magic and like, ban me from the magic society."

"No, stop being dramatic," Lena deadpans. "No one knows I'm dead except my murderer, and they're probably not coming forward. They also think it's important I help catch the person who almost blew an entire city to smithereens, so. You're sort of stuck with me, is what they said. Probationary, of course. Subject to reevaluations."

"They don't want to talk to me?"

"To be fair," Lena says, leaning back in her chair, "they were a little speechless after I told them we made out. Anyway, they told me to go before they changed their minds. I think it was confusion, but also pity."

"Great," Kara says, and finally feels her shock let up enough to register she's... relieved. "We should probably make rules, then. I know you need to stay within a certain distance of me or the magic gets too thin."

"Yeah," Lena hums, closing her eyes and resting her head against the seat. "They said same city, not the same room, so I'll just pay you to be where I am if I travel. Or maybe we'll actually end up dating and you'll do it for free."

"Maybe," Kara says, "but we should discuss that now that it's—"

"Shhh," Lena quiets her. "I just found out I can get my life back. I want to enjoy the moment."

Kara sighs, but she actually caves. It's Lena, after all.  "Fine. Do you at least want ice cream? It can be a celebration."

Lena opens her eyes and smiles. "I always want ice cream. It's a date."

"Yeah, of course," Kara smiles back, rolling her eyes as she starts the car. She waits until they've been on the road a few minutes before she asks, "Are you sure you don't mind being stuck with me?"

Lena reaches over in response and gently squeezes her thigh, letting her hand linger after she does it. Then she blinks at Kara for a second, sighs, and says, "I figured it beats being dead."
