    Your Hand Touching Mine this is how Galaxies Collide

    After speaking with Ms. Grant, Kara let loose her hearing to check on her adopted home world. An emergency with Adélie penguins was louder than anything else and that wasn’t surprising, even Kara didn’t hear something like this every day, superhearing or not. About thirty minutes later, Kara landed discreetly behind the barn deciding to keep her hair down since the ocean breezes had swept through it. She had flown off to rescue those wayward penguins from the coast of Brazil. Apparently, a dangerous storm had carried them far from Antarctica. Instead of joining in the dancing, Kara walked over to a quiet table and sat down. She couldn’t stop thinking about what Miss Grant had said about being her authentic self. 

    “Did you fly off to rescue someone?” Kara looked up to see Lena sitting down across from her, a flirty, but soft smile on her face. 

    “Penguins actually.” Kara didn’t smile, just stared off into the distance. 

    “Really?” The delight was clear in Lena's voice. “What did that entail? Do you rescue animals often?”

    “I do, actually.” Kara met Lena’s eyes now, even if she was not smiling in return. “This was a unique rescue though. There were a swath of penguins that got swept all the way to Brazil due to a severe storm.  I could hear their cries even before I heard the news feeds about what had happened.” Some of Kara’s joy had returned to her eyes while she spoke of the penguins. 

    “You can do that with your hearing?” Lena was striving to hold back the curiosity that was itching in her brain and wanting to dance around in excitement.  

    “Of course, it took a lot of discipline, but yes.” Kara took a small breath, the scent of Lena’s shampoo filling nose. “I got there and hundreds of penguins were trapped and it was too dangerous for rescue teams to get near them, so I retrieved them and flew them back to Antarctica. It’s a gorgeous part of this planet.” Kara had gone back to looking at the field and its vegetation swaying in the gentle breeze, her eyes becoming more distant, a sad wall seeming to fall over them. 

    “Wait, you were in Antarctica and now you’re…” Lena’s voice was full, ready to spill all her excitement and inquiries into the air, she rarely did this, but she’d always felt comfortable doing so around in Kara’s company. 

    Kara cut her off, as if even though she possessed super hearing, she hadn’t heard a word Lena had been saying or the delight she was broadcasting. “I’m supposed to be the strongest person on Earth, but I think I might be the weakest.” 

    “Kara, no, what’s this really about?” Lena’s eyes became concerned immediately, but were clear. 

    “Ms. Grant. My glasses.” Kara paused looking down at her hands, her mind a million light years away. “I think I’m afraid to live my own life. I’m not listening to the advice I gave to others a few weeks ago.” Kara couldn’t seem to stop herself now; the flood gates had been opened almost as if she had been suffering silently for too long. “My entire life I’ve hid behind these glasses they’ve gotten in the way of everything I’ve ever truly wanted to do, every relationship.” Kara paused in her rant to meet Lena’s eyes. 

    Lena rolled her eyes just a bit. 

    “Well, maybe, we have had a bit of a bumpy road, but we’re working on it. And Kara, you're great at your job and living your life. Being sent to the Phantom Zone, that was no small inconvenience, and my brother made you go through it again.” Lena’s voice suddenly got low and dangerous, even as she softly reached her hand out towards Kara. Kara nodded in understanding, her eyes never leaving Lena’s, trying to convey comfort, despite her current disquietude. Kara took a deep breath, “I think hiding who I am might be the reason I couldn’t pass the courage gauntlet.” Kara hesitated and then rested her hand gently over the one Lena had outstretched toward her on the wedding reception table, “I think I created Supergirl that night of the plane crash, because the thought of saving my sister as myself was too terrifying…” Kara looked out into the field, her ramble slowly petering out. 

    There was a fresh and gentle breeze swirling around them. “I’m not sure that’s true, but I can understand why you would think that.” Lena squeezed Kara’s hand from below, she never realized how warm Kara is – something hit her and she raised her eyes seeking out Kara’s. “Maybe it’s not the glasses or, more precisely, Supergirl that are the problem?” 

    “What do you mean?” 

    “Perhaps the problem is that you buried your heritage, you severed yourself from it, diminished what it means to you as an individual.” Kara tilted her head at Lena with curiosity. 

    “I mean I recently found out Lillian programmed the magic out of me as a child - I felt utterly devastated when she told me. Because it dawned on me that I wasn’t able to grow up with full information about myself, to make my own choices, about who I wanted to be. What I wanted to do.” Lena’s eyes dropped down to their joined hands rubbing her thumb over Kara’s. “Lillian telling me the truth, it allowed me to see that I wasn’t here just to protect the Luthor name. In her own way, Lillian finally allowed me to start the journey of breaking free from my life being framed by a last name or anything else.” Lena’s voice grew clearer and more relaxed as she spoke. 

    “You’ve always been Lena to me.” Kara’s quiet statement brokered no argument. Lena met her eyes as something deeper flashed between them. Slowly Kara shifted her gaze out to the field again, thoughts starting to turn in her mind her fingers, unconsciously, tangling with Lena’s. “I don’t know what that would feel like, connecting with someone as my whole self. To not be afraid, to just be who I am.” Kara’s voice was halting, tears lacing her words as her eyes met Lena's. 

    “It’s a process. I’m still figuring it out myself.” Lena gently smiled at Kara, then ventured, “You’re from Krypton, the only person left who remembers it. Have you ever spoken of it? I know a little, but that’s all from Lex or the news about Superman.” Kara tightened her grip on Lena’s hand, the corner of her mouth turning up in a brief smile, she then turned to focus on the gently fluttering trees, after a moment or two, “I’ve been too jahghah,” she whispered. 

    “What?” 

    “I’ve been too jahghah, too restrained. It’s one of the Girod.” Kara’s voice was low as she finally met Lena’s eyes again. 

    “Girod ?” The curiosity was clear in Lena’s voice. Kara stood unable to contain her nervous energy, she tugged on Lena’s hand so they could walk. Their steps fell in sync like breathing in air. 

    “You may be right.” Kara’s eyes were on the dance floor. “Supergirl is not the problem. Supergirl serves a purpose, Supergirl is the light for people, the calm in the storm. I do think I lost track of that coming back from the Phantom Zone.” 

    “If you think about it, Kara, you’ve been struggling with two identities, or hiding one. And then Supergirl is what you can do. What you give the world.” Lena stopped walking so they still had some privacy from the other wedding guests. Kara instinctively turned to face her. “In a way, I just found out I have two heritages, myself, and I’m still trying to figure out who I am. It must have been even harder for you.” 

    “But that’s different, you’re not a Kryptonian, you don’t have…” 

    “Have what… sun gifted abilities, no, but I do have magic. Just because this planet’s yellow sun gives you powers, doesn’t mean you have to hide your heritage from everyone you know.” 

    Kara nodded a little shakily. “You’re right.” Kara took a breath swinging their joined hands a bit, “The Girod are the eleven Kryptonian virtues. Every Kryptonian is taught them at a young age. We are supposed to balance them, and there are key ones, but I think I’ve fallen back too hard on jahghah let myself hide behind its meaning.” 

    Lena could see Kara struggling. “You’re not alone. You don’t have to hide any more. El Mayarah, Kara Zor-El.” 

    Tears started to form in Kara’s eyes again and she gently pulled her glasses off and let words she had been holding back tumble out of her. “Of all the people in my life, you have challenged me the most, pushed me the most.” Lena couldn’t look away from Kara’s glasses free eyes lost in the layers of pain, joy, and kindness in the sharply crystalline otherworldly orbs. After a moment, Kara and Lena moved in to hug one another at the same time both tightly hanging on to one another. 

    “You’ve made me a better person.” Lena said haltingly through tears her head tucked against Kara’s shoulder, it was warm and comforting, Lena wanted to stay right there, but almost immediately the tears started to overwhelm her and Lena broke apart from Kara running to wave goodbye to Alex and Kelly, as they headed out for their honeymoon. 

    Kara hung back for a moment looking down at her glasses and then she tucked them into a hidden pocket in her dress, before she ran forward to join in waving farewell. Later, the reception was still going strong; Kara was standing off to the side while some people kept dancing and others were sitting at tables and chatting. She had found the perfect spot. The sun was bouncing off the trees in the most elegant of ways. Kara felt the sun's rays gently recharging her cells and she almost started to float in the quiet bliss of the moment. Alex and Kelly had left, but the party was far from over and Kara was thankful. It felt nice, a celebration was fitting after what had happened over the last year. 

    “What are you looking at?” Kara had heard Lena walking over, but gave no sign of it. She merely smiled without turning around and pointed to the tips of the trees’ leaves. “The light, it is urvish. It oddly reminds me of Krypton’s moons, the light this time of year. Though the birds make it even more lovely.” 

    “Urvish. I think that means calmness?” Lena answered thoughtfully, the gears in her mind clearly turning. 

    “Almost,” A smile quickly moved across Kara’s face, “your kryptonese is coming along better than you think.” Kara finally turned to look down at Lena, her cerulean eyes meeting emerald green ones. 

    Lena huffed a bit, “Not as fast as I would like. Perhaps there will be more time for lessons now.” Lena paused, then, inquired, “What does it mean? Urvish?” Lena turned her body so she was fully facing Kara. Kara automatically reached up to adjust her glasses and then remembered that she had taken them off. She shifted, crossing her arms in a relaxed manner. 

    “You were close, not calmness, but peace. It is another one of Girod. ” Kara murmured conscious of the party goers around them, even as her eyes twinkled.  

    “It sounds like peace, the word. I like it.” Lena gently smiled trying not to sink into Kara’s intimate space. “I’ve been meaning to ask why the bird houses? I love them, but why?” Lena pointed at one that was resting on a nearby reception table. Kara’s bearing had relaxed even more, her arms still crossed, but her posture was no longer bowed but its normal wide standing tall deportment. 

    “Krypton did not have birds. I’ve always been fascinated by khaoghaos that can fly.” 

    “But you can,” Lena’s voice was full of delight at this piece of information as she also made a zooming motion with her hand. Kara chucked deeply and their bodies moved closer together, “Yes, ironic, I know, but there is something about Rao’s designs, they’re fascinating.” 

    A soft laugh left Lena’s lips, “I can appreciate that, mother nature is a marvel.”

     “Girls, come on, come dance.” Eliza was waving at them, Esme at her side waving even more enthusiastically, from the end of a conga line. 

    “We should join,” Kara grinned and firmly took Lena’s hand, dragging her toward the fun. 

     

    A few weeks later, everyone was slowly making their way out of Kara’s apartment. The Danvers the last to leave, Esme leaping one more time into Kara’s arms to be zoomed around the room while Kara floated. It was one of Esme’s favorite activities. “Be good bud,” Kara kissed Esme on her forehead then put her down. Following her and closing the door after waving goodnight to her family one more time. When she turned Lena was cleaning up the snack bowls and glasses in the kitchen. Kara moved to start putting all the board games away. The energy in the room was cozy, comfortable, and calm. 

    “What types of games did you have on Krypton?” Kara looked up from putting the Settlers of Catan pieces and cards back in the box. 

    “We had one called Zuglahs, that was close to this one, but much more complicated.” Lena had dried her hands off, gotten two more glasses of wine, and sat on the couch next to Kara. She held out one of the glasses in offering, Kara took it with a small nod. “Did you also have something like chess? You’re very good at that game as well?” Len asked as she took a sip of wine. 

    “We did, but it was almost more strategic. Almost three dimensional. I think you would like it.” 

    “Maybe I can find some information at the Fortress of Solitude.” Lena's mind started to get lost in how she could begin the research.

    “That would be wonderful.” Kara replied as she fell back into the couch, taking a deep sip from her own glass of wine, lost in thought.  After a few minutes of comfortable silence, Kara's joyous tone broke it with, “There was also this orb, givel, if you knew how to get it in the right position, like a Rubik’s cube, it would show you galaxies, Krypton was in there.” 

    Kara’s excitement was infectious and Lena couldn’t help but reach out to rest her hand on Kara’s knee, “Do you know where Krypton would be in Earth’s sky?” Lena was worried she had gone too far, that she had ruined the mood, but Kara just leaned toward her, a light in her eyes, one that Lena had not seen in a long time. 

    “I’ll take you to my favorite star gazing spot and show you.” Kara’s eyes were emitting a soft glow around the edges, but Lena felt nothing but comfort and safety, as if they were always meant to be here, in this moment.

    Lena took another sip of wine, her eyes never breaking contact with Kara's, and smiled, “I would love that.” 

    “I’ll have to fly us there and you’ll definitely need a jacket.” Kara was grinning, even as she floated just a little above the couch.
