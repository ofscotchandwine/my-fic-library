    She’s already cried for their lost culture. She’s already cried for the friends she’s never going to see again. She’s already cried for the life she’ll never get to have. And while Kara’s grief and pain had formed into an ache in her heart she uses to remember Krypton by, her father is still wishing that ache will transform into all he’s lost— he’s still wishing all his work will give them Krypton back. 

     

    Kara’s no longer sure she wants it back. Not while she spent years working to make Earth her home. 

     

    “There are more ways to keep Krypton alive than… forcing me into being someone I no longer am,” she whispers as a last confession and hopes her father hears it as it is— a plea. 

     

    She’s too scared to look at him but when she does from the corner of her eyes, she sees him nodding, quietly and solemnly. 

     

    “I’ll see what I can do,” he answers and just like that, he leaves.  

     

     

    While no one had asked, only thrown excessively worried looks her way, they had all decided— consciously or not— to stand by Kara, like a shield of protection all around her, when Zor-El came back. 

     

    No one said anything; they all just looked at him. Nia, with confusion and hostility. Brainy and J’onn, with a new found mistrust. Kelly, with indignation. And Alex and Lena? They were looking at him with straight up wrath in their eyes. Alex looks seconds away from either punching him or screaming at him until her lungs give out. 

     

    Zor-El clears his throat, sees the animosity against him in the room, yet speaks as if it’s not there at all— a trick Kara saw plenty of times when she was young and accompanied him to his meetings. 

     

    “I spoke to your mother and the council,” he says, looking directly at Kara. He waits for her to answer but she can’t. She doesn’t want to. She doesn’t have the energy to end up fighting now, after the conversation just started. It had taken two hours for her to leave the balcony yesterday and find Alex waiting for her with chinese food— it had taken two more for Kara to feel somewhat better. 

     

    “We’ve come to the conclusion that since you’ve spent so much time here on Earth,” he continues in the silence, “It’d be unfair to only give you kryptonians as an option, although that would be preferable. And since your cousin has chosen a human as his mate, you can do so as well.”

     

    Everyone’s shoulders drop, relaxing. But Kara’s don’t. She knew it couldn’t be that easy— her father wouldn’t give up that easy. Her mother had never relented once in her life. The council would never change their minds, not fully. 

     

    “So long you bring them to Argo city in a month, you’ll be excluded from a typical Kryptonian courting,” he explains. He raises his hand when everyone starts to complain, speaking over each other just to try and get Zor-El to change his mind. He still doesn’t look at any of them and Kara sees just the tiniest shade of regret in his eyes. “It’s the best I could do,” he says. 

     

    “You did this!” Alex yells, voicing what Kara is too stunned to do, “You wouldn’t have had to ask for anything if you hadn’t started this.”

     

    “I’m only concern about my daughter’s future—”

     

    “—oh, fuck you,” Kara snaps. Everyone in the room stills. “I’ve spent half my life without you, all because of your choices back on Krypton. You’ve got no right to show up now and ruin the life I’ve made for myself.”

     

    “Ruin your life?” he scoffs. “You live alone! You live and sacrifice for a world full of unintelligent idiots and work a job that a child could have done on Krypton! Your life on Earth is nothing but a weak attempt to replace Krypton."

     

    “Earth is my home,” she says, nostrils flaring. Each one of her father’s words angering her even more. She feels the corner of her eyes burn, but she cannot tell if it’s from tears or heat vision. “I’m happy here. It’s too late for me to be the same Kara Zor-El you sent in that pod.”

     

    “You’re not living up to your full potential, the house of El potential,” he pushes, relentlessly. And Kara knows nothing will stop him. But she’s been too restless, too angry and anxious, since the Phantom Zone and maybe, this is exactly what she needs. A fight. A way to let out every bit of hurt towards her father the same way she once did against red tornado.

     

    “Maybe I don’t want to live up to the potential of the house of El when my father is a genocidal maniac and my mother a careless imprisoner!”

     

    Alex is the only one that’s not too shocked about her words. She has, after all, heard more than one rant from Kara about all this. But no one else had heard her thoughts on this. No one had seen hopeful, kind, cheerful Kara talk back and scream to someone she loves with so much anger. Except maybe, Lena— who had, unfortunately, been on the receiving end of it. So that’s why, while Alex stays motionless in place, Lena’s the only one who’s brave, or careless, enough to step between two livid kryptonians. 

     

    “Maybe… Maybe let’s take a break, okay?” She says, her green eyes looking at Kara, begging her to relax. Her hand is extended but she isn’t touching her, even when she knows that would relax Kara— the simple gesture makes her realize just how angry she must look. She breathes in and out, following Lena’s lead. “That’s it, darling. Just calm down a little.” 

     

    She focuses on Lena, her hand reaches for Kara but still doesn’t touch her. So, Kara closes the distance and gently intertwines their fingers together, careful to not accidentally break Lena’s. 

     

    In the background, she somewhat hears J’onn leading Zor-El away, telling him to go back to Kara’s apartment and stay there. In the background, she hears Alex curse and swear at him— and Kara knows, she knows, Alex is somehow already blaming herself for not seeing this coming, for not protecting Kara, as if anyone could have seen this coming. 

     

    But instead she decides to focus on Lena and the way she tugs Kara’s hand until she’s in her personal space. She wraps her arms around Lena, lets her head hang and fall into her shoulder and breath in her lavender perfume. 

     

    “We’ll figure it out,” Lena says, rubbing her hand up and down Kara’s back.

     

    “We have a month,” Nia says, when Kara finally pulls away from Lena’s embrace with a thankful look. Nia sounds hopeful, manages to smile a little even when her eyes still have a tint of worry in them. “I’m sure we can find a loophole.”

     

    Those turn out to be some famous last words. 

     

    Kara spends the first few days trying to get the remnants of anger out of her body, by punching concrete until it’s nothing but dust and by letting Esme braid her hair and do her make up— her niece brushing away her anger with every laugh she got out of Kara. 

     

    But once she walks into the tower, her brand new and kryptonian problem buried under a disney marathon and popcorn, she sees Brainy and Alex waiting for her, and she deflates. She stops her own whistling and sighs. 

     

    “What’s wrong?”

     

    “Nothing’s wrong,” Alex says, leaning her hip against the table and motions for Kara to look at the screens above Brainy. “We might have figured out how to get you out of that marriage.”

     

    Brainy’s fingers move through the keyboard and images show up in all three screens. “Since they want you to get married, we can just tell them you already are,” he explains. “We curated a digital fake persona that you can show to the council.”

     

    Kara looks at the pictures. In the first one, a man with blue eyes and brown hair. He’s wearing a blue suit. His smile is charming and his teeth are perfectly aligned. He’s so typically handsome the first thing Kara thinks is that he looks boring. 

     

    “Considering your father goes along with this,” Alex says, annoyance at Zor-El more than obvious in her voice. “We can just pretend John Smith is your husband.”

     

    “John Smith?” Kara chuckles, “Really? Who even is he?” 

     

    “He doesn’t exist,” Brainy explains. “His face is created by an algorithm using the physical traits humans consider most attractive. His birth certificate and all his school records will be uploaded to their respective archives once you agree to this.”

     

    She checks the rest of the pictures more carefully. The Harvard Law diploma with his name and a fake signature scribbled on it. The social security number and medical records where it shows John has a peanut allergy. The marriage certificate that says Kara Danvers & John Smith in bold cursive letters. 

     

    “We thought a lawyer would be most adequate,” Brainy keeps going, “considering socio-political changes and disagreements in Argo City, you being married to someone who has experience in that regard might help them get used to the idea.”

     

    “Except he doesn’t exist,” Kara says, deflated.

     

    “It’s the best idea we had, Kara,” Alex says, and even as she says that Kara can tell Alex is not fully convinced of her own idea. She sounds as equally hopeless as Kara must look. “Do you think it’ll work?” 

     

    Kara pulls a chair from under the table and throws herself on it, her fingers trying to rub away the crinkle in her forehead. “No,” she sighs. “They’ll most likely want to meet him. Maybe they’ll let it pass the first time but I’ll have to show up with him eventually.” 

     

    Alex and Brainy nod in response. Brainy doesn’t need any more words to know he needs to scrap the entire idea. With only the pressing of a few keys, it all disappears from the screens— along with Alex’s hope of having found a solution. 

     

    Kara waits, hopeful and foolishly, that her father will approach her and tell her he had figured it all out. He’d tell her he was sorry and that she can live her life however she pleases. But her father hasn’t spoken to her since their last argument and probably won’t speak to her until they’re both standing in front of the portal that’ll take them to Argo City in two weeks. 

     

    So, while Kara’s hope is focused on the problem being magically solved by her father’s regret or the council’s forgetfulness, her friends try to find another solution. 

     

    Kelly gently mentions that maybe she should try and “get out there” and “find someone” during game night, and even as she says it, Kara can hear the uncertainty in her voice. 

     

    “And tell them what, exactly?” Kara laughs before she sits up with her back straight and pretends to be talking to a date. “Hello! I’m Kara. I know we just met but I’m an alien and I need to get married in like, a week. What do you say?” 

     

    Kelly chuckles along with Kara once she’s done but the small squeeze on her shoulder tells Kara just how much Kelly wants to help her solve this situation. The wetness in her eyes tells her just how sad she is for Kara, too. 

     

    She really does consider it when, a couple days later while they’re having lunch on the tower, Nia asks her just how bad it’d be if she never shows up. “They can’t marry you off if you aren’t there to say yes, right?” she asks, then looks at Lena’s and Alex’s stunned expressions and shrugs. “What? It’s just a thought.”

     

    “While that’s the most tempting option we have so far,” Kara says, ignoring the three sour looks sent her way. “I don’t think getting myself blacklisted from ever going to Argo again is the best idea.”

     

    She considers it, though. She really does. She wonders if she can really live the rest of her life without setting foot on Argo again, the last piece of her home. Up until a few years ago, she wouldn’t even have had to think about this— Krypton was gone. Argo was gone. She had no choice but to live without it. 

     

    So could she really choose to give it up when her heart had filled with warmth and hope the second she had seen Argo City again, floating through space?

     

    She doesn’t think she can.

     

    “I figured it out!” Alex walks into the guestroom of her own home, the same place Kara has recently spent nights and nights, trying to figure out how they can stop this madness, these endless, sleepless nights with no solution in sight. But Alex walks in with firm steps and hopeful eyes so, maybe. Maybe. “You need to bond houses right?” she asks but doesn’t wait for an answer. “It can be any house, right? Even already bonded ones.”

     

    Kara sighs, sitting up in the bed and pulling her knees to her chest. “Alex, I don’t think that—”

     

    “—Hear me out, okay?” she says hopeful, so hopeful, Kara can only let her get it out of her system. “You said your friend Thara used to be one of your probable matches back on Krypton. And she was glad to see you when you went to Argo.”

     

    “Thara’s already married,” she sighs. 

     

    “I know. I know,” Alex says, relentlessly. “I remember you once told me that back on Krypton some people didn’t get married for love, just to join houses or because of a business proposal, right? It was one of those fights we had when we were kids— you saying it was fine, me telling you marriage should be more than that.”

     

    Kara nods, she had told Alex that humans were expecting too much— not everyone fell in love. She knew her uncle Jor-El and aunt Lara were one of the few kryptonian couples who married for love, her grandfather had thrown a commotion when he found out that his son had chosen someone with a name of much lower status than an El. But her father had told her, even back then when she was a child, that Jor-El and Lara were an exception— a subtle warning to a ten year old to not go around falling in love. 

     

    “In that discussion, you also said some people had more than one partner,” Alex continues, not even trying to give Kara a chance to talk, “a throuple. Sometimes they got bonded all at the same time and sometimes someone got bonded later on to a couple. Part of that throuple was platonic, part of it was just business. You could do that,” she adds, when she sees the defeat on her sister’s face.

     

    “It wouldn’t work,” Kara sighs. “Not only were those bondings big exceptions on Krypton, my parents want heirs— heirs means first born, pure house of El heirs.”

     

    “So?”

     

    “Thara is already married. Even if all three of us agree to that, which I doubt she’d even want to,” Kara adds, “the first born child would biologically be Thara’s and her husband’s because they got bonded first. It wouldn’t be mine. The council is going through all this trouble to get a new and strong house of El.”

     

    Kara hides her face in between her legs— the uncertainty, anger and loneliness from this conversation instantly makes its way into her eyes, burning them with tears she refuses to let out, yet fails to do so.

     

    “But the second child being the heir is better than nothing, right?” Alex says, even if now her voice sounds uncertain— as if she knows she’s fighting a losing battle. 

     

    “The council is already making an exception for me, allowing me to get bonded to a human. Bringing up the possibility of a three-way marriage would be like laughing in the face of Rao.”

     

    “Kara, come on,” she grabs Kara’s ankle, pulling at it until her legs are extended again, leaving her face out in the open, the tears in her cheeks now visible for Alex to wipe away. “This is the best you can do. Would you rather be married to a stranger?”

     

    “Of course not,” she chokes out. She breathes in and out a couple of times then explains. “But Alex—that would put me and Thara and her husband in such a difficult position. I haven’t talked to her in ages, I barely know who she is. She barely knows who I am. She nor her husband would say yes. And—”

     

    “And?” Alex asks, gently. Knowing kindness is all Kara needs right now.

     

    And what if I ever do find someone I want to be bonded to? Someone I love?

     

    And what if I ever find someone that fills the loneliness in my heart with their presence? What if I finally confess to the one person that does so right now? What if I ever want to be bonded to her?

     

    “And it’d be just too damn weird,” she says instead. Alex chuckles. 

     

    “Fuck. Yeah I know,” she rubs her forehead then her own tired eyes. “I don’t know how to save you from this, Kara. I’m sorry.”

     

    “There’s nothing to apologize for,” Kara says, pulling her into a hug. She whispers to Alex but mostly to herself, “We’ll figure it out.”

     

    Three days before her trip to Argo, with a perfectly working portal, an anxious father and still no solutions to the marriage problem, Kara is more than tempted to fry the portal with her laser vision and buy herself a few days— she’s only stopped by the stern look J’onn sends her way when he caught her looking at it with a fiery trinkle in her eyes. 

     

    She wishes a sudden emergency would show up, distract her for the rest of the day. Anything. A small fire. A car accident. Something that would make her stop thinking about the future that awaits her in Argo, anything that would help her think about what to do. 

     

    She looks at the National City skyline and mentally screams for a solution.

     

    “Hey, you,” Lena says, quietly as not to startle Kara as she stands on the balcony next to her. “How are you feeling?”

     

    “I’m— I—” I’m fine. I’m okay. It’ll get better. She wants to say that. She really does. But one look into Lena’s eyes, her kind and understanding gaze, and she sees that Lena already knows how badly she’s feeling. Because if there’s someone in this world that always manages to understand her, expecting nothing in return, it’s Lena. 

     

    Kara chokes on her own words and Lena answers by wrapping her arms around her— Kara wishes she could stay in the safety and simplicity of Lena’s fingers playing with the tips of her hair. 

     

    “Did Alex’s idea not work?” Lena whispers, her breath hits the side of Kara’s neck and she has to suppress a shiver running down her spine. 

     

    “It’d be unfair to Thara,” she shakes her head. She pulls away from the hug but doesn’t leave Lena’s personal space, her hands fall to the woman’s hip. “She already has a family.”

     

    “So, if she didn’t have a family, you’d have considered the platonic bonding?” Lena asks, an edge in her voice Kara can’t place. 

     

    “I guess,” she answers, the crinkle showing up on her forehead. 

     

    “Because—” Lena’s heartbeat picks up. Her breathing goes shallow. “Because you could… Marry me.”

     

    “What—”

     

    “Marry me instead of a stranger,” Lena says, quickly, pulling away from Kara’s orbit. Her hands play with each other, nervous, and Kara just wants to grab them until they relax under hers. “Or your long lost married friend,” Lena makes a face at the thought and Kara almost forgets her first words when she chuckles. 

     

    But she can’t forget Lena’s words. Because the words “marry me” left Lena Luthor’s lips. And the words were directed at her .

     

    Lena in a white dress walking straight towards Kara is something that has always been in the back of her head but having that idea get closer and closer to reality is overwhelming, in a way.

     

    “Lena, that’s—” she doesn’t know what that is, exactly. Insane? Stupid? Perfect? All that Kara has ever wanted? Except when she imagined it, she thought of years of pining and relaxing dates and confessions in the dark of the night. She never imagined necessity and being forced by her parents. “Why— why are you offering to do that?” 

     

    Kara didn’t even know if she could really do it like this herself. Could she really gamble on losing Lena again? Is it even ethical to marry your best friend when she doesn’t know you’re in love with her?

     

    She had of course thought about this possibility during the past month, but given all these concerns, Kara pushed the idea to the corners of her mind and pretended it wasn’t there at all. 

     

    So, she is surprised when Lena bites her bottom lip, softly pulling at the skin and brushing off her lipstick. “It makes sense, I guess,” she says, eventually. Her eyes avoiding Kara’s. “I hate the idea of you being forced into a marriage as much as you do. You’re my best friend. I want to help.”

     

    You’re my best friend. 

     

    She is her best friend. Isn’t that what marriage is supposed to be, by human standards?

     

    It’d work, in a way. Lena is a scientist, a philanthropist, a business woman— the council would have no complaints about her. Lena is kind, quick on her feet and has a dry humor that her parents would love. Lena is gorgeous, nurturing with Esme and gentle to every kid they have ever encountered on the street— It’s so easy for Kara to imagine her running around with kids that look exactly like her. 

     

    She’s Kara’s best friend. In a way, there’s no one better for her to do this with. But. But. 

     

    “Lena,” she exhales, “I can’t ask you to do this.”

     

    “I’m offering,” she answers. She breathes in and out and with a sadness Kara has only heard when she found Lena drunk and sad so many years ago, she says, “No one was going to marry me anyway—”

     

    “—Lena,” she whispers but Lena just shakes her head, a silent plea to let her finish. 

     

    “There’s no point in denying it. No one touches a Luthor with a ten-foot pole,” she chuckles, sadly. “And if I’m going to get married, I’d be glad that it’s with you.”

     

    “I'm glad if it's you, too," Kara says. Her heart feels like it’ll beat out of her chest any second, much more so as her hand finds Lena’s and squeezes. Lena looks up from their hands. It feels monumental, somehow, this one moment. Like her heart knows it’s a fixed moment in her life even if her mind hasn’t caught up to it yet. 

     

    Lena smiles once she realizes what her answer means. She squeezes Kara’s hand again. And Kara feels like she should kiss her, engagements usually end in kisses, right? But then again, wanting to kiss Lena is not a foreign thought in her mind so she pushes it away.  

     

    "Being your friend was always going to be forever,” Lena says, a confession only for the two of them. “We can even pretend those bracelets are friendship bracelets."

     

    “Of course,” Kara chuckles, even as part of her heart stings at the words. “Our own very expensive and very beautiful friendship bracelets.” 

     

    She pulls Lena to her again, back into the arms she seems to be made for and delights in the feeling of her warmth against her— Kara’s known coldness, Kara’s known loneliness but she also knows the way Lena Luthor wraps herself around her and makes both things disappear. 

     

    “Thank you,” she whispers, kissing the crown of her head. 

     

    “What are friends for?” Lena whispers back, before she leaves a small kiss on the corner of Kara’s jaw.

     

     

    There’s something akin to curiosity in her father’s eyes when she shows up with Lena beside her to walk through the portal. But even if that thing in his eyes is curiosity, disgust or plain indifference, Zor-El doesn’t greet them as they arrive.

     

    She can hear the drumming of Lena’s heart as she nods in his direction, hears as it goes faster when she finally, anxiously, looks to the portal— Kara knows it’s not an issue of safety. Lena’s not scared the portal will suddenly destabilize and blow up. Lena’s not scared the portal will malfunction and send them to the wrong place.

     

    Lena’s afraid of what lies behind the portal and what they’ll have to do when they get there. 

     

    “Are you sure about this?” Kara asks her, leaning closer to her. Her hand finding Lena’s shaky hand and squeezing it. “You can still change your mind. There’s no bracelet on your wrist yet.”

     

    Lena gives her a smile, her free hand flawlessly pulling a loose strand of hair behind her ear— the one strand Kara had been thinking of playing with her own fingers since the moment she saw it. “Of course I’m sure,” she says, “I wouldn’t have asked you to marry me if I wasn’t sure.”

     

    “Okay. Okay,” Kara breathes in and out. The portal roars to life when Brainy presses a few buttons on the other side of the room. Kara grabs hers and Lena’s bags in one hand, the other tightly still holding onto Lena’s hand. “Okay.”

     

    “Are you sure?” Lena asks, eyes carefully observing her. 

     

    “I am,” she nods. She doesn’t lie but she doesn’t say the full truth either, “I just— I haven’t seen my mother in a while. That’s all.”

     

    “Shall we?” Zor-El doesn’t wait for an answer before he walks into the portal, in full kryptonian regalia and with his head high.

     

    It’s a strange feeling. It always is. Whether it’s moving from one place to the other, one planet to the other, one dimension to the other, the disorientation and dizziness is always there. So, as soon as she comes to her senses, her hand drops the bags and goes to Lena’s waist, holding onto her as Lena tries not to fall with her uneven steps. 

     

    As she helps Lena, she inadvertently pulls her towards her. Lena’s chest bumps against hers, her hands fall to Kara’s shoulders. They end up face to face, with only centimeters and tension in between them. 

     

    “You okay?” Lena’s eyes drop to Kara’s lips when she whispers the question. 

     

    Lena doesn’t manage to say anything because the clear sound of Alura clearing her throat makes them jump away from each other— Kara’s cheeks burn red and it’s not because of the sun that Argo orbits. 

     

    “Kara, bezhgam .” Alura greets her daughter with a hug. Lena sees the lines of worry wash away from Kara’s face as she hugs her mother, lines that return as soon as Alura sets her eyes on Lena. “Welcome,” she repeats, not unkindly but not warmly either. “It is good to see you again.”

     

    “It’s good to see you, too,” Lena answers, extending her hand to shake Alura’s. The woman, so much like Kara in her blue eyes and round cheeks, does not reciprocate, and Lena has to awkwardly put her hand down when Kara whispers in her direction that kryptonians do not do that. “Thank you for receiving me in your home,” she clears her throat. 

     

    “You and Kara will not be staying here,” she says.

     

    “We’re not?” Kara asks, confused. “I’m sure we’ll both fit in my bedroom.”

     

    Alura’s lips twitch and she presses them into a thin line, it might have been years since Kara had lived with her mother but she can still see the signs of irritation on her like when she was a little child. 

     

    “We made arrangements for the house next door to be given to you,” she explains. “If you get married you might want to have your privacy, I suspect.”

     

    “If?” 

     

    “There’s plenty to talk about, Kara dear,” she says, placing her hand on Kara’s cheek. She pulls away after a few seconds and looks down to their clothes, disapprovingly. “We have a meeting with the council as soon as you arrive. I'd recommend changing your clothes for it.”

     

    The house is nothing but a typical kryptonian household, one of the many empty cubicles that are given to any newlywed couple— created by the technicians to be as effective as possible with its white empty walls reinforced by desert rocks. 

     

    “It’s different from your parents' home,” Lena mentions, curiously, as she looks around.

     

    “Yeah. Any couple updates their houses as they contribute to society,” Kara explains, sitting down on the white round couch in the middle of the room, placing the clothes that were above it on her thighs. “Wood, paintings, plants, are usually gifts by the council when someone makes an improvement to Krypton.” 

     

    Lena frowns, she walks around slowly, picking up the black pants and jackets on the other side of the room, touching its fabric curiously. “It’s weird,” Lena says, when Kara finds her eyes and tilts her head, a silent what’s wrong? going between them. “It’s not how I imagined your city. It’s so colorless… so different from you.”

     

    Kara chuckles, biting her lip as her hand runs up and down her own clothes. “It was one of the first things I loved about Earth. Humans try to make everything so colorful, so cheerful. I guess it’s one of the first things I picked up.”

     

    Lena smiles at her, “Well, I think color suits you. At least you got blue here.”

     

    Lena points to the fabric on Kara’s lap and raises the black ones in her hands. Kara nods, “Greys and blacks are usually given to low rank citizens. Since we aren’t married yet you can’t use the House of El colors.”

     

    “And the gold?” Lena asks, referring to the several lines and stars along Kara’s long jacket.

     

    “They’re kehlish ,” Kara explains, excitedly putting it on. The coat reaches her legs and fits her perfectly. Every star shines on the dark blue as if it’s a galaxy and Lena cannot find anything more perfect to be wrapped around Supergirl. “Honors. They’re given to you after a worthy deed.”

     

    “Is that what the lines in your father’s blazer are?” 

     

    “Most are because of his experiments and inventions on the Science Guild.” Kara nods, clenching her jaw—  he got rewards for Myriad, a genocidal machine, and who knows what else. She remembers being a little girl, looking up to her father and his twenty golden lines, and wondering how many she’ll ever get.     “Why are you asking this?” Kara asks, interrupting whatever Lena was going to say. “You do not get to start this and be the one to create doubt in the middle of it.”

     

    Alura sighs, almost tiredly. “I do not want you to make a mistake, Kara.”

     

    “It is rushed, yes,” Lena says. She looks at Kara and smiles, “but we were going to end up here eventually, right? What’s the harm in doing it now instead of in a few years?”

     

    Kara wants to hear the truth in her words. Partly, for Kara, it is true. She wants to believe she would have one day been courageous enough to tell Lena she loves her, tell her she wants to be with her in any way she can, to tell her she wants to love her like a lover and not a friend. But Lena is pretending, she’s keeping up the lie that they really are together— even in front of Alura. So her words, a complete fantasy Kara gets to live, are nothing but Lena’s excellent ability to lie on command and deceive flawlessly.

     

    “Lena’s not a mistake,” Kara says, when Alura nods at Lena’s words but keeps looking at her daughter. “She’s never going to be.”

     

    “We’re always going to be together, one way or another,” Lena says. “Kara has her powers on earth and her duties to the world but my duty always is Kara, protecting her and making sure she returns home safe. It’s always going to be like that, whether we get married here on Argo or not. So, what I’m saying is… it’s not a ruse. I’ll stand by Kara, in Rao’s light and beyond.”

     

    At Lena’s words, something shifts in Alura. She uncrosses her arms, she breathes out once and her eyes lose part of the distrust they were carrying. She looks at Lena for a minute without actually looking at her, as if she’s lost in thought somewhere else. And then, she nods. 

     

    “Okay,” she says. She picks up a book from the table behind her and gives it to Lena. “We should start with the basics.”

     

    Alura goes deep into their history, starting with the so-called Golden Age of Krypton, in its beginning, when it was only ruled by the Science Council— everyone was below them, from Krypton Security Forces to the Krypton Bureau of Investigation, and it had been those groups that had later split up to create other guilds; the Religious Guild, the Politics Guild, later divided into the Lawmakers and Military Guilds, and the Housing Guild, later divided into the Artisans and Technicians Guild. 

     

    All guilds ended up having equal power on the council and, in turn, over Krypton. 

     

    “For decades, once the guilds were established,” Alura explained, “the Military Guild apprehended the criminals and, along with the Lawmakers Guild, they’d make those same criminals stand trial. If they were found guilty, they’d be exiled inside the prison satellites and they’d be cleansed of criminal tendencies, once that was done, they’d return to Krypton.” 

     

    “That took a hundred years,” Kara clarified, “it wasn’t as simple as she makes it sound.”

     

    “Nothing ever is,” Alura says, exasperated, “but yes, it was a long process.”

     

    Lena nods, taking the information in. “I thought criminals went to Fort Rozz in the Phantom Zone,” she says, slowly, knowing it’s a sore subject for Kara, who straightens at the name, “isn’t that how some… criminals ended up on Earth?”

     

    “Jor-El discovered the Phantom Zone when we were young,” Alura confirms, “And I created Fort Rozz along with Zor-El. The collaboration got us real recognition on Krypton. This was decades before Kara was born, decades before we knew Krypton was going to collapse.

     

    “But isn’t it the same thing?” Lena asks, furrowing her brows, “A hundred years on a prison satellite or a hundred years in the Phantom Zone?” 

     

    “It wasn’t designed to be the same,” Alura says, regretfully. “Fort Rozz was designed to be a rehabilitation center and at first, it was. The crimes were minimal and the sentences were one or two years. Every criminal would be filled with regret and begging to return during their audiences. When Jor-El and Zor-El discovered Krypton was unstable, we weren’t the only ones. Revolutionaries’ crimes got bigger and more violent and with that, the sentences became longer, until we weren’t even thinking before banishing people for life on Fort Rozz. It was a mistake on our part. A lot of things were…” 

     

    It’s hard to imagine her mother as a young hopeful woman, someone who once tried to change things instead of enforcing old norms. Kara wonders if Alura ever feels like her— divided in two; who she was and who she is. Or if she always stays somewhat in the middle, knowing she does her best, without all of the guilt Kara always carries with her. 

     

    Kara remembers looking up at the sky and thinking her mother had once promised to save Krypton and hating her for failing. Hating that she wasn’t in front of Kara giving her explanations and telling her her reasons as to why she did what she did. Why did she send Kara away? Why didn’t she come with her to Earth? Why didn’t she look for her? Now her mother is in front of her and she’s not sure she even wants the answers.

     

    Kara knows it must hurt Alura to explain as much as it hurts Kara to ask. And so, the questions and the answers find themselves in a stalemate and no one ever moves. 

     

    Once Lena is done, Kara walks the steps towards her mother and gives her a hug. Alura’s shoulders untense as much as Kara’s do. 

     

     

    Kara’s been hated for her choices before. 

     

    She’s despised by the police— always complaining she doesn’t follow their orders or respect their authority. 

     

    She’s resented by the government because she breaks too many windows and too many walls and because she always takes too long to apprehend the angry aliens twice her size. 

     

    She was loathed by her classmates in high school, she was always too shy, too weird, too friendly with the resident nerd, too alien . 

     

    She was resented, if only for a little while, by Alex and J’onn for coming out as Supergirl. 

     

    And she knows she was, for longer than she wants to ever think about, hated by Lena because she kept her identity from her. 

     

    Beside the fact that she’s an alien and there’s humans that will hate her for it, no matter how much good she does, she’s always been hated for choices that are purely her own. 

     

    She sat beside Kenny every day, for weeks, because he was kind and made her smile, no matter how many bullies added Kara to their nerds list. 

     

    She decided to save Alex’s plane knowing nothing would ever be the same.

     

    She flies into robberies and hostage situations as fast as she can because she doesn’t want to entertain the thought of being too late, not when she has superspeed. 

     

    She lets herself be thrown into buildings’ walls and squares’ fountains because as long as an enemy’s attention is on her, it’s not on the humans around her and they can safely run away. 

     

    She kept Lena from her secret because she wanted her best friend, because she was too afraid to ever lose her and so, she kept lying, deliberately and constantly. 

     

    And when the hate came, she took it in stride because she knew she did what she thought was right, even when it blew up on her face. 

     

    Had it been the same for her parents? Had they done what they could when they failed to save Krypton? 

     

    Kara always believed that thinking had been born out of being raised on a foreign planet and after an extraordinary amount of Supergirl missions. But does it turn out it was always her mother’s personality? That small of her she thought she had learned, was it kryptonian all along?

     

    “Kara?” Lena asks, quietly. The bed dips as she sits down on the other side of it. They had continued sharing the bed, without discussion and without complaints since that one night, and now, with Alex sleeping on the living room couch, it made more sense than ever that they’d share again. “Are you okay? You barely ate tonight.”

     

    Lena’s hand reaches closer to hers but it stops itself halfway through the bed, closing in a fist. Instead, she lets her other hand reach forward and places a small cake, a piece of the rainbow cake Lena had bought on the market on their way back home, in the middle of the bed. Kara smiles at it, small and somewhat sad.

     

    “I’m alright,” she says, “just thinking.”

     

    “I know I’ll pass three trials,” Lena says, slowly, “but… I guess there’s still time to change your mind.”

     

    Kara’s head snaps up. “You’ve changed your mind?”

     

    There’s worry in her voice. There’s fear. And, most of all, there’s pain.

     

    “I guess I think part of what your mother says is true,” Lena says, avoiding her eyes. “You shouldn’t make a mistake, not on me.”

     

    “Did you change your mind?” Kara repeats, her eyes desperately trying to find Lena’s, to see what she finds in there and figure out what Lena is feeling. 

     

    “No,” Lena says, Kara sighs, relieved, at it. “Still. I— I failed. On the technician's trial, I failed.”

     

    “Lena,” she breathes out. She smiles a little, at how adorable Lena’s worrying can sometimes be. “That doesn’t matter. It’ll never matter. You could fail all of them and I’d still want to marry you.”

     

    Lena lets out something akin to a giggle, she bites her lip trying to keep from smiling fully and lowers her head but Kara catches sight of a blush on her cheeks anyway. Lena finds her eyes again, tilting her head— Kara knows she’s probably trying to think what to say next, or how to say it.

     

    “Then what's wrong?” she asks. “You’re quiet. You’ve been quieter since we got here. I thought it was because maybe you realized this was all a mistake but—”

     

    Kara shakes her head, breathing heavily. “I— I don’t think I ever thought of my parents as people who made a mistake,” Kara says, biting her bottom lip. “It was a big mistake. Or maybe a sum of small ones that ended up in Krypton’s destruction but— I never thought about it like that.”

     

    “We usually think the best of the people we love,” Lena says, moving over on the bed and mirroring Kara’s position— head and back against the headboard of the bed and legs extended on the mattress. 

     

    “I know,” she sighs. “I don’t think I ever wanted to think of them like that. At first, they were lost heroes, they could do no wrong because they were dead. And dead people couldn’t make any more mistakes. Then, I found out about their mistakes and all I could feel was anger and betrayal and nothing else. I felt like those great people that I loved and used to love me were just… a lie. And now, they’re both here. And maybe the real thing is somewhere in between all of that.”

     

    Kara ends the rant breathless, a beating in her heart that becomes more painful the longer it stays there. The tears well up in her eyes, burning and hurting like acid before they start to fall like a rainstorm, suddenly and quickly. 

     

    “It was so much easier,” she cries, “to leave Krypton behind when all I felt was anger and pain. And all I felt on Earth was the love of my family. But now they’re both here and I'm still mad and I’m still hurt but they’re still here .”

     

    She doesn't look at Lena as she speaks, she keeps her eyes firmly on the books on the small table in front of her. She feels rather than sees the way Lena’s hands travel from her hand to her arm and then pulls Kara closer, letting the blonde rest her head on her shoulder and continue without abandon as she softly caresses her back in comfort. 

     

    “I just—” Kara stops, her own sobs cutting her off. “It’s always one thing or the other. It's either be human, on Earth. Or be Kryptonian, on Argo. And I can't find a balance. I could never find a balance. Now I don't know what to do.” 

     

    Lena sits in silence with her for a few minutes, letting Kara’s sob get smaller and smaller on their own, until the blonde is breathing better than before. 

     

    “I’m not sure there’s a right answer, Kara,” she says, her fingers tangling in golden locks. “But whatever you do, I know it’ll be the best one. If you never want to speak to them again, that’s okay. If you want to stay here until you have the best relationship you can with them, that’d be okay, too. If you want to just be Kara Zor-El, or just Kara Danvers, or just Supergirl, I’ll love each version of you. Because I’ll love any Kara I have in front of me.”

     

    The tears that fall from Kara’s eyes are quiet now, no sobs, no choking. When she finally moves from Lena’s shoulder, Lena wipes Kara’s tears away with her fingertips and Kara looks at her with the softest of looks. Lena feels breathless at the sight of it. She shallows at the intensity of the moment and for a second, Kara thinks Lena is going to kiss her— Kara’s eyes fall to her lips and she breathes in, shakily, but then, her eyes find their intertwined hands. 

     

    “I love you, too,” Kara says, to fill the silence, “you know that, right?”

     

    “I know that,” Lena whispers, squeezing her hand. “I might not get what you’re going through. But I'll listen and carry everything you let me with you.”

     

    “Even if it's a burden?” Kara whispers, tiredly.

     

    “Even then.”

     

    Kara’s head sags and ends up resting on Lena’s chest. She’s barely sitting up on the bed, now— her body is turned and her legs are pulled close to her chest, Kara’s almost on top of Lena. And it’s in that position, she feels herself relax. 

     

    “Promise me something then,” she says, right above Lena’s heart.

     

    “Anything.”

     

    “I’ll burden you, only if you burden me too,” she says. “Through friendship and marriage.”

     

    “I promise,” Lena answers and Kara can hear the small smile on her words.

     

     

    Lena in an all black suit looks gorgeous— and while everyone in the Lawmakers Guild will be looking at the lack of honors on her clothes, Kara can only focus on the sharpness of her jaw and the exposed freckle on her neck. 

     

    She walks into the room with her chin held high and determination in her eyes but her hand, firmly locked with Kara’s, slightly trembles. 

     

    The voices don’t stop when they walk into the room but a couple of people look down disapprovingly at their hands. Kara glares back at them and places the softest of kisses on Lena’s cheek. 

     

    “Good luck,” she whispers, keeping her face close to Lena’s, trying not to get distracted by the smell of her perfume. “You see the man with the black and white robes?” Lena nods, not uttering a word. “He’s part of the Lawmakers Guild, too. My mother always complained about him.”

     

    “And by the way he’s looking at us I’m gonna guess he’s not very fond of me,” Lena finishes, nodding. She finds Kara’s eyes and smirks. “Should we go say hello and piss him off more?” 

     

    Kara chuckles. “Is this how you survive all your galas?” 

     

    “It’s Lex’s method, actually,” Lena says, a sudden sadness in her eyes. “He used to say you needed to get the people that hated you out of the way first because the people that liked you would wait to speak to you. That way, you’d end every gala and social dinner on a good note.”

     

    “That’s really smart,” Kara says, thoughtfully. 

     

    Lena hums, “Well, Lex always did know how to control a crowd.

     

    Kara doesn’t really know what to say to that— like most times Lena mentions Lex or her family, she’s usually just doing an offhand comment, sharing a small memory from her younger years, almost thoughtlessly until she remembers who the people in her stories are. Every time Kara has tried to cheer her up, it just made Lena’s look even more uncomfortable than after she had brought up her genocidal family, so now, she ignores it.

     

    “That’s Ol-Irus,” Kara explains, instead. Motioning to the tall man with black and blue robes, multiple squares of gold along his clothes. “He’s the keeper of The Book of Rao.” 

     

    “That’s still a thing?” Lena asks, surprised. A small box hovers in front of them, multiple glasses with drinks on them. Kara picks one for herself and Lena picks two, dropping Kara’s hand.

     

    “It is,” Kara nods. “It is said it was written by Rao itself with one of Nightwing’s lost feathers and the ashes left by Flamebird.”

     

    “Why doesn’t the Religious Guild keep it?” Lena asks, as she looks around the room, appreciating the wooden walls and high windows.

     

    “Security, tradition,” Kara says, shrugging. “Honestly, I don’t know. But people aligned with the Religious Guild try to have as little possessions as they can because their bodies and souls belong to Rao.”

     

    Lena nods, frowning, and Kara laughs when she sees it, getting an eyeroll out of Lena.

     

    “I know you’re not too fond of all that,” Kara says, chuckling slowly. “Still funny to see your face scrunching up at the idea of not having things .”

     

    “I like my things , Kara,” Lena says in her defense, pushing her shoulder against Kara’s, “thank you very much.”

     

    They reach their first group of people, the three of them wearing similar clothes and it’s only then that they notice the small squares are actually ripped book pages. 

     

    The taller man’s hair is as perfectly cut as his beard, black hair over dark skin. The woman beside him holds herself regal on her black and purple robes, a few less ripped pages on her clothes. And the younger man is comfortable enough in the other two's presence, just enough for Kara and Lena to realize they must be related but physically, at least, he does not look like their son. 

     

    “Ol-Irus,” Lena says, elegantly, as they reach them, extending her hand to offer him the drink. “It's nice to meet you.”

     

    He looks her up and down, a bored expression in his face. “I don't drink,” he says.

     

    Lena's smile falters awkwardly and she places her extra glass on a close by table. The decay in her confident facade is subtle, subtle enough that Kara knows it was only noticed by her. 

     

    “Juice then?” Lena asks, unbothered. “I haven’t tried them all yet but I really liked the Stilen juice.”

     

    He nods, unimpressed. A tray of drinks passes by him and he picks up the greenish liquid himself, making  Lena lose her advantage, now being unable to start up the conversation how she wanted. The woman standing besides Ol-Irus looks them up and down, almost as if deciding whether she should let the awkward air settle until Kara and Lena leave or if she should speak to them instead. She seems to take pity on them— or just hates her companions enough to speak to them.

     

    “Forgive my husband,” she says, smiling kindly at Lena. “He’s just always in a bad mood when so many people are close to the book. I’m Vita-Irus.”

     

    Kara smiles at her and steps a little closer to Lena as she vows slightly in respect, one of her hands comes to rest on Lena’s lower back. Vita’s eyes catch the movement but her expression doesn’t share her inner thoughts and Kara cannot tell if she’s friend or foe about their relationship— even when her husband’s opinion is very clear by the disdain in his eyes.

     

    “This is Lir-Al,” she says, pointing to the younger man, “he’s Ol’s successor.”

     

    “For the Book of Rao, right?” Lena asks, sounding curious. Kara almost laughs because she knows Lena’s not that curious, not really. She’s a curious person in general but when it comes to religious things, she’s always bored or uninterested in them. It’s why she’s been slowly preparing for all future trials except the Religious Guild trial— changing the subject, saying she’s tried, each time the subject comes up. 

     

    “I’m not trying to be disrespectful, Kara,” Lena had said a few days ago. “I understand and accept your beliefs, I just don’t appreciate the idea of organized religion.” 

     

    “I won’t inherit the book itself,” Lir says in a grave and charming voice, the green shirt he’s wearing tenses under his muscles as moves his hand to speak. “I’ll just become the keeper of the book.”

     

    “That sounds interesting,” Lena says. “What does that job entail?”

     

    “It’s a job of the utmost importance,” Ol-Irus says before Lir can answer, his voice loud and commanding. “We assure that the Book of Rao remains safe from the hands of thieves.”

     

    “Who would want to steal an old book?” Lena says, chuckling lightly. Not unkindly but not respectful enough for Ol to take it as the joke that it was. 

     

    “Those are the words of Rao himself!” He exclaims, loudly, making a few heads turn, surprised, in their direction at the outburst. “There’s always rebels and terrorists who want it in their possession to coerce the High Council into doing their bidding.” 

     

    “Lena was not trying to diminish your job,” Kara says when his voice goes high enough to make Lena’s back tense even under her touch. “And I do not think it is that extreme, sir. There hasn’t been an attempt at stealing the book since your position was created.”

     

    “That does not make it any less important!” He exclaims, offended.

     

    “I did not say that,” Kara answers.

     

    “They’re not disrespecting our job, Ol,” Lir-Al says, chuckling lightly as if deescalating this conversation with Ol-Irus is a common occurrence. “I understand Lena’s skepticism, though. It’s hard to believe in the magnitude of the book without having ever seen it. Lena,” he says, kindly, “would you like to?”

     

    Ol-Irus, frown firmly in his face, makes a small noise of displeasure but doesn’t say anything otherwise. Kara understands why— she’s only seen the Book of Rao once. As all kryptonian children do, when they turn ten and their teachers start to tour the Guilds. Not so they could decide where to go in the future— that was set long before they were born— but so they could see how their world worked.

     

    She saw, as a ten year old, the biggest book she’d ever seen— bigger than her father’s or her mother’s or her grandfather’s— locked behind a glass and could still see the gold on it, the dust and the old pages. Standing in front of the book, seeing the drawings of the first kryptonians, the writings of the first few prayers and the legends of Krypton. 

     

    Jealousy tugs at her heartstrings when Lir-Al hand softly touches Lena’s arms, guiding her towards the hallway and into the room that holds the book. 

     

    “Do try not to take him so seriously,” Vita tells her, setting closer towards Kara as she watches her husband walk away with something akin to displeasure and annoyment. “He believes that book is the single most important thing in the universe.”

     

    “It is important but I don’t know about universally important,” Kara says, politely, but chuckling lightly. The woman rolls her eyes with a smirk.

     

    “Exactly,” she says, then lower, pained and irritated. “He loves that goddamn book more than me. I’d burn it if I could."

     

    The words surprise Kara— Kryptonians are never this open about their dislikes. They say subtle words and let you read between the lines instead of outright saying it. But Vita keeps on talking as if she’s said nothing at all, asking Kara about her honors and sharing the stories of the seven ripped book pages on her own gown. 

     

    Kara keeps politely speaking to her, knowing she could be a good ally, eventually— either because one more person liking Lena is a good thing or because she could help sweeten her husband’s ear about them.

     

    Lir-Al joins them, explaining Lena has gone to the bathroom after seeing the book. “I’d like to say she was so captivated by the book she needed a moment to gather herself,” he says, with a smile before he leans closer to Kara, speaking softly, “but I think the most captivating thing in the room would be Miss Luthor herself, don’t you agree, Kara?”

     

    Kara smiles, chuckling awkwardly at the sudden closeness between them before she realizes what he said and she frowns. The well known feeling of jealousy rises up in her chest, until it sits heavy on her heart and turns off the alarms of anger. “I do agree,” she says, trying to remain polite but she knows her jaw is clenched anyway. “Lena has captivated my mind for years now and I imagine she’ll do so for many more years after we are bonded .”

     

    “Right,” Lir chuckles, suddenly nervous at the maddening fire in Kara’s eyes. He had obviously tried to catch what kind of relationship they had— to see if it was the kind of relationship Alura had alluded to, open to any and all desires and free of any real love. 

     

    Maybe Kara should have been more relaxed at shutting down Lir-Al’s interest for Lena— or maybe she shouldn’t have shut it down at all, if Lena’s interested in him. But the thought of Lena with someone else turns her stomach inside out and makes the drink in her mouth feel as bitter as her thoughts.

     

    The warm touch of Lena’s hand falls on her elbow and Kara relaxes instantly, turning towards her— it’s the first time she realizes how in tune she is with Lena. Anyone could have touched her to get her attention and yet the warmth that goes through her is obviously and always because of Lena. 

     

    The smile on her face disappears as soon as she notices Lena’s face. She’s not frowning, not really. But she’s not as relaxed as when they came in— the confident glint in her eyes is now gone, her shoulders are tensed and her back is straight. 

     

    “Are you alright?” Kara asks, her hand coming to rest between her shoulder blades and she begins rubbing her back.

     

    Whatever Lena was going to answer is interrupted by Ol-Irus drunkenly coming back. He walks into the room with uneven steps, holding onto his throat as he coughs, right before he falls down to the floor, the glass in his hand shattering.

     

    Some people scream and some run to him. Felra kneels beside him and checks his pulse, she only murmurs how weak it is but the cutting silence in the room allows her words to be heard by everyone. Vita stands before her husband, hand clutched to her chest, but barely looks at him. 

     

    “How could this happen?” She asks. 

     

    “This is poison,” Kye-Val, the Military Guild’s councilman, says. “Liquid boradium.” 

     

    Krypton’s most precious metal. It used to be found in the corners of the Gold Volcano back on Krypton, only those brave enough to climb it would be able to get it. But its poisonous qualities on its liquid form wasn’t why the Guilds used it— they used it for their electronics in solid form, the perfect conductor to stand the energy. 

     

    Overall, the boradium is all around Krypton but it would take ridiculous amounts of cold to turn it into a liquid. No one but the Science Guild had liquid boradium— safely locked away in the general and guarded table of elements for investigative purposes. Unless someone else had figured out a way to speed up the freezing process— and Kara knows the whispers around the room are entirely about that fear.

     

    Kara hates that quote about the calm before the storm— mostly, because she’s found it to be true. As a couple of medics take Ol-Irus away, the room is quiet and it feels like someone has suddenly opened a window and let the cold air come sweeping through the room. 

     

    But the moment one of the guards comes running into the room, screaming “It’s missing! The Book of Rao is missing!” , the collective gasps in the room almost take Kara’s breath away.

     

    “ You! ” Ayala says, pure venom in her voice. But the finger Kara expected to be pointed at her, is fully directed at Lena. “You did this!”

     

    Kara feels Lena’s entire body tense at the accusation and she moves along with Lena when she takes a step back. “Why would I do this?” she asks, and even though her voice sounds firm, Kara can hear the trembling fear and resignation all at once as she is, again, being pointed at like she’s the bad guy behind it.

     

    “I heard you two arguing before,” Ayala says, looking around the room, trying to find allies in her conclusion. Some people watch Lena with a sudden new mistrust. “Arguing about the very same thing that now is missing.”

     

    “But why would I kill— poison him,” she corrects herself, hoping the former isn’t the case. “I don’t care about the book.”

     

    “I showed you the book before,” Lir-Al says, his eyes moving from Lena to the door where his unconscious mentor left, and there’s a sudden sharpness in his stance— the need to find the guilty when a loved one is hurt. “You said it was magnificent.”

     

    “Okay,” Kara steps up, partly covering Lena from the angry stares of the kryptonians in the room. She speaks before Lena accidentally disrespects the book, given her lack of interest in religion, and potentially angers more people  in her attempts of  defending herself. “Everyone calm down,” she says, hands on her hips. “Lena didn’t do anything.”

     

    “Lena,” Alura says, her voice kinder than Kara’s ever heard it directed at Lena, “if you stole it, just return it now and we will forget this happened. I’m sure we’ll be able to counterattack the poison in Ol’s blood, too.”

     

    “I didn’t steal the damn book,” Lena says, loudly. The accent Kara loves to tease her so much about coming out strong and thick. “Nor did I poison anyone.”
