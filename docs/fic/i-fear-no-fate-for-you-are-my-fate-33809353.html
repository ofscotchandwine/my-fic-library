    Detective Margaret Sawyer moved here over six months ago and there’s been barely a peaceful moment to sit down and just kick back. Not for more than a few hours anyway. She’s from Gotham, for pete’s sake. National City was supposed to be boring, a breeze of a job that she desperately needed.

    “No, I’m good,” she reassures him, offering him a dimpled smile before adding, “I’ve been meaning to get some dental work done anyway.”

    He chuckles, shaking his head before breathing out a heavy sigh. “We’re going to have to report this to Cadmus, aren’t we?”

    She looks over him, taking in the tense shoulders, the folded arms across his chest. Even though it's dark, she doesn't need her sight to feel the way the car is rocking with the force of his leg shaking. 

    “It can wait. You seem fine to me now.”

    His shoulders slump down and he's reaching once again for his bracelets. “Do you think it’s something to do with these? They’ve been feeling weird all day.”

The power dampening cuffs are supposed to make sure that things like what happened earlier, didn't happen at all, to help manage an alien’s powers.     “That red-headed human?”

    She hums quietly, a growing smile on her face as she thinks about seeing those pretty brown eyes again. “We hit it off real good last time.”

They need to get over—whoa,” she stumbles and stops in her tracks when she bumps into his back. “You okay over there?” she asks when she realizes that Brian has completely stopped moving, noticing the way his shoulders seem to tense.

She directs her gaze downwards only to gasp at seeing his hands completely engulfed in flames that seem to keep growing and growing in intensity.

    ”Thank you for being there,” she murmurs before releasing Lillian’s hand.

National City is so… sunny.”

They have never stayed too long whenever they used to visit as a family and over the last few years, every time Lillian had to make the rounds at the branch here, Lena always knew to expect a phone call from her mother, specifically to complain about the city. No matter what it was, her mother always found something to rant about being here. 

    Lena doesn't share this sentiment though.

    There was just something about the city that had kept her happily returning and there was a quiet part of her that always hoped to stay longer than work ever allowed. It wasn’t even that she knew anybody here or had any romantic entanglements living in the city. But still, her restless heart always seemed to feel so settled whenever she set foot on the tarmac of National City International Airport. 

    As if she had returned home. 

    Lena had been LuthorCorp’s CTO, freshly-promoted from where she had been in R&D, just right before Lex unleashed his own brand of chaos and destruction throughout the entire country nearly six years ago. After that world-altering event, there was a rushed decision made by the company's board, uncontested, that Lillian would replace Lex until they deemed Lena ready to take over. 

    She herself was more than content to stay in her role as CTO but she knew that Lillian wasn’t looking to permanently fill in the position that was rightfully hers as the remaining Luthor heir. The night before Lillian took the helm, she had even promised Lena that as the only Luthors left, LuthorCorp was going to be theirs, not Lillian’s but theirs, and she kept her word.

She did pause, however momentarily, when Lena brought up relocating and transforming the National City branch into their official HQ. Lena remembers the flash of alarm that passed her mother's face, which she figured was largely because of how much Lillian hates the city.

Even though the Luthor reputation is secure and stronger than before, with National City’s growing alien population, Lena wanted to make a further point that the Luthor name is no longer one to be feared.

Out of all the things that she has ever been sure about, Lena feels it in her bones that National City is where she’s meant to be. She doesn't think there's anybody else in the world that's as stubborn as her mother, apart from herself, of course. And though Lena can't help but bring it up, her natural curiosity against her mother's pragmatism, the day has been much too long and much too exhausting for her to get into it with her mother right now.

    “Good evening, Alana.” Once she opens the door, she turns to shoot her assistant a tired and grateful smile when she’s then handed a steaming cup of tea. “Oh, thank you, Alana. Were there any calls while we were in the meeting?” she asks before taking a sip.

I have forwarded all the messages to the tablet on your desk in case you wanted to get to them immediately.” She turns to Lillian. “Can I get you anything to drink, Mrs. Luthor? There is an unopened bottle of your favorite red stocked in the minibar of Miss Luthor’s office.”

There’s no doubt that he’ll try calling again but Lena has absolutely no interest in working with him.     “The Tribune will be there?”

    Lena chuckles at the unsubtle disdain in Lillian’s voice. “Cat Grant also RSVP’d yes, so she will be too.” She looks up from her tablet in time to catch the expression on Lillian’s face. “Oh, please mother, don't start on your rivalry with Cat again. We want to keep the good relationship that we have with the media.”

    Lena lets out an unladylike snort before breathing out a discerning sigh. “Like I could ever believe that.” 

    “Make me a deal then.”

    She can’t help but laugh. She should have known this is where Lillian would eventually direct the conversation. Her mother just cannot resist making any kind of deal to get what she wants. Lena swears that she must get some kind of thrill from it.

    “This is about those security hires you wanted me to take on, isn’t it?” she asks, cutting right down to the chase. 

    Ever since the move to National City was confirmed, Lillian had been on her about hiring a security team that she herself personally vouched. She had backed down after a week of Lena refusing to even see them. After all, she has her own team that she has had for years and they have not failed her. Especially now that she’s in a new city, having a team she knows and trusts is a comfort.

    She should have seen that Lillian was once again, playing the long game and waiting for the perfect moment to bring it back up. 

    “I’m not sure what you mean. You know how much I love a good negotiation.” 

    “Sure,” she mutters, petulant and disbelieving in response to the innocent expression on her mother’s face.

    Lena eventually sighs. She at the very least knows when to pick her battles and she really does not want to take a chance with the gala. Everything needs to go well tomorrow night.

    “Fine,” she concedes begrudgingly before pointing her finger at Lillian. “I’m not getting rid of my team though. Your people are just going to have to work with them.”

    Lena scoffs. “Of course not. Who would I even invite?”

    “So things between you and Jack are really over?” Lillian asks, curious and maybe a little disappointed. 

    She shakes her head, setting her tablet down. Her heart aches a little from missing Jack. After all, they had been together for over two years.

    “I know that you really liked him but he didn't want to make the move here and I didn't want to stay. So he and I agreed that it’s better to end things while we still like each other. This way we can still work together on the nanotech with minimum awkward tension between us.”

    “Plus, I don't exactly have many people around me that I can trust.”

    “I’m glad to hear it, darling,” her mother quietly replies.
